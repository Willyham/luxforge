# Luxforge v0.1.0 — release notes draft

**Preparation draft. No qualified v0.1 release exists.** A pushed tag publishes a pre-release build candidate; this text becomes its release notes only after the [release preparation gates](../design/release-v0.1.md#acceptance) pass, `cargo xtask release-verify` reports the packet ready and the release fields below hold verified facts and links. Complete the practical preview checks in the [readiness assessment](../../reports/v0.1-code-audit.md#pre-release-readiness-re-audit) first; unfinished checks must not be presented as passing.

Luxforge is a free, open-source, non-destructive photo editor. This first preview brings browsing, picking and editing into a small desktop workspace, with the same operations available to scripts through a discoverable JSON command service.

## What's in it

- **Select:** browse folders and cards by event and moment, pick photographs, develop picks into the catalog by Year › Place › Day, organize collections, send unedited photographs back and locate missing originals.
- **Develop:** persistent history, undo and redo, named versions and Compare with the original, while the original files stay untouched.
- **Adjustments:** white balance, exposure and tone, the tone curve, capture sharpening and noise reduction, Texture, Clarity and Dehaze, the colour mixer with colour grading, and the vignette.
- **Geometry:** crop, straighten, rotate and reflect, and supported lens distortion and perspective corrections.
- **Local adjustments:** linear and radial gradients and brushes; luminance and colour range masks are an experiment you turn on in Settings › Experiments.
- **Corrections:** Clone and Heal.
- **RAW:** supported recording modes developed from sensor data, with continuous white balance in kelvin and the Standard look.
- **Merges:** HDR merges of RAW brackets and panorama merges, kept as stacks with their frames.
- **Presets and settings:** presets, Lightroom preset import, copy and paste of chosen settings, and Auto tone per photograph.
- **Export:** JPEG, WebP and 8- or 16-bit TIFF, with Web, Print and Master presets and your own; resizing, file-size limits, metadata levels and output sharpening. Exports are new files: originals and existing files are never overwritten.
- **Seeing your work:** the histogram and clipping, image information, a minimap at high zoom, themes and live performance counters.
- **Scripting:** `luxforge-json` and the running-session client `luxforge-ctl`, both inside the download.
- **Updates:** Luxforge checks for updates and installs them when you choose, through Sparkle on macOS and its own updater on Linux.

Reconcile this list with the frozen commit's [feature status](../features.md) before publication. Generative Remove and Replace are behind an experiment that is off by default and are not part of this release.

## Installing

**macOS.** Open the disk image (`.dmg`), drag **Luxforge** onto the **Applications** folder beside it, eject the disk image and open Luxforge from Applications. A signed and notarized build passes macOS's normal checks without disabling Gatekeeper or removing quarantine. Hosted signing is optional: make these instructions match the candidate's actual signing status. Opened from anywhere else, Luxforge offers to move itself into Applications, the only place it can update itself from. The ZIP beside the disk image holds the same app and is the update archive. The command-line tools are in `Luxforge.app/Contents/Helpers/`; use their full path or add that folder to your PATH yourself. Nothing changes your shell configuration.

**Linux.** Extract the tarball and run `Luxforge/luxforge`; both command-line tools and the runtime resources are beside it. `./Luxforge/desktop-integration.sh install` adds Luxforge to your application launcher for you only, and `uninstall` removes that entry again. `INSTALL.txt` in the folder lists the requirements: a Vulkan-capable driver, the X11 or Wayland libraries it names and a working XDG desktop portal with a file-chooser backend. Luxforge updates itself in place when you can write its folder. Nothing needs root, and nothing changes your PATH.

## Your data

Luxforge keeps its catalog, settings and logs outside the application:

| | macOS | Linux |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Catalog and settings | `~/Library/Application Support/Luxforge` | `~/.config/luxforge` and `~/.local/share/luxforge` (or under `XDG_CONFIG_HOME` and `XDG_DATA_HOME`) |
| Logs | `~/Library/Logs/Luxforge` | `~/.local/state/luxforge/logs` (or under `XDG_STATE_HOME`) |

A catalog you created elsewhere stays where you chose. Updating or removing the application leaves this data and every original in place. The catalog refers to your originals; it is not a backup of them. Back up originals and catalogs, and back up a catalog with Luxforge closed, together with its stored artifacts.

## Updates

Luxforge checks for updates automatically (Settings › General › Updates turns this off) and sends only its version to `api.luxforge.app`. A new version appears in a small card at the bottom of the workspace; it installs when you choose, or by itself if you turn on **Install updates automatically**. Until updates are published for a version, download the new version and replace the application by hand.

## Preview limits

This is pre-release software. The current catalog format is **2**. Only the current catalog, recipe, API and module formats are supported; there are no migrations or compatibility guarantees between preview builds. Data a version cannot read is refused, never rewritten: a version that needs a new catalog says so, and the version you have keeps opening your current one. Keep the previous app and a data backup when trying an update.

RAW files open by the bundled camera and recording-mode catalog. A listed camera does not mean every compression or resolution mode works. Nikon High Efficiency NEF is refused; phone HEIC and Apple ProRAW are unqualified. JPEG input supports the current sRGB and greyscale subset. The [user guide](../user-guide.md#raw-development) has the supported scope.

The GPU renders the finished picture and exports within a declared tolerance of the CPU reference. Motion may use a reduced source and is sharp again at rest. A machine whose GPU stage cannot run uses the reference renderer and says so. Cold shader compilation and heavy spatial stacks can delay work. Total process and GPU memory, and photographic Detail and RAW quality, are not universally qualified; no figure here is a performance or memory guarantee.

Luxforge sends no usage statistics in this release. MCP, Lightroom catalog import and Lightroom slider alignment are planned. Windows, Linux ARM64, Intel Macs and Linux distributions other than the tested baseline are outside this release. Native Linux support is unqualified until its release gate passes; a tarball or a hosted software-renderer result alone does not qualify it. Optimised JPEG, file-size limits and WebP can use substantial memory on large photographs; their export plans say so. Recheck these statements against the frozen candidate.

## Help and reports

The [user guide](../user-guide.md) covers the controls and scripting. Report problems through [GitHub issues](https://github.com/Willyham/luxforge/issues) with:

- the version (`/Applications/Luxforge.app/Contents/MacOS/luxforge --version` on macOS, `Luxforge/luxforge --version` on Linux), OS, chip or GPU, and for a RAW file the camera and recording mode;
- the steps to reproduce, and the error message shown (Copy copies it);
- a short excerpt of the log from the folder above, if it helps.

Check logs and screenshots for private file paths or locations before sharing them. Photographs and catalogs are optional; share them only if you want to.

Luxforge is GPL-3.0-or-later. The downloads include the project and third-party notices, and the matching source archive is published beside them. The distribution-specific source, native and asset review is still open; this draft does not claim it is complete.
