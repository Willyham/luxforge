# Instant previews: the GPU's contract

Status: implemented. Since stage 5 of the [GPU-first](gpu-first.md) plan the GPU draws every frame a person sees, in motion and at rest, wherever it can draw the stack, and the reference renderer draws the rest as whole frames. This design is the contract between the two: which renderer draws each frame, how a frame reaches the screen, what a frame may feed and what it never feeds. How the GPU draws is in [GPU previews](gpu-preview.md), and what its frames are held to in [GPU-first](gpu-first.md#the-contract). The `rendered` tier's scenarios and the release gate, `cargo xtask gpu-qualification`, prove the contract on the M4; its latencies against the 2026-10-04 baseline are outstanding (the GPU-first plan's TASK-011). No recipe, history, API mutation or export behaviour depends on which renderer drew a frame on screen.

## Goal

A slider dragged back and forth wildly, at any zoom, shows the value under the pointer within two display frames on the owner's M4 Mac, with every Basic unit active, on 24 MP and 60 MP JPEGs, under a rotated crop, on RAW sources and with Presence and Detail in the stack. The slider, RAW Exposure, burst and settled-histogram thresholds are the [performance plan's provisional budgets](../specs/performance.md#provisional-budgets); a miss is reported with its figures.

"Presented" keeps its harness meaning: the desktop update in which the frame became the photo surface's source, drawn by the redraw that update requests. It is not scanout. A GPU frame is timed from its input to the surface's first draw of the plan of that input's revision (`surface_frame_drawn`).

## What draws each frame

| Frame | The GPU draws it | Where a machine with a GPU cannot | A session without a GPU |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A drag tick at Fit and below 100% | The drafted stack's plan at the reduced stage of the view, from the source the GPU holds ([below 100%](gpu-preview.md#below-100)) | Held for a reason that passes; otherwise one reference whole frame at a time, reduced to the view | The [CPU proxy](#the-cpu-proxy-a-session-without-a-gpu) at the view's bounds |
| A drag tick at 100% and above | The plan over the visible region at full scale; past `REDUCED_AFTER_BYTES` the reduced stage scaled to the view, the softer frame ([at 100% and above](gpu-preview.md#at-100-and-above)) | As above, unreduced | The CPU proxy at Fit's bounds, magnified |
| The picture at rest at Fit and below 100% | The stack at full resolution in tiles, reduced to the view ([the picture at rest](gpu-preview.md#the-picture-at-rest)) | The reference's exact whole frame reduced to the view | The same |
| The picture at rest at 100% and above | The view plan over the visible region | The reference's exact whole frame | The same |
| Histogram and clipping counts | Counted over the picture at rest's full-resolution tiles; in motion the displayed frame's, marked updating | The reference frame's report | The reference frame's report; during a proxy drag the last report, marked updating |
| A crop draft's input stage at Fit | The layer prefix's picture at rest | The reference's exact stage reduced to the view | The same |
| A sample or pixel read | One GPU tile around the pixel, by the desktop's GPU tile worker | The reference's read, naming why | The same |
| Export | GPU tiles streamed in bands | The reference export | The same |

**A drag the GPU draws.** The gesture's `draft.set` is answered synchronously with the tick's preview job, which carries the draft's GPU plan, planned on the catalog owner in `O(layers)` with no pixel read; the photo surface draws it in the frame after the update that handled the input, over a boundary derived on the GPU from the photograph's source it holds ([a tick](gpu-preview.md#a-tick)). Nothing goes to the preview worker for that tick. Every layer a panel builds plans on the GPU, colour in Detail's stage before a resample included; a RAW white-balance drag adds the change as a leading step over the developed planes ([below](#a-raw-white-balance-during-a-drag)), and a geometry drag on a stack of geometry alone is planned from the source.

**A drag the GPU refuses**, on a machine with a GPU (`crates/luxforge-app/src/app/motion.rs`, [a tick the GPU does not draw](gpu-preview.md#a-tick-the-gpu-does-not-draw)):

- **Held** for a reason that passes within a tick or an upload — `compiling` until the status bar's half-second threshold (`COMPILING_AFTER`), `unchanged`, `unplannable` while no view is known, and the boundary, source and surface waits. The tick queues nothing and the frame on screen stays. A hold that lasts half a second renders the newest held tick's reference frame; the desktop looks at a held tick every 50 ms while it holds, and that is the only timer.
- **Rendered** for any other reason — a layer with no GPU program, a slot past the budget even at the reduced stage, a surface fallback that lasts. The reference renders the whole drafted stack, its report and its reduction to the view where the view draws the stage smaller than it is. One such job is in flight at a time and no tick supersedes it: a newer tick's job waits on the desktop, replacing any older one, and is queued once the frame in flight has been taken up. The status bar's notice names the reason's class.

**At rest** the GPU presents a committed stack — after a commit, a release, a history selection, a preset, Return to current or a view change — with no CPU render (`Editor::present_on_gpu`, `crates/luxforge-app/src/app/gpu_counts.rs`): no job is queued, and the queue is cancelled so nothing older reaches the screen. Its counts become the presented content's report and are handed to the owner's store, so an API client's `analysis.request` for it is a hit. The reference renders the stack — its exact whole frame and report, reduced to the view where the view draws the stage smaller than it is — wherever the GPU cannot present it: before the surface has checked its GPU stage, an open's first picture on a cold cache (an open whose picture at rest's programs are already compiled is the GPU's from its first picture, [warming](gpu-preview.md#warming-at-launch-and-open)), a plan or tiles the GPU cannot draw, a comparison, a crop draft's input stage in flight, a content the surface refused after it was presented, or a view plan whose programs are still compiling after half a second, during which the status bar says "Preparing GPU render". A shorter compile is waited out under the frame on screen, marked rendering. The program set is warmed in the background at each open and committed stack, the picture on screen's programs first ([warming](gpu-preview.md#warming-at-launch-and-open)).

**Changing the view.** At 100% and above a zoom or a pan is planned at once: the GPU's view plan over the new region where the GPU presents the stack, the reference's whole frame, which holds every view, where it does not. Where the reference's exact frame is the picture at Fit and below 100%, resizing the window, toggling a panel, changing the display scale or zooming back from a percentage view queues a reduction-only job (`PreviewIntent::Reduce`) over the retained exact frame, with no source development, recipe render or histogram reduction; where the GPU presents the stack the same changes plan its picture again for the new view. No pause in a gesture renders anything: there is no quiet timer, and a pause settles nothing.

## One frame per hop

Measured on the M4 with per-leg timings: the owner answers `draft.set` and plans the preview job in under 0.2 ms, the desktop's own update, model derivation and view take under 0.15 ms together, and every message handed back into the update loop through the runtime — a task result, a worker's wake, an image allocation's answer — arrives about 8 ms later, one frame of the 120 Hz display. A redraw is always in flight during a drag, the main thread waits on its present, and a message that arrives meanwhile waits with it. The per-input path therefore has as few runtime hops as its work allows ([performance rule 12](../engineering/performance-rules.md#rules)):

- The gesture's `draft.set` and its preview job are synchronous owner calls on the desktop thread: `O(layers)` requests, the owner doing no frame work by rule. A `draft.set` whose plan reads a pixel is refused at once rather than waited for, and is sent again on the blocking pool ([draft memo](detail.md#pixel-reads-off-the-owner)). A slider move sends `draft.set` at once; there is no slider tick.
- The photograph is drawn by a photo-surface primitive that owns its full-image texture slot and its GPU stage: a raster handed to the view is written to the slot in the frame that draws it, and a GPU plan is evaluated in the surface's own `prepare`, so no allocation round trip stands between a frame and the screen, and a redraw with no new frame writes nothing. Iced keeps one pipeline for every primitive of the type, so the slots are keyed by the surface id each primitive carries; a surface a frame does not draw is released by the pipeline's end-of-frame trim. The clipping overlay, the mask coverage and the crop draft's input stage are drawn by the same primitive from frames of their own, one holder in the desktop owning them all. At a percentage zoom the surface hands the renderer only the part of the zoomed box on screen, inside the device's 8192 px limit, and a frame wider or taller than that limit, such as a 60 MP exact frame at 100%, is held in a grid of textures that meet without a seam.
- The preview and overlay workers wake the desktop when a result is ready, through one channel subscription; nothing wakes when nothing has finished. Each is one persistent thread on the latest-job primitive (`luxforge_core::latest`), which takes the pending job itself the moment the active one has handed over its last result; at most two finished results wait for the desktop. One `Poll` takes up every finished result that presents nothing and at most one frame, and asks for another only while a result still waits.

## The reference renderer's frames

The preview worker renders the reference's frames: one persistent worker, one active and one replaceable pending job, each tagged with a generation (`crates/luxforge-core/src/preview/worker.rs`). A job is one of three:

1. **The exact whole frame**, from the job's one compilation, which its evaluation made on the catalog owner: reduced into its report when the job asks to be analysed, and reduced to the view's bounds where the view draws the stage smaller than it is (`ExactOutcome::display`, `render::reduce_to_view`), by an area-weighted average of its linear light — the reference frame the GPU's picture at rest is held to. Its counts come from the full exact raster, never its reduction. The desktop retains the exact raster for a later zoom and for the clipping overlay, and uploads the reduced frame while a view smaller than the stage is selected. A frame reduced from the exact one carries `reduced` and no approximation.
2. **A reduction** of a retained exact frame to new bounds (`PreviewIntent::Reduce`), sharing the immutable raster and rendering nothing.
3. **The CPU proxy** of a drag tick in a session without a GPU ([below](#the-cpu-proxy-a-session-without-a-gpu)), its one phase.

The exact phase is cancellable. Every rasterizing pass, the resample, the row pass both pixel domains share and the reducer check a cancellation token at chunk granularity, so a newer request stops an exact phase within about a millisecond of asking and returns a `cancelled` error rather than a frame. The queue delivers that outcome under the job's own generation, carrying no frame, so whatever waits for one generation learns that it has ended; a job replaced in the pending slot never starts, and `PreviewQueue::request_replacing` names it in the same step as the request that replaces it. Each job carries two tokens: a newer request raises its **superseded** token and `PreviewQueue::cancel` its **abandoned** one, which also supersedes it. The exact phase reads the superseded token; the CPU proxy reads only the abandoned one, so the proxy frame of a value the pointer has just left is still rendered and delivered, because it is newer than anything on screen.

Exactness claims are about these frames: fixtures, exact-buffer tests and the [histogram contract](basic-and-histogram.md#histogram-and-clipping-contract) hold the reference, and what the GPU renders, its counts included, is held to it within the declared tolerance ([GPU-first](gpu-first.md#the-contract)). No proxy raster is ever reduced into a report.

### Progress of a long exact phase

A whole-frame exact phase with large spatial work, such as one behind heavy Detail, can run for seconds (about 0.5 s at 24 MP and 2.5 s at 60 MP with Detail on the M4, [Detail](detail.md#proposals-with-recorded-defaults)). Where the reference renderer draws the picture, that wait is shown on the photograph itself, not only in the status bar or the Performance section, and only when it is long enough to matter. Where the GPU draws the stack no exact phase runs.

- **What is measured.** The truthful extent of a whole-frame render is its spatial tiles: every spatial operation's cost is in them, and a render can count them before it starts. When a render's cancellation token carries a progress meter (`Cancel::with_progress`, `RenderProgress`), each driver plans every spatial entry's tile count over the stage it receives before its segment loop and advances the meter by each finished tile, so the reading never moves backwards and finishes at exactly what was planned on both pixel domains. Decode, colour, resample and reduction stages are not counted; a stack with no spatial operation plans nothing and reports no extent. A token without a meter costs nothing.
- **Who reads it.** The preview worker meters a job's whole-frame exact phase and not the CPU proxy. `PreviewQueue::progress` answers the running phase's generation, elapsed time and counts while it runs and nothing once it ends, however it ends. The same fraction, floored to whole percent, is the `preview.render` activity's progress, so `activity.list` and the Performance section report it too.
- **No timer.** The worker wakes the desktop from the meter once the phase has run `PREVIEW_PROGRESS_QUIET` (250 ms), and then at most once every 50 ms; a phase that ends sooner wakes it only with its result. The desktop reads the reading whenever it derives the screen.
- **When the bar shows.** The desktop shows the bar once the phase is projected to take at least a second in all (`RENDER_BAR_PROJECTED`): its time so far over its finished share, trusted only after the 250 ms, with a phase that has finished no tile by then projected long. Once shown it stays with that generation's phase until the phase ends, so a fast finish never makes it flicker; a newer phase earns its own. The bar is a 3 px translucent track along the bottom of the part of the photograph on screen, filled in the accent to the finished share, drawn over the plain photograph at Fit and at a percentage (not over a crop draft's stage or a GPU frame); the status bar reads "Rendering… 42%" beside it unless a GPU frame names its own render. The evidence snapshot records `render_bar` and `render_progress` with every capture.
- **Not covered.** RAW development and redevelopment on the source worker and stacks without spatial work report no extent, so they show no bar; their status still reads "Rendering…".

## A crop draft's input stage

A crop draft shows the crop layer's input stage under its frame: the layers before the crop, a truncated preview job of that prefix (owner decision, 2026-09-26: display resolution at Fit, the exact stage only at a percentage zoom that needs it):

- **Drawn by the GPU at Fit.** At Fit, and at a percentage that draws the stage smaller than it is, the desktop asks for the stage at its display bounds with the GPU (`PreviewRequest::layers` with `gpu`): the owner plans the prefix's picture at rest over the prefix's own evaluation, its tiles at full resolution reduced to the bounds, and the photo surface draws it under the frame, turned and dimmed as the stage is, in place of a one-texel stand-in the presenter holds as the stage's frame; nothing is queued for the reference. The stage is on screen once the surface has drawn its last tile. Where the GPU cannot draw the prefix — the plan's reason, such as a pixel-stage layer, a conversion the surface cannot run, or a surface that refuses the tiles — or the stage already fits the bounds (`at-own-size`, where the reference's exact stage is the frame drawn), the reference renders it, named in the `crop_stage_reference` event, and the GPU is not asked again for that draft ([GPU previews](gpu-preview.md#the-picture-at-rest)).
- **The reference's frame, exact only when a zoom needs it.** Where the reference draws the stage at Fit, its job renders the prefix's exact frame and reduces it to the bounds by the area average, one phase, the reduced frame the one drawn; no report is reduced from the stage. A percentage that draws the stage at or above its size asks for the exact stage alone, once: the draft holds frames only, never the planned job, so the stage is planned again on an owner task from the entry it was planned from, and the answer is requested if the view still wants that phase and it is still the stage on screen. One stage plan or render is in flight at a time, and an answer the draft no longer wants (the draft ended, a Reapply moved the stage, the zoom moved back) is dropped. A zoom back hands the held frame, or the GPU's stage, over again without a render. Each frame is held for the view it serves until the draft ends. A frame is an RGBA raster with no lease on the source; a planned job holds its stack, and a RAW development's planes hold the source worker's [memory gate](raw-integration.md#allocation-and-liveness), so a draft that kept its job would keep a development the owner needs waiting for as long as it is open.
- **Never an input.** The stage is a drafting aid. Apply commits the draft's fields through `draft.commit`; pickers read the owner, never the stage; and the owner refuses a truncated job that asks to be analysed, while the desktop takes the stage's frames up before anything is reduced or retained as the photograph. No committed or reported byte depends on which frame of the stage is shown.
- **Supersession.** A newer request supersedes a stage the reference renders, which ends a starting draft explicitly, as a failed one does; it is never shielded or re-requested, because every such request but a view change comes from a change to the stack or selection that job was planned from. A stage the GPU draws is no job and is superseded by nothing.
- **Its texture.** The photo surface reserves the stage's texture at exactly its frame's size, so at Fit it is display-sized — the reference's reduced frame, or the GPU's one-texel stand-in beside the picture at rest's own reduced output — and a return to Fit from a percentage zoom releases the exact stage's texture rather than keeping it. The surface diagnostics report it as `stage_resident_bytes`.
- **No aliasing at Fit.** The stage at Fit is a linear-light area average drawn at about its own size, so fine texture does not alias while cropping, where the exact stage drawn at Fit through the surface's bilinear sampler without mip levels did. Settled on a generated zone plate: the exact stage at Fit showed full-contrast replica rings and averaged 16 codes too dark beyond the display's Nyquist limit, and the reduced stage shows only a box filter's faint residual rings at the true average ([performance](../specs/performance.md#a-crop-drafts-input-stage-at-fit)). At a percentage zoom the exact stage is drawn at or above its size, where the sampler magnifies.

## The CPU proxy: a session without a GPU

A session the GPU does not draw at all — no adapter, a lost device, a launch with `--no-gpu-render`, or a host whose only adapter is a software one not adopted — drags on the CPU proxy (owner, 2026-10-06, [decisions](../decisions.md#gpu-first-rendering)). Only such a session uses it: on a machine with a GPU a drag the GPU refuses holds its frame or has the reference draw whole frames, and the crop draft's input stage asks for no proxy. All of it lives in the core's `cpu_proxy` module (`crates/luxforge-core/src/cpu_proxy.rs` and `cpu_proxy/`), which the preview worker calls once per job (`CpuProxy::frame`), and the desktop's `crates/luxforge-app/src/app/cpu_proxy.rs`, which `app/motion.rs` and `app/preview.rs` call by name: the one place to remove it, which `cargo xtask check-repository` keeps apart (the `cpu-proxy` rule).

- **What it renders.** A **proxy source** is the prepared source reduced once to the size the display can show, by the view's area average (`crate::proxy`), and a **proxy render** is the whole drafted recipe rendered against it through the reference renderer's compiled path. Every layer a gesture can draft is resolution independent — the orientation layer is a mapping, the crop payload is normalized, the Basic and RAW development layers are pointwise — so the same recipe compiles unchanged against the smaller content stage and produces the same picture at display size, through the same code and colour arithmetic. Each tick asks for it (`PreviewIntent::Interactive`, `Editor::cpu_proxy_tick`) at the view's own bounds below 100% and at Fit's bounds at 100% and above, where the view draws it whole, magnified to the stage's box. A newer tick replaces the pending job and never stops the proxy in progress, so the queue's one active and one pending job bound it, latest winning. The release draws the reference's frame of the committed stack at rest, sharp.
- **Bounds and scale.** `ProxyBounds` are the photo area's physical pixels, sent with every request and clamped to 4096 px a side and 8 MP. The proxy scale fits the recipe's full-resolution output stage into them, read from the job's one compilation at the exact stage. A proxy holds its whole proxy stage, never a window of it: a crop fits its output to the bounds, which raises the scale towards one, so the scale is lowered to keep the whole stage within 8 MP (`ProxyPlan::whole_within`), and a tight crop's part of it is drawn magnified during the drag. When the scale is one there is no proxy and the job takes the exact path.
- **The build.** The reduction is an area average with fractional coverage, separable, on the shared Rayon pool from the proxy pass's own threshold: a JPEG averages in linear light through the decode table and re-quantizes through the threshold table, so a uniform region is exactly its own code; a RAW averages its planar `f32` planes through the image's view. The two passes run in bands of output rows, each pool worker holding one band's intermediate of about 1 MiB (`BAND_BYTES`), and the band size changes no byte.
- **The cache.** The worker holds one proxy source (`ProxyCache`), keyed by the source's identity — its fingerprint and, for a RAW, the development's process-unique number, so a white-balance redevelopment invalidates it — and the plan. A miss releases the held proxy before the replacement is built, so two never coexist. It holds pixels only: a RAW hit takes the planes from the cache and the development settings from the job, so a drafted exposure renders against the cached planes.
- **Eligibility and labels.** The registry decides eligibility: a pixel-stage layer makes the stack ineligible and the tick takes the exact path, as does a compile failure at the proxy size, with the reason on the result. A restoration (Detail) or spatial (Presence) layer is eligible but approximate, since its neighbourhoods scale with the stage it is rendered at, and so is a mask drawing a feature narrower than two proxy pixels, whose field — never the effect — takes a 2 × 2 supersample ([masking](masking.md#point-queries-and-proxies)). The label is read from what the stack compiles to at the proxy size (`ProxyApproximation`), so a neutral Detail or Presence layer is not labelled. The frame reads `Approximate render · N ms` beside the status bar's `Reference renderer`, and adds `Moving preview · Detail approximate` over a restoration layer; `preview_displayed` and the state summary carry the approximation.
- **The histogram and overlays.** A proxy raster is never reduced: the plot keeps the last exact report, marked updating, until the release's report replaces it. While a proxy frame is on screen the clipping overlay is re-derived from it so it follows the drag, its event and state summary saying `approximate: true`, and the exact overlay replaces it when the exact frame arrives. A mask overlay's grid is filled by its own coverage worker from the same evaluation and reads no rendered frame ([masking](masking.md#the-develop-workspace)).
- **Exactness.** A proxy render equals the exact recipe rendered against the exact downscale of the source, byte for byte, on both pixel domains (`cpu_proxy` tests); that is what "exact at its own scale" means.

## A RAW white balance during a drag

A RAW white balance — `set-raw`'s `temperature` or `tint`, which Basic's White balance group reaches through its RAW variants — is applied to the sensor mosaic before the nonlinear demosaic, so an exact frame at a new temperature or tint needs the mosaic redeveloped on the source worker: about half a second on the Z6 and one and a half on the X100VI. A drag therefore previews its drafted value **approximately**, on the planes already developed, and redevelops only for the value it commits.

The retained planes are `R · D(g)` per pixel, where `D(g)` is the native demosaic of the mosaic after the sensor gains `g`, in camera RGB (the Air 2S's gain map and optical warp run per channel inside it), and `R` is LibRaw's `rgb_cam`, the camera-to-linear-sRGB matrix. To first order `D(g') ≈ diag(g'/g) · D(g)`, so planes developed at `g` approximate the planes at the drafted gains `g'` by

`W = R · diag(g'_c / g_c) · R⁻¹`

with `R⁻¹` computed in f64. `LinearSettings::white_balance` carries `W`, and the reference's linear evaluator applies it to each source pixel, `W · p`, at the one point where settings touch source pixels; exposure is Basic's colour unit after it, as on a JPEG, so an Exposure drag on RAW is exact and needs no redevelopment. Without `W` the evaluation is bit for bit the exact one. The path compares the effective gains of the drafted recipe with the developed ones, so it does not depend on which action changed them.

- **On the GPU.** The drag's plan carries `W`, narrowed to `f32`, as a leading pointwise step over each source texel, before the content operations and every light link; its boundary is the one the picture at rest holds, so the drag asks for nothing new ([RAW white balance](gpu-preview.md#raw-white-balance)). At the exact stage the plan's frame is the reference's approximate frame within a code. The matrix and the area average are both linear, so `W` applies to a reduced boundary as to the full frame; they commute to f32 rounding, which is a test.
- **Where it applies.** Only the preview of an open draft (`EditorService::preview_job`), when the draft's effective gains differ from the development held in memory and that development exists. Equal gains need no approximation. A camera matrix with no usable inverse is refused as `preparation-required`, never rendered through a matrix that cannot describe it.
- **Where it never applies.** A committed or historical preview, `render_entry` and every export, `render.sample` and every other pixel read (`sample_entry`, `point_draft`, `sample_plan`), `analysis_plan`, and every pixel an action or query samples from its stage context stay strict: a white balance the planes do not hold is `preparation-required` there, and the refusal names a development at that white balance. The GPU's tile path, which the picture at rest, samples and export read, refuses an approximate white balance too, so nothing at rest is ever drawn through `W`.
- **Labelled and never analysed.** A reference or proxy frame of such a draft carries `PreviewResult::approximate_white_balance`, and the job is never reduced into a report even when it asked for one; the desktop keeps the last exact report plotted and marked updating until the release's report replaces it, marks a clipping overlay derived from one `approximate: true`, and says so in `preview_displayed`, the state summary and the status bar (`Approximate render · N ms`). A GPU frame of the drag reads `GPU preview · N ms` and its counts are plotted marked updating and handed to no one, as every gesture's are; the store only ever receives the release's.
- **Release.** The commit redevelops the mosaic on the source worker, and the last drafted frame stays on screen until the committed picture replaces it. A drafted value whose development is not in memory at all — evicted while a redevelopment or source preparation of an earlier request is in flight — is still `preparation-required` and has no frame of its own; the status bar says it shows on release.
- **What it is checked against.** The `raw-panel` scenario reports the moving GPU frame against the release's picture at rest, the whole frame at Fit and the visible region at 100%, and fails only on a gross error: a worst block past 10 ΔE00, a reference job on a drag's tick, or a frame the GPU did not draw (owner, 2026-10-06, [decisions](../decisions.md#gpu-first-rendering)). It records the scene's highlight-clip share beside the figures as context. The figures are in [performance](../specs/performance.md#a-raw-white-balance-drag-on-the-gpu-against-its-release).

**Accuracy of `W`.** Measured on the three supplied RAW files by the ignored `measure_the_white_balance_approximation_against_redevelopment` test: planes developed at the camera's as-shot gains, rendered through `W` for each Custom target, against an exact redevelopment at the same gains, both rendered to 8-bit sRGB at a display proxy (bounds 2400 × 1600) and at full size. The difference images place the rest where the demosaic couples the channels: on the Z6, thin high-contrast edges (bamboo slats, poles and wire against the sky, distant structures) and small specular glints, with flat and smoothly shaded areas within a code; on the X100VI at 3200 K, a fine per-pixel speckle across the saturated yellow petals and along their edges; on the Air 2S, the sparkling shallow water and sand at every target and, at 3200 K — the largest gain change measured, red × 0.68 and blue × 1.79 — the textured foliage and rock broadly. The approximation is shown only while the pointer is down and is replaced by the redeveloped picture on release; the Air 2S's larger error at strong changes is visible during a drag and is not otherwise bounded.

The Air 2S's error at 100% is broad, not specular: per channel 2.28/0.52/0.87, red biased low by 1.21; 27% of pixels over two codes, p90 of the largest channel 6 and p99 29; the pixels with a channel at 250 or above in the exact frame are 3% of the surface and carry 17% of the error, and without them the mean is still 1.05. On the full sensor, pixels within 3% of a channel's clip ceiling (the RCD input clamp, times the gain map) and their 2-pixel neighbourhood are 3% of the frame and 19% of the error; the rest averages 0.95 codes. It is the demosaic's colour-difference interpolation, which is not equivariant under a gain change (its error goes as `(1 − k) · (G − local mean of G)` at interpolated sites), on a low-exposure sensor whose gain map reaches 4.1–4.7. Two candidate improvements were measured on the full sensor and rejected: clamping at the clip ceiling with clipped channels taken as sensor-saturated lowers the 100% case from 1.13 to 1.06 codes and would need the gain map inside the evaluator; adding a green high-pass correction lowers it to 1.00 and is a neighbourhood operation, which the per-pixel `W` and its commuting with the area average rule out.

The misses are the approximation meeting clipped highlights, not a defect in the path. The OM-1's snow, going from as shot to 3500 K with red × 0.70, draws the snow 32 codes short of white in red on average, and up to 104 codes short, where the release is white. The candidate works per pixel in camera RGB. A channel at 0.99 of sensor white or above stays as it is. Any other channel is scaled by `g'/g`, clamped at sensor white where the gain rises, and returned through `R`. At Fit it runs after the area average, as a product draft would. Measured in the core at the scenario's geometry, all 33 sources would be within the limits with it. ## What is preserved

- Originals, recipes, history, drafts, commits, the API and every mutation are untouched. A frame on screen, the GPU's or the reference's, is desktop preview state and appears in no history and no persisted data.
- The catalog owner plans in `O(layers)` and touches no pixel; the interface thread renders nothing on the CPU and never waits on the GPU or a pixel read.
- The RAW retained mosaic and float development are unchanged. A committed RAW white balance still redevelops the mosaic on the source worker; a drafted one is approximated on the developed planes, labelled, and never analysed.
- Bounds: the preview worker's one active and one pending job; one cached proxy source in a session without a GPU (at most 8 MP, released before its replacement is built) and one band intermediate of about 1 MiB per pool worker while it is built; the photo surface's one current and one retiring full-image allocation; the GPU-preview budget and the photograph's source on the GPU ([limits](architecture.md#limits)). There is no quiet timer, viewport timer, region texture or private pool.

## Evidence

- **Core.** The reference renderer's exactness tests against frozen fixtures and independent references; the reduction to the view's bounds; a job compiling its stack once; cancellation latency; the CPU proxy's downscale (integer scales average exactly, fractional coverage weights sum to one, a uniform image is unchanged, RAW planes through a cropped, oriented view, any band size building the same bytes), its eligibility, its cache identity and the release of the held proxy before its replacement is built, and a proxy render equal to the exact recipe over the exact downscale (`cpu_proxy` tests, `preview::tests`). The GPU's plans against the CPU frame through the reference executor ([GPU previews](gpu-preview.md#verification)).
- **Desktop.** A drag tick the GPU does not draw holds its frame for a reason that passes or has the reference draw its whole frame, one in flight at a time, and the release schedules the reference's exact work and adopts its analysis, while a stack the GPU draws is presented by the GPU with its counts (`motion_tests.rs`, `gpu_counts_tests.rs`); a session without a GPU drags on the proxy (`cpu_proxy_tests.rs`).
- **Rendered.** The `gpu-preview` and `gpu-preview-zoom` scenarios correlate the GPU's frames, plans, boundaries, counts and labels with state and events through drags, pans and releases; `viewport-region` drags on the GPU's region at 100% with the mask's coverage of that region over it, through pans, a release, a cancel and a history preview, and `viewport-idle-fit` observes a Fit refit before evidence ticks again, both with zero blank photo draws; `no-gpu-render` checks that a forced reference session's drags draw labelled proxies and its releases sharp reference frames; `raw-panel` checks a RAW white-balance drag on the GPU against its release. The release gate, `cargo xtask gpu-qualification`, holds the picture at rest, the counts, samples and export to the reference on the corpus ([GPU-first](gpu-first.md#the-contract)).
- **Events and state.** `preview_displayed` carries the frame's path (`surface` or `gpu`), whose picture the person sees, whether the frame is the reduction or a proxy and its render time; `state.reference` the view's bounds, whether the reduction is on screen and whether it is a proxy, with why it approximates; `state.surface.gpu` the drawing path, the plan's fallback and the notice.
- **Timing.** `editor-latency` in drag, commit, burst and paint modes on 24 MP, 60 MP and RAW sources, with `--reference-renderer` for a session the reference draws; the measurement of this contract against the 2026-10-04 baseline is recorded in [performance](../specs/performance.md#gpu-first-against-the-2026-10-04-baseline) (2026-10-06).

## Proposals and later work

Recorded here as proposals, not decisions.

- **Coarser proxy while the pointer moves**, in a session without a GPU. With every Basic unit active, the CPU proxy render is a substantial cost per input (about 45 ms at 24 MP under a rotated crop in the [recorded diagnostic](../specs/performance.md#straightened-crop-resample)). Half the linear display resolution has one quarter as many output pixels and gives a softer picture during motion; a fourfold latency improvement is not established. Measure the reduction, including proxy builds.
- **Reduced pool.** Leaving one or two cores out of the shared Rayon pool for the desktop and owner threads may lower jitter while the reference renders; it is a measurement, not a default.

Caches that would reuse unchanged work, each weighed under [performance rule 14](../engineering/performance-rules.md#rules) and measured before it is adopted:

- **Hold the drafted layer's input at its boundary**, in a session without a GPU. Every tick the CPU proxy draws renders the whole recipe against the proxy, so dragging a layer after a heavy prefix repeats the prefix: a Vignette drag runs the Basic layer and Presence again, and a Presence drag runs the Basic layer again, whose proxy render is about 45 to 50 ms in the recorded diagnostics. A drag the GPU draws already keeps each spatial link's output by content and runs only the links the tick changes ([GPU previews](gpu-preview.md#where-the-code-lives)). The general form holds, for the open draft only, the frame at the latest boundary before the drafted layer that a render already materializes — the byte frame before a spatial segment on the JPEG path, a spatial segment's output on either path — at the proxy scale, keyed by the prefix's identity, and renders each tick from it. It costs about one proxy's bytes. It does not help a drag of the Basic layer itself or of a layer in the same fused colour run, which would need a new intermediate with a numerical contract ([research](../research/interactive-adjustments.md#reuse-work-according-to-what-actually-changed)). On RAW the prefix before the first spatial segment is pulled by rows, never materialized, so holding it is a new frame whose exactness is proven first.
- **Keep a spatial segment's exact output while later layers change.** Where the reference renders the picture, a committed change after Presence, such as the vignette or a crop, runs the exact phase through the colour run and every spatial segment again before the histogram settles; all three Presence fields take about 1.5 to 1.6 s at 60 MP ([performance](../specs/performance.md#tile-size-by-summed-halo)). Holding the last spatial segment's full-resolution output, which the render already materializes, keyed by the prefix's identity, lets such a change start there. It holds the most memory of these: up to 229 MiB for a 60 MP JPEG's frame and 468 MiB of planes for the X100VI, beside the working set, so its budget is the owner's decision. With the GPU presenting most stacks at rest it serves only the stacks the GPU cannot draw; measure how often those change only layers after the spatial stage before weighing it.
- **Keep recent photographs' prepared sources.** One prepared cache holds the current source ([initial RAW](initial-raw.md)), so going back and forth between photographs, as the Develop filmstrip does, decodes each one again and redevelops each RAW, the most expensive step in opening a photograph ([startup and RAW throughput](../specs/performance.md#startup-and-raw-throughput)). A byte budget for recently opened sources, with RAW planes counted by the memory gate as the second development's are, would make a return to a recent photograph a hit, and so would the GPU's source if it were kept per photograph. It shares memory with the second development and the one-development working set, so it is weighed with the whole-editor RAW measurement.
