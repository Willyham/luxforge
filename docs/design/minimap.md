# High-zoom minimap

Status: **implemented and qualified on the M4**; the M4's rendered journeys and the corpus's overview kind pass, the paged derived source's overview excepted, and the M4 measurements are [recorded](../specs/performance.md#high-zoom-minimap). A 199.9% control, unavailable originals, a lens or perspective warp, oversubscribed memory in a real window, panning under Compare and Windows/Linux remain open ([evidence](#acceptance-and-evidence)). The layout and engineering defaults below are recorded defaults.

## Behavior and scope

In Develop, show a small overview of the whole photograph with a rectangle marking the visible region. It appears when percentage zoom is **at least 200**, including typed fractional percentages and macOS pinch; it disappears immediately below 200 or at Fit. Use the actual view value, not the rounded status-bar text or the zoom-stop index. Fit remains Fit even for a small photograph that it enlarges past 200%.

Navigation: click inside the overview to centre the main viewport there; drag the viewport rectangle to pan, retaining the initial grab offset. A press outside the rectangle centres there and can continue as a drag. Clamp to the image edges; an axis that fits stays centred. Zoom stays unchanged. Letterboxing around the thumbnail is inert. A full-image rectangle remains visible when the whole photograph fits, and navigation on fitting axes has no effect.

The minimap is canvas chrome, anchored at the lower right inside the viewport, above the scrollbars and filmstrip: a layer of the canvas's own stack, not the scrollable's content, so it never enlarges the scrollable image, and it stays at the canvas's lower right when either side panel is hidden. Its image area is at most 180 × 120 pt, the stage aspect-fitted into it without distortion and the card hugging it with 4 pt of padding, 12 pt (the chrome inset) from the canvas's right and bottom edges, on the chrome surface with a viewport outline drawn as a light line between two dark ones. On a small canvas the image area takes at most 35% of the canvas on each axis; where the card would meet the mode strip it rises above it; with an image side under 24 pt, or no room inside the inset, there is no card. It uses existing theme tokens and interface scaling. It has no preference, pin/hide control, panel, dependency or keyboard shortcut.

The minimap shows image pixels only, without thirds, clipping colours, mask tints, handles or notices. Its rectangle describes the underlying geometric viewport, including the area under floating chrome, rather than a fragmented region around overlays. It follows scrolling, Space-drag, minimap navigation, pinch, zoom stops, typed/API zoom, resize, display/interface scale and panel/filmstrip changes.

Navigation remains view-only while a crop or mask draft is open. It must not paint, sample, move a crop handle, alter a comparison divider, enter another mode, create/cancel a draft or commit an edit. Pointer events beginning inside the minimap belong to it through release, including outside its bounds; wheel input there follows ordinary pan, and native pinch there must not also reach the photograph. A drag ends on release, Escape or focus loss, retaining its last applied view. Escape consumed by that drag must not cancel an underlying edit draft.

Use the same stage the main canvas is navigating:

- Ordinary, masked and historical views: the selected entry's output after orientation, crop, straighten and other geometry.
- Crop draft: the rotated input-stage box used by the existing crop surface, with the same placement and empty corners; the rectangle refers to that box, not the committed cropped output.
- Before/After divider: a single overview of the fixed After side, labelled After while Compare is active; both halves share its geometry and pan. A held Before view shows the corresponding Original overview. Shift-held uncropped Original uses its own uncropped stage. Switching these views must adopt an overview with matching geometry before enabling navigation.

No photo, Select workspace, gallery, unrenderable preview or zero-sized canvas: no minimap. During opening or a geometry change the eligible card shows a quiet placeholder captioned Updating, with no rectangle, until a matching overview exists; it never shows another photograph or navigates against an old stage. Compare's After side is captioned After. An overview neither renderer could make, or whose storage could not be reserved, is captioned Overview unavailable, and navigation still works against the stage. Failed rendering withdraws stale image pixels consistently with the existing preview-unavailable behavior.

## Geometry and command parity

The geometry (`luxforge-app/src/view/minimap_geometry.rs`) is plain arithmetic over what the editor already holds — the stored zoom, the stage the canvas scrolls over, the canvas region, the display scale and the scrollable's offset — reusing `CanvasView`'s percentage scale and Fit, the scrollable's overhang and the photo surface's physical-pixel snap. One projection serves both the viewport rectangle and navigation; the card's widget (`view/minimap.rs`) owns only pointer capture and publishes semantic view messages, and the editor (`app/minimap.rs`) decides what a press or drag means. None of it holds editing state.

For a percentage view, stage pixels become layout pixels by `s = zoom / 100 / effective_display_scale`. For each axis with stage extent `D`, viewport extent `V` and effective pan `p`, the image origin relative to the viewport is `max(0, (V - D*s)/2) - clamp(p, 0, max(0, D*s - V))`. Invert that transform at the viewport edges, intersect with `[0, D]`, and fit the resulting stage rectangle into the overview's image bounds. Invert the same thumbnail placement for clicks/drags; turn the requested stage centre into clamped pan. The actual crop-box transform and device-pixel snapping must agree with the main surface: the scrollable draws its offset rounded to whole logical pixels, the photograph's edges then snap to the physical grid (a crop draft's box stays unsnapped), and the scrollable clips to its bounds rounded to physical pixels. Do not apply crop/EXIF transforms a second time or confuse layout points with physical pixels.

Navigation uses the existing `view.set` pan fields and per-client `ViewState`; there is no minimap-specific command or durable recipe field. The registered schema states the pan's units and origin: the scroll offset of the zoomed stage's top-left corner from the viewport's, in the desktop's logical layout pixels, where a stage pixel spans percent / 100 / display scale of them; the desktop draws it clamped per axis to the overhang, centres an axis the stage fits and ignores it at Fit. GUI navigation and a same-client API request with equivalent pan values produce the same session and visible region; clients remain isolated.

A press or drag applies its pan in the input's own update: `view.set` on the desktop's session, the real scrollable moved to the same offset and the existing one-in-flight/one-newest-pending pan coalescer told, so a late answer cannot undo a newer local pan or restore an old asset or stage after a switch. Navigation changes no recipe, history, preview content generation or histogram identity. Newly exposed main-canvas regions use ordinary view planning.

## Overview rendering and lifetime

At 100% and above the GPU's picture-at-rest tiles already cover the full output stage for the histogram and clipping counts, and are reduced to the 2048-pixel backdrop the visible region is drawn over. At the minimap's zooms the same final tiles carry a second, independent reduction to the overview's size (`RestTiles::with_overview`; `luxforge-gpu/src/execution/overview.rs`): each tile is accumulated into an overview-sized accumulator as it is reduced to the backdrop, and after the last tile the accumulator is quantized into the overview's own texture, which passes to the photograph's surface. The main region, the backdrop, the counts and the tiles are unchanged; no tile is drawn and nothing uploaded for the overview alone, and there is no second photo surface, source upload or render of the stack. Staged and chained tiles and light sweeps all carry it.

Where the reference renderer drew the stage, the desktop reduces a frame it already retains at the stage's full size — the presented content's exact frame, a crop draft's exact input stage, Compare's After — on the blocking pool, borrowing its pixels (`luxforge_core::reduce_overview`), and the surface uploads the codes once. Where no such frame exists, the GPU's picture at rest of the content is swept with the overview beside it: the ordinary required sweep when the content is new, or one more bounded sweep of the held tiles when its counts have already settled. Nothing requests a render for the minimap. A crop draft's overview comes only from the reference's exact stage frame, which the crop canvas holds at these zooms.

An overview processes first at full stage resolution, then area-averages linear light and quantizes by the output encoding: a navigation thumbnail of the selected edited image, not the source, a camera or catalog preview or a stretched GPU region. Its settled GPU output is held to the current declared display tolerance against the whole-frame CPU reference reduced with the same coverage: `gpu-qualification` judges it on the corpus as its `overview` kind, each stack's picture at rest planned at 200% ([GPU-first](gpu-first.md#the-contract)), with no looser minimap tolerance. Exact CPU tests cover the core's reduction, which is the reference's own view reduction byte for byte.

One current overview and at most one replacement in progress are retained for the active stage; the desktop names those two versions to the photograph's surface, which retires any other. An overview is keyed by the photograph, the content's pixels (the content key and the source's identity, which name the entry's recipe, its geometry and the source's development) or a retained frame's version, the stage and the overview's size. The renderer is part of its validity rather than its key: a GPU-made overview is kept only while the GPU stage is allowed, a reference-made one serves either renderer, both within the declared tolerance. Pan, window position, resize and high-zoom percentage change none of these, so they reuse the overview with no sweep, build or upload; the first threshold crossing without one adds the reduction to a bounded sweep as above and delays neither navigation nor the main frame. A result is adopted only for the version it was started as, so a late, cancelled or switched result never shows; a finished one whose content is not presented yet waits as the replacement until it is.

While an edit gesture is open no overview is made: the last settled one is shown **marked Updating** where its stage is the stage now, and withdrawn where the geometry changed, which disables navigation until the matching one is ready. After commit or cancel the ordinary render's overview is adopted. Photo switches, history changes, Compare changes, late jobs and cancellation cannot reintroduce stale imagery.

The overview raster is aspect-fitted within **256 × 256 physical pixels**. Every GPU allocation it adds — the accumulator, coverage tables and parameters, the finished textures and their placement uniforms, current, replacement and retiring — is held together within **4 MiB** per pipeline (`OVERVIEW_GPU_BUDGET`), charged to the GPU-preview budget before it is created; a picture at rest planned at 100% and above leaves that much of its share free. An overview that cannot be reserved is refused by version: the picture at rest, its counts and every overview already held are untouched, and the card keeps a correctly keyed ready overview or says Overview unavailable, navigation still working. The reference's reduction holds at most **1 MiB** with its output, coverage tables and one band of averaged rows (`OVERVIEW_CPU_BYTES`), one at a time, borrowing the retained frame and keeping its codes only until the surface holds them. The minimap holds no full-photo allocation, source residency or GPU device of its own. Another photograph, Select or no photograph lets everything go. A hidden window starts no overview work and keeps what it holds: the photograph's surface lets its overviews go only when a frame does not draw it, as Select's do, so the overview is shown again at once when the window is. Back in Develop after Select, the overview is made anew with the picture at rest. A picture at rest that the surface began before the source was handed back stops with `source-missing`. It is then drawn again once, under new versions, carrying its overview and counts, as soon as the source is there (`gpu_rest_again`). Only a second stop fails the overview. The desktop logs `overview_ready` once for each overview as the card first shows it. The surface's diagnostics carry what it holds, a refusal, its counters and the pipeline's overview bytes; evidence snapshots report them under `surface.gpu.minimap_overview` (reference and GPU builds, sweeps, uploads, reuses, refusals and stale results) beside the card's own `minimap` record (placement, stage, rectangle and input counters).

## Acceptance and evidence

1. Exactly 200% shows the minimap; 199.9% and Fit do not. Test 200.1%, all stops above 200, 1600%, pinch crossing and API/typed zoom. Status rounding never decides visibility.
2. The viewport rectangle and click/drag result agree with independent geometry calculations at centre and every edge, landscape/portrait, a fitting axis, 1×/2× display scale, interface scaling, panel/filmstrip changes and resize. Letterboxing is inert; captured drags remain bounded and end safely.
3. Navigation reaches the existing command service, moves the scrollable and changes only view state. Equivalent same-client API pan has the same result; other clients, recipes, source checksums, history, draft contents and histogram identity stay unchanged.
4. Crop, masks, warped geometry, historical selection and both Compare forms obey the stage and event rules. Clicking the minimap with a brush/picker/crop mode active never edits the image. Rapid switches, cancellation, unavailable originals and failed/stale outputs show no mismatched image or actionable old rectangle.
5. Panning with a ready overview performs no overview render, source upload or unchanged-thumbnail upload. All storage is reserved within the named caps, including replacement/retirement; no new idle timer/poll or owner/UI-thread image work is introduced. Tests exercise exhausted budgets and no-GPU/reference fallback.
6. A real background rendered journey on the owner's M4 correlates minimap bounds/identity/rectangle, session pan, main-surface geometry, logs and captures on generated 24 MP and 60 MP JPEGs and the existing supplied RAW fixtures. Include threshold, click/drag, edge clamp, edits, mode interception, crop, Compare, resize and recovery. The CPU reference is exact; settled GPU overview comparisons use the declared tolerance. Headless/software checks do not establish native GPU correctness.
7. Record photo-sized release measurements of first-overview latency, pan input-to-presented-frame p50/p95, added retained/peak bytes, overview rebuild/reuse/upload counts and idle behavior on the M4. Use 199.9% as a threshold control and 200%/800% with the minimap, declaring that differing zoom windows are not an isolated rendering comparison. Measure warmed/cold availability separately; no latency or total-memory claim follows from the design itself.

### Current evidence

Every item above has ordinary tests; the rendered and measured parts are partly open.

- **Run and passing.** The geometry's tests hold the threshold (Fit, 199.9, 200, 200.1, 1600), the rectangle and click/drag pans against hand-computed coordinates for landscape and portrait, fitting axes, edges, the letterbox, the snapped photograph and the unsnapped crop box, 1× and 2× display scale, and degenerate input. `view.set`'s schema and per-client sessions are held by the owner's tests. The overview's lifecycle tests cover reuse with no work, single uploads, late and switched results, Updating during motion, withdrawal on a geometry change, budget refusal and release; the core's tests hold the CPU reduction exact and within 1 MiB. Native Metal device tests on the M4 Pro hold the GPU overview within the declared display tolerance of the reduced reference on synthetic pointwise, geometry and spatial stacks, chained, staged and after light sweeps, unchanged picture and counts beside it, and refuse it, never the picture, past its budget. The background `zoom` journey on generated 24 MP and 60 MP JPEGs on the M4 (47 frames each) checks parts of items 1 to 4: visibility at Fit, 199.9%, 200%, 800% and 1600%, card placement and the rectangle against geometry worked independently, the photograph's pixels under the view, UI click, drag with grab and edge clamp, the letterbox, wheel and pinch over the card, an equivalent API pan on the desktop's own session, and interception under a crop draft (Escape ending the drag and keeping the draft), the neutral picker, an armed brush and Compare; its record shows ordinary pans reusing the overview with no build, sweep or upload ([performance](../specs/performance.md#high-zoom-minimap)).
- **Rendered on the M4.** The background `minimap` journey (`cargo xtask smoke --scenario minimap`, `--source` for another photograph) passes on the generated 24 MP and 60 MP JPEGs and on the supplied Nikon Z6, Fujifilm X100VI and DJI Air 2S RAW files. It makes two launches per photograph.
  - The ordinary editor's launch covers the following:
    - the threshold, exactly at 200% and fractional either side, through the view, `view.set` and a pinch crossing each way, and at 800%;
    - a drag of the rectangle into every corner, and a click;
    - the tools and state panels hidden, the filmstrip collapsed, the window resized and the interface at 125%, each with a click;
    - an edit, the Original selected in History and current again;
    - a straightened 1:1 crop, a radial mask with its own adjustment, and the mask mode;
    - the window hidden and shown;
    - a switch to a photograph that fits the canvas on both axes, and back;

  - The reference renderer's launch (`--no-gpu-render`) covers navigation, edits, the crop, the mask and a resize, with every overview the reference's.
  - Every frame correlates the card's placement, image area, stage and rectangle against geometry worked independently for its canvas, display and interface scale. It also checks each gesture's pan against the desktop's session and the overview's version and stage. Wherever the card shows a ready overview, the thumbnail's middle must agree in linear light with the photograph's, so another photograph's, entry's or edit's overview cannot pass.
  - The counters show navigation, layout and zoom above the threshold reusing the overview with no build, sweep or upload, and the hidden window doing no overview work.
  - Item 6's corpus comparison runs on the M4 as `gpu-qualification`'s `overview` kind. It passes within the declared display tolerance everywhere except the paged derived source, whose overview is an unrendered gap.
- **Measured on the M4.** `editor-latency --mode minimap` takes item 7's figures from one actual drag on the card at 200% and 800% on the 24 MP and 60 MP JPEGs, on the GPU and on the reference: each move's pan to the draw that shows it, the first overview cold and warm, the overview's retained and peak bytes and its lifecycle counts ([performance](../specs/performance.md#high-zoom-minimap)). A move that cannot be matched to its own draw fails the run.
- **Open.** Item 7's 199.9% control, which the minimap's own mode cannot take since the card is hidden there, and idle behaviour with the card shown; unavailable originals; a lens or perspective warp; oversubscribed memory in a real window; 1× display scale in a rendered window, where only the geometry's tests hold it; Windows and Linux.   - Panning under Compare at a percentage zoom leaves the evidence capture's photograph not ready. This reproduces at 150% with no card, so the journeys cover Compare with a letterbox press and a grab that does not move.

## Performance-rules review

The [checklist](../engineering/performance-rules.md#review-checklist), answered for the minimap:

- **Originals.** No minimap path reads, hashes or decodes an original. The GPU overview is reduced from tiles drawn from the source the surface already holds; the reference's from a frame the desktop already retains.
- **Full-frame allocations.** None. The overview is at most 256 × 256 (171 KiB of RGBA for a 3:2 stage); its GPU storage is bounded at 4 MiB per pipeline inside the GPU-preview budget, its CPU reduction at 1 MiB, and the retained frame it reduces is borrowed, never cloned.
- **Point queries.** No projection, validation or no-op navigation renders a frame: the rectangle and pans are arithmetic over view state.
- **Owner thread.** Only `view.set`'s session pan, as for any pan. Reductions run on the GPU or the blocking pool; the update loop handles plain data.
- **Desktop messages.** A minimap press or drag sends `view.set` through the existing pan coalescer and moves the scrollable: no `asset.state`, history read, preview job or upload. A first overview may add one bounded sweep of the held tiles, or one reference reduction and upload, per content or stage change.
- **Timers, polls and subscriptions.** None. Overview completion wakes through the existing surface and retirement wakes; a hidden window starts no overview work.
- **Reuse.** The overview is a two-entry cache (current and replacement) keyed by photograph, content or retained-frame version, stage and size, bounded by the caps above; pan, resize and zoom above 200% reuse it with no sweep, build or upload, which the journey's counters show. Its retained bytes and its reuse over a 40-move drag are [measured](../specs/performance.md#high-zoom-minimap); its rebuild cost is not isolated.
- **`editor-performance`.** Not run, and it does not isolate minimap projection or presentation; no speed claim is made.
- **Image tests.** Exact CPU reduction tests; declared-tolerance native device comparisons of the GPU overview; `gpu-qualification`'s `overview` kind on the M4's corpus; tests that the overview leaves the picture at rest's codes and counts unchanged, and that every overview byte is released.
