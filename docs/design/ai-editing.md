# AI editing

Status: **decided by the owner on 2026-10-05; planning complete, implementation not started.** The plan runs in its own order once the GPU-first integration branch has merged, with the Corrections foundation scheduled beside its first stage. Written on 2026-10-04 at the owner's request for a plan that brings AI editing into Luxforge: object removal, generative fill and replace, automatic masks that follow what a photograph contains and, as an option, sky replacement. Everything runs on **local models the person downloads on request**: nothing is bundled, nothing is fetched without a disclosure and a grant, and nothing leaves the machine unless a remote provider is configured and consented, which this design shapes and defers. Its plan is [tasks/ai-editing.json](../../tasks/ai-editing.json).

It builds on what exists. The [object removal research](../research/object-removal/README.md) of September 2026 compared the models, runtimes and licences. The standalone object removal prototype of 2026-09-28 and 29 (the `probes/object-removal` workspace and its design, on the `claude/object-removal-research-d3374b` branch and not yet on `main`) measured a segmenter, a fill and a hover loop on the M4; its figures are quoted below. The [Corrections](corrections.md) proposal supplies the repair stage, the brush and the frozen patch that every removal commits through; this design takes over its AI Remove and leaves it Clone, Heal and that foundation. The [masking workspace](masking-workspace.md)'s model-selection kind (its phase E) is delivered here with a class menu that changes with the photograph. The [module capabilities](module-capabilities.md) contract supplies downloads, consent, tasks and derived artifacts unchanged, apart from the `local-runtime` capability this design is the first consumer of. [Settings and flags](settings-and-flags.md) supply the Settings surface and the experiment flags.

The owner accepted the scope on 2026-10-05 ([Decisions](#decisions)) and the roadmap's exclusion of generative editing is lifted. Nothing below is built, and no command named here exists.

## What a person gets

Four tools, named as Lightroom names them where the behaviour is the same, in the order they are proposed to land.

1. **Remove.** With the Remove tool, the object under the pointer is highlighted as the pointer moves, and a click removes it. A rough brush stroke removes the object it covers; when several objects lie under it, each gets a numbered pin and the person picks; when none is found, the painted area itself is removed. The shadow or reflection an object casts is part of the selection, painted or suggested, never guessed silently. The fill comes from a local model and takes a few seconds; the selection stays visible and cancellable while it runs. The result is a correction in the recipe: undoable, visible in history, carried through later crops and adjustments, exported with everything else, and rendered from stored pixels, so it never needs the model again.
2. **Select.** The Masks panel's New mask menu gains a Select group that lists what *this* photograph contains: Subject, Background, Sky, the people in it one by one, and the landscape classes present (Water, Mountains, Vegetation, Ground, Architecture), with Objects for anything the person points at. Choosing one creates a mask whose coverage a model computed once and the host stored; the person refines it with the existing brushes and ranges, and every slider in the maskable sections works on it as on any mask. A selection says which model made it and when, and offers Recompute when the photograph or the model changed.
3. **Generative fill and Replace.** Remove's Fill setting gains a **Generative** tier: a local generative model rebuilds the region as believable scene content rather than a blur, offers three variations and three more on Regenerate, and commits only the one the person accepts. With a prompt it becomes Replace: the region is filled with what the prompt describes. The generative model is a large download (gigabytes) and needs a capable machine; it is offered, with its requirements, and never installed by default.
4. **Sky replacement** (optional). A Sky section composites a chosen sky behind the Select Sky mask with horizon, scale, flip, exposure, warmth, defocus, edge and relight controls, from a sky the person imports or one of a small permissively licensed set offered for download. The composite is an ordinary deterministic layer: the model contributes the mask, not the pixels, so it renders exactly on both renderers. A generative sky is Replace with a sky prompt inside the same mask.

What this design does not propose: AI denoise or upscaling, generative expand, text-to-image, face or body retouching, automatic "find distractions" suggestions, a built-in chat, accounts or a hosted service of Luxforge's own. Remote providers are shaped (one typed adapter, one data class, remembered consent) and delivered last, after the local tiers.

## How the pillars apply

- **Originals are sacred.** A model's output is a derived artifact the recipe references: an alpha raster for a selection, a patch for a fill, a sky raster for a composite. Rendering, sampling and export read the artifact and never call a model, so an edit survives the model's removal, a change of runtime and a different machine. A stale result is marked, never regenerated silently and never discarded.
- **Everything is programmable.** Every gesture has a method: analysis is a task, a hover pick is a query, a selection is a task that publishes an artifact and a mask command that binds it, a fill is a task whose candidates the client reads and an action that accepts one. An agent can remove an object, select the sky or replace it through `luxforge-json` with the same requests the desktop sends, and the acceptance tests do exactly that.
- **Fast, bounded and honest.** Inference runs off the owner and interface threads, on the capability lanes and one dedicated hover worker; every model declares its memory and is unloaded when idle; every region, crop, candidate and download has a limit refused by name. A preview that differs from the exact render says so. A hover decode, an analysis, a fill and a generative candidate each have a budget measured on the M4 with photo-sized inputs, and a miss is published, not hidden.
- **Small core, deliberate extension points.** The core gains one narrow inference port, one analysis cache, one model-selection mask kind and the `local-runtime` capability. The models, their pre- and post-processing and the fill pipeline live in a module; the runtime lives in a crate the core never links, injected as the transport is. A second provider, local or remote, registers without changing a recipe shape.
- **Open source, first on the owner's Mac.** Project code stays GPL-3.0-or-later; the runtime is MIT; weights are downloads whose licence, provenance and training-data terms are stated before the download, under a policy the owner sets. Core ML accelerates on the Mac; the same models run on Windows and Linux through the same port, measured for function there and for speed only natively.
- **Prove it.** Exact-buffer tests for everything deterministic (the removal region, the guided upsampler, the composite, the sky transform), outside-mask byte identity for every fill, bit-identical artifacts across two runs for every selection, a fake runtime so the journeys run in CI without a download, and native M4 runs with the real models recorded with their scope.
- **Beautiful defaults, familiar feel.** Remove behaves like Lightroom's Remove with Detect objects and its People pins; Select like Lightroom's AI masks; Generative like Generative Remove's variations. None is a rendering target.

Three services are shared by every feature: the inference port with its model resources, the per-photo analysis cache, and one picker that turns a point, a box or a stroke into candidate objects. Each feature is a thin consumer: Remove reads a candidate and asks for a fill; Select reads a class map and publishes an alpha; Sky reads that alpha and composites.

### The inference port and runtime

The core declares a narrow port and links no runtime: a `Session` opens a verified model file on a named backend and runs named tensors in and out, and a `Generator` takes an image, a mask and an optional prompt and yields candidates one at a time, cancellably. The implementations live in crates of their own, handed to the host through `HostConfig` exactly as the transport and the secret store are, and `cargo xtask check-repository` gains a dependency rule that keeps every runtime crate out of `luxforge-core`'s manifest, so the core's tests build no native code. A deterministic fake backend implements both traits inside `luxforge-core` itself, beside the proof modules, served only to developer runs and tests: `luxforge-testbase` may depend on no workspace crate and the core may name no crate that depends on it, so the fake can live nowhere else.

**Recommendation: ONNX Runtime through `ort`, statically linked, as the port's first backend.** The research left the choice between `tract` and ONNX Runtime to measurement, and the prototype measured it. `tract` 0.23.8 loads one of the five pinned models: its ONNX shape inference refuses the dynamic batch and point dimensions of the EfficientViT-SAM and SAM 2.1 decoders, and it does not implement the blocked `DequantizeLinear` of the LaMa export. ONNX Runtime 1.28 runs all five on the CPU provider and, over copies of each model whose free input dimensions ONNX Runtime itself fixes at 1, on Core ML's GPU with masks matching the CPU's at IoU 0.998 or better. On the M4 Pro, EfficientViT-SAM L0 then encodes a 24 MP photograph in about 110 ms and a 60 MP one in 130 to 160 ms, decodes a hover point in about 5 ms, loads warm in about 1.2 s and compiles once in about 7 s, inside a 1 GB footprint. LaMa stays on the CPU provider: on Core ML it runs in 188 partitions, no faster, with a 9 GB footprint against 1.5 GB. The segmenter proposed is EfficientViT-SAM L0; SAM 2.1 Small finds small objects EfficientViT misses (kayaks on dark water, two walkers) but loads in 10 to 20 s even cached, holds 3.5 GB and compiles for 30 to 90 s the first time, so it is offered as an alternative the person can choose, presented as "Finds smaller objects; slower to start and uses more memory", not the default ([Decisions](#decisions)).

What adopting `ort` costs, and how each cost is held:

| Cost | How it is held |
| --- | --- |
| A prebuilt static archive from one maintainer's CDN, `ort` still a release candidate | The archive's SHA-256 and the `ort` version are pinned; `xtask` checks the hash and the published attestation; a source build of ONNX Runtime is not attempted (it needs CMake, Python and C++20) |
| The Core ML provider takes only static shapes | The fixed-shape copy is written by ONNX Runtime at install time, once per model file, into the model's own directory, and recorded as part of the install; a shape the copy does not take runs on a CPU session of the original |
| Core ML compiles on first load | The compile cache lives under the application data root, keyed by model hash, runtime version and OS build; the first load of a model shows its compile as progress, never as a hang |
| fp16 on the Neural Engine changes masks | Every model pins its compute units and precision; the Neural Engine setting is not used until a model passes an fp16 parity check against its fp32 output |
| Windows and Linux | DirectML from the operating system's copy on Windows, CPU elsewhere; CUDA only as a user-installed, later option. Functional checks there, no native speed claims |

**The generative tier's runtime is chosen by its qualification task**, not here. A 4B-parameter flow model is not one ONNX graph that ONNX Runtime's Core ML provider will take whole; the candidates are `stable-diffusion.cpp` (MIT; Metal, Vulkan and CUDA; GGUF weights; inpainting) built from vendored, pinned sources with the `cc` crate as LibRaw is, and MLX behind a C ABI on macOS alone as a comparison. The one-toolchain rule holds: no CMake, no Python, no second build system. If neither builds within the rule at the measured quality, the local generative tier waits and the design's remote tier carries generative fill.

### Models: manifest, download, activation and memory

A model is a capability **resource** of the `luxforge.ai` module, declared with the fields the contract already has (pinned URL, bytes, SHA-256, licence, provenance, redirect origins) and two it adds: a **use restriction**, one plain line present only when the licence restricts how the person may use their own results, and the **runtime requirements** (backend, precision, resident memory, minimum machine memory). Multi-file models (an encoder and a decoder, a transformer and its autoencoder) are one resource with several files, installed together or not at all. Downloads become resumable: the staging file keeps its offset and the install resumes a matching partial transfer, hashing the whole file at the end as it does today. The disclosure before a download states the size, where the files go and the use restriction when there is one, and a download is a grant per resource version and origin, as today.

**Model policy (owner, 2026-10-05): quality first.** Models are chosen and ranked by measured results, and neither a weight licence nor the terms of a model's training data keeps a model out of the lists or appears in the disclosure. Weights are the person's own downloads from their publishers, pinned by hash, not dependencies of the project, so the open-source pillar is unaffected. The one thing shown is what affects the person: when a licence restricts how they may use their results (non-commercial, field-of-use), the card and the disclosure say so in one line, and the default pick for each feature is the best model whose terms do not restrict the person's own use of their photographs. The candidates the research and the web check of 2026-10-04 found, by class of terms:

| Terms | Candidates |
| --- | --- |
| Unrestricted (Apache-2.0, MIT) | EfficientViT-SAM, SAM 2.1, RF-DETR-Seg, Depth Anything 3 Small and Base, FLUX.2 klein 4B with its Qwen3 text encoder and VAE, MediaPipe multiclass, fal's klein removal LoRA, BiRefNet and BiRefNet_HR, MI-GAN, the EoMT ADE20K checkpoints, Moebius, Big-LaMa |
| Use-restricted for the person (OpenRAIL++-M use clauses, the SAM License's field-of-use terms, the DINOv3 licence) | OSOR-SDXL, RORem, SAM 3 and 3.1, EoMT on DINOv3 |
| Non-commercial for the person | ObjectClear and TurboClear, EdgeSAM, SegFormer, RMBG, Qwen-Image-2.1, every FLUX.1 [dev] remover, FLUX.2 klein 9B |

Every choice between models is presented as a plain trade-off the person can act on, such as "Better on small objects, slower to load" or "Higher quality, needs 24 GB of memory", with the model's technical name secondary (owner, 2026-10-05).

**Lazy activation.** Nothing loads until a tool needs it. Picking the Remove tool, opening the Select group or pressing Generate first asks for the models the tool needs: an uninstalled model shows the resource card with Download; an installed one loads on the module lane while the tool shows that it is loading, and Clone and Heal work meanwhile. This is the activation the capabilities contract deferred, defined by this consumer as the **`local-runtime`** capability: a module that declares it may hold loaded sessions between tasks in a host-owned slot with a declared resident budget per model, is told to unload on a memory signal or after an idle interval (recorded default 10 minutes), and is never loaded by `module.list`, a catalog open or a launch. The capability brings two methods, because a tool's warm-up on pick and the Models tab's Unload now must be requests any client can send: `module.runtime.load {module_id, resource_id}` loads a model ahead of its first task and `module.runtime.unload {module_id, resource_id?}` releases one or all of them; `module.status` reports what is loaded and what it holds. Settings gains a **Models** tab: every known model with its state, size, its use restriction if it has one and Remove, the resident set and an Unload now, each feature's default tier, and one line naming this machine's memory against each model's minimum.

### Analysis and the per-photo cache

Three tasks prepare a photograph, one per model set, because a task's declared resources must all be installed before it may run and the sets are installed separately: `task.analyse {asset_id}` runs the segmenter's encoder and serves Remove and Objects; `task.analyse-scene {asset_id}` runs the semantic model, the salient-subject model and the people detector and serves Select; `task.analyse-depth {asset_id}` runs the depth model. Each renders the same **analysis input** on the module lane, the repair stage's output (the developed, oriented photograph with its repairs applied, before colour, spatial and geometry work) reduced to at most 1024 px on the long side, and a one-channel **guide**, that stage's luminance at the artifact resolution (at most 4096 px on the long side, at most 16 MiB), because a mask refined against the 1024 px reduction cannot recover the hair and branches the reduction lost. They publish nothing: the embedding (about 16 MiB), the per-class and subject probabilities, the people instances, the depth map and the guide go into a **bounded, recomputable analysis cache** keyed by the source fingerprint, the hash of the repair prefix (`spatial::prefix_hash` over the layers before the first colour layer) and each model's identity and version. The cache holds a few photographs' analyses within a declared budget (recorded default 256 MiB in memory, least recently used, counted in the machine minimum beside the resident models), is separate from the artifacts recipes reference, is never written to a catalog, and is listed under performance rule 14 with its key, bound and invalidation like every disposable cache. Each result reports what it found (the classes with their area share and confidence, each person's bounds, the model versions), so a client can draw the Select menu or the pins without another request, and repeating a task over an unchanged input answers from the cache. Until the Corrections repair stage exists, the analysis input is the developed content stage, which is exactly the repair stage's output when there are no repairs, under the same prefix-hash rule, so nothing here waits for that stage.

The analysis input is chosen so a removal is seen by every later selection: after an object is removed, Select Subject analyses the repaired photograph. A colour edit does not change it, which is what makes a stored selection stable under ordinary editing, as Lightroom's are. Every pixel an AI task reads (the analysis input, the guide, a fill's crop of the stage the repair operation receives, in linear values) comes through the [GPU-first](gpu-first.md) plan's tile contract, `luxforge_core::tiles`, built on its integration branch for stage 4 and not yet wired: a `TileService` the host gives the owner answers rectangles of a stage, or an output stage as ordered bands, from the GPU provider when it has one and from the reference renderer's `ReferenceTiles` otherwise, each answer naming the renderer that drew it. The analysis input is reduced on the module lane from those bands until the contract offers a reduced read from the GPU's resident source. No AI task renders through a private path, and every artifact's provenance records the renderer that drew its input in the shape `session.state` and an export's result use (`{record, reason}`).

### Picking: hover, click, box and brush

Hover cannot go through the job table, whose lane runs one job with four queued and whose jobs the desktop reads every 100 ms. A **hover worker**, started with the first Remove or Objects pick and stopped with it, holds the loaded decoder and the current embedding and keeps only the latest pointer position, so a burst of moves decodes once. Its output is view state and never an edit. In the prototype's hidden window this loop drew the overlay at a p50 of 20 ms and a p95 of 33 to 35 ms from the pointer event on Core ML, which meets the median of the 16 to 33 ms budget and sits at its edge in the tail; on the CPU provider the p95 was 45 ms, a miss.

The same picker answers a click, a dragged rectangle (a box prompt), Alt-click (a negative point) and a brush stroke, for Remove and for New mask › Objects alike. A stroke becomes prompts the way the prototype does: its padded bounding box as a box prompt and a few farthest-point-sampled positive points, decoded in one batch; each candidate scored on predicted IoU, stability, stroke coverage and object coverage over one working raster; parts yielding to wholes; duplicates suppressed largest first; and a decision of **One**, **Several** or **Nothing** with every tie broken in a fixed order, so the same stroke always gives the same answer. Its programmatic form is `query.pick-object {asset_id, prompt: {point|points|box|stroke}}`, which returns the candidates (identity, scores, bounds, a low-resolution coverage preview) and the decision, and `task.select-object {asset_id, candidate|prompt}` publishes the chosen candidate as a full-resolution alpha artifact. That is the UI/API parity rule for hover: the desktop's overlay is the same query, and anything the pointer can select an agent can name.

### Where results live

| Result | Where it goes | Who reads it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A selection | An 8-bit alpha artifact at the content stage's aspect, long side capped at 4096 px (at most 16 MiB), refined from the model's output by the guided upsampler | A `model` mask component ([Select](#select-automatic-masks)) |
| A removal or generative fill | A frozen patch: the composited region's pixels in the repair stage's linear representation, with its coverage mask, crop transform, encoding transform and provenance, published as an artifact only when accepted | A repair operation in the Corrections module's repair layer ([Remove](#remove)) |
| A sky | A raster artifact of the imported or downloaded sky, at most 4096 px on the long side | The Sky layer ([Sky replacement](#sky-replacement-optional)) |
| An analysis | The bounded cache, never a catalog | Hover, Select's menu, Remove's proposals |
| Candidates | Bounded job results addressed by job and index, held under one byte budget and released oldest-first, read through `job.read`, not artifacts | The candidate strip; Accept publishes one |

The **guided upsampler** is one pure-Rust, deterministic function the core owns, used by every model mask and every removal region: it upsamples a model's logits or probabilities against a luminance guide at the output's own resolution (the cached guide for a selection; the source-resolution luminance over the region's window for a removal, as the prototype's `refine` does) with a fast guided filter over a bounded window, and it has an `f64` reference and bit-identity tests like every mask equation. Because it is deterministic, a selection's artifact is identical across two runs on the same input and model, which is what makes Recompute honest.

Two rules follow from the renderers and the artifact store. Under GPU-first a component kind the GPU cannot evaluate forces the preview off the GPU, so the model kind ships a coverage program like every kind: its artifact is bound as a texture (charged to the 2 GiB GPU-preview budget like every program buffer) and sampled, thresholded and feathered in WGSL; the repair composite and the sky composite have GPU programs on the same terms, and the GPU-first release gate (`cargo xtask gpu-qualification`, which `verify --tier rendered` runs over `fixtures/preview/corpus.json`) holds every GPU output a change touches to the reference renderer within the declared tolerance; each of these kinds adds corpus stacks that carry it (a model mask, a repair patch, a sky, with their artifacts as checked-in fixtures the fake backend reproduces), since the gate judges only what the corpus holds. Before stage 2 the picture at rest is still the reference renderer's and the gate reports it as not yet GPU-rendered, so a repair composite reaches the screen through the boundary the reference draws and its program is judged in the frames a draft draws; from stage 2 it is part of every frame. The CPU implementations stay as that reference, whole-frame and exact against their `f64` references, not as a production path to tune. And every artifact a stack references must bind within the prepared-artifact cache's 256 MiB budget at once, so a patch is the region's bounding box in half-float linear RGB (at most 64 MiB), Accept and `mask.add-model` check the stack's total before committing and refuse by name past it, and raising the budget is an owner question the budgets table carries.

## Remove

### The flow

Remove is a canvas mode of the Corrections module, entered from the mode strip (proposed letter `Q`, Lightroom's), its Remove mode beside Clone and Heal in the Corrections section. Entering it starts the analysis if the cache lacks one and shows "Analysing" until hover is ready; the brush works at once. A click on a highlighted object, or a stroke, builds the **selection**: the chosen object's refined core, the stroke's own footprint, and an **effect region** for its shadow or reflection, which starts from the prototype's contact-band heuristic when the person turns it on and is refined with Add and Subtract. Several objects under one stroke show numbered pins; the person clicks the ones to remove and confirms. Lightroom's Detect objects includes the shadow automatically; Luxforge shows the suggested region and lets the person accept it, because no licence-clean model predicts it yet and the selection is what the fill is composited inside, so it must be visible and editable. When a learned, effect-aware model becomes available under acceptable terms, its predicted region takes the suggestion's place.

The selection is a draft on the core's `draft.*` lifecycle with the Corrections brush descriptor; a fill is a job; Accept commits one history entry. Escape during the job cancels it and leaves the recipe untouched. The Fill setting chooses the tier: **Fast** (the deterministic model, installed with Remove), **Generative** (the local generative model, when installed) or **Service** (a configured remote provider profile, later). Each tier is its own task, because a task's resources are declared statically and an optional model must never become a prerequisite of another tier: `task.fill-fast`, `task.fill-generative {model}` and the remote module's `task.fill-remote {profile}` share one request shape and one result shape, and only the last declares a `remote-image-request` capability and its consent.

### The removal region

The region is the prototype's, and its rules are restated so the editor's implementation is tested against them: a hard-sigmoid prior over the decoder's logits, a guided filter against luminance over a bounded window, an **object core** of full coverage where the decoder places the object inside or the filter gives it half coverage, the stroke footprint and the effect region joined by maximum, a grayscale disc dilation whose radius scales with the object (half a logit cell plus 4% of the core's shorter side, at least 2 px, the dilation and feather together capped at about 15 px per 1.5 MP of frame scaled linearly), and a soft outer edge one logit cell wide. A stroke that finds no object removes its footprint as painted, feathered over 15% of the brush radius. Every stage is a function of its inputs and has an exact-buffer test; nothing is allocated outside the region's window.

### The Fast fill

The prototype's fill pipeline is the Fast tier's starting point: crop the region plus context of one region-length on every side, squared and reflection-padded; encode to the model's input; run the model; resize back; re-synthesise grain matched in level, spatial correlation and channel correlation to the flattest parts of a ring around the region; correct colour and tone at the edge by pyramid push-pull; carry detail across the edge with a Laplacian pyramid; composite so that every pixel with zero coverage is copied byte for byte. On the M4, on a loaded host and on JPEGs developed outside Luxforge, LaMa on the CPU provider filled regions of 20 to 60 MP photographs in 1.7 to 3.0 s, the region's refinement adding up to 0.5 s, inside 1.1 to 1.8 GB for ordinary objects and 3.5 GB for a 15 MP region, and left every pixel outside the region byte-identical on every corpus run.

**Its one open problem is quality on large objects**, which is the softness the owner saw. The pinned LaMa export takes a fixed 512² input and the crop is three times the region's longer side, so the fill is enlarged 8.9× for a boy on a bridge, 10 to 13× for a flower or a patch of water, 19.7× for a tall pole and 29.8× for a 60 MP quadrant; grain is re-synthesised but structure is not, and the bridge deck under the boy comes back visibly softer than the deck around it. The full-precision Big-LaMa export scored within 0.1 dB of the quantized one and gave the same smeared deck, so the weights are not the cause; the enlargement is. The plan's fill-quality task settles the remedy by measurement on the prototype's corpus and its synthetic ground truth, in this order:

1. **A larger model input.** Big-LaMa is fully convolutional and stays coherent to about 1536 px; an export at 1024² or 2048², or a dynamic-shape export, cuts the enlargement by two to four times for the price of a slower fill (estimated 4 to 16 times the 512² time on the CPU; measured, not assumed).
2. **A second pass at a finer scale.** Fill at 512² for the coarse structure, then tile the region at a finer scale and fill each tile with the coarse result as its context, so texture is synthesised at a scale closer to the photograph's.
3. **Detail transfer.** Keep the model's output for low and middle frequencies and rebuild high frequencies from the surrounding context by patch matching (HiFill's contextual residual aggregation), which is how 8K fills were produced from a 512 px network.

The same study runs **MI-GAN** beside LaMa, and the better measured result wins whatever its source (owner, 2026-10-05): MI-GAN has 5.95 M parameters, and on an iPad Pro M2 it ran in 1.2 s at 1024² and 3.9 s at 2048² (RETHINED's Table 2 and Figure 3), so a native 2048² fill cuts the enlargement four times without a second pass, at a cost measured on the M4 before it is chosen. The task records the quality (ring and region PSNR and LPIPS against the pasted-object ground truth, and a blinded owner preference), the time and the memory of each, and freezes the Fast pipeline and its model. The Fast tier will still not invent content it has never seen: a large person in a complex scene, or anything whose shadow the selection missed, is the generative tier's job, and the Fast tier says so when a region is large relative to its context (recorded default: a notice when the crop's enlargement exceeds 4×).

### Candidates, Accept and the frozen patch

A fill job holds the revision and the repair prefix it was generated against. Its result is one or more **candidates**, each a composited preview of the region at display resolution and the full-resolution patch bytes, addressed by the job and an index so any client can accept any live candidate. One job holds at most 4 candidates of at most 64 MiB each; across jobs the host holds candidates within one byte budget (recorded default 1 GiB), releases the oldest finished job's candidates first and reports a released candidate on `job.read`; Regenerate is a new job for the same selection and the strip shows every live candidate for it; leaving the tool releases nothing another client holds, and nothing is persisted. Accept rechecks the revision and the prefix, publishes the candidate as an artifact and commits the repair operation as one history entry labelled `Remove` (or `Generative fill`, `Replace "…"`). The operation stores the artifact, the selection and effect region, the crop and encoding transforms, the input-prefix hash and the provenance: model identity, version and hash, runtime, backend and precision, seed, steps and prompt hash where they apply. Rendering reads the patch and never the model, as the Corrections design requires. A late, cancelled or stale candidate cannot touch the current recipe.

### Stale patches

A patch was generated against a prefix: the RAW development's white balance and any earlier repair. When that prefix changes, the patch's pixels no longer match their surroundings. The Corrections design proposed that render, sample and export then fail explicitly until the person regenerates or removes the operation. The owner chose the softer rule on 2026-10-05, and the Corrections design now carries it: the stale operation **keeps rendering** from its frozen patch, its row and the status bar say it is stale and why, `recipe.describe` reports it, and `export.plan` names stale repairs so the desktop asks before exporting and an API client passes an explicit acknowledgement. Nothing is omitted silently, and a white-balance change never blanks the photograph. Lightroom behaves the same way for the edits that follow a removal, since its global adjustments also come after its repairs; whether it regenerates after a white-balance change was not verified. Clone and Heal recompute from their geometry and never go stale; a stored selection is a mask and keeps rendering with a Needs recompute note, as the masking workspace proposes.

## Generative fill and Replace

### The models

The generative tier needs a model that fills a masked region with plausible content from the context, locally, under terms the project can offer. The research's conclusion stands after the prototype and a web check on 2026-10-04: **no licence-clean model is at once effect-aware, fast and high quality.** Two permissive candidates are proposed for qualification, as two sizes of the same tier:

| Candidate | What it is | Cost | Terms |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Moebius** (June 2026) | A 0.22 B-parameter latent inpainter distilled from PixelHacker that takes an image and a binary mask, in four checkpoints of about 0.9 GB; 26 ms per step on a data-centre GPU, and an MLX port fills 512² in about 4 s on Apple silicon with a hard 512 px limit in that port | About 1 GB to download; memory and time on the M4 measured by the qualification task | Apache-2.0 in its repository, MIT on its model card; confirmed before it is pinned |
| **FLUX.2 klein 4B** (January 2026) | A four-step flow model and editor with the capacity for scene content. Its transformer is 7.75 GB in bf16, its VAE 168 MB and its text encoder is Qwen3-4B at 8 GB in bf16 (3.5 GB at 4-bit). There is no Fill checkpoint: diffusers' inpainting pipeline blends latents RePaint-style over the stock weights and its reviewers warn that quality varies on complex scenes, and fal publishes an Apache-2.0 removal LoRA for it trained on 100 pairs, under which large objects still leave artifacts and shadows persist | On Apple silicon at 1024² and 4 steps: about 17 s including a cold load at 4-bit on an M4 Max with a 6.5 GB peak, 24 to 31 s on an M1 Max, a 14 GB peak on an 18 GB M3 Pro without tiling. No M4 Pro figure is published; the research's estimate of 15 to 30 s stands until measured | Apache-2.0 for the 4B model, its VAE and its encoder; the 9B model is non-commercial and excluded |

Moebius is the first to qualify, because a 1 GB model that synthesises structure at the LaMa price point is what most machines can run and most removals need; klein 4B is the high-capacity option for large regions and for Replace. The SDXL-inpainting derivatives trained on removal pairs (OSOR-SDXL, with a head that predicts the shadow and reflection region, and RORem) are faster than klein and handle effects, and carry OpenRAIL++-M use clauses; under the owner's quality-first policy they are qualified beside the others and offered with their one-line restriction if they win. ObjectClear, TurboClear and the FLUX.1 [dev] removers are qualified as well; being non-commercial for the person, they are never a default pick and are offered, if at all, with that restriction stated. A later, licence-clean route the research names and this design keeps open is a removal fine-tune of klein (or of Moebius) on OSOR's Apache-licensed pairs and the owner's own tripod-shot pairs; it is a training project, not a task of this plan.

**Runtime.** `stable-diffusion.cpp` (MIT) runs klein 4B with Metal, Vulkan and CUDA from GGUF weights and has a maintained Rust binding (`diffusion-rs`, MIT); whether its masked path serves klein, and whether it or a plain implementation over the inference port serves Moebius, is what the qualification task establishes. An ONNX export of klein exists but does not run on Core ML, so ONNX Runtime is not the generative tier's route on the Mac.

### Prompt-free fill, prompted Replace

A removal is **prompt-free**: the model is conditioned on a fixed "empty scene" text embedding computed once and shipped as part of the model resource, which removes the text encoder, 3.5 to 8 GB of it for klein, from the download, the memory and the runtime for the common case. **Replace** takes a prompt of at most 256 characters, which needs the text encoder: a second, optional resource the Replace field offers to download the first time it is used. Both run the same pipeline as the Fast tier around the model: the same crop (capped at the model's native size on the crop's longer side, the region at most about a third of it), the same encoding from the stage's linear light to the display-referred input the model was trained on with a recorded inverse, the same grain, edge and detail stages, the same byte-identical composite outside the coverage. Instruction editors that regenerate the whole frame without a mask are excluded by that rule.

### Candidates, seeds and provenance

Generate produces three candidates from three seeds, in sequence rather than in a batch so peak memory stays at one candidate's; Regenerate produces three more as a new job; the strip shows every candidate still held for the region under the retention rule above. Each candidate records its seed, steps, sampler, precision, backend and prompt hash, and the accepted patch stores them, but the frozen patch is the source of truth: the same seed on another machine, driver or runtime version is not promised to reproduce the pixels, and nothing ever re-generates an accepted result.

### Cost, memory and admission

Each generative model declares a minimum machine memory and a resident budget; a machine below the minimum sees the model with its requirement and cannot install it, and a generation that would exceed the budget is refused with `resource-limit` before any allocation. The recorded defaults, to be replaced by the qualification task's figures: at most 30 s per 1024 px candidate and 90 s for three on the M4 Pro, a resident set of at most 16 GB and a minimum of 24 GB unified memory for klein 4B (the floor Adobe set in May 2026 for Photoshop's on-device generative Remove, a 5 GB download that needs an M1 Pro or later with 24 GB), a minimum the qualification task sets for Moebius, and every model unloaded after 10 minutes idle or on a memory signal. The generative tier sits behind an **Experiments flag** (`ai.generative`) until its qualification passes, so the Fast tier can ship first; the flag gates only whether the tier is offered, never how a committed patch renders. The workflow (the tier choice, the candidate strip, Regenerate, the prompt, Accept with provenance) is built and proven over the fake generator before any local model, so a no-go on every local candidate leaves a finished workflow for the remote tier to fill.

### The remote tier (later)

A remote provider is a second module with the same job and result shape: a typed adapter with a **`repair-crop`** data class (an sRGB 8-bit crop of at most 2048² with its mask, the prompt, the candidate count, and nothing else: no path, no EXIF, no full frame unless a separate `full-frame` class is declared and consented), bearer authentication with the key in the OS credential store, consent asked once per provider profile and data class and remembered until revoked (owner, 2026-10-05, replacing the capability contract's per-asset default; the grant scope loses its asset and the change lands with this tier), with the disclosure stating the bytes and the provider's retention note, response validation (dimensions, format, finite values, size) and the same candidate strip and Accept. A user-run loopback service with a documented request shape is the same adapter over the `loopback` endpoint class, which is how a person with their own GPU box or a ComfyUI bridge plugs in. The first hosted provider is an open question; the shape is fixed now so that local and remote fills are interchangeable to the recipe, as Photoshop's Generative Fill now offers Adobe's own and partner models behind one selector.

## Select: automatic masks

### Classes and the dynamic menu

The analysis result lists the classes the photograph contains. The New mask menu's **Select** group draws exactly those, each with its area share as a hint, and nothing for a class that is absent: a studio portrait offers Subject, Background and the person; a seascape offers Sky, Water and perhaps Mountains; Objects is always offered and People entries appear one per detected person. While the analysis runs the group says so; when the Select models are not installed, one entry opens their resource card with the combined size. An agent reads the same list from the analysis task's result.

| Luxforge class | From | Model class mapping (proposal) | Presence rule (starting values) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Subject | Salient-object model (BiRefNet) | the model's foreground | foreground area at least 1% of the frame |
| Background | Subject, inverted | the complement | whenever Subject is present |
| Sky | Panoptic model | sky, cloud | area at least 0.5%, mean confidence at least 0.6 |
| Water | Panoptic model | sea, river, lake, water, waterfall, swimming pool, fountain | same |
| Mountains | Panoptic model | mountain, hill, rock, cliff | same |
| Vegetation | Panoptic model | tree, grass, plant, flower, palm, bush, leaves, field (natural) | same |
| Ground | Panoptic model | road, sidewalk, path, earth, sand, gravel, snow, floor | same |
| Architecture | Panoptic model | building, house, skyscraper, tower, bridge, wall, fence, roof | same |
| Person N | Instance detector (RF-DETR-Seg), refined by a box prompt to the segmenter | each person instance above a score floor (0.5), largest first, at most 16 | per instance |
| Objects | The picker | whatever the person points at | always |

The semantic model proposed is the research's: an EoMT panoptic or semantic model on a DINOv2 backbone (MIT code and MIT checkpoints, Apache backbone; the ADE20K large checkpoint at 512 px has 0.3 B parameters), trained on COCO-Stuff rather than ADE20K where a checkpoint exists, because COCO-Stuff has cloud and snow classes and CC BY annotations. A 2 MB MIT sky segmenter (a U²-Net-p export) is measured beside it as a cheap Sky-only model for machines that skip the Select set. SAM 3's concept prompts are not the route to these classes: its licence is use-restricted and no evaluation of it on sky, water or other stuff classes was found. The table's mapping and thresholds are starting values the qualification task tunes on the corpus and records; the user guide states the classes as a fixed vocabulary, so a photograph never offers a class the guide does not name.

### From a class to a mask

Choosing a class runs `task.select-class {asset_id, class}` (or `task.select-person {asset_id, person}`, `task.select-object {asset_id, candidate|prompt}`) on the module lane. It reads the analysis cache, takes the class's probabilities at the model's resolution (the subject's from the salient model `task.analyse-scene` ran), refines them with the guided upsampler against the cached guide to the artifact size, publishes the 8-bit alpha artifact with the class, model identity, version, input fingerprint and dimensions in its metadata, and the desktop then sends `mask.add-model {mask?, mode, artifact, class, model, model_version, input_fingerprint, feather, threshold}` as one history entry labelled `Add sky (model)`. Subject at the HR model size (2048²) is the quality option the qualification task measures; Background is Subject's artifact with the component inverted, one artifact and no second task. A person is the detector's instance refined by a box prompt to the segmenter inside the person's bounds, so hair and edges come from the segmenter and the guided filter rather than the detector's coarse mask; body parts (hair, face skin, body skin, clothes, from a permissive multiclass model on a crop of each person) are a later slice, and eyes, lips and brows wait for a face parser with acceptable training-data terms.

The `model` component is the masking workspace's: position-based, sampled bilinearly from the artifact, thresholded and feathered by the frozen `smooth`, with bounds from the artifact's non-zero rectangle and its smallest feature from the artifact's resolution, so the coverage overlay, the proxy rule and `render.sample` treat it like a gradient and the sampled byte equals the rendered byte inside, outside and across its feather; its coverage program samples the bound artifact texture so the preview stays on the GPU. The artifact is bound at compile time beside a layer's artifacts, found by admission, pinned by the prepared-artifact cache and kept by retention while any entry references it; that widening of the kind table's compile binding is the one core change Select needs beyond the kind itself. A model component combines with brushes, gradients and ranges through the delivered algebra, so Select Sky minus a brushed subtraction is two components of one mask.

### Staleness

A component records the input fingerprint and the model version it was computed from. When the source changes, the repair prefix changes (an object was removed after the selection) or the installed model's version differs, the row shows **Needs recompute** with the reason and two actions: Recompute runs the task again and `mask.set-model` replaces the artifact as one entry; Keep leaves it. The stored selection renders meanwhile. A crop, a straighten or a lens correction changes nothing, because the artifact lives in content space with every other mask.

### Depth (later)

A depth model (Depth Anything 3 Small, Apache) gives a `depth-range` component kind on the range widget, a focus-like selection by distance; it joins the analysis when its model is installed and is a later slice.

## Sky replacement (optional)

The owner asked for this as a possibility. It is cheap once Select exists, because the model's only contribution is the mask.

A **Sky** module adds one layer at the repair stage, after the Corrections repair layer, whose effect composites a sky raster behind the photograph inside the layer's mask, which defaults to a Select Sky component and is an ordinary mask the person refines. The sky comes from an artifact: a photograph the person imports (which returns the consented `read-user-file` capability, per canonical path), or one of a small set offered as a resource, chosen from CC0 skies. Poly Haven (CC0 with explicit redistribution permission, 299 HDRIs tagged as skies) and ambientCG (CC0, 431 HDRIs) are the first candidates; their files are equirectangular, so preparing a sky reprojects its upper hemisphere to a rectilinear backdrop once, and the result is the artifact. The controls, generated from the descriptor, are the ones Luminar Neo's Sky AI and Photoshop's Sky Replacement share: **Horizon** (vertical position), **Scale**, **Shift**, **Flip**, **Exposure** and **Warmth** of the sky, **Defocus** (a bounded blur of the sky alone), **Edge** (a feather and an inward shift of the matte against bright fringes, Photoshop's Shift Edge and Fade Edge) and **Relight** (the amount by which the foreground's low-frequency colour is pulled toward the sky's, applied through the inverted mask as a second composed layer, Photoshop's Foreground Lighting). Luminar's water reflections, close-gaps refinement and relight-human switch are later. Luminar offers the tool only when at least 15% of the frame is sky; the Select presence rule plays that part here.

It renders exactly: the composite is `out = (1 − M) × input + M × sky(T(p))` in linear light with a bounded read of the sky raster through the horizon, scale, shift and flip transform, which is the masked-primitive blend the renderer already does with a position-based source; it has an `f64` reference and exact fixtures on the CPU and a WGSL program within the declared tolerance on the GPU, like every module under GPU-first. The mask's edge at trees and hair is the guided upsampler's work, the Apache-2.0 method Google published for sky optimisation. A sky artifact is at most 4096 px on its long side and at most 64 MiB, pinned through the prepared-artifact cache. A **generative sky** needs no new code: Replace with a sky prompt inside the Select Sky mask.

## Budgets and limits

Accepted by the owner on 2026-10-05 as starting figures, each replaced by a measured figure before its feature is called done, under one rule: **quality first**. A budget that would degrade a result is raised, with the measurement that shows the need, rather than the result reduced; the optimisation comes after the quality is right. Every measurement runs on the M4 Pro with photo-sized inputs and recorded scope ([performance rules](../engineering/performance-rules.md)).

| Figure | Budget |
| --- | --- |
| Hover, pointer to drawn overlay | p50 at most 20 ms, p95 at most 33 ms |
| Analysis ready after the tool is picked, models loaded | at most 300 ms at 24 MP and 500 ms at 60 MP for the segmenter; at most 1.5 s more for the semantic, people and depth models |
| Warm model load | at most 2 s for the segmenter and the Fast fill together; at most 3 s for the Select set; a first compile shown as progress |
| Fast fill, region to accepted preview | at most 5 s at 60 MP for a region of at most 16 MP |
| Generative candidate | at most 30 s per 1024 px candidate, 90 s for three |
| A selection, class to artifact | at most 2 s including refinement to 4096 px |
| Resident memory | segmenter 1 GB, Fast fill 2 GB, Select set 3 GB, generative 16 GB; each model's figure recorded and unloaded on idle or a memory signal; a model's machine minimum counts the resident models, the held candidates and the analysis cache together |
| Downloads | Remove's models about 240 MB (the segmenter and the Fast fill); Select's set stated per model before download; the light generative model about 1 GB; klein 4B about 4 to 8 GB without its text encoder, 3.5 to 8 GB more for Replace |
| Artifacts | a selection at most 16 MiB; a patch at most 64 MiB (half-float linear RGB over the region's bounds); a sky at most 64 MiB; every stack's artifacts within the 256 MiB bind budget, which is raised with a measurement if patches need it |

Admission limits, refused with `resource-limit`: a region of at most 32 MP and half the frame for either tier (the crop around it is bounded only by the frame and is reduced to the model's input size, which is where a large region pays in enlargement, flagged by the notice; tiling at the model's resolution is a later option); at most 4 candidates per job and 1 GiB of candidates held across jobs; a prompt of at most 256 characters; at most 16 people; a stack whose artifacts would not bind within the 256 MiB budget, checked before Accept and before `mask.add-model`; the delivered stroke, mask and lane limits.

## Platforms

Everything portable runs on the three platforms through the same port; Core ML is an accelerator on macOS, DirectML on Windows, the CPU everywhere, with the backend recorded in every artifact's provenance. Apple's Vision requests are not used for selection: their instance masks cover noticeable foreground objects only, the brush-seeded request needs macOS 27 and Swift, and the same photograph must select the same way on every platform. Windows' NPU-only imaging APIs are not used either. Native GPU performance is claimed only where measured natively; a VM or CI run proves function. The fake runtime makes every journey run in CI.

## Verification and acceptance

- **Core, exact.** The stroke decision (one, several, nothing, ties, parts and wholes) on recorded candidate sets; the removal region's prior, window, core, dilation and feather against `f64` references; the guided upsampler bit-identical to its reference; the fill's crop, encoding inverse, grain estimate, push-pull and pyramid blend on synthetic fixtures; every fill's pixels outside the coverage byte-identical to the input; the model kind's coverage equation and `render.sample` equal to the rendered byte; the model and depth kinds' GPU coverage programs and the repair and sky composites' GPU programs held to the reference renderer by `cargo xtask gpu-qualification` for every output kind and view they touch.
- **Artifacts and recipes.** A selection's artifact identical across two runs; publish, commit, reopen, undo, redo, restore, branches and versions for selections, patches and skies; missing and corrupt artifacts failing by name; a removed model still rendering every accepted edit; the stale states for a changed source, prefix and model version; export binding the same artifacts and naming stale repairs.
- **Capabilities.** Multi-file resources, resumed downloads, the plain-language labels and the use restriction in the disclosure with no training-data note, `local-runtime` activation, unload on idle and on signal, `module.status` reporting the resident set, refusals below the machine minimum and above every admission limit.
- **Parity.** Every desktop gesture (hover, click, Alt-click, box, stroke, pins, Fill tier, Generate, Regenerate, Accept, Select, Recompute, Keep, every Sky control) sends a request `Copy as JSON request` shows, and the acceptance drives the same journeys through `luxforge-json`.
- **Rendered.** Smoke scenarios `remove`, `ai-select`, `generative`, `sky` and `remote` (over the loopback fake provider) over the fake runtime: the analysing state, the hover highlight, pins, the selection tint, a fill's progress and result, the candidate strip, the Select group for a photograph with and without a sky, a model row's Needs recompute, the Sky section; each frame's state correlated with the plan and the logs.
- **Native, real models.** Opt-in runs with the models installed (`--models DIR`, as the authentic RAW tests take their directory), on the prototype's corpus and the CC0 corpus: hover and analysis latencies, fill and candidate times, peak memory, download sizes and the quality scores, with the host's load recorded; a blinded preference test by the owner against Lightroom's Remove and Generative Remove on the same photographs; Windows and Linux functional runs of the same journeys.
- **A skipped check is not a pass.** A tier without its model installed is `skipped` with the reason, never green.

## Decisions

Decided by the owner on 2026-10-05, recorded in [product decisions](../decisions.md#ai-editing). The research's nine decisions are folded in; its decision 9 (removal before semantic masks) was taken on 2026-09-28 and is reflected in the order above.

| # | Decision | Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Scope | **Accepted**: Remove, Select, generative fill and Replace, and sky replacement; the roadmap's exclusion of generative editing is lifted |
| 2 | Weights licence policy | **Quality first, no licence gate.** A licence keeps no model out; only a restriction on the person's own use of their results is shown, in one line, and the default pick for each feature is the best model without one |
| 3 | Training-data provenance | **Not a criterion and not disclosed.** The best model is chosen on measured results |
| 4 | Fast fill model | **The best measured result wins**, LaMa or MI-GAN, whatever its source |
| 5 | Stale patches | **Keep rendering the frozen patch, mark it stale, ask before export**; the Corrections design is updated to match |
| 6 | Inference runtime | **Delegated**, to be chosen for quality first, then performance, then maintainability: ONNX Runtime through `ort` with Core ML over fixed-shape copies for the segmenters and semantic models and the CPU provider for the fill is the measured choice under that order; `tract` re-measured when it loads the models |
| 7 | Segmenter | EfficientViT-SAM L0 by default, SAM 2.1 Small as an alternative, **each choice presented as a plain trade-off** ("Finds smaller objects; slower to start and uses more memory"), never by its technical name alone |
| 8 | Generative models | Moebius and FLUX.2 klein 4B qualified first and ranked on quality, the OpenRAIL++-M removers beside them, **presented the same way** ("Higher quality, needs 24 GB of memory") |
| 9 | Effect region | **Accepted**: a visible, editable part of the selection, suggested by a heuristic |
| 10 | Budgets and limits | **Accepted as starting figures, including the 256 MiB analysis cache and the 1 GiB of held candidates, under the quality-first rule**: a budget is raised with a measurement rather than a result reduced; optimisation comes after |
| 11 | Hardware minimum | **Accepted**: refuse to install below the model's declared minimum, 24 GB unified memory by default, with the requirement shown |
| 12 | Remote provider consent | **Remembered per provider profile and data class until revoked**, in place of the capability contract's per-asset default; the contract change lands with the remote tier |
| 13 | Repair stage (Corrections) | **Accepted**: after source development and before colour, in content coordinates; `PointReplace` is deleted rather than reused |
| 14 | Sky replacement | **Accepted**: a deterministic layer over the Select Sky mask, with imported skies or a CC0 set |

Recorded defaults the owner can revise: the prototype's move onto `main` as the AI harness (`probes/object-removal`, outside the editor's workspace, as the owner placed it); the plain-language labels for each model choice; the Remove letter `Q`; three candidates per Generate; a 10-minute idle unload; a 256 MiB analysis cache; the class vocabulary and presence rules; Background as inverted Subject; the Fast tier's 4× enlargement notice; a prompt limit of 256 characters; the `ai.generative` flag's existence until qualification; Poly Haven as the sky set's first candidate.
