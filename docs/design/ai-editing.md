# AI editing

Status: **decided; in implementation, generative fill first.** The Corrections foundation it depends on (the repair stage, the shared brush, frozen patches and the candidate seam) is delivered. Generative fill comes ahead of the Fast tier and Select: its workflow is built on the inference foundation with a brush selection while the generative models are qualified, and object picking and the Fast tier join the same Remove mode later ([Delivery order](#delivery-order)). This design brings AI editing into Luxforge: object removal, generative fill and replace, automatic masks that follow what a photograph contains and, as an option, sky replacement. Everything runs on **local models the person downloads on request**: nothing is bundled, nothing is fetched without a disclosure and a grant, and nothing leaves the machine unless a remote provider is configured and consented, which this design shapes and defers. Its plan is [tasks/editing/ai-editing.json](../../tasks/editing/ai-editing.json).

It builds on what exists. The [object removal research](../research/object-removal/README.md) compared the models, runtimes and licences. The standalone object removal prototype (the [`probes/object-removal`](../../probes/object-removal/README.md) workspace, which holds its contract and measurements and is the AI harness the later qualification tasks extend) measured a segmenter, a fill and a hover loop on the M4; its figures are quoted below. The [Corrections](corrections.md) design supplies the repair stage, the brush and the frozen patch that every removal commits through; this design owns AI Remove, and Corrections owns Clone, Heal and that foundation. The [masking workspace](masking-workspace.md)'s model-selection kind (its phase E) is delivered here with a class menu that changes with the photograph. The [module capabilities](module-capabilities.md) contract supplies downloads, consent, tasks and derived artifacts unchanged, apart from the `local-runtime` capability this design is the first consumer of. [Settings and flags](settings-and-flags.md) supply the Settings surface and the experiment flags.

The scope is settled ([Decisions](#decisions)). Built so far: the inference foundation, the generative workflow ([The delivered workflow](#the-delivered-workflow)), the `model` mask kind with its artifact binding ([The model kind](#the-model-kind)) and the guided upsampler ([The guided upsampler](#the-guided-upsampler)). Everything else below is a proposal, and a command it names exists only where the [API](#api) table says it is implemented.

## What a person gets

Four tools, named as Lightroom names them where the behaviour is the same, in the order they are proposed to land.

1. **Remove.** With the Remove tool, the object under the pointer is highlighted as the pointer moves, and a click removes it. A rough brush stroke removes the object it covers; when several objects lie under it, each gets a numbered pin and the person picks; when none is found, the painted area itself is removed. The shadow or reflection an object casts is part of the selection, painted or suggested, never guessed silently. The fill comes from a local model and takes a few seconds; the selection stays visible and cancellable while it runs. The result is a correction in the recipe: undoable, visible in history, carried through later crops and adjustments, exported with everything else, and rendered from stored pixels, so it never needs the model again.
2. **Select.** The Masks panel's New mask menu gains a Select group that lists what *this* photograph contains: Subject, Background, Sky, the people in it one by one, and the landscape classes present (Water, Mountains, Vegetation, Ground, Architecture), with Objects for anything the person points at. Choosing one creates a mask whose coverage a model computed once and the host stored; the person refines it with the existing brushes and ranges, and every slider in the maskable sections works on it as on any mask. A selection says which model made it and when, and offers Recompute when the photograph or the model changed.
3. **Generative fill and Replace.** Remove's Fill setting gains a **Generative** tier: a local generative model rebuilds the region as believable scene content rather than a blur, offers three variations and three more on Regenerate, and commits only the one the person accepts. With a prompt it becomes Replace: the region is filled with what the prompt describes. The generative model is a large download (gigabytes) and needs a capable machine; it is offered, with its requirements, and never installed by default.
4. **Sky replacement** (optional). A Sky section composites a chosen sky behind the Select Sky mask with horizon, scale, flip, exposure, warmth, defocus, edge and relight controls, from a sky the person imports or one of a small permissively licensed set offered for download. The composite is an ordinary deterministic layer: the model contributes the mask, not the pixels, so its CPU reference is exact and its GPU composite is held within the declared tolerance. A generative sky is Replace with a sky prompt inside the same mask.

What this design does not propose: AI denoise or upscaling, generative expand, text-to-image, face or body retouching, automatic "find distractions" suggestions, a built-in chat, accounts or a hosted service of Luxforge's own. Remote providers are shaped (one typed adapter, one data class, remembered consent) and delivered last, after the local tiers.

## How the pillars apply

- **Originals are sacred.** A model's output is a derived artifact the recipe references: an alpha raster for a selection, a patch for a fill, a sky raster for a composite. Rendering, sampling and export read the artifact and never call a model, so an edit survives the model's removal, a change of runtime and a different machine. A stale result is marked, never regenerated silently and never discarded.
- **Everything is programmable.** Every gesture has a method: analysis is a task, a hover pick is a query, a selection is a task that publishes an artifact and a mask command that binds it, a fill is a task whose candidates the client reads and an action that accepts one. An agent can remove an object, select the sky or replace it through `luxforge-json` with the same requests the desktop sends, and the acceptance tests do exactly that.
- **Fast, bounded and honest.** Inference runs off the owner and interface threads, on the capability lanes and one dedicated hover worker; every model declares its memory and is unloaded when idle; every region, crop, candidate and download has a limit refused by name. A preview that differs from the exact render says so. A hover decode, an analysis, a fill and a generative candidate each have a budget measured on the M4 with photo-sized inputs, and a miss is published, not hidden.
- **Small core, deliberate extension points.** The core gains one narrow inference port, one analysis cache, one model-selection mask kind and the `local-runtime` capability. The models, their pre- and post-processing and the fill pipeline live in a module; the runtime lives in a crate the core never links, injected as the transport is. A second provider, local or remote, registers without changing a recipe shape.
- **Open source, first on the owner's Mac.** Project code stays GPL-3.0-or-later; the runtime is MIT; weights are downloads whose licence, provenance and training-data terms are stated before the download, under a policy the owner sets. Core ML accelerates on the Mac; the same models run on Windows and Linux through the same port, measured for function there and for speed only natively.
- **Prove it.** Exact-buffer tests for everything deterministic (the removal region, the guided upsampler, the composite, the sky transform), outside-mask byte identity for every fill, bit-identical artifacts across two runs for every selection, a fake runtime so the journeys run in CI without a download, and native M4 runs with the real models recorded with their scope.
- **Beautiful defaults, familiar feel.** Remove behaves like Lightroom's Remove with Detect objects and its People pins; Select like Lightroom's AI masks; Generative like Generative Remove's variations. None is a rendering target.

Three services are shared by every feature: the inference port with its model resources, the per-photo analysis cache, and one picker that turns a point, a box or a stroke into candidate objects. Each feature is a thin consumer: Remove reads a candidate and asks for a fill; Select reads a class map and publishes an alpha; Sky reads that alpha and composites.

### The inference port and runtime

The core declares a narrow port and links no runtime (`crates/luxforge-core/src/inference/`), and gives modules one host-planned pixel read (described under [Analysis](#analysis-and-the-per-photo-cache)), because the generative fill reads its crop before any analysis exists. An `InferenceRuntime` names itself and the backends it offers and opens a model file whose identity (id, version, SHA-256) the install verified, on the first backend of the capability's list it offers, at a declared precision. A `Session` reports its placement (backend, device, precision, partitions) and its input and output tensor specs and runs named tensors in and out, each tensor at most 1 GiB. A `Generator` takes one request, a crop in the model's encoded domain (display-referred RGB `0..=1`, at most 4096 px a side), its mask, optional conditioning (a prompt of at most 256 characters or a precomputed embedding), a seed and 1 to 1000 steps, and yields one candidate, reporting progress after each step and stopping with `cancelled` at the next step boundary once its job is cancelled; a module calls it once per candidate. Every result carries its **provenance**: model id, version and hash, runtime and version, backend, precision, seed, steps, sampler and the prompt's SHA-256, never the prompt. Every trait is object-safe, `Send` and `Sync`. The implementations live in crates of their own, handed to the host as `HostConfig::inference` exactly as the transport and the secret store are; `None` means the host runs no local inference. A deterministic fake runtime (`inference::fake`) implements both traits inside `luxforge-core` itself and is installed only in developer runs of the desktop and `luxforge-json` and in tests: its session and generator outputs are pure functions of the model's hash, the inputs, the seed and the steps, distinct seeds give visibly different fills, and it has knobs to slow each step and to fail an open, a run or a given step. `luxforge-testbase` may depend on no workspace crate and the core may name no crate that depends on it, so the fake can live nowhere else. Two dependency rules in `cargo xtask check-repository` keep the native code out of the core: `core-links-no-runtime` refuses `ort`, `ort-sys`, `luxforge-onnx` and every other model runtime crate in `luxforge-core`'s manifest, and `onnx-runtime-home` lets only `luxforge-onnx` name `ort`, so the core's tests build no native code. A runtime may also derive files from a model at install time (`InferenceRuntime::prepare`, which returns the paths it wrote so the install can record them); the default derives none. `CompositeInference` (in the core) serves several runtimes as one, dispatching each open by backend name, refusing a backend two members claim, and naming itself after its members.

**Recommendation: ONNX Runtime through `ort`, statically linked, as the port's first backend.** The research left the choice between `tract` and ONNX Runtime to measurement, and the prototype measured it. `tract` 0.23.8 loads one of the five pinned models: its ONNX shape inference refuses the dynamic batch and point dimensions of the EfficientViT-SAM and SAM 2.1 decoders, and it does not implement the blocked `DequantizeLinear` of the LaMa export. ONNX Runtime 1.28 runs all five on the CPU provider and, over copies of each model whose free input dimensions ONNX Runtime itself fixes at 1, on Core ML's GPU with masks matching the CPU's at IoU 0.998 or better. On the M4 Pro, EfficientViT-SAM L0 then encodes a 24 MP photograph in about 110 ms and a 60 MP one in 130 to 160 ms, decodes a hover point in about 5 ms, loads warm in about 1.2 s and compiles once in about 7 s, inside a 1 GB footprint. LaMa stays on the CPU provider: on Core ML it runs in 188 partitions, no faster, with a 9 GB footprint against 1.5 GB. The segmenter proposed is EfficientViT-SAM L0; SAM 2.1 Small finds small objects EfficientViT misses (kayaks on dark water, two walkers) but loads in 10 to 20 s even cached, holds 3.5 GB and compiles for 30 to 90 s the first time, so it is offered as an alternative the person can choose, presented as "Finds smaller objects; slower to start and uses more memory", not the default ([Decisions](#decisions)).

What adopting `ort` costs, and how each cost is held:

| Cost | How it is held |
| --- | --- |
| A prebuilt static archive from one maintainer's CDN, `ort` still a release candidate | `ort-sys` refuses an archive whose SHA-256 differs from its own table, which the lockfile's checksum pins. `check-repository` pins `ort` and `ort-sys` (version and checksum), ONNX Runtime 1.28.0 and each target's archive (URL, length, SHA-256), the feature sets that choose the archive, and refuses a Cargo configuration that points the build at another library (`xtask/src/onnxruntime.rs`). `cargo xtask onnxruntime-attestation --output NEW` downloads the three archives and verifies pyke's published SLSA build attestation for each with `gh attestation verify`, recording the result. A source build of ONNX Runtime is not attempted (it needs CMake, Python and C++20) |
| The Core ML provider takes only static shapes | The fixed-shape copy is written by ONNX Runtime at install time, once per model file, into the model's own directory, and recorded as part of the install; a shape the copy does not take runs on a CPU session of the original |
| Core ML compiles on first load | The compile cache lives under the application data root, keyed by model hash, runtime version and OS build; the first load of a model shows its compile as progress, never as a hang |
| fp16 on the Neural Engine changes masks | Every model pins its compute units and precision; the Neural Engine setting is not used until a model passes an fp16 parity check against its fp32 output |
| Windows and Linux | DirectML from the operating system's copy on Windows, CPU elsewhere; CUDA only as a user-installed, later option. Functional checks there, no native speed claims |

**`luxforge-onnx`** implements `InferenceRuntime` and `Session` (not `Generator`) over `ort` 2.0.0-rc.13 and ONNX Runtime 1.28.0, statically linked. It names itself `onnxruntime` with the linked library's version and offers `coreml` then `cpu` on macOS, `directml` then `cpu` on Windows and `cpu` elsewhere; the capability's list chooses among them. The desktop and `luxforge-json` hand it to the host from `luxforge_onnx::host_runtime_with`, which composes it with the runtimes it is given (the generative tier's, below) and in developer runs with the fake under the backend name `fake`, so a developer module lists `fake` to get the fake and real backends otherwise.

- **Placement is what ONNX Runtime reports.** Each session records its graph assignment (C API 24's `Session_GetEpGraphAssignmentInfo`); a Core ML session holding every node is `gpu`, one holding none `cpu`, a split `mixed` with the number of Core ML partitions. A CPU session is `cpu` with no partition count, because the CPU provider records one subgraph a node.
- **Pinned settings.** Core ML runs as an ML Program on the CPU and GPU only: the Neural Engine is never used, static input shapes are required, and low-precision GPU accumulation is enabled only for an fp16 model. Every session is optimised at the basic level. DirectML runs without memory patterns or parallel execution, as it requires.
- **The fixed-shape copy.** On macOS, `prepare` (or the first Core ML open when the install did not) writes `NAME.coreml-fixed.onnx` and `NAME.coreml-fixed.json` beside the model: ONNX Runtime fixes every named free dimension at 1 and saves the graph. The record holds the source hash, the runtime version, the fixed dimensions, the original and fixed tensor specs and the copy's length and SHA-256; a copy whose record, length or hash disagrees, or that another runtime version wrote, is rewritten, through a staged file and a rename. A model with an unnamed free dimension is refused on Core ML as incompatible and still runs on the CPU. A static model needs no copy. The session reports the original's specs, sends inputs at the fixed shapes to Core ML and any other shape to a CPU session of the original, opened on first need.
- **The compile cache** is `<data>/inference/coreml/<sha256>-onnxruntime-<version>-<OS build>`; opening a model prunes its other keys, a failed open over an existing cache removes it and retries once, and `luxforge_onnx::remove_compiled` removes a model's entries when its resource is removed. Without a readable OS build the cache is not used.
- **Cancellation and errors.** A load is cancellable mid-way (ONNX Runtime's load canceller, set by a watcher that lives only while the load runs); a run is checked before and after, not interrupted. A missing file maps to `source-unavailable`; a malformed, unsupported or not-implemented graph to `incompatible`; a bad argument or tensor to `validation`; an output over the tensor limit to `resource-limit`; anything else to `render`.
- **Evidence.** Unit tests run a two-node model the tests encode themselves on every offered backend: backend choice, placement and the assignment, the copy written once, rewritten when stale or damaged and not written for a static model, a batch the copy does not take matching the CPU exactly, input validation, cancellation and error mapping. An ignored native test (`cargo test -p luxforge-onnx -- --ignored`, models from `LUXFORGE_ONNX_MODELS` or the main checkout's `private/models/object-removal`) opens the pinned EfficientViT-SAM L0 encoder and decoder on the CPU and on Core ML over the fixed copies and holds Core ML to the CPU at a mask IoU of at least 0.998 for eight points on a synthetic picture, and the embedding within 1e-3. On the M4 Pro both models were `mixed` (2 and 3 partitions, fp32), the IoU 1.0 at every point and the largest embedding difference 2.1e-5. The prototype's corpus, Windows and Linux runs are outstanding.

**The generative tier's runtime is `stable-diffusion.cpp`**, chosen by its qualification ([Generative fill and Replace](#the-models)). A 4B-parameter flow model is not one ONNX graph that ONNX Runtime's Core ML provider will take whole; `stable-diffusion.cpp` (MIT; Metal, Vulkan and CUDA; GGUF weights; masked inpainting) builds from vendored, pinned sources with the `cc` crate as LibRaw does, within the one-toolchain rule: no CMake, no Python, no second build system.

**`luxforge-diffusion`** implements `InferenceRuntime` and `Generator` (not `Session`) over that build, stable-diffusion.cpp `master-956-1b0ba10` with ggml 0.25.3 `d25b121c`, vendored under `crates/luxforge-diffusion/vendor/` and compiled by `native/build.rs`, which the AI harness's build script shares. It names itself `stable-diffusion.cpp` with that version and offers `metal` on macOS and `diffusion-cpu` on Linux (named apart from ONNX Runtime's `cpu`, since the composite refuses a backend two members claim); Windows compiles no runtime and it offers no backend there. The desktop and `luxforge-json` pass it to `host_runtime_with` in every run, so the composite dispatches klein's opens to it and every other open to ONNX Runtime.

- **One candidate a call.** `open_generator` opens klein from an installed resource whose files sit as `luxforge_diffusion::layout` names them; the tests hold that layout to the registered descriptors. A generator opened from the generator resource takes removals only; one opened from the text encoder resource, with the generator as its one companion, takes prompts too. Each `generate` builds the harness's inputs (the crop's 8-bit codes with the hole mid-grey, the hole mask and the crop with the object outlined in red), runs four flow steps at CFG 1 through the masked path (removal with fal's LoRA and the shipped conditioning; Replace with "Replace the highlighted object with …" and no LoRA), reports each finished step and returns one candidate. Generations run one at a time in a process.
- **Conditioning inspection.** Only the qualification probe records text-encoder conditionings for its `save_condition` API. The production shim has no inspection store, so encoded Replace prompts and their tensor copies are not retained there after a generation or model unload; the model's own bounded conditioning cache follows its context's lifetime.
- **Conditioning admission.** Opening a generator validates its conditioning's dimensions and complete payload lengths before opening weights, without allocating tensor payloads. Loading then checks the same bounds before allocating each field: at most two fields, each at most 2²⁸ float values, and never more values than the file actually contains.
- **Placement and provenance.** A Metal generator is `gpu`, a CPU one `cpu`, both `mixed` precision (a 4-bit transformer with bf16 layers and autoencoder); the sampler is the runtime's default sampler and scheduler for the model, as it names them, and every candidate names the generator resource's identity whichever slot opened it.
- **Admission and cancellation.** Before anything is allocated it refuses a side over 1024 px as `resource-limit` with `data.limit: "generation-side"` (the runtime reserves its compute buffers for the first size it meets, so the measured memory holds only at that size), a side that is not a multiple of 16 as `validation`, and a prompt to a generator without its encoder or a tensor embedding as `incompatible`. A cancel is answered at the next step boundary or between the encode, sampling and decode stages; a running step finishes first (about 30 s at 1024 px on the M4 Pro), and a cancelled generation answers `cancelled`, never a late candidate.
- **Prepare.** An install runs `prepare`, which opens the Metal backend once so its kernels compile at install rather than at the first generation; it writes nothing beside the model.
- **Evidence.** Unit tests cover the layout against the descriptors, the backend names, the file lookup and its refusals, admission, the inputs and the outline. Ignored native tests (`cargo test -p luxforge-diffusion --release -- --ignored`, models from `LUXFORGE_GENERATIVE_MODELS` or the main checkout's `private/models/generative`) reproduce the harness's 1024 px removal candidate byte for byte from its dumped input and prove that a cancel answers at the next step with nothing published; `tests/loopback_install.rs` installs both resources over a loopback HTTP server through the real transport, the conditioning release asset included, runs `prepare` and loads both `local-runtime` slots.

### Models: manifest, download, activation and memory

A model is a capability **resource** of the `luxforge.ai` module, declared with the fields the contract already has (pinned URL, bytes, SHA-256, licence, provenance, redirect origins) and two it adds: a **use restriction**, one plain line present only when the licence restricts how the person may use their own results, and the **runtime requirements** (backend, precision, resident memory, minimum machine memory). Multi-file models (an encoder and a decoder, a transformer and its autoencoder) are one resource with several files, installed together or not at all. Downloads are resumable: the staging keeps what an interrupted transfer fetched and the next install of the same pins resumes each file with an HTTP range, hashing the whole file at the end. The disclosure before a download states the size, where the files go and the use restriction when there is one, and a download is a grant per resource version and origin. The runtime requirements are the `local-runtime` capability's, which the resource listing reports beside the resource's minimum machine memory; a machine below it sees the requirement and cannot install the model. After an install, the runtime prepares the model (the fixed-shape Core ML copy), recorded as part of the install; removal takes it and the model's Core ML compiles. These are delivered ([module capabilities](module-capabilities.md#lifecycle-jobs-and-resources)).

**Model policy: quality first.** Models are chosen and ranked by measured results, and neither a weight licence nor the terms of a model's training data keeps a model out of the lists or appears in the disclosure. Weights are the person's own downloads from their publishers, pinned by hash, not dependencies of the project, so the open-source pillar is unaffected. The one thing shown is what affects the person: when a licence restricts how they may use their results (non-commercial, field-of-use), the card and the disclosure say so in one line, and the default pick for each feature is the best model whose terms do not restrict the person's own use of their photographs. The candidates the research and a web check found, by class of terms:

| Terms | Candidates |
| --- | --- |
| Unrestricted (Apache-2.0, MIT) | EfficientViT-SAM, SAM 2.1, RF-DETR-Seg, Depth Anything 3 Small and Base, FLUX.2 klein 4B with its Qwen3 text encoder and VAE, MediaPipe multiclass, fal's klein removal LoRA, BiRefNet and BiRefNet_HR, MI-GAN, the EoMT ADE20K checkpoints, Moebius, Big-LaMa |
| Use-restricted for the person (OpenRAIL++-M use clauses, the SAM License's field-of-use terms, the DINOv3 licence) | OSOR-SDXL, RORem, SAM 3 and 3.1, EoMT on DINOv3 |
| Non-commercial for the person | ObjectClear and TurboClear, EdgeSAM, SegFormer, RMBG, Qwen-Image-2.1, every FLUX.1 [dev] remover, FLUX.2 klein 9B |

Every choice between models is presented as a plain trade-off the person can act on, such as "Better on small objects, slower to load" or "Higher quality, needs 24 GB of memory", with the model's technical name secondary.

**Lazy activation.** Nothing loads until a tool needs it. Picking the Remove tool, opening the Select group or pressing Generate first asks for the models the tool needs: an uninstalled model shows the setup with Set up; an installed one loads on the module lane while the tool shows that it is loading, and Clone and Heal work meanwhile. The core holds models through the **`local-runtime`** capability ([module capabilities](module-capabilities.md#local-runtime)): a module that declares it holds a loaded session or generator between tasks in a host-owned slot, counted at its declared resident size against the host's resident budget (16 GiB by default; idle models are released least recently used first to make room); a model is released after an idle interval (`RUNTIME_IDLE_UNLOAD`, 10 minutes), on the host's memory signal (`OwnerHandle::memory_signal`), when its resource is removed and when the owner stops, and is never loaded by `module.list`, `schema.list`, `module.status`, a catalog open or a launch. Two methods make a tool's warm-up on pick and the Models tab's Unload now requests any client can send: `module.runtime.load {module_id, resource_id}` loads a model ahead of its first task on the module lane, and `module.runtime.unload {module_id, resource_id?}` releases one or all of them and cancels their loads; a task's first use loads the model too. `module.status` reports the resident set, each model's placement and what it holds. Settings has a **Models** tab ([delivered](module-capabilities.md#desktop)): every known model with its state, size, trade-off, its use restriction if it has one and Remove, the resident set and an Unload now, and one line naming this machine's memory against each model's minimum; each feature's default tier joins it with the first feature that offers one.

### Analysis and the per-photo cache

Three tasks prepare a photograph, one per model set, because a task's declared resources must all be installed before it may run and the sets are installed separately: `task.analyse {asset_id}` runs the segmenter's encoder and serves Remove and Objects; `task.analyse-scene {asset_id}` runs the semantic model, the salient-subject model and the people detector and serves Select; `task.analyse-depth {asset_id}` runs the depth model. Each renders the same **analysis input** on the module lane, the repair stage's output (the developed, oriented photograph with its repairs applied, before Detail, colour and geometry) reduced to at most 1024 px on the long side, and a one-channel **guide**, that stage's luminance at the artifact resolution (at most 4096 px on the long side, at most 16 MiB), because a mask refined against the 1024 px reduction cannot recover the hair and branches the reduction lost. They publish nothing: the embedding (about 16 MiB), the per-class and subject probabilities, the people instances, the depth map and the guide go into a **bounded, recomputable analysis cache** keyed by the source fingerprint, the hash of the repair prefix (source interpretation, upstream layers and referenced masks, repair operations and their artifact/stroke identities, and sampling mode; `spatial::prefix_hash` alone is insufficient) and each model's identity and version. The cache holds a few photographs' analyses within a declared budget (recorded default 256 MiB in memory, least recently used, counted in the machine minimum beside the resident models), is separate from the artifacts recipes reference, is never written to a catalog, and is listed under performance rule 14 with its key, bound and invalidation like every disposable cache. Each result reports what it found (the classes with their area share and confidence, each person's bounds, the model versions), so a client can draw the Select menu or the pins without another request, and repeating a task over an unchanged input answers from the cache.

The analysis input is chosen so a removal is seen by every later selection: after an object is removed, Select Subject analyses the repaired photograph. A colour edit does not change it, which is what makes a stored selection stable under ordinary editing, as Lightroom's are. The delivered [GPU-first](gpu-first.md) tile service, `luxforge_core::tiles`, answers bounded rectangles of a stage off the owner, through the desktop's GPU tile worker or `ReferenceTiles` in `luxforge-json`, with `{record, reason}` naming the renderer. A task reads its photograph through the **host-planned read** ([module capabilities](module-capabilities.md#the-host-planned-read)): one that declares `reads: repair-input` is given, when it is requested, the asset's current entry bound as one immutable evaluation and the input identity of the stage the repair layer receives, and on the module lane `ModuleContext::stage_reader()` reads bounded rectangles of that stage's linear values (`ReadStage::Before {layer, mode}`, `ReadValues::Linear`) with cancellation, answering each with the identity a fill's candidates are accepted against. The analysis tasks add the repair stage's output as a second boundary and reduce it on the module lane in bounded tiles for the analysis input and the guide. Module reads never enter the owner's tile queue, whose nine waiting calls stay the eight clients' and the desktop's: they hold one reader per owner, refuse a second `resource-limit`, and answer at most 4 Mi pixels a read. `TileService::stream` answers output-stage RGBA8 bands for export, not bands of an arbitrary prefix.

**AI input renderer.** Every analysis input, guide, fill crop and remote `repair-crop` body is read from the whole-frame reference on every host, through the host-planned read, which reads the reference independently of the production GPU provider. Source, repair-prefix and model identities remain in the cache key; runtime, backend and precision remain in provenance. This removes renderer rounding variation from the inputs; identical reference input does not promise identical model output across execution providers or precision.

### Picking: hover, click, box and brush

Hover needs a latest-pointer worker rather than serial module jobs: the module lane runs one job with four queued, although the desktop reads job changes through event-driven waits. A **hover worker**, started with the first Remove or Objects pick and stopped with it, holds the loaded decoder and the current embedding and keeps only the latest pointer position, so a burst of moves decodes once. Its output is view state and never an edit. In the prototype's hidden window this loop drew the overlay at a p50 of 20 ms and a p95 of 33 to 35 ms from the pointer event on Core ML, which meets the median of the 16 to 33 ms budget and sits at its edge in the tail; on the CPU provider the p95 was 45 ms, a miss.

The same picker answers a click, a dragged rectangle (a box prompt), Alt-click (a negative point) and a brush stroke, for Remove and for New mask › Objects alike. A stroke becomes prompts the way the prototype does: its padded bounding box as a box prompt and a few farthest-point-sampled positive points, decoded in one batch; each candidate scored on predicted IoU, stability, stroke coverage and object coverage over one working raster; parts yielding to wholes; duplicates suppressed largest first; and a decision of **One**, **Several** or **Nothing** with every tie broken in a fixed order, so identical model outputs and stroke inputs give the same decision. Its programmatic form is `query.pick-object {asset_id, prompt: {point|points|box|stroke}}`, which returns the candidates (identity, scores, bounds, a low-resolution coverage preview) and the decision, and `task.select-object {asset_id, candidate|prompt}` publishes the chosen candidate as a full-resolution alpha artifact. That is the UI/API parity rule for hover: the desktop's overlay is the same query, and anything the pointer can select an agent can name.

| --- | --- | --- |

The **guided upsampler** ([below](#the-guided-upsampler)) is one pure-Rust, deterministic function the core owns, used by every model mask and every removal region. Each artifact a stack references must bind within the prepared-artifact cache's 256 MiB budget at once. A patch is the region's bounding box in half-float linear RGB (at most 64 MiB); Accept and `mask.add-model` check the stack's total before committing and refuse by name past it. Raising that budget follows the owner's quality-first measurement rule. Every new coverage kind and composite ships a GPU program held to the reference by the release gate (`cargo xtask gpu-qualification`, run by `verify --tier rendered`) for every output kind and view it touches, and its warm-up; a missing program reports `no-program` and the reference draws the affected stack's picture, reads and export. ### The model kind

Built: the `model` component kind (`crates/luxforge-core/src/mask/model.rs`, a row of the kind table), its coverage program (`model.wgsl`) and its `f64` reference (`model_alpha` and `model_coverage` in `crates/luxforge-reference/src/mask.rs`), with the generated `mask.create-model`, `mask.add-model` and `mask.set-model`. The tasks that compute a selection, the Select menu and the desktop's model row are not built.

**The artifact.** A selection is a `mask-alpha` artifact: `width × height` 8-bit codes, row-major, each side 1 to 4096 px (at most 16 MiB), with both dimensions in its metadata. It is stretched over the whole content stage: texel `(i, j)` has its centre at the stored position `((i + 0.5) / width, (j + 0.5) / height)`. A selection task publishes it at the content stage's aspect.

**The payload.** `{artifact, class, model, model_version, input_fingerprint, prefix_hash, threshold, feather}`: the artifact by identity; the class (at most 64 characters), the model resource's identity (64) and version (128) that computed it; `input_fingerprint`, the source identity, and `prefix_hash`, the repair stage's output identity, each lowercase hex SHA-256; `threshold` 1 to 100 (default 50) and `feather` 0 to 100 (default 100), percentages. The artifact has no default, so the kind is never offered as a button: a task and `mask.create-model`, `mask.add-model` or `mask.set-model` set it, and the panel's generated fields cover the two numbers.

**Coverage**, position-based and bit-identical to its reference:

```text
kx = width · H / W,  ky = height                       -- once per component
t = threshold / 100,  w = (feather / 100) · min(t, 1 − t),  lo = t − w,  span = w + w
ax = u · kx − 0.5,  ay = v · ky − 0.5                    -- the pixel-centre spelling of u, v
alpha = bilinear codes at (ax, ay), edges clamped, / 255
c = span == 0 ? (alpha >= t ? 1 : 0) : smooth(clamp((alpha − lo) / span, 0, 1))
```

The band `[lo, lo + span]` is centred on the threshold and never leaves `[0, 1]`, so a zero code is exactly zero coverage at every setting. At the defaults the band is `[0, 1]` and coverage is `smooth(alpha)`, the model's own soft edge eased; a feather of 0 is a hard edge at the threshold. Bounds are the raster's non-zero rectangle widened by one texel for the bilinear read (the whole stage when inverted); the smallest feature is one texel, in stage pixels on the finer axis, times `span`, and 0 for a hard edge, so the proxy rule supersamples a raster finer than the proxy's grid as it does a thin gradient. The overlay, the thumbnails and `render.sample` treat it like a gradient. The kind passes the kind-conformance suite (`crates/luxforge-core/tests/mask/kinds/model.rs`).

**Binding.** The kind table's compile binding carries the recipe's bound artifacts (`Recipe::artifacts`), and every compile of a mask passes them: the registry's layer compile, the coverage overlay and thumbnails, and the photo-surface and tile-worker GPU planners through it. The artifact store finds a mask component's artifact through the kind table (`mask::component_artifact`) beside the layers' own, so binding, preparation, the `preparation-required` needs, admission, each entry's `artifact_refs`, retention, collection and export cover it exactly as a layer's artifact: a mask's artifact is referenced whether or not a layer draws the mask. Admission also checks each component's recorded artifact against the kind (`mask-alpha`, both dimensions, `width · height` bytes) and refuses another naming the artifact and the component. An unknown artifact is `validation: unknown artifact <id>`; a missing or wrong-length file is `source-unavailable: artifact <id> is missing` or `is corrupt`, and an artifact a compile was not bound with is `source-unavailable` naming the component and the mask. A stack whose artifacts exceed the 256 MiB prepared-artifact budget is `resource-limit` with `data: {limit: "artifacts", artifact_bytes, budget}` at the commit that would pass it, `mask.add-model` included. The raster's non-zero rectangle and its GPU block are derived from the bytes on first use and kept with the prepared artifact, so a compile after the first is `O(1)` and every plan shares one block.

**On the GPU.** The coverage program reads the raster through the description's storage block, four codes to a word (code `k` at bits `8 · (k mod 4)` of word `k / 4`), with no texture binding: a 4096² raster is 4 Mi words, 16 MiB. The hosts concatenate it with the step's other blocks into the one block buffer, which they charge to the photo surface's 2 GiB GPU-preview budget and to the tile worker's 2 GiB `GPU_TILE_BUDGET` at its power-of-two capacity, and which must fit the device's requested 128 MiB storage-binding limit: past it the surface draws the CPU frame (`buffer-limit`) and the tile worker answers from the reference. The shared block's `Arc` makes a gesture's uploads skip it. Warm-up covers the program wherever a stack holds the kind. The program ships enabled; on a host with no adapter its device test (`gpu_mask_model_coverage_meets_the_contour_rule`) skips, and its native qualification on the M4, and the release gate over the corpus rows below, are outstanding.

**Qualification rows.** `fixtures/preview/corpus.json` carries two `mask-model` rows, developer runs: the developer module `luxforge.selections` publishes the checked-in 96 × 64 raster `fixtures/masks/proof-selection.pgm` through `task.publish-proof-selection`, whose identity is fixed (`artifact-d808f410…`), and `mask.create-model` binds it at the defaults (`mask-model`) and as a hard edge at threshold 40 intersected with a linear gradient (`mask-model-composed`), each carrying the corpus's masked Basic layer.

**`mask.list`** adds `model: {class, model, model_version, artifact, computed_ms, stale}` to a model component's row: `computed_ms` is the artifact's publication time from the catalog, and `stale` lists `source` (the input fingerprint differs from the asset's source identity), `repair-prefix` (the prefix hash differs from the repair stage's output identity now) and `model-version` (a registered module now declares the model resource at another version), in that order; empty when current, and `null` when the stack cannot be evaluated. The repair stage's output identity is the SHA-256 of a new top correction's input identity (the content stage, the layers before the repair layer and every repair operation), so a colour edit, a crop or a lens correction never stales a selection and a removal does. A stale selection keeps rendering. Recompute is the task again then `mask.set-model`; Keep sends nothing.

### The guided upsampler

Built: `luxforge_core::mask::guided::upsample` and its `f64` reference `luxforge_reference::guided::upsample`, bit-identical at 1024 and 4096 px with the guide at the output resolution. It turns a model's probabilities at the model's resolution (an `f32` plane read clamped to `[0, 1]`) into 8-bit codes at the guide's resolution, against a luminance guide at that resolution: the analysis task's cached guide for a selection, the source-resolution window for a removal region, never the 1024 px analysis input.

- **Window.** Everything is computed over the prior's non-zero cells grown by one cell for the bilinear read and by the filter's reach, `(2r + 2) · s` output pixels, clamped to the output; outside it the codes are zero, and nothing is allocated over the whole output.
- **Grid.** The coefficients are computed on the output subsampled by `s = max(1, radius / 4)`, raised until the whole output's grid holds at most 2²⁰ samples, with a radius of `r = max(1, round(radius / s))` samples: He and Sun's fast guided filter. Each grid sample is the mean of the guide over its block and the prior bilinear at the block's centre.
- **Confidence weighting.** Each sample's statistics are weighted by `c = 1/16 + |2p − 1|`: `mI`, `mp`, `E[I²]` and `E[Ip]` are weighted box means, `a = cov / (var + eps)`, `b = mp − a · mI`; then `ā` and `b̄` are plain box means. Box sums come from an integral image built row by row.
- **Output.** `ā` and `b̄` are enlarged bilinearly between block centres and `q = ā · I + b̄` is taken at every output pixel, then `round(clamp(q, 0, 1) · 255)`.
- **Defaults.** A radius of one prior cell in output pixels, at least 2, and `eps` 10⁻³ (`Params::for_sizes`). An edge sharpens when the filter's box spans the prior's ramp; a larger radius suits a softer prior, which the selection qualification chooses per model.
- **Bounds.** At most nine `f64` planes of the grid and one integral image (80 MiB at the 2²⁰-sample cap, which only a frame-filling window on a 4096² output reaches) and one byte a window pixel; a small object's selection on a 4096 px output peaks under 1 MiB (`tests/guided_window`). Malformed planes, a zero radius and a non-positive `eps` are refused by name before anything is allocated; cancellation is checked once a grid row and once an output row.

## Remove

### The flow

Remove is a mode of the Corrections band beside Clone and Heal, inside the delivered Corrections workspace (`Q`). The generative workflow builds it first with a brush selection only (a stroke's footprint as painted, the Nothing case below); hover, click, box, pins and the effect region arrive with the Fast tier. Entering it starts the analysis if the cache lacks one and shows "Analysing" until hover is ready; the brush works at once. A click on a highlighted object, or a stroke, builds the **selection**: the chosen object's refined core, the stroke's own footprint, and an **effect region** for its shadow or reflection, which starts from the prototype's contact-band heuristic when the person turns it on and is refined with Add and Subtract. Several objects under one stroke show numbered pins; the person clicks the ones to remove and confirms. Lightroom's Detect objects includes the shadow automatically; Luxforge shows the suggested region and lets the person accept it, because no effect-region model has been qualified yet and the selection is what the fill is composited inside, so it must be visible and editable. When a learned, effect-aware model is qualified under the quality-first policy, its predicted region takes the suggestion's place.

The selection is a draft on the core's `draft.*` lifecycle with the Corrections brush descriptor; a fill is a job; Accept commits one history entry. Escape during the job cancels it and leaves the recipe untouched. The Fill setting chooses the tier: **Fast** (the deterministic model, installed with Remove), **Generative** (the local generative model, when installed) or **Service** (a configured remote provider profile, later). Each tier is its own task, because a task's resources are declared statically and an optional model must never become a prerequisite of another tier: `task.fill-fast`, `task.fill-generative {model}` and the remote module's `task.fill-remote {profile}` share one request shape and one result shape, and only the last declares a `remote-image-request` capability and its consent.

### The removal region

The region is the prototype's, and its rules are restated so the editor's implementation is tested against them: a hard-sigmoid prior over the decoder's logits, a guided filter against luminance over a bounded window, an **object core** of full coverage where the decoder places the object inside or the filter gives it half coverage, the stroke footprint and the effect region joined by maximum, a grayscale disc dilation whose radius scales with the object (half a logit cell plus 4% of the core's shorter side, at least 2 px, the dilation and feather together capped at about 15 px per 1.5 MP of frame scaled linearly), and a soft outer edge one logit cell wide. A stroke that finds no object removes its footprint as painted, feathered over 15% of the brush radius past its edge. Every stage is a function of its inputs and has an exact-buffer test; nothing is allocated outside the region's window.

### The Fast fill

The prototype's fill pipeline is the Fast tier's starting point: crop the region plus context of one region-length on every side, squared and reflection-padded; encode to the model's input; run the model; resize back; re-synthesise grain matched in level, spatial correlation and channel correlation to the flattest parts of a ring around the region; correct colour and tone at the edge by pyramid push-pull; carry detail across the edge with a Laplacian pyramid; composite so that every pixel with zero coverage is copied byte for byte. On the M4, on a loaded host and on JPEGs developed outside Luxforge, LaMa on the CPU provider filled regions of 20 to 60 MP photographs in 1.7 to 3.0 s, the region's refinement adding up to 0.5 s, inside 1.1 to 1.8 GB for ordinary objects and 3.5 GB for a 15 MP region, and left every pixel outside the region byte-identical on every corpus run.

**Its one open problem is quality on large objects**, which shows as softness. The pinned LaMa export takes a fixed 512² input and the crop is three times the region's longer side, so the fill is enlarged 8.9× for a boy on a bridge, 10 to 13× for a flower or a patch of water, 19.7× for a tall pole and 29.8× for a 60 MP quadrant; grain is re-synthesised but structure is not, and the bridge deck under the boy comes back visibly softer than the deck around it. The full-precision Big-LaMa export scored within 0.1 dB of the quantized one and gave the same smeared deck, so the weights are not the cause; the enlargement is. The plan's fill-quality task settles the remedy by measurement on the prototype's corpus and its synthetic ground truth, in this order:

1. **A larger model input.** Big-LaMa is fully convolutional and stays coherent to about 1536 px; an export at 1024² or 2048², or a dynamic-shape export, cuts the enlargement by two to four times for the price of a slower fill (estimated 4 to 16 times the 512² time on the CPU; measured, not assumed).
2. **A second pass at a finer scale.** Fill at 512² for the coarse structure, then tile the region at a finer scale and fill each tile with the coarse result as its context, so texture is synthesised at a scale closer to the photograph's.
3. **Detail transfer.** Keep the model's output for low and middle frequencies and rebuild high frequencies from the surrounding context by patch matching (HiFill's contextual residual aggregation), which is how 8K fills were produced from a 512 px network.

The same study runs **MI-GAN** beside LaMa, and the better measured result wins whatever its source: MI-GAN has 5.95 M parameters, and on an iPad Pro M2 it ran in 1.2 s at 1024² and 3.9 s at 2048² (RETHINED's Table 2 and Figure 3), so a native 2048² fill cuts the enlargement four times without a second pass, at a cost measured on the M4 before it is chosen. The task records the quality (ring and region PSNR and LPIPS against the pasted-object ground truth, and a blinded owner preference), the time and the memory of each, and freezes the Fast pipeline and its model. The Fast tier will still not invent content it has never seen: a large person in a complex scene, or anything whose shadow the selection missed, is the generative tier's job, and the Fast tier says so when a region is large relative to its context (recorded default: a notice when the crop's enlargement exceeds 4×).

### Candidates, Accept and the frozen patch

A fill job holds the revision and the repair prefix it was generated against. Its result is one or more **candidates**, each a composited preview of the region at display resolution and the full-resolution patch bytes, addressed by the job and an index so any client can accept any live candidate. One job holds at most 4 candidates of at most 64 MiB each; across jobs the host holds candidates within one byte budget (recorded default 1 GiB), releases the oldest finished job's candidates first and reports a released candidate on `job.read`; Regenerate is a new job for the same selection and the strip shows every live candidate for it; leaving the tool releases nothing another client holds, and nothing is persisted. Accept rechecks the revision and the prefix, publishes the candidate as an artifact and commits the repair operation as one history entry named by the candidate's label (`Add Generative fill 1`, `Add Replace "…" 1`; the Fast tier's label is `Remove`). The operation stores the artifact, its rectangle, the input-prefix identity and the provenance: model identity, version and hash, runtime, backend and precision, seed, steps and prompt hash where they apply, and the encoding and its scale; storing the selection and effect region with it is outstanding. Rendering reads the patch and never the model, as the Corrections design requires. A late, cancelled or stale candidate cannot touch the current recipe.

### Stale patches

A patch was generated against a prefix: the RAW development's white balance and any earlier repair. When that prefix changes, the patch's pixels no longer match their surroundings. Render, sample and export do not fail on it, as the Corrections design implements: the stale operation **keeps rendering** from its frozen patch, its row and the status bar say it is stale and why, `recipe.describe` reports it, and `export.plan` names stale repairs so the desktop asks before exporting and an API client passes an explicit acknowledgement. Nothing is omitted silently, and a white-balance change never blanks the photograph. Lightroom behaves the same way for the edits that follow a removal, since its global adjustments also come after its repairs; whether it regenerates after a white-balance change was not verified. Clone and Heal recompute from their geometry and never go stale; a stored selection is a mask and keeps rendering with a Needs recompute note, as the masking workspace proposes.

## Generative fill and Replace

### The models

The generative tier needs a model that fills a masked region with plausible content from the context, locally, under terms the project can offer. The qualification (measured on the M4 Pro; figures, sheets and method in the [AI harness](../../probes/object-removal/README.md#generative-fill)) ran two permissive candidates through the Fast fill's own crop, grain, blend and composite, against LaMa on the 16 pasted cases:

| Candidate | Result | Cost on the M4 Pro | Terms |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **FLUX.2 klein 4B**, Q4_0 GGUF, with fal's removal LoRA and the crop outlined in red as its reference image | **Passes on quality.** It rebuilds what the region hid: a road continues across it, a hidden flower, a bicycle wheel and a moored boat are completed, a walker behind the object gets a head. klein Q8_0 scored the same as Q4_0 (mean +0.08 dB) for 1.8 GB more download and 2.4 GB more memory, so Q4_0 is the pick. klein without a reference image, filling through the masked path alone, leaves a flat, hatched patch and is not used; with the outlined reference it works without the LoRA too, and the LoRA (76 MB, trained on klein's base model and applied to the distilled four-step model at multiplier 1.0) is the better of the two. The SDXL-inpainting derivatives trained on removal pairs (OSOR-SDXL, with a head that predicts the shadow and reflection region, and RORem), which carry OpenRAIL++-M use clauses, and ObjectClear, TurboClear and the FLUX.1 [dev] removers, which are non-commercial for the person, were not qualified; under the quality-first policy they remain candidates, offered with their one-line restriction if they win, and the non-commercial ones never as a default. A later, licence-clean route this design keeps open is a removal fine-tune of klein on OSOR's Apache-licensed pairs and the owner's own tripod-shot pairs; it is a training project, not a task of this plan.

**Runtime.** `stable-diffusion.cpp` (MIT, pinned at `master-956-1b0ba10` with its ggml `d25b121c`) runs klein from GGUF weights on Metal. It is vendored as a pinned source subset and built with the `cc` crate, as LibRaw is, without CMake, Python or a second build system: the one-toolchain rule holds. Its generated tokenizer tables (139 MB) are left out, since the text encoder takes its `tokenizer.json`. Two local changes, staged by exact-anchor replacement, let a recorded text conditioning stand in for the encoder and let ggml's Metal backend run a graph in short slices ([Sharing the GPU](#sharing-the-gpu)). Metal compiles the runtime's kernels at the first generation on a machine (16 s) and caches them. Its masked path restores the known latents after every step; klein is given the crop with the region mid-grey, four flow steps at CFG 1 from noise, and the crop with the region outlined as its reference. The harness and `luxforge-diffusion` drive it through a small C ABI of their own over the vendored build (`native/shim.cpp`); the `diffusion-rs` binding was not evaluated. ONNX Runtime is not the generative tier's route on the Mac: Moebius ran on it, and klein's ONNX export does not run on Core ML.

### Prompt-free fill, prompted Replace

A removal is **prompt-free**: klein is conditioned on a fixed text embedding, the encoding of the LoRA's prompt "Remove the highlighted object from the scene", computed once with the pinned runtime and shipped as part of the model resource (512 × 7680 values, 15.7 MB as f32). That removes the text encoder, 2.5 to 4.3 GB of it, from the download, the memory and the runtime of the common case. Supplied from its file with no text encoder loaded, the embedding reproduces the prompted removal byte for byte for the same seed, and the process peaks 2.4 GB lower. An embedding is accepted against the prompted output when, per seed, it is within 0.5 dB region PSNR and 0.005 SSIM, about a seventh of the spread between two seeds, so a difference is smaller than a Regenerate; an embedding from the 4-bit encoder met that against the 8-bit one (at most 0.22 dB apart). It is recomputed whenever the encoder, the prompt or the runtime's conditioning changes. **Replace** takes a prompt of at most 256 characters, which needs the text encoder: a second, optional resource the Replace field offers to download the first time it is used. Replace runs klein with the region outlined as the reference and the prompt "Replace the highlighted object with …"; the model places the asked-for object in a coherent scene (a balloon among the clouds, a rowing boat on the lake, a dog on a bridge deck), but it also redraws what it overlaps (a walker behind an olive tree lost his head). The Fast fill's seam correction, which carries the colour difference at the region's edge into the fill, helps a removal (0.6 dB in the region and 2.3 dB at the edge over the 16 cases) but turns a new object's surroundings into a lighter box or halo, so Replace composites over the region's own feathered edge without it, where the balloon and the boat sit seamlessly. Both run the same pipeline as the Fast tier around the model: the same crop (the region's longer side tripled, squared and resampled to the working size), the same encoding from the stage's linear light to the display-referred input the model was trained on with a recorded inverse, the same grain, edge and detail stages, the same byte-identical composite outside the coverage. Instruction editors that regenerate the whole frame without a mask are excluded by that rule.

### Candidates, seeds and provenance

Generate produces one candidate and Regenerate one more as a new job from the next seed; a client may ask a job for up to four, made in sequence rather than in a batch so peak memory stays at one candidate's; the strip shows every candidate still held for the region under the retention rule above. Each candidate records its seed, steps, sampler, precision, backend and prompt hash, and the accepted patch stores them, but the frozen patch is the source of truth: the same seed on another machine, driver or runtime version is not promised to reproduce the pixels, and nothing ever re-generates an accepted result.

### Cost, memory and admission

Each generative model declares a minimum machine memory and a resident budget; a machine below the minimum sees the model with its requirement and cannot install it, and a generation that would exceed the budget is refused with `resource-limit` before any allocation. Measured for klein 4B Q4_0 on the M4 Pro on a quiet host (load average under 4, [the harness report](../../probes/object-removal/README.md#quiet-host-remeasurement)): 31 to 32.5 s per candidate at 512, 66.5 to 68 s at 768 and 121 to 123.5 s at 1024, three in sequence 1.7 to 1.9, 3.5 and 6.2 minutes, the two runs at each size within 1.5% of each other. Four flow steps are four fifths of a candidate and grow with the image, reference and text tokens; a reference at half the working size is faster (79 to 84 s at 1024) but misaligns the fill with its surroundings, and Q8_0 is no faster. **The local generator works at 1024 px, one candidate at a time**: Generate makes one candidate and Regenerate adds one, each shown as it finishes, and the budget is the measured time, about 2 minutes per 1024 px candidate on the M4 Pro. The process peaks at 4.25 GB with no text encoder at every size, because the runtime reserves its compute buffers up front, and at 7.9 to 8.4 GB with Replace's Q8_0 encoder (6.6 GB with Q4_K_M). The resident budget is 5 GiB for a removal and 9 GiB with the encoder: `luxforge.ai` declares two `local-runtime` slots, `run-klein` holding the generator at 5 GiB and `run-klein-replace` holding the encoder with the generator as its companion at 9 GiB, each counted against the host's resident budget, and a load that would pass it once idle models are released, or a generation over the 1024 px it was measured at, is refused `resource-limit` before anything is allocated. The minimum machine is **16 GB for removal and 24 GB for Replace** (from the measured peaks; 24 GB is also the floor Adobe set in May 2026 for Photoshop's on-device generative Remove); neither minimum has been measured on a machine that size, which the final qualification does. Every model is unloaded after 10 minutes idle or on a memory signal. The generative tier sits behind an **Experiments flag** (`ai.generative`) until its qualification passes; the flag gates only whether the tier is offered, never how a committed patch renders. The workflow (the tier choice, the candidate strip, Regenerate, the prompt, Accept with provenance) is built and proven over the fake generator independently of any local model, so a no-go on every local candidate leaves a finished workflow for the remote tier to fill.

### Sharing the GPU

Upstream ggml runs each graph on Metal as two command buffers, so one klein denoising step, a quarter of a candidate, holds the GPU as one piece of work. The editor draws through its own Metal queue in the same process, and its frames wait for the step to end: on the M4 Pro the interface stops responding during a generation, with the main thread blocked creating a command buffer because its queue's limit of 4096 uncompleted ones is reached.

The `ai.generative-gpu-sharing` experiment (off by default; [settings and flags](settings-and-flags.md)) runs a generation's GPU work in slices instead. The owner reads the flag as a fill is requested and hands it to the job, so a change applies from the next Generate or Regenerate; every generation of that job then asks the generator to share the GPU (`GenerateRequest::share_gpu`), and the job's result records it as `share_gpu`. `luxforge-diffusion` sets ggml's Metal backend to slices of about 8 ms of GPU time (`GPU_SLICE`) for that generation and back to whole graphs as it ends. A sliced graph is encoded slice by slice, the next while the current one runs, and each slice is committed only once the one before it has completed, so the generator holds at most one command buffer on the GPU and the editor's frames run between slices. Each slice's node count is adapted towards the target from the GPU time the previous slice took, between 1 and 4096 nodes; a single kernel longer than the target is one slice. Slices end only between fused groups, and each slice's encoder spans the rest of the command buffer upstream would have encoded those nodes in, never crossing upstream's split points, so a slice encodes upstream's kernels and a candidate should be the same bytes with the flag on or off, as the flags rule requires. The ignored native test `a_generation_that_shares_the_gpu_makes_the_whole_graph_candidate` (`cargo test -p luxforge-diffusion -- --ignored`, with the pinned klein files) checks that on the Mac. Linux's CPU backend and the fake generator ignore the request. Outstanding: that native test's first run, and the M4 measurement of what the slices cost a candidate and of the editor's frame times while a fill runs, which decide the target, whether the flag's behaviour becomes the default and whether the flag is removed.

**The request.** `task.fill-generative {asset_id, selection-points, selection-radius, effect-points?, effect-radius?, model, candidates?, prompt?, seed?, mutation}`. A module task's parameters are flat, so the selection and the effect region are each a Corrections brush stroke given as its path (`*-points`, the content stage's normalized coordinates, as a correction's points) and radius (`*-radius`, content heights, the correction's range; the effect radius defaults to the selection's), captured exactly as a correction's stroke is. The region is each stroke's footprint as painted at full coverage, feathered over 15% of its radius past its edge (the mask brush's profile at full opacity), joined by maximum: the Nothing case of [the removal region](#the-removal-region). The feather lies outside what was painted, as the region's soft outer edge does: a correction's inward feather would leave an object edge within the footprint's outer 15% under partial coverage `c`, where the composite `in + c · (patch − in)` shows `1 − c` of it as a faint trace of the object inside the fill. `candidates` is 1 to 4, default 1; `seed` is 0 to 2⁵³−1, default a new one each request, and candidate *n* takes `seed + n`. A prompt (whitespace collapsed, a blank one is none, at most 256 characters by the declared schema, so a longer one is `validation`) makes the fill Replace and needs the text encoder: without it installed the request is `not-ready` with `error.data.requirements` naming the encoder's download capability (`download-klein-text-encoder` for klein's, `download-fake-text-encoder` for the fake's), which a client turns into the encoder's download offer, and nothing is generated. The ready job's `result.result` is `{tier: "generative", model, candidates, seeds, next_seed, rect, prompt, share_gpu}`, `share_gpu` saying whether the job's generations shared the GPU ([Sharing the GPU](#sharing-the-gpu)); Regenerate sends the same selection with `seed: next_seed`.

**Admission.** Before anything is queued or read, from the strokes' supports alone: a selection that covers no pixel is `validation`; a region whose bounds' patch (eight bytes a pixel) and preview exceed one candidate's 64 MiB is `resource-limit` with `data.limit: "candidate"`; bounds over half the frame, `"half-frame"`; a patch that would not bind with the stack's artifacts within 256 MiB, `"artifacts"`. The `ai.generative` flag off, the model not installed or no inference runtime is `not-ready` listing every unmet requirement together, the flag as `{kind: "flag", id: "ai.generative", state: "off"}`.

**The job.** One job reads the photograph's repair input once, in bands of at most 4 Mi pixels, then makes each candidate in turn: reserve it (the candidate seam refuses past its bounds before anything is made), generate with progress per step ("candidate 1 of 1, step 3 of 4"), finish it through the pipeline below and publish it. Cancellation is checked between bands, at every generation step and between candidates; a cancelled job's candidates are released `cancelled`, and nothing it made after the cancel reaches the candidates or the recipe. Each candidate is labelled `Generative fill` or `Replace "<prompt>"` (cut with an ellipsis to the label's 32 characters) and carries the model's provenance (`model`, `model_version`, `model_sha256`, `runtime`, `runtime_version`, `backend`, `precision`, `seed`, `steps`, `sampler`, `prompt_hash` for Replace) with `tier`, `encoding`, `encoding_scale` and `model_side`. The job's candidates follow the delivered retention rule: Regenerate is a new job and leaves the earlier jobs' candidates live within the budget.

**The pipeline.** The probe's, in the region's linear light: a square crop of the region's bounds with one region-length of context on every side, reflection-padded past the frame; area reduction of the square to the model's side, with the model's hole every model pixel any covered stage pixel reaches; the display encoding `255 · sRGB(v / k)`, with `k` 1 for a context within white and otherwise the 99.9th percentile of the context's brightest channel on a 1/32-stop scale, at most 64 and its recorded inverse `k · sRGB⁻¹(c / 255)`; generation; Catmull-Rom enlargement back over the blend window; grain matched to the flattest part of a ring around the region when the fill was enlarged; push-pull seam correction and a Laplacian pyramid blend; and the composite, which leaves every pixel with zero coverage untouched. Each stage has an exact test against an `f64` reference or its recorded rule.

**Accept and the lock.** A candidate is accepted with the delivered `edit.accept-correction-patch {asset_id, job_id, candidate, mutation}` from any client, which commits one Corrections patch entry under the candidate's label, labelled in history `Add Generative fill 1` (or `Add Replace "…" 1`). While a fill is queued or running its photograph is locked through the shared command service: every edit of it (each `edit.*` action, `history.undo`, `history.redo`, `history.restore`, the draft's begin, reapply and commit, and an accept) and a second locking task on it are refused `conflict` with `data: {reason: "locked", asset_id, job_id, method: "task.fill-generative"}`, and `batch.apply-settings` skips it as `locked`; every other photograph stays editable, and `job.read`, `job.read-candidate` and `job.cancel` answer as usual. A cancel releases the lock at once. A candidate draft (below) can begin and change while a fill locks its photograph, since it commits nothing; its commit is refused `locked` as an accept is. An accepted patch is stored data: it renders, after a reopen and in a host with no `luxforge.ai` and no runtime, from the frozen artifact.

#### Candidate drafts

Any client can see a live candidate in the photograph before accepting it by drafting the accepting action: `draft.begin {asset_id, action}` with the Corrections module's candidate-accepting action (the begin names no candidate; `job_id` is required by `draft.set`, not by the begin), and `draft.set {fields: {job_id, candidate, opacity?}}`. The draft is planned exactly as the accept would be (the job a task the caller may read, the candidate live, of this photograph and generated against the input a new top correction has now) with the candidate's patch in place of the published artifact, so its render is the committed entry's render: exact on the CPU reference and within the GPU's declared tolerance of it. A refusal is the accept's own (`conflict` with `data.reasons`, `validation` for another photograph or a candidate that is not live), answered by `draft.set` and by any read or preview of the draft; a refused `draft.set` leaves the draft as it was.

- **The patch, held in memory.** The owner reads and verifies the candidate's stored patch (its SHA-256, as publishing does) on the source worker, never on the catalog owner or interface thread. The `draft.set` that names a candidate not yet prepared parks as a planning read parks, under an `artifacts.read` activity labelled `Reading candidate`, and runs again from its request once it is, so every check is made again on the owner's state of that moment; a newer `draft.set` from the same client supersedes one still waiting (`cancelled`). A caller that may not wait is answered as a planning read's is, `not-ready` with reason `pixel-read-required`, having changed nothing; the desktop sends such a set unparked and again on the blocking pool, so its update thread never waits on the read. The draft holds the patch as a prepared artifact under the identity publishing will give it (`artifact-` of its SHA-256), at most one per session, released when the draft names another candidate, closes or is cancelled. Drafted renders bind it from the draft and the catalog's artifact store is not consulted for it; every other artifact of the stack binds as before. A candidate released after its patch was prepared still renders from the held patch, and its commit is refused as the accept refuses a released candidate. The candidate's admission already checked that its patch binds with the stack's artifacts within the 256 MiB prepared-artifact budget, so no new budget is introduced. Because the drafted and the published artifact share an identity, the GPU's resident patch words and the drawn-patch cache built for the draft serve the committed entry too.
- **Commit.** `draft.commit` of a candidate draft is the accept: it runs the accept flow with the draft's request at the draft's base revision, parks as the accept parks (and is refused `not-ready` with `data.reason: accept-publishes` where the caller may not wait), and closes the draft when the entry commits; a refused accept keeps the draft. `edit.accept-correction-patch` from a client with no candidate draft is unchanged. The commit may reuse the prepared patch's bytes for publication; the published artifact is the same either way.
- **Lifecycle.** A candidate draft is an ordinary draft in everything else: one per session, conflicted when the photograph's revision moves under it, cancelled with `draft.cancel`, and never persisted or listed in history.

**What a client reads.** `module.status {module_id: "luxforge.ai"}` adds `fill_tiers: [{tier, task, offered, missing: [{kind: flag|runtime|model, id, state}], flag: {id, on}, models: [{model, title, resource, state, runtime, text_encoder: {resource, state}}]}]`, from which the desktop lists the tiers it can fill with, explains why one is not offered and offers the text encoder's download.

### The remote tier (later)

A remote provider is a second module with the same job and result shape: a typed adapter with a **`repair-crop`** data class (an sRGB 8-bit crop of at most 2048² with its mask, the prompt, the candidate count, and nothing else: no path, no EXIF, no full frame unless a separate `full-frame` class is declared and consented), bearer authentication with the key in the OS credential store, consent asked once per provider profile and data class and remembered until revoked (in place of the capability contract's per-asset default; the grant scope loses its asset and the change lands with this tier), with the disclosure stating the bytes and the provider's retention note, response validation (dimensions, format, finite values, size) and the same candidate strip and Accept. A user-run loopback service with a documented request shape is the same adapter over the `loopback` endpoint class, which is how a person with their own GPU box or a ComfyUI bridge plugs in. The first hosted provider is an open question; the shape is fixed so that local and remote fills are interchangeable to the recipe, as Photoshop's Generative Fill now offers Adobe's own and partner models behind one selector.

## Select: automatic masks

### Classes and the dynamic menu

The analysis result lists the classes the photograph contains. The New mask menu's **Select** group draws exactly those, each with its area share as a hint, and nothing for a class that is absent: a studio portrait offers Subject, Background and the person; a seascape offers Sky, Water and perhaps Mountains; Objects is always offered and People entries appear one per detected person. While the analysis runs the group says so; when the Select models are not installed, one entry opens their resource card with the combined size. An agent reads the same list from the analysis task's result.

The semantic model proposed is the research's: an EoMT panoptic or semantic model on a DINOv2 backbone (MIT code and MIT checkpoints, Apache backbone; the ADE20K large checkpoint at 512 px has 0.3 B parameters), trained on COCO-Stuff rather than ADE20K where a checkpoint exists, because COCO-Stuff has cloud and snow classes and CC BY annotations. A 2 MB MIT sky segmenter (a U²-Net-p export) is measured beside it as a cheap Sky-only model for machines that skip the Select set. SAM 3's concept prompts are not the route to these classes: its licence is use-restricted and no evaluation of it on sky, water or other stuff classes was found. The table's mapping and thresholds are starting values the qualification task tunes on the corpus and records; the user guide states the classes as a fixed vocabulary, so a photograph never offers a class the guide does not name.

### From a class to a mask

Choosing a class runs `task.select-class {asset_id, class}` (or `task.select-person {asset_id, person}`, `task.select-object {asset_id, candidate|prompt}`) on the module lane. It reads the analysis cache, takes the class's probabilities at the model's resolution (the subject's from the salient model `task.analyse-scene` ran), refines them with the guided upsampler against the cached guide to the artifact size, publishes the 8-bit alpha artifact with the class, model identity, version, input fingerprint and dimensions in its metadata, and the desktop then sends `mask.add-model {mask?, mode, artifact, class, model, model_version, input_fingerprint, feather, threshold}` as one history entry labelled `Add sky (model)`. Subject at the HR model size (2048²) is the quality option the qualification task measures; Background is Subject's artifact with the component inverted, one artifact and no second task. A person is the detector's instance refined by a box prompt to the segmenter inside the person's bounds, so hair and edges come from the segmenter and the guided filter rather than the detector's coarse mask; body parts (hair, face skin, body skin, clothes, from a permissive multiclass model on a crop of each person) are a later slice, and eyes, lips and brows wait for a qualified face parser.

The `model` component is [delivered](#the-model-kind): position-based, sampled bilinearly from the artifact, thresholded and feathered by the frozen `smooth`, bound at compile time beside a layer's artifacts and kept by retention while any entry references it. A model component combines with brushes, gradients and ranges through the delivered algebra, so Select Sky minus a brushed subtraction is two components of one mask.

### Staleness

A component records the input fingerprint, the repair stage's output identity and the model version it was computed from, and `mask.list` reports each reason it is stale ([The model kind](#the-model-kind)). When the source changes, the repair prefix changes (an object was removed after the selection) or the model's declared version differs, the row shows **Needs recompute** with the reason and two actions: Recompute runs the task again and `mask.set-model` replaces the artifact as one entry; Keep leaves it. The stored selection renders meanwhile. A crop, a straighten or a lens correction changes nothing, because the artifact lives in content space with every other mask.

### Depth (later)

A depth model (Depth Anything 3 Small, Apache) gives a `depth-range` component kind on the range widget, a focus-like selection by distance; it joins the analysis when its model is installed and is a later slice.

## Sky replacement (optional)

It is optional, and cheap once Select exists, because the model's only contribution is the mask.

A **Sky** module adds one layer at the repair stage, after the Corrections repair layer, whose effect composites a sky raster behind the photograph inside the layer's mask, which defaults to a Select Sky component and is an ordinary mask the person refines. The sky comes from an artifact: a photograph the person imports (which returns the consented `read-user-file` capability, per canonical path), or one of a small set offered as a resource, chosen from CC0 skies. Poly Haven (CC0 with explicit redistribution permission, 299 HDRIs tagged as skies) and ambientCG (CC0, 431 HDRIs) are the first candidates; their files are equirectangular, so preparing a sky reprojects its upper hemisphere to a rectilinear backdrop once, and the result is the artifact. The controls, generated from the descriptor, are the ones Luminar Neo's Sky AI and Photoshop's Sky Replacement share: **Horizon** (vertical position), **Scale**, **Shift**, **Flip**, **Exposure** and **Warmth** of the sky, **Defocus** (a bounded blur of the sky alone), **Edge** (a feather and an inward shift of the matte against bright fringes, Photoshop's Shift Edge and Fade Edge) and **Relight** (the amount by which the foreground's low-frequency colour is pulled toward the sky's, applied through the inverted mask as a second composed layer, Photoshop's Foreground Lighting). Luminar's water reflections, close-gaps refinement and relight-human switch are later. Luminar offers the tool only when at least 15% of the frame is sky; the Select presence rule plays that part here.

Its deterministic composite is `out = (1 − M) × input + M × sky(T(p))` in linear light with a bounded read of the sky raster through the horizon, scale, shift and flip transform, which is the masked-primitive blend the renderer already does with a position-based source; it has an `f64` reference and exact fixtures on the CPU and a WGSL program within the declared tolerance on the GPU, like every module under GPU-first. The mask's edge at trees and hair is the guided upsampler's work, the Apache-2.0 method Google published for sky optimisation. A sky artifact is at most 4096 px on its long side and at most 64 MiB, pinned through the prepared-artifact cache. A **generative sky** needs no new code: Replace with a sky prompt inside the Select Sky mask.

## Budgets and limits

Starting figures, each replaced by a measured figure before its feature is called done, under one rule: **quality first**. A budget that would degrade a result is raised, with the measurement that shows the need, rather than the result reduced; the optimisation comes after the quality is right. Every measurement runs on the M4 Pro with photo-sized inputs and recorded scope ([performance rules](../engineering/performance-rules.md)).

| Figure | Budget |
| --- | --- |
| Hover, pointer to drawn overlay | p50 at most 20 ms, p95 at most 33 ms |
| Analysis ready after the tool is picked, models loaded | at most 300 ms at 24 MP and 500 ms at 60 MP for the segmenter; at most 1.5 s more for the semantic, people and depth models |
| Warm model load | at most 2 s for the segmenter and the Fast fill together; at most 3 s for the Select set; a first compile shown as progress |
| Fast fill, region to accepted preview | at most 5 s at 60 MP for a region up to the candidate cap (about 8 MP) |
| Generative candidate | about 2 minutes per 1024 px candidate on the M4 Pro, one per Generate and one per Regenerate; the harness measures 121 to 123.5 s on a quiet host, and the editor 139 to 153 s on a shared one (load 20 to 56); the editor's quiet-host figure is outstanding |
| A selection, class to artifact | at most 2 s including refinement to 4096 px |
| Resident memory | segmenter 1 GB, Fast fill 2 GB, Select set 3 GB, generative 5 GiB for a removal and 9 GiB with Replace's text encoder (klein 4B Q4_0 measures a 4.25 GB peak alone in the harness and 4.0 to 4.3 GB in `luxforge-json`, and 6.6 GB with the shipped Q4_K_M encoder in the harness, 7.9 to 8.4 GB with Q8_0); each model's figure recorded and unloaded on idle or a memory signal; a model's machine minimum counts the resident models, the held candidates and the analysis cache together |
| Downloads | Remove's models about 240 MB (the segmenter and the Fast fill); Select's set stated per model before download; klein 4B 2.72 GB at Q4_0 with its VAE, the removal LoRA and the shipped conditioning, and 2.51 GB more for Replace's text encoder at Q4_K_M with its tokenizer (4.29 GB at Q8_0, not offered) |
| Artifacts | a selection at most 16 MiB; a patch at most 64 MiB (half-float linear RGB over the region's bounds); a sky at most 64 MiB; every stack's artifacts within the 256 MiB bind budget, which is raised with a measurement if patches need it |

Admission limits, refused with `resource-limit`: a region whose bounding box fits one candidate and at most half the frame for either tier. The delivered candidate seam stores a patch as half-float RGBA (eight bytes a pixel) and holds at most 64 MiB of patch and preview, so a region's bounding box is at most about 8 MP; the cap is raised, with the measurement that shows the need, if real removals need more (the crop around it is bounded only by the frame and is reduced to the model's input size, which is where a large region pays in enlargement, flagged by the notice; tiling at the model's resolution is a later option); at most 4 candidates per job and 1 GiB of candidates held across jobs; a prompt of at most 256 characters; at most 16 people; a stack whose artifacts would not bind within the 256 MiB budget, checked before Accept and before `mask.add-model`; the delivered stroke, mask and lane limits.

**Generation and shared memory.** A new generation locks its photograph through the shared command service and the desktop, while another photograph remains editable. Progress and cancellation stay responsive. Once accepted, a patch is stored data and renders without inference. Qualification measures the interactions still permitted during inference, including edits on another photograph, and combined resident models, candidates, cache and renderer resources. The renderer's two separate 2 GiB budgets are ceilings rather than measured residency and exclude overhead that GPU memory accounting has yet to bound. Re-check the 24 GB generative starting minimum against the combined measured footprint; same-photograph slider edits during generation are not an acceptance requirement.

## Platforms

Everything portable runs on the three platforms through the same port; Core ML is an accelerator on macOS, DirectML on Windows, the CPU everywhere, with the backend recorded in every artifact's provenance. Apple's Vision requests are not used for selection: their instance masks cover noticeable foreground objects only, the brush-seeded request needs macOS 27 and Swift, and the same photograph must select the same way on every platform. Windows' NPU-only imaging APIs are not used either. Native GPU performance is claimed only where measured natively; a VM or CI run proves function. The fake runtime makes every journey run in CI.

## Verification and acceptance

- **Core, exact.** The stroke decision (one, several, nothing, ties, parts and wholes) on recorded candidate sets; the removal region's prior, window, core, dilation and feather against `f64` references; the guided upsampler bit-identical to its reference; the fill's crop, encoding inverse, grain estimate, push-pull and pyramid blend on synthetic fixtures; every fill's pixels outside the coverage byte-identical to the input; the model kind's coverage equation and `render.sample` equal to the rendered byte; the model and depth kinds' GPU coverage programs and the repair and sky composites' GPU programs held to the reference renderer by `cargo xtask gpu-qualification` for every output kind and view they touch.
- **Artifacts and recipes.** A selection's artifact identical across two runs; publish, commit, reopen, undo, redo, restore, branches and versions for selections, patches and skies; missing and corrupt artifacts failing by name; a removed model still rendering every accepted edit; the stale states for a changed source, prefix and model version; export binding the same artifacts and naming stale repairs.
- **Capabilities.** Multi-file resources, resumed downloads, the plain-language labels and the use restriction in the disclosure with no training-data note, `local-runtime` activation, unload on idle and on signal, `module.status` reporting the resident set, refusals below the machine minimum and above every admission limit.
- **Parity.** Every desktop gesture (hover, click, Alt-click, box, stroke, pins, Fill tier, Generate, Regenerate, Accept, Select, Recompute, Keep, every Sky control) sends a request `Copy as JSON request` shows, and the acceptance drives the same journeys through `luxforge-json`.
- **Rendered.** Smoke scenarios `remove`, `ai-select`, `generative`, `sky` and `remote` (over the loopback fake provider) over the fake runtime: the analysing state, the hover highlight, pins, the selection tint, a fill's progress and result, the candidate strip, the Select group for a photograph with and without a sky, a model row's Needs recompute, the Sky section; each frame's state correlated with the plan and the logs.
- **Native, real models.** Opt-in runs with the models installed (`--models DIR`, as the authentic RAW tests take their directory), on the prototype's corpus and the CC0 corpus: hover and analysis latencies, fill and candidate times, peak memory, download sizes and the quality scores, with the host's load recorded; a blinded preference test by the owner against Lightroom's Remove and Generative Remove on the same photographs; Windows and Linux functional runs of the same journeys.
- **A skipped check is not a pass.** A tier without its model installed is `skipped` with the reason, never green.

Recorded defaults of the model kind and the guided upsampler: the feather is a band of alpha centred on the threshold rather than a spatial distance, because a spatial feather needs the neighbourhood treatment `expand` waits on, and the defaults (threshold 50, feather 100) keep the model's own edge, eased; the raster is stretched over the content stage whatever its aspect; `prefix_hash` is the repair stage's output identity; a model is stale for another version when a registered module declares it, not by reading installed files; the kind is not offered in the New mask menu until its task is; the upsampler's confidence weight is `1/16 + |2p − 1|`, its grid holds at most 2²⁰ samples and its default radius is one prior cell.

Recorded defaults the owner can revise: the setup sheet only for downloads whose capability declares an introduction (the AI editing models today), every other consent staying the canvas notice; the introduction's words declared by the module and its pictures drawn by the client from four named kinds; the sheet closing on Download, with the progress in the Remove group; the prototype as the AI harness (`probes/object-removal`, outside the editor's workspace); the plain-language labels for each model choice; the Remove letter `Q`; a 10-minute idle unload; a 256 MiB analysis cache; the class vocabulary and presence rules; Background as inverted Subject; the Fast tier's 4× enlargement notice; a prompt limit of 256 characters; the `ai.generative` flag's existence until qualification; Poly Haven as the sky set's first candidate.

Open questions, not decided by default: the first hosted provider; whether to fund a klein removal fine-tune on CORNE and owner pairs; whether Apple's macOS 27 iterative segmentation is ever worth a Swift shim as a Mac-only accelerator; what a CPU-only machine should be offered (Remove at CPU hover latency, or Remove without hover).
