## Outcome and scope

While a person drags a slider or moves or paints a mask, at Fit or at any percentage zoom, the desktop evaluates the part of the recipe the gesture changes on the GPU. It draws the result in the same display frame as the input. With no gesture open the GPU draws the committed stack itself: the picture at rest.

The CPU renderer is the reference every GPU frame is held to, and draws whatever the GPU cannot, as whole frames. The histogram and clipping counts of a stack the GPU presents at rest are the GPU's, counted over the picture at rest's full-resolution tiles ([the picture at rest](#the-picture-at-rest)); export is drawn in GPU tiles and a sample, a pick or a seed is one GPU tile around its pixel, each read back by the desktop's tile worker and held to the reference within the display limit ([export](export.md#behavior), [architecture](architecture.md#point-queries-and-pixel-reads)). Mask coverage grids, history and every other API answer read no GPU pixel.

### Why

| A painted stroke | p95 42.1–50.2 ms over four masked layers, missing the provisional bound. On a bare masked layer the worker render is only 4.5–5.4 ms; the queue wait and result delivery dominate ([further performance](../research/further-performance.md)) | The hop that dominates is removed, not just the kernel |
| 100% motion | The reference renderer's whole frame, a frame at a time | A full-scale region at motion speed |
| Presence and Detail | All three Presence fields 1.5–1.6 s exact at 60 MP; a Presence drag at Fit 16.5–22.7 ms p50; Detail drags are expected to miss the Fit budget ([Detail](detail.md#proposals-with-recorded-defaults)) | Their drags, and drags of layers before them, run on the GPU |

The recorded verdict that a GPU colour stage was not justified ([further performance](../research/further-performance.md#rgba-ownership-gpu-and-simd-choices)) weighed the kernel alone. This design removes the worker hop, the per-tick upload and contention with exact work, which that verdict did not weigh.

### In scope

- A general GPU node graph and external GPU modules.

## Decisions

Decided by the owner on 2026-10-01:

- **Speed comes first for interactive previews.** The constraint is that settling must not produce a large, noticeable jump in the image.
- **GPU arithmetic is for previews only.** The CPU stays the reference for settled frames, analysis, sampling and export. Apple GPUs have no 64-bit floating point, so the CPU's `f64` arithmetic, bit-identical mask coverage and byte-identical sample/render contract cannot be reproduced on them. The owner replaced this on 2026-10-04: the GPU becomes the renderer of record and its correctness a declared tolerance against the CPU reference, never bit identity ([GPU-first](gpu-first.md)); the picture at rest, its histogram and clipping counts, export and pixel reads are the GPU's where it can draw the stack ([export](export.md#behavior)).
- **Order of work.** Error limits first, then the Fit colour stage, then Presence and Detail, with mipmapped minification beside them.

## The preview error limit

**What is compared.** By the [GPU-first](gpu-first.md#the-contract) design's [recorded default](gpu-first.md#proposals-with-recorded-defaults) of 2026-10-05: the picture at rest against the reference frame reduced to the view's size, and the picture in motion, a drag's GPU frame, against the frame it settles to, with its distance from the reference reported beside it. The reference is the stack's exact whole frame from the reference renderer, as export renders it, reduced to the GPU frame's size by an area-weighted average of its linear light at Fit and at a percentage below 100% (`luxforge_reference::tolerance`, independent of the renderer), and its visible region, unreduced, at 100%. Every frame is the 8-bit sRGB pixels the surface draws. `cargo xtask gpu-qualification`, the release gate, makes these comparisons over the corpus at Fit, 33%, 50% and 100%, gating the picture at rest; `--gate-motion against-rest` or `--gate-motion against-reference` gates a drag's frame too ([verification](#verification)).

- The frame a drag settles to is the GPU's picture at rest of the committed stack: at Fit and below 100% the picture at rest in tiles, and at 100% and above the view plan, which a drag at the same view draws over the same boundary. A program's own corpus qualification enables it against the CPU frame it stands in for: the CPU's moving proxy at Fit, Detail's included, and the exact visible region at 100% ([qualifying a program](#qualifying-a-program)).
- Statistics cover the photograph alone, not the canvas beside it, as the owner decided for the white-balance gates on 2026-09-30.

**The measure.** Per-pixel CIEDE2000 (ΔE00), from sRGB through CIELAB under D65, computed in `f64` by an independent reference. As a common guide, ΔE00 below 1 is not perceptible, 1 to 2 is perceptible on close inspection and 2 to 10 at a glance. Four statistics:

- **Mean ΔE00** over the photograph: the overall shift.
- **Worst block**: the largest mean ΔE00 of any 16 × 16-pixel block. A region that changes is what reads as a jump, more than scattered pixels do. Blocks tile the photograph from its top-left corner; the last block in each direction moves inward to end at the edge, so none is partial.
- **p99 ΔE00**: edges and fine detail. Nearest rank: the largest value left after discarding the worst 1% of pixels, rounded down.
- **Signed mean ΔL\***: whole-picture brightening or darkening, which reads as a jump even when small.

**Limits** (proposed):

| Statistic | Pointwise programs: colour units, masks, vignette | Spatial programs: Presence, Detail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mean ΔE00 | ≤ 0.5 | ≤ 1.0 |
| Worst 16 × 16 block mean ΔE00 | ≤ 1.0 | ≤ 2.5 |
| p99 ΔE00 | ≤ 2.0 | ≤ 5.0 |
| Signed mean ΔL\* | within ±0.25 | within ±0.5 |

The pointwise mean matches the precedent the owner already set: the RAW white-balance draft gate is a mean within one code at Fit. The spatial limits are deliberately generous. They leave room for half-precision storage, single-precision running sums and estimates taken at the scale the GPU holds. Under a 150 ms dissolve, a regional difference of about 2 reads as the picture settling rather than popping. The first task measured today's accepted CPU approximations under the same measure, for comparison ([recorded baseline](../specs/performance.md#preview-error-baseline)):

- the Presence proxy against the exact downscale
- the half-scale 100% motion region against the exact region, a CPU path the GPU-first plan has since deleted
- a RAW white-balance draft against its release

These limits gate **enabling** a program, against the CPU frame it stands in for, and the **release**: `gpu-qualification` fails when any corpus cell it judges passes one, by the recorded default the picture at rest against the reference, a drag's frame being reported and not gated while what it is held to is the owner's open question. They do not judge frames at run time. A program, or a stack class, that misses any limit against the CPU frame ships disabled, and those stacks take the CPU path until it passes. The picture at rest meets every limit against the reference on every corpus cell it draws (all 1,044 cells at Fit, 33%, 50% and 100% on 2026-10-06, the largest 0.087 / 0.509 / 0.963 / 0.049). A drag's frame at 100% is the picture at rest's own; at Fit, 33% and 50% it processes the source reduced to the view's size, where the picture at rest and the reference process it at full resolution and reduce after, and it is past the limits on many stacks against either (past them on 462 of the 783 cells there, by up to 32.2 / 52.9 / 64.4 / 40.1, against either) ([performance](../specs/performance.md#gpu-qualification-against-the-reference)). The limits stand; what a drag's frame is held to is the owner's, and the recorded default runs until the owner records otherwise ([GPU-first proposals](gpu-first.md#proposals-with-recorded-defaults)).

**Hard rules, not limits.**

- A finite CPU value is never non-finite on the GPU.
- No tile or region seams.
- A mask edge's half-coverage contour lies within a quarter of a display pixel of the CPU's.
- A drag's GPU frame never feeds a stored report, a sample, a mask grid, an export, an artifact or a history entry: its counts are plotted marked updating and handed to no one, and every stored count, sample and export is drawn from the stack at full resolution in tiles, the GPU's or the reference's.

## Design

### Where the code lives

- **Programs belong to modules.** Each module's GPU program is WGSL text in a `.wgsl` file beside its CPU unit in `luxforge-core`: a `static GpuProgram` with its entry name, kind (colour, coverage or spatial), uniform word count and enabled flag (`crates/luxforge-core/src/render/gpu/program.rs`). The core names no GPU crate.
  - `PointwiseColor::gpu` and each mask component's `gpu` answer an optional `GpuDescription`: the program, its uniform block of 32-bit words and, where needed, a shared storage block (the curve's knots, a brush's segment grid). The default is none, which takes the CPU path.
  - `SpatialUnit::gpu` answers an optional `GpuSpatialUnit` (`crates/luxforge-core/src/render/gpu/spatial.rs`): its program, its words, the planes it writes, the compute passes that write them and its apply ([spatial programs](#spatial-programs)). A global estimate it reads is the plan's light, computed by the plan's light link ([the global estimate](#spatial-programs)).
  - The words are a pure function of the coefficients `describe()` writes, so two units that describe themselves identically produce identical uniforms. A shared test helper checks it for each unit that has a program.
  - A program declares only functions and constants, each named after its entry (`lf_<module>_<unit>`). Its entry has its kind's signature: a colour unit is `fn(rgb: vec3<f32>, pos: vec2<f32>, words: u32, block: u32) -> vec3<f32>`, placed in content space or after the geometry tail; a mask component's coverage is `fn(pos: vec2<f32>, rgb: vec3<f32>, words: u32, block: u32) -> f32`. The surface prepends the prelude that binds every program's words and blocks, read through `lf_word`, `lf_f32`, `lf_block_word` and `lf_block_f32`.
  - A program that misses its limits ships disabled: it is still validated, and its stacks take the CPU path and name it.
  - **The shipped programs**, listed publicly as `GPU_PROGRAMS` in the order the units run, all enabled ([qualification](#qualifying-a-program)):

    | Program | Beside | Words | Block |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | `lf_detail` (spatial) | `modules/detail/detail.wgsl`, described by `modules/detail/gpu.rs` | each smoothing pass's form and its kernels' taps, every tap's offset and weight; each level's thresholds, its details and whether a level came before it; sharpening's gain, coring threshold squared and masking scale squared | none |
    | `lf_basic_white_balance` | `modules/basic/white_balance.rs` | the composite 3 × 3 matrix, row by row | none |
    | `lf_basic_exposure` | `modules/basic/exposure.rs` | the gain | none |
    | `lf_basic_tone` | `modules/basic/tone.rs` | Contrast's branch (identity, positive, negative) and the eight coefficients the unit holds | none |
    | `lf_basic_colour_adjust` | `modules/basic/colour.rs` | Vibrance's gain and Saturation's | none |
    | `lf_curve_tone_curve` | `modules/curve/unit.rs` | the knot count, the curve's ends and its black level | the knots, inverse widths and per-segment coefficients |
    | `lf_mixer_mixer` | `modules/mixer/unit.rs` | the hue warp's eight cubics, the eight chroma gains and the eight luminance amounts (48) | none |
    | `lf_vignette_vignette` | `modules/vignette/unit.rs` | the shape, its coefficients, the stage's half sides, the falloff, the branch and the amount (11) | none |
    | `lf_presence` (spatial) | `modules/presence/presence.wgsl`, described by `modules/presence/gpu.rs` | each pass's form, radius, run and regularization; each apply's gain, limit and reduction; a light link's selection (divisor, minimum count, floor) | none |

    Each is the CPU unit's own `f32` formulation in the same order, every word the `f32` the unit holds. The mixer's description leaves out a slider at zero of either sign, so its words add `+0.0` to each coefficient, which the program processes as the CPU does. Where they differ:
    - The shared colour equations (the sRGB transfer, Rec. 709 luminance, the Oklab conversion) are restated in each program that needs them, under its own names, since a program declares every name it uses. The restated constants (the Oklab matrices, the mixer's centres and gaps, the vibrance weights) are held to the CPU's `f32` values bit for bit by the core's tests, through `naga`.
    - WGSL has no cube root: the Oklab programs take `pow`'s estimate and refine it with one Newton step written so no intermediate overflows.
    - Tone's logistic exponent is held within ±80, past which the logistic is 0 or 1 to every bit, as the CPU's overflowing `exp` gives it.
    - The vignette's mask is evaluated in `f32`; the CPU evaluates it in `f64`. A GPU has no `f64`.
    - Detail's limiter holds its `tanh` argument within ±16, past which `tanh` is 1 to every bit an `f32` holds. Where a squared sharpening residual flushes to zero and the coring threshold is zero, the coring's quotient is its limit, the residual itself, where the CPU holds the subnormal square.
  - **The mask coverage programs**, listed publicly as `MASK_GPU_PROGRAMS` in the kind table's order, all enabled ([mask coverage](#mask-coverage)). Each is a `.wgsl` file beside its CPU field in `crates/luxforge-core/src/mask/`, and `ComponentField::gpu(stage)` answers it with the terms the field compiled against that stage, each narrowed to `f32` once:

    | Program | Beside | Words | Block |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | `lf_mask_linear` | `mask/linear.rs` | the stage's height, `u0`, `v0`, `du`, `dv` and `l2` (6) | none |
    | `lf_mask_radial` | `mask/radial.rs` | the stage's height, the centre, the rotation's cosine and sine, the two radii, `r0`, the span and the hard edge (10) | none |
    | `lf_mask_brush` | `mask/brush.rs` | the stage's height, the grid's corner and cell side, its columns and rows, and the offsets of the block's parts (9) | the field's own grid index: its segments, its cell table, the entries and the stroke records |
    | `lf_mask_luminance_range` | `mask/range.rs` | the band's edges and shoulders on the encoded axis (4) | none |
    | `lf_mask_colour_range` | `mask/range.rs` | the sample count, the radius the refine maps to and five Oklab `(a, b)` pairs (12) | none |

    Each evaluates its field's frozen expressions in the CPU's order, in `f32`, at the pixel `pos` of the stage the mask was compiled against, with mask space at that pixel's centre over the stage's height. Where they differ:
    - The luminance axis's Rec. 709 weights and sRGB encode, and the Oklab rows the colour range and a limited brush stroke read, are restated under each program's own names and held to the CPU's `f32` values bit for bit by the core's tests. The cube root is `pow`'s estimate refined by one Newton step, as the colour programs take it.
    - A pixel of a brush tests exactly the segments the CPU's own uniform grid index lists in the cell it falls in, so its cost is that cell's occupancy, which the painted-stroke cap bounds at 64; it folds the nearest segment's capsule profile per stroke, the flow, a stroke's colour limit and the screen union or erase across strokes in stored order.
    - A hard edge is compared in `f32`: a sample whose centre lies within about 10⁻⁴ of a pixel of the edge may take the other side from the CPU's ([ties](#mask-coverage)).
  - **The brush's storage block.** It serializes the field's index in the order a painted stroke grows it: the segments in stored stroke order (five words each), then the cell table, the entries (each a stroke and a segment in one word) and the stroke records (seven words each). A stroke being painted is always the component's last, so a tick leaves every earlier stroke's segments where they were, and the surface writes only the 1 KiB chunks that changed ([a tick](#where-the-code-lives)). The host decimates the whole captured path again on every tick, and on a curved path that moves positions it had kept, so a painted tick rewrites the stroke's own segments from the first moved position on, with the index after them; only a path whose kept positions stay put appends its new segments alone. The block is bounded by the stroke limits, at most `BRUSH_GPU_BLOCK_WORDS_MAX` words: every point a mask may hold as a segment, the largest grid's cell table with every cell at the occupancy cap, and a record per stroke, 316,034 words (1.21 MiB).
  - The core's tests validate every shipped program through `naga` 27.0.3, the version wgpu 27.0.1 resolves to, as a dev-dependency only, with a copy of the surface's prelude and a harness entry point; a spatial program with a copy of the spatial declarations as stubs and a harness that calls every kernel and apply its units' descriptions name. They fail for a `.wgsl` file the shipped list omits. The desktop's tests hold every shipped program to the surface's own `PRELUDE` and `validate_step`, so the two copies of the prelude cannot drift apart unnoticed. `cargo xtask check-repository` refuses a GPU or GUI crate, `naga` included, in the core's normal and build dependencies (`gpu-free-core`) and in `luxforge-cli`'s normal dependencies (`headless-cli`).
- **Planning stays in the core.** `gpu_plan` (`crates/luxforge-core/src/render/gpu/plan.rs`) compiles the stack once, as the CPU frame of the same view is compiled: at the proxy stage at Fit and below 100%, at the exact stage at 100% and above and for the picture at rest's tiles. It walks the compilation on the catalog owner in `O(layers + units + components)`, reads no pixel and holds nothing that scales with the image. From the source, or from the input of a named layer, it answers the ordered plan to the terminal output, or the reason there is none.
  - **The boundary**: the stage that layer receives, and whether a colour operation before it in the same segment hands it an unclamped value, which the boundary must then hold as it is.
  - **Content operations** over the boundary's texels, in recipe order: every colour operation of the boundary's segment from the boundary on.
  - **The spatial operations** (`GpuPlan::spatial`, each a `GpuSpatial`) the content operations' frame enters, in recipe order, while each segment that follows opens with one and no exact step lies between the boundary and it: a restoration layer's (Detail's) and a spatial layer's (Presence's) chained, each one's input the boundary through everything before it. Each holds every unit's planes, passes and apply composed in the CPU's order, a later unit's plane taking a plane of the same size an earlier unit no longer needs, when that plane's format holds it ([plane sharing](#plane-sharing-and-precision)), each pass naming its unit and each unit its halo (`GpuSpatial::halos`); a plane an apply reads has one writer — where a unit's last pass writes its result into a plane an earlier pass of the operation wrote, its own or an earlier unit's, the composition gives the result a plane of its own and frees the other for later units' scratch (`single_writer`); whether the CPU clamps its input and output, which the byte path's quantized frames do and the RAW linear path's float frames do not; its mask; the light plane it reads a global estimate from, when it reads one (`GpuSpatial::light`); and the colour operations its segment holds after it, which run on its output before the next one enters, or, after the last one, before the tail when a resample or warp follows (`GpuSpatial::after`). That last is every colour layer the host places after Detail and before the geometry, which the CPU runs in Detail's own segment between its operation and the resample: each runs pointwise at the boundary's stage, at the coordinate its units address through the segment's exact steps (a turn's) and its mask's through the steps before it, exactly as the CPU runs it, and the tail carries the segment's exact steps with the rest and reads the frame they keep. An operation's applies read at most 11 planes (`GPU_CHAIN_APPLY_PLANES`): a pass binds its four inputs and the planes of the applies before it in its own operation beside its link's input within the 16 sampled textures a shader stage has ([a tick](#a-tick)); an operation past it names `spatial-chain`. The tail then starts at the last one's frame. A layer that opens a stage boundary receives the frame its segment wrote, so a boundary there continues no colour run.
  - **Global estimates** are the plan's lights (`GpuPlan::lights`, [the global estimate](#spatial-programs)): `gpu_plan` plans, beside the plan, the light link of every spatial operation that reads one, Dehaze's atmospheric light, from the source over the whole content stage at full scale whatever stage the plan draws, compiling the stack once more at that stage when the plan's is another. The operation reads light `k`, its link's place among them, from the slot's light plane `k`. A drag's lights follow the [recorded default](gpu-first.md#proposals-with-recorded-defaults) (`drag_lights` in `render/gpu/preview.rs`): a drag that leaves a light's input as the committed stack holds it draws with that light; one that changes it only through restoration or spatial layers, a Detail drag under Dehaze, with the light of the stack it started from; one that changes it through a colour layer computes it every tick over the source, every spatial layer before it left out. Planning reads no pixel and reduces nothing.
  - **The path.** `GpuPlanRequest::linear` plans a frame of the RAW linear path, which clamps no stage boundary: neither the spatial operation's input and output nor a resample's input.
  - **The geometry tail**: the exact steps after the boundary and every resample and warp after them, as one `GeometryMap`, with the rectangle of the boundary whose edge the CPU's resample replicates and whether the CPU clamps before it.
  - **Output operations** over the output stage, in recipe order: the colour operations of the last segment when a resample separates it from the boundary, the vignette among them. With no resample after the boundary the tail is exact, and every operation, the vignette included, runs in the content pass at the coordinates the CPU hands it.
  - **Clipping marks**: the linear thresholds at which the output quantizer gives codes 0 and 255.
  - **What changed since an earlier plan** (`GpuPlan::changes_since`, `crates/luxforge-core/src/render/gpu/changes.rs`): nothing, a rectangle of the boundary's stage, or anywhere (`GpuChange`), from the descriptions alone in `O(units + components + segments)`. A brush component compares its segments and stroke records (`mask::brush::gpu_changed`): segments added, removed or moved change coverage only within their box grown by the largest stroke radius, taken back through the mask's position map to the pass's pixels and halved under the thin-feature supersample. Any other change to a mask over the same stage and position map — a shape moved, a component added, changed or inverted, the mask's inversion or amount, a stroke record — changes coverage only inside its bounds before and after, outside which each is exactly zero, so it is that rectangle: a radial dragged across a Fit stage changes 3.6% of it a tick (`gpu_mask_a_moved_radial_is_evaluated_where_its_bounds_were_and_are`). Any other difference — a unit's words, a mask's position map or supersample, the boundary, the tail or an output operation — is a change anywhere.
  - Each operation carries its units' descriptions and the exact integer map from its pass's pixel to the `pos` the CPU unit's `apply_row` is handed. A masked one also carries the map to its mask's own stage, the components with their modes and inversions, the mask's inversion, `amount / 100`, the bounds outside which coverage is zero and, for the proxy's thin-feature rule, the 2 × 2 supersample on the doubled stage. The blend is the CPU's, against the operation's own input, and the input itself where coverage is exactly zero.
  - Reasons for no plan, each naming its layer and a stable code: a pixel-stage layer in the stack (`pixel-stage`); a boundary at a source or geometry layer, which only a plan asked for from a layer's input can name, and no gesture's plan is (`boundary-stage`); a spatial or restoration layer after the boundary that the plan cannot hold: a unit without a program, or an exact step before it or between it and the spatial layer before it (`spatial-unit`); a spatial or restoration layer that would chain more apply planes than a pass can bind (`spatial-chain`); a colour layer between two resamples, which no stack the panels build holds, since the host places colour before the geometry (`between-resamples`); a unit or mask component without a program (`no-program`); a disabled program (`disabled-program`). Only the qualification corpus may ask for disabled programs to be planned as if enabled.
  - The plan addresses whole stages. A boundary or output that holds a window of one offsets its pixels by the window's origin.
- **Execution lives in the photo surface** (`crates/luxforge-ui/src/photo_surface/gpu_preview.rs`), which receives Iced's wgpu device and queue in `PhotoPrimitive::prepare`. A whole-frame photograph is handed a `GpuPlan` through `PhotoSurface::gpu_preview`: the boundary (`GpuBoundary`, `rgba16float` or `rgba32float` as its `BoundaryFormat` says), a `TexelMap` from a boundary texel to the stage the programs address, and ordered `GpuStep`s, each a `GpuProgram` of WGSL text, uniform words and a storage block, and the step's `PositionMap`. It is plain data, so `luxforge-ui` still never names a core type. A whole-frame photograph is the Fit view's and, below 100%, the percentage view's display proxy filling the view's box, and runs a whole frame's plan through that photograph's placement. A percentage view of retained full and region frames, at 100% and above, runs a plan of its own stage's region (`GpuPlan::region`, a `GpuRegion`) and draws its frame alone at that rectangle of the photograph; it ignores a whole frame's plan, as a whole-frame photograph ignores a region's. A crop stage ignores a plan.
  - **The calling convention.** Every assembled shader starts with the public `PRELUDE`. It declares two read-only storage bindings: `lf_words`, a header (the texel map, then for each step its program's two base indices and its position map's six coefficients) followed by every program's uniform words in program order, and `lf_blocks`, every program's storage block in program order, at least one word. Its helpers are `lf_word`, `lf_f32`, `lf_block_word` and `lf_block_f32`. A program declares only functions and constants, each name prefixed with its entry's, and its entry is none of the surface's own names (the prelude's, `lf_boundary`, `lf_vertex` and `lf_fragment`) and starts with none of the prefixes the surface generates names under (`lf_surface`, `lf_tail`, `lf_output`).
  - **The pointwise colour step** (`GpuStep::Colour`): `fn <entry>(rgb: vec3<f32>, pos: vec2<f32>, words: u32, block: u32) -> vec3<f32>`. `rgb` is scene-linear sRGB, unclamped. `pos` is the pixel's integer coordinate in the stage the CPU unit's `apply_row` addresses: the texel map takes a boundary texel to its stage pixel, and the step's own `PositionMap`, an exact integer map `(a·x + b·y + tx, c·x + d·y + ty)` whose linear part is a signed permutation, takes that pixel to `pos`. `words` and `block` are the program's base indices. A colour step before the geometry tail runs in the content pass at the boundary's texel; one after it runs in the tail's pass, where its map takes the output pixel to `pos`, which is how a vignette after a resample is placed. A masked step after the tail is placed the same way: its mask's map starts from the output pixel. Clipping marks will join `GpuStep` as a variant of their own.
  - **The masked colour step** (`GpuStep::Masked`, `crates/luxforge-ui/src/photo_surface/gpu_preview/mask.rs`): a colour operation's units, chained as colour steps are at the step's position map, and a `Coverage` holding its components (`CoverageComponent`: a coverage program, its mode and its inversion), the mask's inversion and `amount / 100`, the mask's own `PositionMap` from the stage pixel to the pixel of the stage the mask was compiled against, the half-open rectangle of that stage outside which coverage is exactly zero, and the thin-feature supersample. A coverage program is `fn <entry>(pos: vec2<f32>, rgb: vec3<f32>, words: u32, block: u32) -> f32`: the component's falloff before its inversion, at `pos`, for `rgb`, the operation's own input.
    - The surface generates one function per masked step that places the pixel on the mask's stage, returns zero outside the rectangle, and folds the components as the CPU's mask does (`max(m, c)`, `min(m, 1 − c)` or `min(m, c)` after each component's own `1 − c`), then the mask's inversion and its amount; under the supersample it is the mean of that fold at the four pixels `2q + (i, j)` of the doubled stage. The fragment then blends `(1 − M)·in + M·units(in)` per channel against the operation's input, and where `M` is exactly zero leaves the input as it is and runs no unit.
    - Only the programs are code. The modes, inversions, amount, rectangle, position and supersample are the step's words: its own base holds the mask's position map, the rectangle, the two flags and the amount, then each component's mode, inversion and base indices, then each unit's base indices, then the programs' own words; its block is the components' blocks then the units'. Inverting a component or moving the amount writes words and compiles nothing. An entry may not start with `lf_surface`, the prefix of every name the surface generates.
  - **The spatial step** (`GpuStep::Spatial`, `crates/luxforge-ui/src/photo_surface/gpu_preview/spatial.rs`): a spatial operation's program, its planes (each declared by its channels and whether half precision holds it, and kept in `rgba16float`, `r32float`, `rg32float` or `rgba32float` or in a texture an earlier step left free that holds it ([plane sharing](#plane-sharing-and-precision)); each the boundary's size or the stage's `s × s` blocks the boundary reaches, anchored at the stage origin, or a fixed size), its compute passes in order and one apply per unit, under the **spatial convention**. Every module holding a spatial program also declares `lf_plane(slot, at)` (a bound plane's texel, edge-clamped), `lf_plane_size`, `lf_source(at)` (the input of the pass's unit at a boundary texel: the boundary through every step before the spatial one, clamped where the step says, then the applies of the units before it; a stub in a pass that reads only planes), `lf_origin` (the stage pixel of texel `(0, 0)`), `lf_size`, `lf_store` and a 1024-value `lf_shared` workgroup scratch, which `SPATIAL_PRELUDE` stands in for when a program is validated alone. A kernel, `fn(at: vec2<i32>, words: u32, block: u32)`, reads its pass's inputs as planes `0..4` and writes through `lf_store`; it runs once for every `span` texels of its output, or on the 256 lanes of one workgroup. An apply, `fn(rgb: vec3<f32>, at: vec2<i32>, words: u32, block: u32, planes: u32) -> vec3<f32>`, is a unit's pointwise last step, reading its planes from slot `planes` on. Each pass is a compute pipeline of a generated module that is its kernel and its shape alone (its output's format, how many planes it reads, the steps before the spatial one and the applies its source runs, which a pass that reads only planes runs none of): everything that places a pass in its plan — its step's header, its words, its span, the words of the applies its source runs, the texel its first invocation starts at and the rectangle of its output `lf_store` writes — is data, read from the pass's 256-byte slice of a parameter buffer the planes own and the budget charges with them; the frame's pass binds every apply's planes as a second bind group and runs the step's clamp, applies, mask blend and clamp in the chain. A pass that reads only planes, as the core's description says of it (`GpuPass::reads_source`), declares a stub `lf_source` that answers each texel's own coordinates, which no photograph holds, so a pass described wrongly fails its comparison with the CPU's frame. Each pass's module reaches wgpu as the `naga` module the surface validated, compacted to its entry point (wgpu's `naga-ir`): `naga` leaves a module of one entry point uncompacted, so the driver would otherwise compile the whole spatial program, every kernel and apply, for every pass. A masked operation's `Coverage` is the masked colour step's: its words come first in the step's, in that step's layout with no units, and the surface's coverage function for it blends the applies' output against the operation's input per channel, and leaves the input where coverage is exactly zero. No colour plane holds a unit's output: a pass that needs a unit's input computes it at the texel it reads, so the step's memory is its planes alone.
  - **The geometry tail** (`GpuStep::Geometry`, `crates/luxforge-ui/src/photo_surface/gpu_preview/tail.rs`): the content steps' result carried to the output stage, as the CPU's exact steps, resamples and warps after the boundary carry it. A plan with one runs two render passes: the content steps write an intermediate the boundary's size, and the tail's pass draws the output stage from it. For each output pixel it maps the pixel's centre to the boundary stage through the plan's affine matrix, through a perspective warp's homography (`GpuGeometry::projective`: the map's own steps multiplied into one 3 × 3 matrix, a crop or straightening around the warp included, evaluated in `f32` with its division), or through a lens warp's coordinate grid interpolated in `f32` exactly as `CoordinateGrid::sample` does, then blends the four texels around it in linear light, each tap clamped to the rectangle of the boundary stage the CPU's resample reads and offset by the window the boundary holds. On a JPEG the CPU quantizes at every segment boundary, so the tail does too: the content pass writes 8-bit codes into an `rgba8unorm` intermediate, the tail decodes them through the CPU's table, and its blend is quantized and decoded again. A developed RAW's segments hold unclamped `f32` values, so its intermediate is `rgba32float`; values stay unquantized and unclamped through the tail, including values outside half-float's range. The identity-tail regression verifies bit-for-bit `f32` preservation, and the native RAW geometry test matches CPU output codes exactly. The former half-float candidate lost linear range and darkened near-black output ([failed candidate](../specs/performance.md#plane-precision)). The steps after the tail run in its pass at the output pixel. A spatial step must come before the tail, since it reads the boundary's own texels.
  - **The generated shader.** The surface generates the entry points: a vertex stage of one triangle, and a fragment stage that loads the boundary texel, or the tail's sample, and chains the entries in step order; and, for a spatial step, each pass's compute entry. The last pass writes the 8-bit code the CPU's output quantizer gives each channel: the value clamped to `[0, 1]`, NaN to zero, and compared against the core's 255 code thresholds, each the first `f32` of its code's exact interval, which a core test holds to both of the CPU's quantizer paths at every code boundary. It writes the codes through an `Rgba8Unorm` view of the output, which the draw samples through an `Rgba8UnormSrgb` view as it samples the CPU frame of the same codes; the hardware's own sRGB encoder rounded some values to the neighbouring code. The widget crate cannot reach the core, so the desktop installs the quantizer's tables once at start (`install_output_encoding`); a surface with none installed compiles no pass, and its frames stay the CPU's. Two layers of one unit share their function. `validate_step` checks one program against this convention with the `naga` wgpu uses, as the surface does before compiling: for a spatial step, every kernel and apply its passes and applies name at its signature, and every plane, input, word and source in range, with no apply run by a pass that reads only planes.
  - **The materialized chain** (`crates/luxforge-ui/src/photo_surface/gpu_preview/chain.rs`). A plan holding spatial steps runs as a chain of links: the content steps before the first spatial step form the first link, each spatial step with the colour steps after it the next, and the last link holds everything after the last spatial step, the geometry tail, the output steps and the clipping marks among them. Every link but the last writes its steps' linear values into an intermediate the boundary's size and format, which the next link binds as its own boundary, so a pass's `lf_source` is one texel load and a chain costs the sum of its links rather than evaluating every earlier step again at every texel a filter reads. Each intermediate keeps the content key of what it holds — its input's key, the link's words, its blocks' contents and pipeline, each block's words hashed once, when the link first writes it — and a link whose key did not change is not run, so a tick runs the links from the first one it changes. A link is its own sequence, holding only its own steps' programs, so two masked layers of one shape share every pipeline. The intermediates are charged to the budget with the slot ([bounds](#bounds)). An intermediate holds the boundary's format, written rounded to the nearest half on a JPEG, so a link's output is held at half precision where the CPU keeps a segment's values in `f32`: the largest effect measured is a spatial step reading a colour link's output near black, Basic under Dehaze behind a straightened crop on the Presence fixture, a signed ΔL\* of +0.049 and a mean of 0.058 against the spatial limits of 0.5 and 1.0 ([performance](../specs/performance.md#gpu-previews-at-100)); `rgba32float` intermediates would remove it at twice their memory.
  - **A tick.** `prepare` derives the boundary from the source in one pass when its version changes ([the GPU source](#the-gpu-source)). It writes each link's words with one `queue.write_buffer`, and of the blocks only the 1 KiB chunks that changed. Each link, the last one included, keeps the shared blocks it last wrote and where each starts (`gpu_preview/blocks.rs`): a block the tick hands again at the same place — a lens warp's grid, a curve's knots, a committed brush's segments — is neither compared nor copied, and only the others, a new block or one a longer or shorter block before it moved, are compared with what the buffer holds there and copied where a chunk differs, so the chunks written are those comparing the whole packing would find, and a drag that changes only words compares and copies no block. The link holds each block it wrote, never its address, so the allocation cannot be reused by other contents while it is compared. Then it encodes link by link each spatial step's compute passes the tick needs and the link's frame pass, the last link's into the output through the geometry tail's pass when there is one, and submits them ahead of the frame's own submission. A pass runs only when what its output depends on changed since its plane was written: the boundary's version, the texel map and the steps before the spatial one, its own words (from its offset to the next one a pass or apply of its step names), the words and planes of the applies its source runs, and its inputs. Each texture keeps the key of what it holds, a pass whose output an apply needs runs with the inputs that are not already current, and a new sequence or new planes run every pass the applies need (`spatial::Schedule`). Each link holds the planes its spatial step's applies read, a texture each, charged to the budget with it; its scratch planes are textures of the slot's one pool, which every link takes in turn ([plane sharing](#plane-sharing-and-precision)). A kept texture's key is the link's own. A pool texture's is the pool's record of who wrote it last and what: the holder, which is the writing link's schedule, drawn from a counter in the slot when the link's planes are created and again at every reset, and the pass's key. A link reads that key only from a record of its own current holder, so a link whose scratch another link wrote since runs the passes that write it as for planes never written; an incremental tick, which writes only where it runs, keeps only its apply planes' keys and gives up every pool record it holds (`keep_only`). An apply whose unit is the identity at the tick's words (`GpuApply::identity`: a Texture or Clarity gain of exactly 0, Dehaze at an amount of 0, which only a drafted layer's GPU shape holds) returns its input before it reads a plane, so its planes need not be current and are in no key of what reads through it: a pass only they need does not run, keeps no key and records nothing in the pool, and runs in the tick its unit leaves zero, as for planes never written when another link wrote them since. Like the words, the flag is the tick's, no part of the sequence. Detail never sets it: its identity is the change of zero its passes write. Links that share no texture class never forget each other's scratch. A pass count moves only where another link wrote a link's scratch since it last ran: over two masked Presence layers of every field a Dehaze drag of the first runs all 20 of its passes after the second has run, where alone it runs 19 (`gpu_presence_a_dehaze_drag_after_another_link_ran_reruns_what_that_link_wrote`), and every single-link count below stands. Frames stay bit for bit what a whole evaluation draws, with NaN written into every pool texture before each link's passes too (`gpu_presence_chained_masked_layers_dragged_in_turn_draw_what_a_whole_evaluation_does`, [where a pass runs](#where-the-code-lives)). Over Detail then Presence, a Clarity drag runs none of the chain's 27 passes, a Texture drag 5, a drag of the colour layer between them Presence's 13 and a Detail drag 17, each frame equal to the bit to one that ran every pass (`gpu_presence_after_detail_reruns_only_the_passes_a_tick_changes`). A drag that moves only an apply's word runs no pass: of all three Presence units' 20 passes, a Clarity drag runs none, a Texture drag the 5 of Clarity's that read Texture's output, and a Dehaze drag 19, each frame equal to the bit to one that ran every pass (`gpu_presence_a_drag_reruns_only_the_passes_it_changes`). With Texture and Clarity at zero a Dehaze drag runs 5, and with Clarity at zero a Texture drag none, each frame equal to the bit to a run of every pass (`gpu_presence_a_neutral_unit_runs_no_pass_until_it_leaves_zero`). In the editor the `gpu-preview` scenario counts the same from `gpu_preview_spatial_passes`: over a committed Dehaze and Clarity, each GPU tick of a Texture drag runs 5, of a Clarity drag none, and of a Basic drag under Presence all 20 its plan holds, its light link computing the light again beside them. `draw` samples the output as it samples the photograph, through the same placement, snapping and filter. A whole frame's output is held in the photograph's own size bucket, and a region plan's in the bucket the CPU's region picture of that rectangle reserves (the region and two texels, each side rounded up to 64, or its exact size where that would pass the 32 MiB region set), each with its edge texels repeated past the frame as an upload repeats them, so a GPU frame and the CPU frame of the same codes draw identically. A redraw of an unchanged plan encodes nothing. The UI thread only encodes GPU commands; it never waits for the GPU or reads pixels back.
  - **Where a pass runs.** A masked spatial step's passes run only over its mask's bounds, taken back through its position map to the boundary's texels and grown by every unit's reach and its apply's (`GpuSpatial::pass_rect`), and its frame runs the applies only where coverage is not zero, so a small mask costs its area rather than the stage's; a pass that reads the whole input — a workgroup's, or one that writes a plane of a fixed size — runs whole. A unit's reach (`GpuSpatial::reach`) is its halo, the texels before its own where each running sum starts its run (15 of its plane's texels for a pass along an axis, scaled by the plane's blocks) and two of its largest blocks. A pass placed over a rectangle starts its invocations where the whole plane's pass starts them and writes only the rectangle's texels (`lf_store`'s limit), so every texel within the reach is the whole pass's, bit for bit (`gpu_presence_a_masked_layer_runs_its_passes_over_its_mask_alone`). The planes keep the rectangle they hold the step's values over; a mask that grows past it runs every pass again, over the whole boundary when the input did not change, so a mask that grows over an unchanged input runs them once. The scratch pool changes nothing about where a pass runs — the pass rectangle, the rectangle the planes hold, `needed` or an incremental tick's rectangles. A pass over a rectangle writes only that rectangle, and what an apply needs depends on scratch texels only within the cone its unit's reach bounds, which the tick writes itself, so a scratch texel outside it is never read, whichever link wrote it. If a pass read a cone wider than `reach` says, a link holding its own scratch would read its own earlier, often still correct, values there and pass its tests, where a pooled link reads another link's. So a switch for tests only (`Qualifier::set_poison`) writes NaN bits into every pool texture and forgets its records before each link's passes, and every bit-exact test of partial and incremental evaluation passes with it on as well as off ([verification](#verification)).
  - **Incremental ticks.** The desktop hands each plan with a serial and what changed since a plan the surface evaluated (`GpuChange`, from the core's `GpuPlan::changes_since` against the last 16 it handed), measured only from a plan drawn over the same boundary, texel map and region with the same clipping marks: any other changes anywhere, a new boundary's texels and the marks being everywhere. When the slot holds the values of that plan over the boundary it holds now, each link evaluates again only where its input's changes reach and keeps the rest of what its intermediate and planes hold: a colour link draws its frame pass over the change alone; a spatial link's units each run their passes around the change so far grown by the unit's reach — the pass that writes a plane the unit's apply reads over that rectangle alone, so the plane keeps its values everywhere else, and every other pass over it grown by the reach once more, so what that pass reads there is this tick's — clipped, while the planes hold the step's values only where its mask needs them, to where the mask can cover anything grown by what reads each apply plane there: the apply's own upsample, a reduced plane's neighbouring block, and the reach of every later unit whose input runs the apply (`GpuSpatial::needed`), and out to the boundary's edge on a side where the step's pass rectangle reaches it, as a run over that rectangle leaves the planes, so a mask that grows toward that edge inside the rectangle reads its input's latest values there; and the link's output changes where its input did and, inside its mask, where its last unit's change reaches, grown by its apply's upsample. A plane an apply reads has one writer ([planning](#where-the-code-lives)), so the texels a writer leaves keep their values. A mask that grows past what its planes hold, as a painted one does at nearly every tick, runs its passes over the whole boundary once, after which they hold its values everywhere and a tick runs only around its change. An identity geometry tail, which a plan with colour operations after its last spatial one carries before them, takes each output pixel from its own texel, so it is drawn over the change too; a tail that resamples draws whole. A change anywhere, a plan the slot did not evaluate last, a boundary it did not evaluate that plan over, or a new pipeline evaluates the whole chain (`a_change_measured_over_another_boundary_is_evaluated_whole`, `a_change_is_measured_only_over_the_same_boundary_and_marks`). Every tick's frame is the frame a whole evaluation of the same plan draws, bit for bit and with the pool poisoned too, over a stroke painted, taken back over half its length and painted again in one slot, the brushed layer's planes held whole and held only where its mask needs them (`gpu_mask_a_painted_stroke_is_evaluated_where_each_tick_changes_it`), and over a masked Presence layer whose mask is painted toward the boundary's edge after a masked Basic layer before it was painted along that edge (`gpu_mask_a_mask_grown_toward_the_edge_reads_its_inputs_latest_values`). The host decimates a painted stroke's whole path again at every tick, which moves some kept positions back along the stroke, so the editor-latency stroke changes 6 to 7% of the stage a tick on average; over a brushed mask and two radials, each holding a masked Basic and a masked Presence layer, a tick costs the M4's GPU 2.3 ms at a Fit stage and 4.3 ms over a 100% region, against 6.6 and 14.6 ms evaluated whole (`gpu_mask_a_painted_stroke_costs_where_it_changes`). In the paint harness's layout at Fit, the masked Basic layers then the masked Presence layers with the stroke on the first mask, so every Presence link after it is evaluated incrementally around a change that grows link by link with each link's reach, a tick costs 2.0 ms over 3 masks, 8.8 ms over 10 and 14.5 ms over 16 (`gpu_mask_the_paint_harness_layout_costs_a_tick_at_fit`, [performance](../specs/performance.md#painting-over-masked-spatial-layers)).
  - **Pipelines.** One compiled sequence is kept per link's program sequence and the format it writes (each step's kind, a masked step's count of units and components, a spatial step's clamp, planes, passes and applies, a tail's kind and quantizing, and each program's role, entry and source): its content pass, a tail's pass and a spatial step's compute pipelines, 64 compiled sequences at most (`PIPELINE_CACHE`), ready and failed ones together, the least recently asked for or warmed evicted when a compile ends, and at most as many waiting to compile. ### The GPU source

Every boundary a plan is drawn from is derived on the GPU from the photograph's **source**, which the photo surface's pipeline holds for every surface (`crates/luxforge-ui/src/photo_surface/gpu_preview/source.rs`). The boundary is the first segment's input before its first operation, the source itself at a view's scale ([how the plan reaches the surface](#where-the-code-lives)), so the GPU evaluates every layer of the stack, and a drag and the stack's picture at rest over one source and view share one boundary.

- **What is held.** The desktop hands the surface the prepared source of the photograph on screen (`GpuSource`, `PhotoSurface::gpu_source`), from the pixels the preview jobs already share (`PreviewSource`): a JPEG's upright 8-bit codes as `rgba8uint`, 4 bytes a pixel, read by integer and decoded through the CPU's own 256-entry table, never through the hardware's sRGB view; a developed RAW's three `r32float` planes of the view's crop window, 12 bytes a pixel, written straight from the planes the core shares (`LinearImage::shared_planes`) with no copy on the CPU, the view's orientation applied as a boundary is cut. A side past the device's largest texture, 8192 px on Iced's device, is held in up to two textures across and two down; a pass reads them by coordinate, so a boundary that straddles two has no seam. The source's version changes with its pixels — another photograph, a new development such as a white-balance release — and never otherwise, so it is uploaded once whichever surfaces hand it.
- **Its upload is spread.** The pipeline writes at most `UPLOAD_PER_FRAME`, 32 MiB, of the source's rows a frame across every surface, from the caller's buffer on the interface thread's `prepare`, never waited on; each frame's chunks are carried by that frame's submit, so at most two frames' are staged at once. An upload's start and end each wake the desktop once, and while one runs the widget asks for the next frame, so an idle editor stays asleep. A 24 MP JPEG arrives over three frames and a 60 MP RAW over about 22. The desktop lets its reference to the pixels go once the surface reports it holds every row, or could not hold the source, and hands them again from the next job if the pipeline let the source go.
- **Charged and retired.** The source is charged to the GPU-preview budget before anything is created: 96 MB for a 24 MP JPEG, 240 MB for a 60 MP JPEG and the Air 2S, 292 MB for the Z6, 478 MB for the X100VI and 720 MB for a 60 MP RAW ([bounds](#bounds)). A source no surface handed in a frame retires at that frame's end through the retirement worker, charged until the GPU is done with it: another photograph or a stage that cannot draw, since the desktop hands a source only while its gate allows a plan ([the gate](#the-gate)).
- **Cut.** At the exact stage — a 100% region's window, a tile of the picture at rest, or at Fit a photograph whose output fits the display bounds — one fixed pass cuts a window of the source at full scale through the view's orientation (`Derivation::Cut`): a JPEG's code decoded and held as the nearest half float, a RAW's value as the `f32` it is, bit for bit the boundary the CPU renders at the source (`Render::boundary`, kept for the tests as the reference, `a_cut_boundary_is_the_cpus_boundary_bit_for_bit`). The window is what the drawn output reads, planned on the owner by the GPU's window walk (`WindowPlan::of_gpu_rect`): the region and every margin the spatial operations need on the GPU, their summed halos alone, since the GPU evaluates every pixel of the window at once and needs no tile grid, and at Fit what a crop, a straightening and a warp read, with the resample's taps and margin, clamped to the stage. Every window starts where the plan's anchor puts it, so its texels are the whole stage's bit for bit ([at 100% and above](#at-100-and-above)). On the 60 MP JPEG a 3026 × 1826 region read through Detail, then Texture and Clarity, holds 3778 × 2578 texels.
- **Reduce.** At Fit and below 100% the pass averages the source at the proxy plan the CPU's proxy is built at, the window a crop reads included, with the CPU build's own coverage weights (`Derivation::Reduce`, `ProxyPlan::coverage`), across each source row and then down; a JPEG's average is quantized through the output thresholds and decoded again, as the CPU's proxy is. It is within a code of the CPU's proxy, whose sums are `f64` where these are `f32`. This boundary serves a drag and the stack's view plan; the picture at rest at those views is process-first, its tiles cut at full scale ([the picture at rest](#the-picture-at-rest)).
- **Format.** A JPEG's boundary is `rgba16float`, its values each 8-bit code's linear value; a value past the half range is held at the largest finite half. A developed RAW's, on the linear path (`GpuPlanRequest::linear`), is `rgba32float`, every value as the CPU holds it: half rounding of a near-black value can flip the sign of the luminance Presence divides by, which draws green where the CPU draws magenta ([performance](../specs/performance.md#isolated-near-black-pixels)).
- **When it is derived.** The moment a plan names a boundary the slot does not hold: the committed stack's job, as it is queued, derives its view plan's boundary, which every gesture over the stack then draws from at its first tick, and a tick whose plan names another key derives that one and lets the old go (`key-changed`). Nothing is rendered or copied on the CPU and no preview job carries a boundary. A lens warp's coordinate grid is the one part computed on the CPU, once per key, on the runtime's blocking pool; until it arrives a tick names `boundary-pending`.
- **Key and lifetime.** The key (`BoundaryKey`) holds the source's identity, development and view included, the hash of the layers before the boundary, the boundary layer, the proxy plan with its scale and window, and at the exact stage the output region and the window the boundary holds, including spatial support margins: equal keys hold equal texels. The boundary is held for the open draft and, once the draft ends, as the stack's own between gestures; it is let go when its key changes (the window moved, the bounds changed, a pan left the region), another photograph is opened or the GPU stage is refused, and the GPU-preview slot is retired with it.
- **Until it is ready.** A tick over a source still uploading holds the frame on screen naming `source-uploading`, and over a source the pipeline does not hold `source-missing`, each passing within the upload's frames or the next job ([a tick the GPU does not draw](#a-tick-the-gpu-does-not-draw)). At the exact stage, at Fit or at 100% and above, the desktop knows the window before it derives it, so a boundary that would pass `BOUNDARY_MAX_BYTES`, 256 MiB (owner, 2026-10-02), or a slot the budget, is never derived and names `budget-exceeded` with the figure and the bound in the drag's evidence; a reduced boundary is bounded by its proxy bounds, the display's or the displayed size, each held to 8 MP.

### A tick

1. The input updates the draft on the owner synchronously, as today. The answer carries the draft's GPU preview: its plan and the boundary it starts from, or the reason there is none. The one tick whose plan reads a pixel — a colour-limited stroke's first, which reads its seed from a GPU tile — is never waited for on the interface thread: the owner refuses it at once, changing nothing, and the desktop sends it again on the blocking pool, so that tick's plan arrives a hop later with its answer and the moves meanwhile are coalesced behind it ([draft memo](detail.md#pixel-reads-off-the-owner)).
2. With the boundary held and the surface having evaluated it, nothing goes to the preview worker for that tick: no job and no upload (`Editor::gpu_tick`, `crates/luxforge-app/src/app/gpu_preview.rs`). The desktop converts the plan over the held boundary and hands it to the surface tagged with the tick's draft revision. In Mask mode the overlay's coverage still follows the tick through its own worker, over the frame on screen.
3. In the next `prepare`, the surface writes the words and the changed blocks and encodes the passes:
   - a spatial step's compute passes the tick changes;
   - the colour and mask programs in content space, in recipe order, and the spatial step's applies;
   - the geometry tail, by bilinear sampling through the affine matrix, the homography or the coordinate grid (linear-light texels, as the CPU's resample blends), quantized as the CPU's segments are on a JPEG;
   - the finish programs (the vignette) and any colour operation of the output stage, at the output pixel;
   - the output encoding, the CPU's output quantizer's codes.
4. The frame draws in that same update.

Any other tick names why, in `state.surface.gpu.plan_fallback`: the GPU stage cannot draw at all, or the launch refused it (`no-adapter`, `device-lost`), the plan's own reason (`unplannable` among them, for a job whose bounds or region were not known yet), a region's boundary or slot, or one at the exact stage at Fit, would pass its bound or the budget (`budget-exceeded`), a lens warp's grid is still being computed (`boundary-pending`), the source is not held (`source-missing`), the surface cannot run the plan (`warp-grid`, `boundary-size`, `position-range`, `region-outside`, `light-link`), or the surface has not evaluated it yet or fell back (`surface-pending`, or the surface's reason, `compiling`, `source-uploading` and `budget-exceeded` among them), and holds its frame or has the reference renderer draw it ([below](#a-tick-the-gpu-does-not-draw)). A reason that lasts is also said in the status bar ([the fallback notice](#labels-and-overlays-during-motion)). A GPU frame is drawn only once the frame on screen is the draft's own, so a drafted frame never shows under an older CPU frame's revision. The release commits through the owner as before, and the committed stack's own plan takes the drawn plan's place, the same programs over the same boundary ([the picture at rest](#the-picture-at-rest)).

### A tick the GPU does not draw

Decided by the owner on 2026-10-05 ([decisions](../decisions.md#gpu-first-rendering)): a tick the GPU does not draw holds its frame for a reason that passes within a tick or an upload, and otherwise the reference renderer draws one whole frame of the drafted stack per tick, the latest winning, labelled (`Editor::cpu_tick`, `crates/luxforge-app/src/app/motion.rs`). There is no region on the CPU, and a session with a GPU asks for no proxy; a session the GPU does not draw at all drags on the display-size proxy instead ([fallback and portability](#fallback-and-portability)).

- **Held.** `compiling` until the status bar's half-second threshold, `unchanged`, `unplannable` while no view is known (a job planned with no GPU preview), and the boundary, source and surface waits (`boundary-pending`, `boundary-uploading`, `source-uploading`, `source-missing`, `surface-pending`). The tick queues nothing and the frame on screen stays; the surface draws the tick's plan once the wait is over, or the next tick does. Every drag's first tick waits on its boundary this way, so no drag starts by queuing a whole frame. A run of held ticks that lasts the same half second, from its first tick, renders the newest held tick's reference frame (`gpu_preview_hold_ended`, why `lasted`); one the surface draws first ends there (why `drawn`), the gesture's frame. The desktop looks at a held tick every 50 ms while it holds.
- **Rendered.** Any other reason — a layer the GPU has no program for (`pixel-stage`, `no-program` and the rest of the stack's class), a slot past the budget even at the softer drag frame's reduced stage, a surface fallback that lasts — queues the tick's reference job: the whole drafted stack and its report, reduced to the view where the view draws the stage smaller than it is (a drafted RAW white balance approximated on the developed planes is never reduced, and is labelled approximate). One such job is in flight at a time and no tick supersedes it: a newer tick's job waits on the desktop, replacing any older one (`gpu_preview_tick` with `waits`), and is queued once the frame in flight has been taken up (`gpu_preview_reference`), so a drag the reference draws shows a new frame as fast as the reference renders one. The cost is the reference's whole frame: 48–274 ms for a pointwise stack at 24–60 MP, about 0.1 s for a full-size RAW frame and seconds for all three Presence fields at 60 MP ([performance](../specs/performance.md)).
- **The three ordinary cases** — a RAW white-balance drag, a geometry drag on a stack with no content layer and a 100% drag past the budget — have GPU paths of their own (owner, 2026-10-05): [RAW white balance](#raw-white-balance), the source plan and [the softer drag frame](#at-100-and-above).
- **A pan during a paused drag.** At 100% and above a pan past the region the drag's last tick drew plans the view again as the drag's next tick (`Editor::drag_view_planned`): drawn on the GPU over the new region, or held or rendered as any tick. Where the reference's whole frame of the newest revision is on screen or on its way it holds every view, and nothing is planned.

Colour runs before geometry, as on the CPU, so a strong curve across an edge is not interpolated in the wrong order. A perspective warp with no lens is a homography, with any crop or straightening around it, and the tail evaluates it at every pixel: in `f32` it lands within 0.0018 px of `GeometryMap` on photo-sized stages up to the strongest perspective (`a_perspective_homography_is_the_cpu_map_in_f32`). A lens warp's coordinate grid is computed once per draft from `GeometryMap` (`GpuGeometry::grid`, `crates/luxforge-core/src/render/gpu/grid.rs`), at a density that keeps the content it reads within 0.1 display pixel of exact and, at any magnification, within 0.025 output pixel:

- Nodes at a regular output spacing hold the boundary coordinate the map gives them. The spacing starts at 64 px and narrows until the displacement it causes, measured at every cell centre and edge midpoint, is within half the tighter bound: the output-pixel one at Fit and 100%, the display one divided by the magnification past a fourfold zoom.
- A displacement too small to see still moves the value a high-frequency source gives a pixel, hence the output-pixel bound. With the display bound alone (about 0.04 px at the pixels) a lens warp over the zone plate's chirp left a worst 16 × 16 block of 0.98 at 100% against the pointwise limit of 1.0, and a perspective warp drawn through a grid 1.15 at Fit; with it the lens cell's worst block is 0.23 ([performance](../specs/performance.md#the-corpus-error-report)).
- On the fixtures' strongest lenses the spacing is 4 px on a 1024-pixel Fit stage, 5 to 6 px on a 2048-pixel one and 10 px at 6048 × 4024 and over a 100% region. The worst displacement measured at the pixels, through the map's exact inverse, is 0.0105 px.
- The surface interpolates the nodes in `f32` arithmetic. A sampler's linear filtering would not hold the contract: its weights carry too few bits.
- A pixel's cell is found in integers, its stage pixel over the spacing, and its fraction across the cell is its offset from the cell's first node over the spacing, so a pixel interpolates to the same bits through any part of the grid, whichever node a tile's or region's part starts at. A division by the spacing of a coordinate measured from where the part starts would not: the shading language may divide by a reciprocal, and on the Air 2S's lens grid that moved 1.9% of a whole stage's codes, by up to 5, between tiles of 512 and 1024 px (`gpu_rest_the_picture_at_rest_is_the_same_at_every_side_and_order`, `gpu_rest_raw_tiles_are_the_same_at_every_side`).
- A grid holds at most 262,144 nodes (2 MiB); the strongest Fit fixture needs about 112,600 (879 KiB), and the strongest lens over a whole 6048 × 4024 stage, larger than any region the surface draws, 244,800 (1.9 MiB). A warp that needs more takes the CPU path.

### Below 100%

At a percentage zoom below 100% the view draws the **displayed-size proxy** of the whole stage, as Fit draws the display-bounded one: the job's bounds are the stage's displayed size in physical pixels, held to 4096 px a side and 8 MP as every job's are, and the reference's frame is reduced to them ([instant previews](instant-preview.md#the-reference-renderers-frames)). A drag there is the Fit drag at those bounds, differing only in the proxy's size and the view's placement (`Editor::gpu_ask`, `crates/luxforge-app/src/app/gpu_preview.rs`).

- **The plan.** A tick asks the owner for Fit's plan at the job's bounds (`GpuView::Fit`), which addresses the reduced stage the GPU plans at them (`crates/luxforge-core/src/render/gpu/fit.rs`): the output stage fitted to the bounds, the stack compiled once against the source scaled so, and the window of it a crop reads, walked by the GPU's window walk from the source (`WindowPlan::of_gpu_rect`, boundary 0) and anchored to the plan. Its boundary is the source reduced to that stage, keyed by its plan and bounds and derived on the GPU as at Fit ([the GPU source](#the-gpu-source)), within the 2 GiB budget. A committed stack's job plans its picture at rest and its warm list at the same bounds, so a drag at the zoom a commit was made at starts from the stack's own boundary and draws on the GPU from its first tick.
- **The frame.** The surface draws the plan's whole frame in place of the photograph's frame, through the same placement, snapping and filter: the frame fills the view's box, larger than the window or smaller, and the output is held in the photograph's size bucket, so a GPU frame and a CPU frame of the same codes draw identically at every pan. Below 100% the view always draws the photograph as a whole frame, as Fit does, an exact frame drawn smaller than it is included.
- **A pan or another zoom.** A pan moves the view over the proxy without changing it, so the boundary stays and the next tick draws on the GPU with nothing derived. Another percentage below 100% is another proxy: the view's job, or the first drag there, derives that proxy's boundary and lets the other go (`key-changed`), as a resize does at Fit.
- **Settle.** The release draws the committed stack's view plan in the drag's frame's place and then its picture at rest in tiles, the stage at full resolution reduced to the view, dissolving in over it, as at Fit ([the picture at rest](#the-picture-at-rest)).
- **Fallback.** A tick the GPU cannot draw holds its frame or has the reference draw it and names its reason as at Fit ([a tick the GPU does not draw](#a-tick-the-gpu-does-not-draw)); the zoom itself is no reason. The release gate measures 33% and 50% on the corpus ([verification](#verification)).

### At 100% and above

At a percentage zoom of 100% or more a drag is drawn over the **visible region** at full scale (`Editor::gpu_ask`, `crates/luxforge-app/src/app/gpu_preview.rs`).

- **The region.** A tick asks for the plan over the region of the output stage the view shows, as the desktop computes it (`viewport_rect`, a guard pixel each side), or over the region its drag already asked for at this zoom while that region still holds the view, so a pan inside it keeps the boundary. The owner plans it at the exact stage, keyed by the region, with the zoom as the magnification a warp's grid is made dense enough for.
- **The boundary.** The first tick derives the region's window from the source, cut at full scale ([the GPU source](#the-gpu-source)), so the gesture's GPU frames start without waiting for any CPU render. A committed stack's job at 100% or more plans its view plan over the visible region, whose window is derived as the job is queued, and warms the region's plans. A spatial step sees that window alone.
- **The window's anchor.** The window a region's boundary holds — as a tile's of the picture at rest, and a crop's at the exact stage at Fit — starts where the plan's anchor puts it (`GpuPlan::anchor`, `anchored`): the window the halos read, moved up and left by the plan's lead, the sum of `(span − 1) × s` over every pass that runs a running sum `span` texels at a time over a plane of `s × s` blocks, then down to a multiple of every such pass's `span × s`. A running sum's drift depends on where its run starts, and its `f32` sum carries the rounding of every value since its seed, which reads its radius before the run's first output; so over an anchored window every run and every reduced block starts where it does over the whole stage, seeded from texels the whole stage holds, and every texel the window draws is the whole stage's, bit for bit, whichever window draws it (`gpu_rest_a_stage_in_tiles_is_the_stage_in_one_region_bit_for_bit`, across Presence, Detail, a lens warp and a perspective warp, every scratch plane starting from NaN). Anchored without the lead, a 360 × 240 Presence stage drawn in tiles of 96 differed from the stage drawn as one region by a code at one to six of its 86,400 pixels. Presence's three units take a lead of 300 px and a multiple of 64, Texture alone 60 and 16, and a stack with no running sum, Detail's or a colour stack's, keeps the halos' window: a region's window origin moves by up to the lead and the multiple less one, 363 px up and left for all three Presence units.
- **The frame.** The plan names its region (`GpuPlan::region`). With no tail the content pass reads the region out of the held window; with one, the tail draws the region's output pixels, each offset by the region's origin through two words after the texel map, so every program sees its pixel's whole-stage coordinate. The surface places the output at the region of the photograph and draws it alone: never a CPU region of other content beside it.
- **No job.** While the frame holds the view and the surface has evaluated it, a tick sends nothing to the worker, and the motion it notes plans nothing either (`Editor::gpu_draws_view`). A pause in a drag the GPU draws settles nothing on the CPU; the release commits, and the committed stack's region plan, the picture at rest at this zoom, takes the drag's frame's place.
- **A pan.** A view the region does not hold withdraws the plan at once, so the surface draws the CPU's frame, until the next tick, or the paused drag's view planned again, plans the new region and derives its window; that tick lets the old one go (`key-changed`).
- **The budget.** The plan's boundary request names the window the boundary will hold (`SourceBoundary`), planned on the owner by the GPU's window walk. A region whose boundary would pass the 256 MiB bound on one, or whose slot — the boundary, a tail's intermediate, the frame in its size bucket with its placement uniform, and the chain over the window: each link's intermediate before the last, every link's kept planes and parameters and the pool of scratch planes once (`region_charge`, through the surface's own `texture_charge` and `chain_charge` over the plan's steps converted with no boundary): everything the slot charges but its links' words and blocks buffers — would pass the GPU-preview budget less the source the surface holds, which the same budget charges, derives no boundary, with the figure and what the budget leaves the slot in the drag's evidence (`state.surface.gpu.gpu_preview.drag.over_budget`): it is drawn from its reduced stage, the softer drag frame (below), or, when that does not fit either, has the reference draw its whole frames naming `budget-exceeded` ([a tick the GPU does not draw](#a-tick-the-gpu-does-not-draw)). A slot the surface finds over the budget once the boundary is held names it too, and the drag's next tick at that zoom draws the softer frame ([bounds](#bounds)), as does a slot the budget holds only once the slot it replaces has retired: that tick has the reference draw its frame, keeps its boundary and plan and asks for nothing again, and the frame after the retirement draws on the GPU, nothing allocated or released twice (`a_larger_plan_waits_for_the_planes_it_replaces_then_holds_its_own`, `gpu_preview_a_tick_the_budget_refuses_while_a_slot_retires_keeps_its_boundary`). The corpus measures the largest window the owner's display holds; a larger one, such as an external display's, draws a larger region, and Detail beside all three Presence fields stops fitting at a region about 36 to 40% wider and taller than that window's ([performance](../specs/performance.md#after-detail-presence)).
- **The softer drag frame.** A region whose plan's own figures pass the figure its request names (`PreviewRequest::reduce_regions_after`), `REDUCED_AFTER_BYTES`, 512 MiB, unless a test names a smaller one, carries beside it the same draft planned at the reduced stage of the view's area (`GpuPreview::reduced`): `GpuView::Fit` at the region's displayed size, the frame Fit draws at the view's size, whose spatial halos are the reduced stage's. Where the region's slot would pass the budget beside the source, on this tick or on an earlier one of the drag at this zoom, the tick draws that plan instead: its boundary the source reduced to the stage, its frame the whole reduced output placed over the photograph's full output stage as a region of its own stage (`GpuRegion::full_stage`), which the surface magnifies to fill the photograph. Every tick is drawn on the GPU; the drag names `budget-reduced` (`state.surface.gpu.gpu_preview.drag.softer` the zoom it went softer at), which the status bar says as `Softer while dragging`. The release draws the committed stack's region plan at rest, sharp. The reduced frame is held to the budget beside the source too, and only when it passes it does the drag have the reference draw its frames naming `budget-exceeded` (`gpu_window_a_region_past_the_budget_draws_the_reduced_stage_scaled_to_the_view`).
- **Spatial layers.** A Detail or Presence layer's GPU shape charges the planes of its units at zero over the window too, so the owner plans the drafted layer in its GPU shape and, beside it, in the CPU's shape, the units its values need, from the same boundary (`GpuPreview::cpu_shape`). A tick draws the GPU shape while its slot fits the budget, and the CPU's shape when only that one does, where a value crossing zero meets another sequence, which compiles while the CPU's frames are drawn; when neither fits it takes the CPU path naming the CPU shape's charge. The drag's evidence names the shape drawn (`state.surface.gpu.gpu_preview.drag.shape`).
- **Dehaze.** The exact visible region reads the whole stage's atmospheric light, and so does a region plan: its light link computes it from the whole content stage at full resolution over the source, whatever window the plan draws over ([the global estimate](#spatial-programs)). A drag of Presence reads the light at rest; a drag of a colour layer before Presence computes it every tick, so a Basic drag under Dehaze draws on the GPU over the region too.
- **Dehaze behind Detail.** The light reads Detail's whole output, which only a sweep of the whole stage through Detail at full resolution gives. No slot runs that sweep yet, so a slot computes such a light by its stand-in over the source with Detail left out, at rest and in motion alike; the reference's whole frame keeps Detail whole before Dehaze, so its light is the whole frame's:
  - **A drag of Presence**, or of a layer after Detail that does not feed Presence, leaves the light's input as it was and reads the light at rest.
  - **A drag of Detail** changes the input only by Detail's filters, which barely move the block means the light is chosen from: it reads the light of the stack it started from.
  - **A drag of a colour layer** before Presence — Basic between Detail and Presence, where placement puts it, or before Detail — computes the light every tick over the source with Detail left out, the [recorded default](gpu-first.md#proposals-with-recorded-defaults): five sharpen-stress cells under Dehaze −100 miss the limits in motion so ([performance](../specs/performance.md#the-per-frame-lights-reduction-factor)), a question for the owner.
  - **A drag after Presence** runs Presence on the GPU from the stack's first content layer, as every gesture is planned, reading the light at rest.
  - The drag's first tick holds the frame on screen while the region's window is cut from the source. The release draws the committed stack at rest ([the picture at rest](#the-picture-at-rest)).
  - A Detail drag's slot holds Detail's planes and Presence's over the window, within the budget in the largest window the owner's display holds and past it in a larger one, where the drag is drawn as the softer frame ([performance](../specs/performance.md#after-detail-presence)).
- **Overlays.** At a percentage zoom the mask overlay's coverage is keyed on the region the GPU draws (`Editor::gpu_view_region`): the coverage worker computes a grid of that region, for each tick and at rest, and the surface lays it over the GPU region frame at its rectangle. Over the reference's whole frame the coverage is the whole stage's grid. A clipping overlay is drawn by the plan's own marks, as at Fit.
- **Settle.** The release hands the committed stack's region plan, its picture at rest at this zoom, in the drag's frame's place over the same window. Where the GPU cannot plan the committed stack, the CPU frame that replaces the drag's dissolves in over the GPU region frame, as at Fit ([settle](#settle-and-the-dissolve)): the view's whole frame of the settled content. The surface draws the dissolving region output at its rectangle under the view's own pictures, laid over it at the dissolve's share, and a change of zoom or a pan cancels it, as any input does.
- **Evidence.** Each captured frame records `state.surface.gpu.visible_region` and `plan_region`, the view's region and the region of the plan handed to the surface, each `[x0, y0, x1, y1]`, and the drag's `boundary.region` and `zoom`. The `gpu-preview-zoom` scenario drags at 100%, 200% and, panning, 800%, and at 100% drags Presence over the light at rest, each tick of a Texture drag running 5 compute passes and of a Clarity drag none, as at Fit, and a Basic drag under Presence with Dehaze, on the GPU, its light computed every tick, each naming its lights `source`. With Detail committed under Presence and Dehaze, it drags Texture and Detail, both on the GPU, their light the stand-in with Detail left out (`stand-in`); a Basic drag between them is the core's test (`behind_detail_a_region_plan_reads_the_light_at_rest_or_its_stand_in`).

### RAW white balance

A Temperature or Tint drag on a RAW changes its source layer, the development, which redevelops the mosaic only at the release. Its draft's evaluation approximates the drafted white balance on the planes the entry developed by one matrix, `W = R · diag(g'/g) · R⁻¹` (`WhiteBalanceApproximation`, [RAW white-balance drafts](../decisions.md#raw-white-balance-drafts)), and the GPU draws it from the source it already holds, those planes:

- **The plan.** The drag is planned from the source as every gesture is, with `W` narrowed to `f32` as a leading pointwise step over each source texel (Basic's white-balance program, the one linear-sRGB 3 × 3 multiply, reported under the RAW layer), before the content operations and before every light link and its stand-in, since the change reaches every light's input. Its boundary is the one the picture at rest holds, so the drag asks for nothing new: at Fit and below 100% the source reduced to the view, at 100% the visible region cut from it. A drag back to the planes' own white balance has no step. A RAW stack's warm list holds the drag's sequence, so at Fit its first tick finds it compiled; a percentage view's plans are not warmed.
- **Exactness.** At the exact stage the plan's frame, run by the reference executor over the developed planes, is the reference renderer's approximate frame within a code (`a_raw_white_balance_drag_draws_the_cpus_approximate_frame`). The tile path, which the picture at rest, samples and export read, still refuses an approximate white balance, so nothing at rest is ever drawn through `W`: the release redevelops and the picture at rest is the redevelopment's.
- **What it is reported against.** The moving GPU frame is reported, not gated, against the release's picture at rest, the whole frame at Fit and the visible region at 100%, by the pointwise statistics, the `raw-panel` scenario failing only on a gross error: a worst block past 10 ΔE00, a CPU or reference job on a drag's tick, or a frame the GPU did not draw (owner, 2026-10-06, [decisions](../decisions.md#gpu-first-rendering)). On the corpus RAWs only the X100VI at Fit is within the pointwise limits ([performance](../specs/performance.md#a-raw-white-balance-drag-on-the-gpu-against-its-release)), the largest worst block 8.75 ΔE00 (the Air 2S at 100%, 2500 K). At Fit much of the difference is the drag's frame rather than `W`: against the redeveloped stack's own drag frame the change is within the limits on the Z6 and X100VI, while that frame is already past them against the picture at rest on the Z6 and Air 2S, whose lens profiles warp the stage. At 100% the region is the picture at rest's own plan and the difference is `W`'s: the worst block over the largest view's centred region, 1.16 to 2.33 on the Z6 and X100VI.

### The picture at rest

With no draft open, the GPU draws the committed stack itself in place of the CPU's frame (`Editor::gpu_at_rest`, `crates/luxforge-app/src/app/gpu_preview.rs`): after a commit, a history selection, a preset, an open, and a gesture's release. Every committed stack's job carries the stack's own plan from the source at its view (`GpuRest::view`, planned on the owner by `plan_rest`), and at every view its tiles at full resolution (`RestTiles`), reduced to the view at Fit and below 100% (`RestTiles::reduction`) and the histogram's alone elsewhere; the desktop holds both for the stack the job is for, and lets them go when a job plans none, so an earlier stack's plan is never drawn at rest.

- **The view plan.** At 100% and above it is the picture at rest: the region plan over the visible region's window, cut from the source at full scale, the stack processed first at full resolution. At Fit and below 100% it is drawn at once, over the source reduced to the view, the same programs as a drag's last tick over the same boundary, so a release changes nothing on screen; where the view draws the stack at its own size or larger it is the picture at rest there too. It carries the clipping overlay's marks while one is shown, converted again when the overlay changes. The surface draws it unheld (`gpu_hold` false, no gesture's tag), keeping its slot for the next gesture's first tick.
- **The tiles.** Where the view draws the output stage smaller than it is, the picture at rest is process-first, by the [recorded default](gpu-first.md#proposals-with-recorded-defaults): the stack at full resolution in tiles of the output stage, each tile the whole stack's plan over its rectangle from its own window of the source, every window [anchored](#at-100-and-above), reduced to the view's size by an area-weighted average of their linear light, the reduction the reference frame is held to (`crates/luxforge-ui/src/photo_surface/gpu_preview/rest.rs`). A tile is drawn a frame, each a fresh evaluation through a slot of the picture at rest's own, its codes decoded through the output's table, weighted by the reduction's coverage and added in a fixed order into an `f32` accumulator of the view's size; after the last, one pass quantizes it through the output's thresholds. The accumulator holds 16 bytes a view pixel, at most 8 MP (`REST_VIEW_PIXELS`), all charged to the GPU-preview budget.
  - *The side.* A tile's side is the longest of 2048, 1024, 512 and 256 px (`REST_TILE_SIDES`) whose tile in the middle of the stage, its window grown on every side, fits the rest's share by the plan's own figures and carries at most about 24 MP·links, the window's texels times the plan's spatial links (`REST_TILE_WORK`, an estimated 50 to 70 ms of the M4's GPU, so a gesture started meanwhile waits behind at most one tile); 256 px where none does. The figures are what the tile's slot charges, with no device (`rest_slot_bytes`, `crates/luxforge-core/src/render/gpu/preview.rs`): the boundary and an intermediate for each link before the last, a tail's intermediate, the output in its size bucket with its uniform, each link's kept planes and parameters and the scratch pool once, equal to the widget crate's `texture_charge` and `chain_charge`; and the light links' block planes and tile (`rest_light_bytes`). Only the words and blocks buffers, under a megabyte, are left out (`gpu_rest_a_tiles_charge_is_what_its_slot_allocates`, `gpu_rest_a_tiles_charge_is_the_slots_own_on_a_device`).
  - *The share* (`RestTiles::share`) is the 2 GiB budget less the source the surface holds (4 bytes a JPEG pixel, 12 a RAW's), the view plan's slot by the same figures and the accumulator with its rest output, at most 1.25 GiB (`REST_SHARE_MAX`).
  - *The order.* Tiles are drawn by their slot's shape, the window's size and the rectangle's: each shape's tiles together and row by row, the shapes in the order a row-by-row walk meets them, so the slot is refitted once a shape rather than wherever an edge tile falls between two of the middle's. The codes, the counts and the picture reduced from them are the same at every side and in either order (`gpu_rest_the_picture_at_rest_is_the_same_at_every_side_and_order`).
  - *Planned sizes,* in the evidence window's Fit bounds (`gpu_rest_the_measured_stacks_are_tiled_within_the_rests_share`): the 60 MP drag stack (Detail, the full Basic layer, Presence's three fields at +100) is 15 tiles of 2048 px, its slot 1,101 MB; on a 60 MP RAW, whose source takes 723 MB, the same stack's share is 1,085 MB and it is 70 tiles of 1024 px, 628 MB; the Air 2S's three-segment stack 24 tiles of 1024 px, 1,015 MB, a 2048 px tile's 1,412 MB passing the share; Detail alone at 24 MP 6 tiles of 2048 px. The Air 2S's masked stack (Detail, a global Presence and three masked Presence layers) stays at 330 tiles of 256 px: its windows sum every spatial layer's halo and lead, so even a 256 px tile's slot takes 1,117 MB and 34 MP·links.

  - *Staged sweeps.* Beside the chained tiles every picture at rest carries the same stack planned in staged sweeps, or why it is drawn chained (`RestTiles::staging`, `GpuSweeps`, `crates/luxforge-core/src/render/gpu/sweeps.rs`), and the photo surface draws it so (`GpuRest::stages`); an export's stream is planned so by `plan_stream_sweeps` within the tile worker's budget, its tiles row by row, and the tile worker draws it so ([below](#qualifying-a-program)). The plan's spatial operations are grouped in order, a sweep ending before one only where the halos and leads already in it pass 64 px (`SWEEP_SPLIT_REACH`), so Detail rides with the Presence layer after it and a stack whose one large reach is its last link, the 60 MP drag stack among them, is one sweep and stays chained. Every sweep but the last writes its tiles' rectangles of its last link's output into a stage texture of the content stage in the boundary's format, covering the bounding box of what the next sweep reads; at most two are held, used in turn (`SWEEP_STAGE_TEXTURES`). Each sweep's tiles read the window its own links need of the stage before them (`WindowPlan::gpu_window_between`), anchored to its own links, and take the longest side whose middle tile's slot and light links fit the rest's share beside the stage textures and carry at most about 24 MP·links; a sweep no side fits leaves the stack chained, naming what it needs (`Chained::OverBudget`). The Air 2S's masked stack plans four sweeps — Detail with the global Presence in 24 tiles of 1024 px, then each masked Presence layer in 6 of 2048 px — over two stage textures of 319.4 MB, 42 tiles where it is chained in 330; the three-segment stack two, in one texture (`gpu_rest_the_measured_stacks_are_planned_in_staged_sweeps`).
  - *Drawing the sweeps.* The desktop converts each sweep's tiles as it converts chained tiles (`sweep_plan_over`, `crates/luxforge-app/src/app/gpu_plan.rs`): a tile runs the content operations when its sweep is the first, its sweep's spatial operations with the colour operations after them, and the geometry tail and output operations when it is the last; a sweep before the last ends in an identity tail, so the slot's tail intermediate holds its last link's output over the window, unclamped, as a chain's intermediate does; and a sweep runs only the light links its operations read. The surface creates the stage textures when it starts the picture (`StageHolder`, `crates/luxforge-ui/src/photo_surface/gpu_preview/staged.rs`), each charged first, and draws the chained tiles instead where they do not fit. It draws every sweep's tiles in turn, a tile a frame: a first sweep's boundary is cut from the source; a later sweep's slot is fitted for the tile (`PhotoPipeline::fit`) and its window copied out of the stage texture the sweep before it wrote (`GpuBoundary::staged`); a sweep before the last copies its tile's rectangle of the tail intermediate into its stage texture; the last sweep's tiles are reduced and counted as chained tiles are. The stage textures retire once the last tile is in, or with the picture at rest when a newer one replaces it. Staged and chained draw the same codes and counts, byte for byte, at full resolution and reduced, over content operations, masked Presence, Dehaze's light, three sweeps through both textures and a lens tail on both paths (`gpu_rest_staged_sweeps_draw_the_chained_tiles_codes`), and over the Air 2S's masked stack (`gpu_rest_the_air_2s_masked_stack_staged_is_chained`), where the staged tiles' evaluations ran 66 links over 133.7 MP of windows, 183.8 MP·links, against 1,650 links over 1,533.8 MP, 7,669.1 MP·links, chained (`RestFigures::sweeps` and its evaluation figures). No corpus recipe is staged: none has a spatial layer after one reaching past 64 px.

  Tiles are handed as the picture only while no draft is open and no crop draft, comparison or clipping overlay is shown, the overlay's marks being the view plan's. Every tile's own codes are counted into the histogram and clipping counts as it is drawn; where the picture is not drawn from the tiles — at 100% and above, where the view draws the stage at its own size, or while the clipping overlay is shown — and the GPU presents the stack, the same tiles are handed, under a version of their own, for their counts alone, through a percentage view's surface too, and draw nothing ([histogram and clipping contract](basic-and-histogram.md#histogram-and-clipping-contract)).
- **A layer prefix.** A truncated job asked for with the GPU (`PreviewRequest::layers` with `gpu`), a crop draft's input stage, carries the prefix's picture at rest planned over the prefix's own evaluation: its view plan and its tiles, reduced to the view where the view draws the stage smaller than it is, or the reason the GPU cannot draw the prefix in both (`a_crop_drafts_input_stage_is_planned_as_its_prefixs_picture_at_rest`). The desktop asks for it at Fit and below 100%, and the photo surface draws it under the crop frame in place of the stage's stand-in frame, turned and dimmed ([instant previews](instant-preview.md)); the reference draws the stage where the GPU cannot.
- **No CPU render.** A committed job whose stack the GPU draws at rest is not queued (`Editor::present_on_gpu`, `crates/luxforge-app/src/app/gpu_counts.rs`): the preview queue is cancelled, raising its floor, and the presentation records the content, its stage and its entry under that generation, the photograph's frame on the presenter — an earlier frame of the same photograph — kept as the surface's base, which the view plan and then the tiles are drawn over. `preview_displayed` says `path` `gpu`, and the request ends there. Its tiles' counts, read back a frame or two after the last tile, are its report, plotted and handed to the owner's store; a capture at rest waits for them (`state.histogram.gpu_counts_pending`). A zoom or a pan at 100% and above is planned again at rest at once, with nothing rendered on the CPU, and a view of the same stack keeps the counts in hand; until the new region's plan lands the region held is drawn, the rest of the view the canvas background, or, from Fit or below 100%, the whole frame's plan scaled to the view, never the earlier frame under it; a capture there waits for the region's plan.
- **The reference.** The job is queued as before — the reference renderer's exact frame alone, which its histogram and clipping counts are reduced from, reduced to the view where the view draws the stage smaller than it is (`ExactOutcome::display`, `render::reduce_to_view`) — wherever the GPU cannot present the stack (`Editor::gpu_presents`): before the photo surface has checked its stage, with the stage refused or lost, an open whose picture at rest's programs are not compiled yet (a cold cache, the launch's first photograph among them), tiles not planned (a pixel-stage layer, `spatial-unit`, `budget-exceeded`), a comparison or a crop draft's input stage in flight. Content the surface could not draw or count once the GPU presented it — a tile's fallback, failed counts, a lost device — is refused (`gpu_presented_refused`) and asked for again from the reference. Content whose view plan's programs are still compiling keeps the frame on screen, marked rendering, while the compile lasts less than the half-second `compiling` threshold, the one the status bar's notice uses: the compile thread's wake when the compile ends, or the desktop's one wake at the threshold (`Editor::gpu_compile_deadline`), brings the next look, and only a compile that outlasts it is refused, named `compiling`, and rendered by the reference. An exact frame on screen at Fit is reduced again on the worker for a new view with no render. No render bar is drawn over a GPU frame.
- **A hidden window.** The picture at rest is drawn on the frame clock, and a minimized or hidden window keeps that clock on macOS: in a background launch on the M4, a commit made through the live API session while the window was natively minimized, during an evidence `observe` step that holds no capture and no frame subscription, had its picture at rest drawn and its counts adopted as its report 142 ms after the commit, and every `analysis.request` from 0.5 s after it answered `ready` with the committed entry; the same held with the window ordered out (`hide_window`). So `analysis.request` never waits on visibility, and nothing answers from the reference while hidden (the owner's decision of 2026-10-06 asks that only if frames stop). Windows and Linux are not checked.
- **The dissolve.** The view plan is on screen while the tiles are drawn; the frame the last tile comes in starts the picture at rest's own dissolve over it, 150 ms in linear light as the [settle dissolve](#settle-and-the-dissolve) runs, after which the rest output alone is the photograph. The widget asks for frames while tiles remain and through the dissolve, and for none after it.
- **History and Compare.** A history selection, Return to current and a preset are committed jobs like any other, so the entry shown is drawn at rest by the GPU from the job's plans, an owner hop after the selection. Compare draws both sides on the GPU at Fit and below 100%: when it begins, the GPU picture of the stack on screen — its view plan and its picture at rest in tiles — is retained and drawn as the After side on Compare's own surface, in place of the retained After frame, which stays its fallback; the Before side, the original entry, is drawn at rest by its own committed job's plans. When Compare ends, the retained picture is handed back to the photograph at once, so the stack is the GPU's in the next frame, before its own job plans it again (`gpu_compare_retained`, `gpu_compare_restored`; `state.surface.gpu.compare.picture`). At 100% and above the Before side is its region plan and the After side the retained exact frame. Over a stack the GPU presented with no CPU frame ([the picture at rest](#the-picture-at-rest)) there is no such frame, and the photograph under the GPU's picture is an earlier stack's: Compare asks the reference renderer for the stack's frame and begins when it lands (`GpuPreviews::compare_waits`, `Editor::frame_ready`), the status bar reading `Rendering the photograph to compare…`, so the After side is never another stack's pixels.
- **Labels and evidence.** The status bar reads "GPU render · N ms", N the interface thread's time over the frames that drew the tiles, or the view plan's frame, and "Rendering…" while a held-back view plan's tiles are still to land. While the view plan's programs are still compiling, the photograph is the reference frame, its render slot naming it, and the notice beside it says "Preparing GPU render" ([warming at launch and open](#warming-at-launch-and-open)). `state.surface.gpu.picture` names the photograph's source — `rest`, `view`, `gesture` or `reference` — beside `rest` (version, tiles drawn, done, the interface time), `drawn_rest` and `rest_dissolve`; `preview_displayed` names whose picture of its content the person sees (`picture`: `gpu` or `reference`), and the events `gpu_at_rest`, `gpu_rest` and `gpu_rest_drawn` record the plans and the first draw of each picture at rest. The state's `rest.attribution` and `gpu_rest_drawn`'s `attribution` say what its tiles did, for attributing a slow picture at rest: their evaluations summed (`refits`, `rebinds`, `links_run`, `spatial_passes`, `lights_encoded`, `lights_restored`, `window_texels`, and `link_texels`, the window's texels times the links that ran), the frames a tile waited for a retirement (`retirement_waits`), and each tile's GPU span from its preparation to when the queue reported it done, summed and largest, an upper bound with the wait for the next submit in it, as the queue has reported them (`gpu_tiles_reported`, `gpu_span_ms`, `gpu_span_max_ms`): the device Iced creates has no timestamp queries. A gesture's frame carries the same evaluation figures in `surface_frame_drawn`'s `evaluation` and the state's `gpu_evaluation`. A capture at rest waits for the picture at rest's last tile and its dissolve, or for the view plan's frame.
- **Where the CPU frame is still the photograph.** A stack the GPU cannot plan at rest, and any view while the GPU stage is refused, draw the reference renderer's frame, labelled as before. At 100% and above, a pan past the region the view plan was planned for draws the view plan's region until the pan's re-plan, asked for at once, lands; where the reference draws the stack its whole frame holds every view.
- **Measured.** At 33% on the `gpu-preview-zoom` quadrant fixture, a Basic exposure drag's last frame, over the source reduced first, differs from the process-first picture at rest by a mean ΔE00 of 0.36, a worst block of 1.82 and a p99 of 8.0, past the pointwise limit, which the dissolve hides; at 50% it is within it. On the corpus the gate reports a drag's frame past the limits of the picture at rest on 462 of 783 cells at Fit, 33% and 50%, and within them on every cell at 100%, where the two are one plan ([performance](../specs/performance.md#gpu-qualification-against-the-reference)).

### Warming at launch and open

The first picture never waits on a cold shader cache: the program set compiles in the background, the picture on screen's programs first, and the reference renderer's frame, labelled, is the photograph until the GPU's replaces it (`crates/luxforge-app/src/app/gpu_warm.rs`, [GPU-first](gpu-first.md), stage 5).

- **Order.** A frame asks for its own sequences — the picture at rest's view plan and its tiles — before it hands the compile thread the warm list, so the thread takes them first; then the warm list's open stack's part, a drag of each layer the stack holds; then the rest of the program set, the first drag of every module it does not hold, Detail's and Presence's among them ([pipelines](#where-the-code-lives)). A new list lets go of the warmed sequences an older one left waiting and queues its own in its order, so a photograph opened during another's warm-up starts its own at once. A frame's sequence still goes ahead of every warmed one, and waits at most for the one compile the thread is running (`the_picture_on_screen_compiles_first_then_the_open_stack_then_the_rest`, `the_open_stacks_drags_warm_before_the_first_drags_of_the_rest`).
- **When.** At every open and every committed stack, as its job's warm list arrives. The launch's first warm-up is its first photograph's: the GPU stage, its device and its compile thread exist only once a photograph's surface is drawn, so nothing compiles before ([proposals](gpu-first.md#proposals-with-recorded-defaults)). A sequence already compiled costs nothing, so after the first warm-up a later one compiles only what the new stack adds.
- **An open's first picture.** By the owner's decision of 2026-10-06, an open whose picture at rest's programs — its view plan's links and light links, and its tiles' — are already compiled in the stage's cache (`luxforge_ui::photo_surface::gpu_programs_ready`, asked without queuing anything) is presented by the GPU alone, as a commit is (`Editor::gpu_presents`, `crates/luxforge-app/src/app/gpu_counts.rs`): no reference job is queued, another photograph's frame is taken off, and the GPU's view plan is drawn as soon as the source is on the GPU, then its tiles, whose counts are its report. Until the view plan is drawn the opening photograph's cached preview stays on screen, or, with none, an empty frame placed at its stage (`preview_displayed` names `opened`). On a cold cache — the launch's first photograph, whose surface creates the stage, or a stack whose programs nothing has compiled — and without a GPU, the reference renders the open's first picture, labelled as below (`a_warm_open_is_presented_by_the_gpu_with_no_reference_job`, `a_cold_open_and_an_open_without_a_gpu_draw_the_reference_first`). On the M4, three opens of plain stacks in one background launch, one run each and not a timing claim: the second and third, warm, drew the GPU's view plan 108 and 191 ms after `open_requested` (24 and 60 MP) and their pictures at rest at 164 and 312 ms, where the same opens through the reference drew its frame at 98 and 174 ms and the pictures at rest at 152 and 348 ms; the view plan waits for the source's upload, which a plain stack's reference render does not.
- **The label.** While the photograph at rest is the reference frame because the picture at rest's programs are still compiling — an open's first picture on a cold cache, or a stack the GPU presented whose compile outlasted half a second — the status bar's render slot names that frame and the notice beside it reads "Preparing GPU render", said at once and gone with the GPU frame that replaces it; a stack the GPU presented that waits on a shorter compile says nothing but "Rendering…" (`Editor::gpu_rest_compiling`, [the classes](#the-fallback-notices-classes)); `state.surface.gpu.picture` is `reference`, then `view` or `rest`, and `rest_compiling` says why. A gesture's own `compiling` keeps its half-second rule.
- **Recorded.** The compile thread times each warm-up, from a warm list handed to an idle thread until its queue has drained, frames' sequences included, and when the newest list's open stack's part had compiled (`WarmUpFigures`), and wakes the surface once at its start and once at its end; no timer or poll. The desktop lists a running warm-up on the owner's activity board as `gpu.warm`, "Preparing GPU renderer", which the [Performance section](performance-panel.md) shows once it has run half a second and `activity.list` reports, ends it with the warm-up, and records each one once as the `gpu_warm_up` event, `{period, version, sequences, open_sequences, open_ms, ms, first}`, beside `state.surface.gpu.warm_up`. An idle check's settle lasts while a warm-up runs, since its end wakes the editor.
- **What still compiles.** A drag draws its layer in its GPU shape, every unit present, which the warm list holds; the picture at rest draws the committed stack in the CPU's shape, only the units its values need. So a release that changes a layer's set of units — a field moved off neutral, or back — commits a stack whose view plan holds a link nothing has compiled yet, and the photograph is the reference frame, labelled, for that one compile: 7 ms after the `gpu-preview` scenario's first release on a warm cache, and 119 to 261 ms after its releases on a bundle's empty cache. A release that keeps the units, and every drag, waits on nothing ([proposals](gpu-first.md#proposals-with-recorded-defaults)).
- **Measured.** On the M4 with a background bundle identifier of its own, whose Metal cache starts empty, the `gpu-preview` scenario's first warm-up compiled its 6 sequences in 1.5 s with the picture at rest's own in 41 ms, the reference frame on screen for 38 ms before the GPU's replaced it; a first run of the new build took 4.4 s for 11 sequences, two warm lists running together ([performance](../specs/performance.md#warming-at-launch-and-open)). On a warm cache the same set takes about 37 ms.

### Settle and the dissolve

Two dissolves run, each over 150 ms in linear light. At rest the picture at rest in tiles dissolves in over the view plan's frame ([the picture at rest](#the-picture-at-rest)); a release, whose committed stack's view plan takes the drag's frame's place over the same boundary, needs no other. And where a CPU frame replaces a GPU frame of the same content — the reference's frame of a tick of the open draft the GPU could not draw, or the commit of a stack the GPU cannot draw at rest — the surface dissolves from the GPU frame to it, as below. A pause in a drag the GPU draws settles nothing on the CPU.

**The desktop** (`crates/luxforge-app/src/app/gpu_settle.rs`) follows the GPU frame on screen after every message: the gesture tick the surface drew last, tagged with its draft revision and boundary, while its plan is drawn in place of the CPU frame. When a newer CPU frame replaces it, at any zoom with no comparison on screen, the desktop hands the surface a dissolve from that revision to the new frame — at Fit and below 100% the photograph's frame, at 100% or more the frame the view draws its current content from, its whole frame or else its region — if the new frame shows the GPU frame's content or newer:

- **Held.** A CPU frame of the open draft at the GPU frame's revision or a newer one: the reference's frame of a tick the GPU did not draw. The plan is held behind it for the next tick, and the dissolve runs behind the held plan.
- **Committed.** The frame of the entry the draft committed, once the draft has ended and the entry on screen is no longer the one the GPU frame was drawn over, where the GPU draws no picture of the committed stack at rest; where it does, its plan is drawn in place of the CPU frame and nothing replaces the GPU frame.
- **Never older content.** A cancelled draft shows the entry it was drawn over again, and a CPU frame of an older revision is older content: each replaces the GPU frame with no dissolve (`gpu_settle_swapped`).
- **Cancelled by any input.** A newer tick of the draft, or another gesture beginning, cancels a running dissolve (`gpu_dissolve_cancelled`, why `input`), and the surface draws what that input brings. So does a change to what is drawn over the photograph or of the view: a clipping overlay turned on or off, a change of zoom, a pan at a percentage zoom or a comparison (why `view`), so the CPU frame's own overlay is drawn at once rather than after the dissolve. A newer CPU frame of the same content, such as the same exact frame reduced again for the view, keeps the dissolve running to it (`gpu_dissolve_retargeted`); other content cuts it (`gpu_dissolve_cut`). It ends after its 150 ms, at the next message (`gpu_dissolve_ended`); the surface's own wake at its end is that message.
- **Evidence.** Each start records the GPU frame's revision (`from`), the CPU frame's version (`to`), its boundary and its case (`gpu_dissolve_started`); `state.surface.gpu.settle` reports the running dissolve, how many began and were cancelled, and the last decision. A change of what is drawn over the photograph is recorded when it takes effect (`gpu_settle_view`).

**The surface** (`crates/luxforge-ui/src/photo_surface/gpu_preview/dissolve.rs`): `PhotoSurface::dissolve` takes a `Dissolve`, the GPU frame it starts from as the caller identifies it (a draft revision), the version of the CPU frame it ends on, and when it began.

- **Linear light.** For the 150 ms the surface keeps its GPU-preview slot, draws the slot's output, and lays the CPU frame over it through the photograph's own pipeline, placement and filter — at a percentage zoom the region output at its rectangle, under the view's whole frame — with the dissolve's share (the time since it began over 150 ms) as its opacity. The stage runs only on an sRGB-typed target, where the hardware blends in linear light, so the picture is `(1 − t)·gpu + t·cpu` of the linear values. Read back on the M4, a mix at one half of linear 0 and 1 draws code 188, where a gamma-space blend would draw 128. Once the share reaches one the frame is the CPU's alone and the slot is released.
- **Redraws are requested only while a dissolve runs**, so an idle editor stays asleep (performance rule 8). The widget asks for the next frame from its own update, at each redraw's own time, and the redraw that finds the dissolve ended asks for nothing. No timer, subscription or desktop update runs for it; its start and its end each wake the desktop once, as a change of drawing path does.
- **An input during a dissolve cancels it** and draws the next GPU frame: a plan drawn beside a dissolve cancels it, and the plan is evaluated in the same slot. A plan held behind the CPU frame keeps it: the held plan's unchanged words leave the slot's output as the GPU frame last shown.
- **Nothing to dissolve from.** When the last frame drawn was the CPU's, or the stage fell back, the CPU frame is drawn alone. A dissolve into a frame not yet in the photograph's texture, which a deferred upload can leave, is not drawn either, and the slot is released. A whole frame's output dissolves only under a whole-frame photograph and a region's only under the percentage view of its own stage.
- **Evidence and label.** Each captured frame's `state.surface.gpu.dissolve` records the dissolve the draw drew: `from`, `to`, the `gpu_boundary` under it and its `progress`. During a dissolve `drawing_path` is `cpu`, the CPU frame over the GPU one, and `drawn_gpu_boundary` names the GPU output under it. The status bar's render slot names the CPU frame from the dissolve's first frame, as it does for any CPU frame.

### Labels and overlays during motion

- **Status.** While the surface draws the GPU stage's output for a gesture's plan, the status bar's render slot reads "GPU preview · N ms", and while it draws the committed stack at rest — its view plan, or its picture at rest in tiles ([the picture at rest](#the-picture-at-rest)) — "GPU render · N ms", in place of "Rendering…" or "Rendering… N%" while a CPU render still runs; the bar over the photograph is unaffected. A gesture drawn on the GPU at any zoom shows the first, as does the evidence run's GPU identity hook. The histogram keeps its existing updating state.
  - **What N is.** N is the interface thread's time to prepare the GPU frame on screen in `prepare`: fitting the slot, writing the words and blocks, encoding and submitting the passes; for a picture at rest in tiles, that time summed over the frames that drew its tiles and quantized them (`state.surface.gpu.rest.prepare_ms`). The GPU's own execution is not in it, and nothing waits for or reads back the GPU. Evidence records it as `state.surface.gpu.gpu_preview_frame_us` and the status bar's `gpu_ms`. Only a draw knows which path drew it, so the label describes the frame the surface drew last: a change of drawing path wakes the desktop, and the update that wake runs names the new path.
  - **Why not GPU timestamps.** Iced 0.14's compositor requests its device with no optional features, and no setting changes that, so the device the surface receives has no timestamp queries on any adapter.
  - **Why not the queue's completion callback.** wgpu 27 runs a `Queue::on_submitted_work_done` callback from the maintenance of the first `queue.submit`, `device.poll` or `instance.poll_all` that finds the work complete: wgpu-core's `Queue::submit` ends with a nonblocking `Device::maintain`, and on Metal the fence's completed value is set by each command buffer's completion handler. Iced submits every frame it draws, so the callback needs no poll or timer. But the figure it gives runs to the submit that learns of the completion, not to the completion. On the M4 Pro (Metal), for a 2400 × 1600 boundary over 60 frames, a frame submit made straight after the draw found the pass done in 0 to 2 of 60 frames, and a test-only blocking wait, which itself wakes late, saw the GPU done 1.6 to 2.1 ms p50 after the start for the identity program and 3.4 to 4.2 ms for a chain of transcendental-heavy programs, against 0.08 to 0.29 ms p50 of preparation (`gpu_frame_completion_is_measured`, four runs on a host at load average 25: diagnostics, not baselines). In the editor, the `gpu-identity` scenario's 320 × 480 identity frames, microseconds of GPU work, reported completion 2.9 ms and 10.2 ms after the start: whenever the next frame was submitted. As a label it would mostly read the frame cadence, so the surface keeps it as evidence only, `state.surface.gpu.gpu_preview_done_us` (the newest pass the queue has reported complete, read live), an upper bound on when the GPU finished.
- **Fallback notice.** While the open gesture's latest tick was not drawn on the GPU for a reason that lasts, the status bar says why: a short muted phrase right after the render slot, with a tooltip of one sentence (`StatusBarModel::fallback`, `crates/luxforge-app/src/state/status.rs`). While the reference renderer draws every frame, because the GPU stage cannot run on this graphics device, was refused by the launch or lost its device, the same place says so, at rest as well as during a gesture. Nothing is drawn on the photograph. The place, timing and wording are recorded defaults the owner can revise; the wording lives in one table, `CLASSES`, of that file.
  - **Classes.** Each reason belongs to one class, with one phrase and one tooltip ([the table](#the-fallback-notices-classes)). A code in none of them says nothing: the reasons that pass within a tick or two or a job (`boundary-pending`, a lens warp's grid still computed; `source-uploading`; `source-missing`; `surface-pending`), and the two the table does not name, `unchanged` (a draft that changes no layer yet) and `unplannable` (planning failed, which the reference answers in its own way).
  - **The layer.** The stack's phrase names the layer the plan's reason names, by its label in the recipe list, the title of its module: `Not on the GPU: Pixel` over a developer's pixel replacement. The core's answer carries the label (`GpuPreview::layer`), read from the stack the plan was made from, which holds the neutral layer a drafted layer's first commit would add, so an index never points at a row the committed list lacks; the drag keeps it beside the reason. A reason that names no layer (`warp-grid`, `boundary-size`, `region-outside`, and `position-range`, whose layer the converter names by index only) reads `Not on the GPU`, with a tooltip that says the stack.
  - **The reference renderer.** Its class is read from the session's `renderer` (`renderer_notice`), the value every API client reads in `session.state`, never from a tick: it is said while the session names the reference for `no-adapter` or `device-lost`, from the first frame of a launch that refused the stage and from the update that adopts the desktop's report of a lost device, at rest and during a gesture, before any reason a tick gives. A tick that names either code says nothing of its own; `surface-pending`, before the surface has checked its stage, says nothing, and neither does a GPU renderer or an owner's reference with no reason. Only a GPU frame on screen would displace it, and a stage that cannot draw shows none.
  - **When.** A gesture's reason is said from the first tick whose reason lasts, until the next GPU frame or the end of the gesture's settle. A tick drawn on the GPU clears it, and so does a GPU frame on screen: the render slot names that frame, so the notice and `GPU preview · N ms` never share the bar, and the surface's change of drawing path wakes the desktop so that update derives both. The settle ends with the gesture's drag: the release commits on the CPU, and once the frame replacing the drafted one is presented, or nothing more is coming, `after_message` lets the drag go (`gpu_preview.rs`, [settle and the dissolve](#settle-and-the-dissolve)). The hook is the drag's rather than the dissolve's, because a gesture drawn on the CPU throughout has no GPU frame to dissolve from and every gesture's drag ends there.
  - **`compiling`** says its phrase only once it has lasted half a second (`COMPILING_AFTER`). The drag records when its ticks first named it and how long that had lasted at each tick, so a tick decides what is said and the clock never does: a drag that holds still keeps what it last said, whatever time passes, and the idle editor stays asleep. There is no timer, poll or subscription for it (performance rule 8). Any tick that names another reason ends the run, and a later compile starts its half second again.
  - **Evidence.** Each captured frame records the notice as `{phrase, tooltip}`, or `null` when it says nothing, as `state.status_bar.fallback` beside `render` and as `state.surface.gpu.fallback_notice` beside `plan_fallback`, so a frame's reason and its notice are read together. The headless API draws no GPU preview and has nothing to report.
- **Clipping.** The CPU's clipping overlay is a cell grid derived from the CPU frame on screen, so over a GPU frame it would mark another frame's pixels. While a clipping overlay is shown, a GPU tick's plan, and the warm sequences, end with the surface's clipping marks (`GpuStep::Clipping`, `crates/luxforge-ui/src/photo_surface/gpu_preview/clipping.rs`): in the last pass, after every colour and geometry step, a pixel with a channel the CPU's quantizer takes to code 0 (a NaN included) or 255 — by the thresholds the core's plan names — is laid over with the overlay's own colour for its class at its own opacity, the photograph's codes and the colour decoded and blended in linear light as the surface composites the CPU overlay. Read back on the M4 within a code of the reference's composite for every class combination. While the GPU output is drawn in place of the photograph, the CPU frame's overlay is not drawn. The marks are approximate, per pixel of the stage the plan draws where the CPU overlay ORs each display cell, and evidence says so (`state.surface.gpu.clipping_marks`, with `approximate: true`). The whole-image counts during a drag are the displayed frame's, marked updating; at rest the view plan keeps the marks where the GPU presents the stack, and the reference frame's own overlay replaces them where it does not. A clipping toggle during a drag reaches the plan with the next tick.
- **Mask tint.** During a GPU mask drag, the tint is the coverage worker's grid as on the CPU path: each tick asks for it, and in the `gpu-preview` scenario it lands about 8 ms after the tick, so it follows the drag at the worker's pace, and it stays authoritative throughout. It is not drawn from the program's coverage, which would be disproportionate: a tint is a function of the mask's geometry rather than of the frame's pixels, so the worker's grid is already the GPU frame's own tint, where clipping's was another frame's. Drawing it on the GPU would carry the selected mask's coverage from the content pass through the geometry tail as a second channel and compose it in each overlay mode, and the selected mask is often in no plan at all: one with no adjustment bound to it, or bound to a layer before the boundary.

### The gate

`Editor::gpu_preview_allowed` and `Editor::gpu_plan` (`crates/luxforge-app/src/app/gpu_settle.rs`) are the desktop's one gate. It refuses while the photo surface's GPU stage cannot draw at all, or the launch refused it, with the stage's reason (`no-adapter`, `device-lost`; `Editor::gpu_stage_refusal`, `crates/luxforge-app/src/app/renderer.rs`), and the status bar says the reference renderer draws. A refusal hands the surface no plan and no source, so the source retires and nothing is derived, and the next tick lets a drag's held boundary go, naming the reason, because nothing would draw from it. There is no preference: the GPU draws every frame it can (owner, 2026-10-05), and `--no-gpu-render` is the launch switch for a session the reference renderer draws.

The evidence snapshot reports which path drew each frame (`gpu` or `cpu`) and why a GPU-eligible gesture fell back, and the session state which renderer draws the picture (`renderer`, [GPU-first](gpu-first.md#the-contract)):

- a clipping overlay is shown
- the source is not held yet, or a lens warp's grid is still computed
- the sequence is still compiling
- no adapter
- the device was lost
- a shader failed to compile
- the budget would be exceeded
- a unit has no program, or its program is disabled
- a pixel-stage layer is in the stack

### Fallback and portability

- **A session the GPU does not draw at all drags on a CPU proxy** (owner, 2026-10-06, [decisions](../decisions.md#gpu-first-rendering)). While the stage cannot draw — no adapter, a launch with `--no-gpu-render` or a host with only a software adapter not adopted, which refuse it as `no-adapter`, or a lost device — every drag tick asks the reference for the drafted stack's display-size proxy (`PreviewIntent::Interactive`, `Editor::cpu_proxy_tick`) in place of a held frame or a whole frame: the drafted stack rendered against the source reduced to the view's bounds at Fit and below 100%, and to Fit's bounds at 100% and above, where the view draws it whole, magnified to the stage's box. A newer tick replaces the pending job and never stops the proxy in progress, so the queue's one active and one pending job bound it, latest winning. The frame is labelled `Approximate render · N ms` beside the status bar's `Reference renderer`, and `Moving preview · Detail approximate` over a restoration layer; it adopts no histogram. A pan holds no plan of its own: the proxy is a whole frame. The release draws the reference's frame of the committed stack at rest, sharp: its exact frame reduced to the view at Fit and below 100%, its exact frame at 100% and above. All of it lives in the core's `cpu_proxy` module, which the preview worker calls once per job (`CpuProxy::frame`), and the desktop's `app/cpu_proxy.rs`, which `app/motion.rs` and `app/preview.rs` call by name: the one place to remove it, which `cargo xtask check-repository` keeps apart (`cpu-proxy`). A session with a GPU never asks for a proxy: a tick its GPU does not draw holds its frame or has the reference draw whole frames ([a tick the GPU does not draw](#a-tick-the-gpu-does-not-draw)).
- Losing the device mid-gesture lets the held boundary go, and the next tick is the proxy, naming its reason; the desktop reports the reference renderer for `device-lost` to every session, and nothing waits for recovery.
- Every gesture works with no GPU path at all. `--no-gpu-render` refuses the stage for a launch, as on a machine whose adapter cannot run it, and the `no-gpu-render` scenario checks that every frame is the reference renderer's, named in the session and, at rest too, in the status bar, that a drag at Fit and at 100% draws its proxies, labelled approximate, and that each release draws the reference's sharp frame.
- **A host with only a software adapter.** wgpu's request ranks a software adapter (Mesa's lavapipe, WARP) last but never leaves it out, so Iced draws a host with no hardware adapter on it. Off macOS, which has no software Metal, the launch enumerates the renderer's backends before its window opens (`adapters::probe`, `adapters::choose`): a hardware adapter draws on the GPU; only a software one draws through the GPU stage on it once the software adapter is adopted (`adapters::SOFTWARE_ADAPTER_ADOPTED`, the owner's decision of 2026-10-05) or when the launch passes `--software-adapter`, and otherwise the launch refuses the stage as `--no-gpu-render` does, its session naming the reference for `no-adapter`, its tile worker answering `no-adapter` and its `launch_renderer` event `software-adapter-not-adopted`; no adapter at all refuses it too. A stage drawing on the software adapter is the session's GPU record with `software: true`, the status bar's "Software GPU preview" and "Software GPU render", and `software` in the evidence's adapter record. Measured on 2026-10-06, lavapipe is not adopted ([performance](../specs/performance.md#software-adapters)). A host offering a hardware adapter Iced cannot draw the window on, so that its request falls to the software one, is named a hardware host by the probe; that case is not handled.
- Linux CI runs the rendered scenarios, `gpu-identity`, `gpu-preview` and `no-gpu-render` among them, through Mesa's software Vulkan adapter (lavapipe), every launch passing `--software-adapter` and recording that adapter, and the reference tests with no adapter at all ([CI](../engineering/development.md#ci)); Windows CI is disabled. These are functional checks, never native performance or GPU evidence, which is the owner's M4.

### Bounds

- **One budget.** Every GPU-preview texture and buffer is charged to one 2 GiB budget, beside the photo-texture ceiling and reported with it: the boundary, intermediates, spatial accumulators, coordinate grids and storage blocks.
  - In practice that is each surface's one GPU-preview slot: the boundary, each link's intermediate, words, blocks and kept planes, a geometry tail's intermediate, the output and its placement uniform, the last link's words, blocks and kept planes, and the pool of scratch textures every link takes in turn, once (`chain_charge`, below), the budget shared by every surface. A slot lives as long as the held boundary, the stack's own between gestures included. Beside the slots the pipeline holds the photograph's source once, for every surface ([the GPU source](#the-gpu-source)), and a surface drawing a picture at rest holds that picture's own slot: its tile slot, an `f32` accumulator of 16 bytes a view pixel and its output ([the picture at rest](#the-picture-at-rest)). A plan whose planes would pass it names the budget and is drawn as a tick the GPU does not draw ([a tick the GPU does not draw](#a-tick-the-gpu-does-not-draw)), or at 100% and above from its reduced stage, the softer drag frame; a picture at rest's tile the budget holds only once what the tiles before it released has retired waits a frame for the retirement.
  - An allocation that would pass it is refused before anything is created.
  - A released, replaced or outgrown resource retires through the photo surface's retirement worker and stays charged until the GPU is done with it, so the in-use figure returns to zero once a released slot has retired.
  - Each captured frame's `state.surface.gpu` reports `gpu_preview_budget_bytes`, `gpu_preview_in_use_bytes`, `gpu_preview_scratch_bytes` (the part of it that is the slots' scratch pools), `gpu_preview_peak_bytes`, `gpu_preview_passes` and `gpu_preview_spatial_passes` (the compute passes the spatial steps have dispatched, which a tick that moves only an apply's gain leaves unchanged) beside the photo-texture figures, with `drawing_path` (`gpu` or `cpu`), `gpu_fallback` (its reason, and the figures that refused it) and `drawn_gpu_boundary`.
- **One charge.** A pure function of the widget crate, `chain_charge` (`crates/luxforge-ui/src/photo_surface/gpu_preview.rs`), computes what a chain holds from its steps and the boundary's size, origin and format, with no device: each link's intermediate before the last, 8 bytes a texel on a JPEG and 16 on a RAW; each link's kept planes and its passes' parameter slices; and the pool once. The surface, the qualification and the desktop charge a plan the figures built on it:
  - `slot_charge`, which adds the boundary, the output in its size bucket with its placement uniform, and the links' words and blocks buffers, and which the qualification reports (`Qualifier::charged_bytes`);
  - the live slot's bytes, which equal it (`gpu_preview_in_use_bytes`);
  - the desktop's `region_charge` (`crates/luxforge-app/src/app/gpu_preview.rs`), over the plan's steps converted with no boundary before one is derived, which adds what `slot_charge` adds but the buffers, 6 to 40 KB in the paint harness's layouts (`gpu_window_a_chained_masked_plan_is_held_to_the_slots_own_charge`).

### Spatial programs

Presence and Detail become compute programs over the boundary, run as the surface's spatial step. They use the CPU's operations, not its exact arithmetic. The GPU holds a whole proxy stage or region, so it has no tile halo to recompute.

- **Presence** (`modules/presence/presence.wgsl`, described pass for pass by `modules/presence/gpu.rs`): separable box means and minima, the self-guided and guided filters' coefficients and smoothing as finishes of the vertical pass, the 4× block reduction anchored at the stage origin and the bilinear upsample, the soft clip, and the atmospheric light. Every coefficient is a word holding the `f32` the CPU unit holds; the restated luminance constants are held to the CPU's bits through `naga`.
  - **Texture**: two self-guided smoothers of the encoded input at full resolution, four passes each. The fine one is held; the coarse one never is, since its last pass writes the band itself, into a one-channel plane of its own that no other pass writes, so the plane its apply reads has one writer ([planning](#where-the-code-lives)): four full-resolution planes (a pair of sums, the coefficients, the fine smoother and the band: 24 bytes a pixel), of which the apply keeps the band's 4.
  - **Clarity**: the encoded input reduced 4×, one self-guided smoother there, the apply upsampling it.
  - **Dehaze**: the atmospheric light, read from the light plane its light link writes ([the global estimate](#spatial-programs)), the colour reduced 4× beside its dark channel normalized by that light, the dark channel's box minimum, the raw transmission and its guide, the transmission's guided refinement, and the apply upsampling it within the floor. At an amount of 0, which only a drafted layer holds, the apply leaves its input alone before it reads a plane and a tick runs none of its passes, as for a Texture or Clarity gain of 0 ([a tick](#where-the-code-lives)); its light link still runs, so a drag across zero changes words alone.
- **Detail** (`modules/detail/detail.wgsl`, described pass for pass by `modules/detail/gpu.rs`): each unit's input in Oklab; separable smoothing in difference form, the horizontal pass then the vertical, every tap's offset and weight a word, so the same two kernels run the B3 à-trous levels at full resolution, the sampled Gaussians a unit compiled at a proxy's scale holds, and sharpening's blur and guide together; each wavelet level's band, its 3 × 3 energy and the soft shrinkage that energy gates, added to the change the levels before it accumulated; sharpening's coring, the guide's gradient-energy gate, the 3 × 3 extrema and the `tanh` limiter; and each apply's reconstruction from Oklab with the neutral snap. The restated Oklab matrices and the snap are held to the CPU's bits through `naga`.
  - **Noise reduction**: its Oklab input, lightness held less one half, then for each level the two smoothing passes and the shrinkage. Four half-precision `rgba16float` planes: the current and next levels' smoothing, swapped each level, and two that each level's horizontal pass and shrinkage take in turn, so the change accumulated so far is never overwritten before the next level reads it. The last level's change, which the apply reads, takes a plane of its own, so it has one writer: 40 bytes a pixel.
  - **Capture sharpening**: its Oklab lightness, the blur and guide of it smoothed together, and the cored, gated and limited change of lightness the apply reads, which takes a plane of its own so it has one writer. Its three planes of one or two channels before it take three that noise reduction leaves free, so the two units hold 48 bytes a pixel; alone, its planes are `r32float` and `rg32float`, 28 bytes a pixel.
  - Only the Oklab passes read their unit's input, and every level's shrinkage binds three planes, the first reading its smoothing twice in place of a change it has none of, so the 17 passes of both units are 6 pipelines. A drag changes words alone. The CPU omits a unit at zero, and the fourth level while Colour is zero; a drafted layer holds both units and every level, a zero threshold shrinking nothing and a zero gain sharpening nothing, so a strength crossing zero changes words alone too.

**Running sums are reseeded at each run.** A box mean's pass runs once for every 16 outputs along its axis: a direct sum seeds the run, then the entering value is added and the leaving one subtracted, in `f32`. Over part of a plane the runs start where the whole plane's start, every 16 texels from its first, so the texels a run writes are the whole pass's bit for bit, and the reach of a pass over a rectangle counts the 15 texels a run may start before its own ([where a pass runs](#where-the-code-lives)). The drift grows with the run, not the row, and measured against the CPU's `f64` running sums it is at most 1.1 × 10⁻⁶ of a unit for every run from 1 to 64 and radius from 1 to 38 ([qualifying](#qualifying-a-program)). A two-level prefix sum would need a plane for the prefix and two more passes for every box; reseeding needs neither, so it is the cheaper of the two, and the run is a word, measured rather than fixed in the kernel.

**The global estimate.** Dehaze's atmospheric light is computed every frame on the GPU from its whole input stage at full resolution, as the measurement of the per-frame light's input set it: no light from the stage reduced by 2 to 16 per side met the spatial limits on every cell, and the full-resolution one met them on all ([performance](../specs/performance.md#the-per-frame-lights-reduction-factor)). Lights taken over anything less than the whole stage missed by far more: the proxy's own light by up to a mean of 8.78, a 100% window's by 2.1 to 17.9, and the CPU moving frame's by up to 5.34. Every plan that reads a light carries its light link (`GpuPlan::lights`, planned by `gpu_lights` from the source over the whole content stage, `GpuLight`): the colour operations before the Dehaze layer, run per texel over the source and clamped where the byte path clamps, and Dehaze's light passes (`SpatialUnit::gpu_light`). The Dehaze unit reads its light from the slot's light plane its link writes and runs no light pass of its own (`SpatialUnit::gpu`). The surface runs a plan's light links before its chain in the same frame, the main slot's and every tile of the picture at rest alike (`PhotoPipeline::evaluate_lit`, `gpu_preview/light.rs`): each cuts the stage from the source the pipeline holds in tiles of 2048 px, or of the device's largest texture rounded down to 16, each in turn into one texture, reduces each tile's 16-pixel blocks into the whole stage's block plane at their own place, then one workgroup selects the light into the slot's light plane, one `rgba32float` texel the slot's pool holds whatever the boundary. A link whose content key, the source's version, the stage and its steps' words and blocks, is the light plane's already encodes nothing; a light that changes makes the slot evaluate the plan whole, every link reading it drawn again, wherever the tick's own change lies. The pipeline keeps the last eight lights its links wrote, one texel each under its content key (`LightCache`, `LIGHT_CACHE`): a slot refitted to another shape, as a picture at rest's tile of another window size refits it, starts with a new pool whose light planes hold nothing, and copies an unchanged light in from the kept texel instead of computing it again over the whole stage; a changed light is computed and kept, overwriting the least recently used once eight are held. The kept lights take 128 bytes at most, charged to the GPU-preview budget as scratch before each is created, and retire with the source every key names (`gpu_light_a_refit_slot_restores_its_unchanged_light`). Each block is one tile's and is summed in the order the whole stage sums it, so the light is the same bits however the stage is tiled: a 300 × 200 stage held in two textures each way at a 160 px limit lights as one texture does. On the M4 it is the CPU's preparation of the stage it reads within 2.9 × 10⁻⁶ (the existing test's bound, 2 × 10⁻⁵, gates it), and on the linear path the reference's own light within 1.9 × 10⁻⁶; on the byte path the source's cut is held in half floats, as every boundary of a JPEG is, which moves the light from the reference's by up to 1.4 × 10⁻⁴ (`gpu_light_tests`). The slot's frame of a Presence plan reading its light is the harness's frame with that light read back, bit for bit (`gpu_light_the_slot_runs_its_plans_light_links_before_its_chain`). A Dehaze layer behind a spatial layer, Detail or an earlier Presence layer, reads that layer's whole output, which only a sweep of the whole stage through it at full resolution gives; no slot runs that sweep yet, so its link carries a stand-in over the source with those layers left out (`GpuLight::stand_in`), which the slot computes in its place at every view, and the picture at rest of such a stack draws with it (five sharpen-stress cells under Dehaze −100 miss the limits with Detail left out). A link holds the stage's block plane (16 bytes a block, 3.8 MB at 60 MP) and a few kilobytes of words and parameters; a surface's links run one after another and cut their tiles into one tile texture between them (32 MiB of a JPEG's half floats or 64 MiB of a RAW's `f32` at most), so 16 links over a 60 MP RAW take 127.9 MB where a texture each took 1,134.6 MB. All of it is charged to the GPU-preview budget before it is created (`lights_charge`) and counted in a region's charge; each light plane takes 16 bytes of the slot's pool. The tile runner, which holds a window of the source for each tile it draws, computes each light its plan reads once for the source, before the tiles that read it: each of the link's tiles is cut from a window of the source of its own, uploaded, reduced into the block plane and let go in turn, and the light is read back and written into every tile's light plane (`light::read_light`). It is the slot's light over the whole source, bit for bit, over a 2300 × 2200 stage of four of the link's tiles on both paths (`gpu_light_a_tile_runner_computes_the_slots_light_a_window_at_a_time`, [the tile runner](#qualifying-a-program)).

### Plane sharing and precision

A spatial unit declares each plane by how many channels it holds and whether half precision holds them (`GpuPlaneFormat`): four channels at half precision (`Colour`, kept in `rgba16float`), one, two or four accumulators (`Scalar`, `Pair`, `Quad`, in `r32float`, `rg32float` and `rgba32float`), or one or two channels half precision holds (`HalfScalar`, `HalfPair`). `rgba16float` is the one half-precision format the surface's device stores to, since Iced's device has no optional features, so half precision saves memory only for three or four channels: a `HalfScalar` or `HalfPair` plane with a texture of its own takes `r32float` or `rg32float`, which costs no more.

- **Sharing.** Within an operation, a plane takes a plane of the same size that an earlier unit no longer needs, when that plane's format holds it: as many channels or more, at its precision or a finer one, the smallest such (`compose`). Every pass of the earlier unit has run before the later one's first, so a plane an apply reads may take one as well as a scratch plane; the apply then reads a plane of its own that only its unit's last writer writes, and the plane it took serves that unit's earlier passes as scratch (`single_writer`): an incremental tick runs the writer only where its output changes, and an earlier writer would overwrite the values the plane keeps ([incremental ticks](#where-the-code-lives)). A pass writes the format of the texture its plane takes, which the steps alone decide, so a sequence's pipelines serve every boundary.
- **Kept and scratch.** A plane is kept when an apply reads it: the link's frame pass reads it, and so does every later tick that changes only an apply's word or part of the input, since an incremental tick rewrites a kept plane only where its change reaches. Every other plane is scratch: only the link's own passes read it, within the tick that writes it, and a later tick reuses it only to skip a pass whose output is still current, which an incremental tick never does.
- **One pool across the links.** Each operation runs as a link of the chain, and a link holds at most one spatial step, since the chain splits before each. A link holds its kept planes, a texture each. Its scratch planes are textures of the slot's one pool (`spatial::Pool`), which every link takes in turn, the last link included.
  - **Classes.** A pool texture's class is the texture format the link's own layout gives the plane, and the plane's size: the boundary's, its `s × s` blocks or a fixed size. `Scalar` and `HalfScalar` planes share `r32float`'s classes, `Pair` and `HalfPair` planes `rg32float`'s. Every link of a slot shares the boundary's size and origin, so a class has one extent.
  - **The mapping.** A link's k-th scratch plane of a class, in its own plane order, is the pool's k-th texture of that class (`PlanesKey`), so a link's layout is its own steps' alone and the same in any chain, and its distinct scratch planes, alive together, are distinct textures. The pool holds, for each class, as many textures as the most scratch planes of that class any one link holds: a further link of a shape the chain already holds adds its kept planes and intermediate and no scratch.
  - **What never shares.** Kept planes: another link's passes would overwrite one beyond the rectangle this link rewrites, where an incremental tick keeps its values, and an apply-only drag reads it without running a pass, so no texture an apply reads is written by another link's pass. Nor do textures of different formats: each pass writes the format its own steps give its plane and the pool supplies a texture of exactly that class, so pass modules, compiled sequences, the warm list and the pipeline caches are each link's own, two masked layers of one shape share every pipeline, and no value is stored in a format the link alone would not use. Detail with noise reduction holds every plane in `rgba16float` and Presence holds `r32float`, `rg32float` and `rgba32float`, so Detail with noise reduction beside Presence shares nothing; sharpening alone keeps its planes in `r32float` and `rg32float`, which share with Presence's.
  - **Lifetime.** The pool is fitted before any link's planes, from every link of the plan, and follows the plan's need ([recorded default](#proposals-with-recorded-defaults)). A texture the plan needs beyond what the pool holds is charged, then created, and moves no binding; one past the need retires through the surface's retirement worker, charged until the GPU is done with it; a new boundary size or origin replaces every texture, as it replaces every link's planes. A refusal part-way through leaves what was created held and charged, the tick encodes nothing, and the next fit adds only what is still missing. Removing or replacing a texture bumps the pool's generation, and a link whose bind groups were built under another generation rebuilds them and forgets what its planes hold. The qualification session fits its pool and planes through the slot's own constructor (`Pool::fit`), so the two never lay out differently.
  - **Charged once.** The budget is charged each link's kept planes and the pool once (`chain_charge`), and so is the desktop's charge before a region's boundary exists (`region_charge`, [bounds](#bounds)).
  - **Alternatives considered.** One plane assignment over the whole plan, as the surface shared planes before the chain existed, would let a later link's kept plane take an earlier link's scratch, which that link's next run overwrites where incremental ticks keep its values, and one schedule for the slot would lose each link's own keys. Forgetting every pool texture whenever another link has run is simpler, but links that share no class, such as Detail with noise reduction and Presence, would run passes for nothing; the records forget only what another link wrote. Sharing across formats, Presence's half-precision-holdable planes taking Detail's `rgba16float` scratch, would save 12 bytes a pixel once a slot rather than a layer, change Presence's precision whenever Detail is in the chain ([performance](../specs/performance.md#plane-precision)) and make a link's pass modules depend on the links before it; it is not proposed. Aliasing memory between textures is not available: wgpu 27 has no placed resources or heaps, so a pool of real textures is the only form sharing can take.
- **Detail then Presence.** Detail's half-precision planes hold sharpening's scratch as well, each unit's apply reading a plane of its own: 48 bytes a pixel. Presence, a link of its own after Detail's, shares no texture class with it, so the pool holds both links' scratch side by side, Texture's planes in full precision since they take no half-precision texture: Texture 24 bytes a pixel and all three units about 28.6, Clarity's and Dehaze's planes on their 4× grids. The chain holds about 77 bytes a pixel of planes over its window, and the intermediate Detail's link writes, the boundary's size, between them. Texture's running sums stay `f32` in any case, since the vertical pass takes the variance from them and it cancels.
- **Writing a half.** Spatial passes that write half-precision planes, and the pass writing a chain's intermediate over a JPEG's `rgba16float` boundary, round to the nearest half, ties to even, before the write. The M4's own storage and render-target conversions round toward zero; the explicit rounding keeps Detail's chained smoothings unbiased (`a_half_planes_texels_are_the_nearest_half`), and each link's output too, which toward zero drew Detail then Presence darker by a signed ΔL\* of −0.012. The earlier `rgba16float` RAW-tail intermediate also lost extended linear range and darkened near-black pixels; the current `rgba32float` tail, and a chain's `rgba32float` intermediates over a RAW's boundary, preserve those values ([failed candidate and remaining qualification](../specs/performance.md#plane-precision)).
- **Offsets.** A half's step is finer the nearer zero, so Detail holds Oklab lightness less one half in every plane it smooths (`lf_detail_lightness_offset`) and Texture holds its fine smoother less one half (`lf_presence_fine_offset`). Every kernel that reads them takes differences, or adds the offset back, so no value they compute depends on it, and the offsets halve the error half precision adds.
- **What half precision costs.** Measured plane by plane on the synthetic units against the CPU (`gpu_detail_plane_precision_is_measured`): every Detail plane at half precision moves Detail's figures from a mean ΔE00 of 0.0001 to 0.027 to 0.035, worst block 0.33 and p99 0.89, with no bias, far inside the spatial limits. Texture's running sums at half precision would move Texture alone to a mean of 0.19, so they stay `f32` ([performance](../specs/performance.md#plane-precision)).

### Mipmapped minification

Before/After draws its After side at Fit from the GPU's retained picture at rest, and keeps the exact raster as that side where the GPU cannot draw it ([before and after](before-after.md#after-at-fit)), as the reference renderer's whole frame is drawn under its size wherever the GPU draws no picture. A full-resolution texture drawn at that size through the bilinear sampler with no mip levels aliased: on the zone plate it showed full-contrast replica rings and read 15 codes too dark beyond the display's Nyquist limit, the same failure the crop draft's input stage showed ([instant previews](instant-preview.md#a-crop-drafts-input-stage); [performance](../specs/performance.md#a-minified-after-side-at-fit)).

The photo surface now gives a full-resolution photograph drawn under half its size on both axes a chain of mip levels, and otherwise leaves its texture as it was. No arithmetic in the recipe changes, so no error limit applies.

- **Linear light.** Each level is the mean of the 2 × 2 texels above it, written by a render pass that reads the level above through an sRGB-typed view of the texture and writes the next level through the same, so the mean is taken in linear light whether the pipeline's textures are sRGB-typed or not. The sampler's trilinear filter reads between the two levels that bracket the drawn scale.
- **GPU only, never waited on.** The passes are encoded and submitted from `prepare` on the UI thread, once for each new frame written while it is drawn that small, with no readback and no wait.
- **Accounting.** The levels belong to the photo slot that holds them: a chain is a third more than its base, counted in the slot's bytes, the shared photo-slot budget and the retirement of the slot, and reported as `mip_resident_bytes` and `mip_generations`. A texture that has a chain keeps it until it is rebuilt; a texture drawn at its size has none. A chain's texture is cut to the frame's exact size, which for a 24 MP frame is smaller than the bucket a plain texture reserves.
- **When the chain does not fit.** The chain needs the photograph in one texture and its whole size within one full-allocation cap. A photograph the device holds in tiles (wider or taller than 8192 pixels) has none, because each tile's filtering apron would put a seam into every level. Before/After then draws the exact render reduced to the view, which it holds of that photograph, at Fit instead, so a 60 MP After has no full-size texture while compared at Fit.

## What is preserved

- Originals, recipes, history, drafts, commits and every mutation are untouched. The reference renderer's frames, analysis, reads, overlays and the reference renderer's export produce the same bytes as before, which the existing exact tests and fixture hashes prove.
- The owner thread plans in `O(layers)`, a picture at rest's tiles in `O(tiles × segments)`, and touches no pixel. The UI thread encodes GPU commands and copies at most 32 MiB of the source a frame into staging; it never renders on the CPU, waits on the GPU or reads a pixel back.
- Every GPU-preview allocation is bounded and released with its draft. The preview queue's one active and one pending job and its cancellation are unchanged; a view at 100% and above is planned at once, the GPU's region of it or the reference's whole frame, with no quiet interval.

### Qualifying a program

**The readback.** The photo surface's `qualification` module (`crates/luxforge-ui/src/photo_surface/gpu_preview/qualification.rs`) runs a plan through the stage's own assembled shader on a headless device and reads the output back: as `f32` from an `rgba32float` target, where a non-finite value cannot hide behind an encoding, or as the codes of the stage's own sRGB-typed format. It is built only with the crate's `qualification` feature, which only a `[dev-dependencies]` table may turn on (`check-repository`'s `gpu-qualification-only-in-tests`), so no build of the desktop reads a GPU pixel back but through the tile runner below, on its tile worker's own device.

**The tile runner** (`crates/luxforge-ui/src/photo_surface/gpu_preview/tiles.rs`) is production code, outside the `qualification` feature, and the desktop's one production readback: the tile worker below owns one, through which every export the desktop streams is drawn. It draws one tile of a stack as the picture at rest draws each tile — a fresh evaluation over a boundary cut on the GPU from a window of the source, through the stage's one compile, its chain and its pool — ending in the output quantizer's codes or the linear values before them, then copies the output out and submits once (`TileRunner::submit`); reading it back waits for that submission alone and maps the copy (`TileRunner::finish`), and at most two tiles are in flight (`TILES_IN_FLIGHT`), so the GPU draws one while its caller encodes the next or reads the one before back, all on its caller's thread; it starts no thread of its own. Between tiles it keeps the window of the source its last tile read, which a tile of the same window reads again, and the slot of its last tile's shape — the boundary, each link's intermediate and buffers, a tail's intermediate and the output — which a tile of that shape draws into again, each tile still a fresh evaluation, every texture written whole before it is read, its planes and pool its own. Its device is its own, with its own queue, on the adapter the window's renderer reports drawing with, found by backend and name and requested with Iced's own descriptor (`adapters::open`, after `iced_wgpu` 0.14.0's `window/compositor.rs`): an adapter the host does not offer under that backend and name is refused by name (`adapter-mismatch`) and never replaced, a launch that refused the stage opens nothing (`refused`), and a lost device answers `device-lost` from then on. Everything a tile creates — the window of the source, the slot, the output and its readback copy — is charged before it is created to a budget of its own, `GPU_TILE_BUDGET`, 2 GiB by the owner's decision of 2026-10-06, apart from the 2 GiB GPU-preview budget, beside what the tiles in flight hold: their readback copies, and their slot and any window let go since until the GPU is known to be done with them, which a tile waits for when only they pass the budget. A tile past it is refused (`tiles-budget`) having created nothing. For a stream drawn in staged sweeps it also holds the stream's stage textures (`TileRunner::hold_stages`, a `StageHolder` of `crates/luxforge-ui/src/photo_surface/gpu_preview/staged.rs`): each the content stage in the boundary's format, at most two textures across and two down, charged to the same budget before any is created and released when the stream ends however it ends (`TileRunner::release_stages`). A tile of a sweep before the last, converted as the picture at rest's are (`sweep_plan_over`), copies its rectangle of its last link's output, unclamped, which the sweep's identity tail leaves in the slot's tail intermediate, into a stage texture and is finished without a readback (`TileRunner::submit_to` with `TileOutput::Stage`, `TileRunner::finish_stage`); a tile of a later sweep copies its boundary out of the stage texture the sweep before wrote, texel for texel, in place of cutting it from a window of the source (`TileInput::Stage`). By its own charge, an estimate, a 2048-pixel tile in the interior of a 60-megapixel RAW through Detail and all three Presence fields is charged 1,330.8 MB, 1,576 MB with its band's whole window of the source, within the budget less a read's reserve, and a 1024-pixel one 622.6 MB. Between tiles it holds that window and slot, at most two spare readback copies, at most 64 compiled sequences (`TILE_PIPELINE_CACHE`) and the last eight lights it computed (`TILE_LIGHTS`), each kept under the source's version and its link's steps, words and blocks. A light its plan reads and it does not keep is computed before the run, charged on its own beside the window it keeps, which goes first when only that makes room: the link, the largest window of the source one of its tiles is cut from and a readback (`read_light_charge`), for a RAW a 64 MiB tile texture and a 48 MiB window beside the block plane. On the M4 Pro (Metal), every tile of eight families — a colour stack, a masked stack, Presence, Detail, a lens warp, a perspective warp, Presence after Detail and a masked Presence after Basic — the two Presence families with Dehaze, their light computed by the runner — on the byte and linear paths, 192 tiles of up to 96 pixels a side over a 360 × 240 stage, drawn by the headless surface on one device and by the runner on a second device of the same adapter, read back the same codes bit for bit, the runner's scratch pool poisoned and not, and two runs of each read back the same codes and linear bits (`app::gpu_tiles_tests`).

No program gives a non-finite value anywhere, and no CPU unit does either on this grid. Exposure's arithmetic is exact. Over pixels whose inputs lie within [−1, 16], the largest linear difference is 8.3 × 10⁻⁴ relative. The ColourAdjust and mixer maxima come from pixels with one channel at ±65,504 beside small ones: an Oklab round trip at that scale leaves both processors' small channels as rounding of the large one, and they round differently. The Tone curve's worst case is the flat curve at encoded 0.5, which is exactly the threshold between codes 127 and 128, so every pixel of the frame lies on it.

**The hardware encoder.** The drawn figures add the sRGB-typed target's own encoding, which does not round every value as the CPU's quantizer does. Exposure's program is exact, so its drawn mean of 0.096 at −5 EV is the encoder alone, concentrated in the shadows. The flat curve on a code threshold misses the signed ΔL\* limit through the encoder alone: drawn −0.260 against a program's −0.013, where the encoder takes code 127 for a value the CPU quantizes to 128. A flat curve at encoded 0.45 draws within 0.002. Encoding the output in the shader to match the CPU quantizer is the alternative; it is open, and the output encoding is part of the [tick's](#a-tick) last step.

### Mask coverage

**The readback** (`crates/luxforge-app/src/app/gpu_mask_tests.rs`). Each case plans a mask through a masked Basic layer, converts the plan's mask and reads its coverage back from the surface's own masked step, whose one unit adds one to every channel, so the output less the input is the coverage. The CPU's coverage of the same mask over the same half-float inputs is the reference: the point-sampled field, or under the thin-feature rule the mean of the doubled stage's four pixels, which the plan takes exactly when the CPU's proxy does. The input is a photograph-like 8-bit frame with structure everywhere: a hue wheel whose saturation grows outward over lightness falling down the frame.

**The contour rule, measured.** Along every edge between two neighbouring pixel centres that either coverage crosses one half on, the distance between the two crossings, each where its own values interpolated linearly along that edge reach one half; where one does not cross there, its crossing is extrapolated along the same line, which is how far its contour would have to move. So a pixel put on the other side of one half counts by how far that moves the contour: little where the field is steep, a lot where it is flat. A pixel whose coverage differs by more than 10⁻³ is a **tie** when the CPU's own field takes the GPU's value with its samples moved together by one step of a stage 15 (point-sampled) or 30 (supersampled) times finer, or its input scaled by 1 ± 10⁻⁵: a hard edge or a value threshold passing that close to a sample, where `f32` and `f64` may each take either side. A tie counts as the distance it moved.

**Cases**, each at the corpus's Fit proxy of a 24 MP photograph (1716 × 1144, where a thin feature takes the supersample) and at an exact 1536 × 1024 stage: the corpus's gradient, a rotated one, one from off the frame, a ramp under two Fit pixels and an inverted one at an amount; the corpus's radial, a hard edge, a small thin ellipse, a ramp to the centre and an inverted ellipse under a whole-mask inversion; the corpus's stroke, an erase over hard, feathered and one-point strokes, a stroke limited to a colour and a hard stroke under two Fit pixels wide; the corpus's luminance band, a hard band, one-unit shoulders and a long shadow shoulder; the corpus's colour samples, one picked colour at a tight refine and five at a loose one; and the algebra: the corpus's linear less its radial, an inverted radial intersected with a luminance band and a linear under a whole-mask inversion at 75, and a radial less a brush stroke.

On the M4 Pro (Metal), every case's contour lies within 0.004 px of the CPU's (largest: 0.0037 px, a gradient from off the frame and a stroke limited to a colour), and the largest coverage difference outside ties is 6.6 × 10⁻⁵. The one case with ties is the erase over hard strokes at Fit, supersampled: two pixels whose hard stroke's edge passes 4 × 10⁻⁶ and 7 × 10⁻⁵ doubled-stage pixels from one of their subsamples, which differ by 0.175 in coverage and tie within 1/30 of a pixel. Over the colour programs' extreme synthetic grid (negative, over-range and a half float's extremes), the value-based kinds and a limited stroke are finite wherever the CPU is, within 3.1 × 10⁻⁵ of it.

**A painted stroke.** A stroke painted over 200 ticks after a committed one, each tick planned as the owner plans a draft: every tick's block stays within `BRUSH_GPU_BLOCK_WORDS_MAX` (382 words at most against 316,034), the committed stroke's segments never move, and at every tenth tick the GPU frame meets the pointwise limits against the CPU frame of the same tick (worst: mean 0.007, worst block 0.42, p99 0.32, signed ΔL\* +0.0005). On its curved path the host's decimation moved a kept position on all 199 later ticks, so each rewrote the stroke's own segments and the index: 37,866 of the 39,891 words its blocks held over the 200 ticks were written, about 760 bytes a tick. A zigzag whose kept positions stay put appends exactly one segment a tick (the core's `brush_gpu_tests`).

**The masked corpus.** The masked colour recipes (every kind and the linear-less-radial algebra, each carrying a masked Basic layer) run through the same harness: all 42 measured cells meet the pointwise limits, and `cargo xtask preview-error --class pointwise` exits 0 on every pair ([performance](../specs/performance.md#gpu-mask-coverage-at-fit)). The five programs are enabled on these figures.

## Later

- A pan at 100% and above without a draft drawn on the GPU at once, from a window of the source the pipeline holds, with no job for the view, which would also shorten a cold Dehaze first region (863.91 ms at 60 MP).
- **Proposal: padding the region a drag asks for**, so a small pan inside the padding keeps the boundary rather than asking for a new one. Padding of `p` pixels on every side adds `2p(w + h) + 4p²` pixels to the region and its window: for the largest window's 3026 × 1826 region, 64 pixels adds 0.64 MP (11.5%) and 128 adds 1.31 MP (23.7%). At 12 bytes a pixel for a JPEG's colour or masked slot and 20 for a RAW's that is 7.6 or 12.7 MB at 64 pixels and 15.7 or 26.2 MB at 128. Spatial slots grow by the window's share, which their margins make smaller than the region's. Padding colour and masked plans by 128 pixels, and spatial plans only as far as the budget allows, keeps every case that fits now fitting.
- **Proposal: the ring of a region in strips.** Past Texture's and Detail's own halos, the outer ring of a 100% region's window is read only by Clarity's and Dehaze's 4× reductions, yet Detail's and Texture's full-resolution planes cover it whole. Evaluating the ring in strips of about 256 rows with their halos, reducing each strip into the 4× planes as it goes, would hold Detail's two apply planes and Texture's band over the region and Texture's margin alone, beside the strip planes. The boundary stays whole. The cost is a strip schedule, about 11 strips of 25 passes on a first draw and a Detail tick, and about 38% more Detail and Texture work in the ring for the strips' halos. Nothing in the corpus needs it now; it would leave room for larger windows and for padding.
- **Proposal: planes and windows sized to what each masked link needs**, not built. Every link's intermediate and kept planes, and the pool, cover the whole boundary, so a masked Texture and Clarity layer costs about 4.25 bytes a pixel of kept planes and its intermediate, 8 or 16, however small its mask; and at 100% the window is one for every link, blind to masks, grown by every chained link's halo ([bounds](#bounds)). Two changes, separable, with figures estimated for the paint harness's layout in its own view:
  - **A. Mask-bounded growth.** The core's GPU window walk would grow a masked link's need only where its mask's bounds meet what its output must hold, since outside its bounds a masked link's output is its input. 10 masks would charge about 1.44 GB and fit; 16 about 2.38 GB and would not. The boundary's key would then depend on the masks' bounds, which a stroke or a radial drag changes, so the drafted mask's link keeps its full halo and the stack's own boundary serves only gestures on the same mask; and it needs a bit-exact test that coverage is exactly zero outside `GpuMask::bounds`.
  - **B. Each link's intermediate and kept planes sized to its own output's need**, the region grown by the reach of the links after it, rather than the whole window. Alone it would charge 1.88 GB at 10 masks and 3.53 GB at 16.
  - **Both.** 1.07 GB at 10 masks and 1.65 GB at 16, each within the budget in the harness's view; the largest centred view would still need about 2.46 GB at 16.
  - **Over a mask's bounds.** Holding a masked layer's kept planes and intermediate over its mask's bounds and reach alone, at Fit as at 100%, would go further, so a small mask's layer would cost its area. It needs a growth policy for a painted mask and a way for the next link to read the unchanged input outside the bounds, which B needs too.
- **Detail's sharpening change in one channel.** Sharpening's apply reads one channel of its change of lightness from a two-channel plane, 8 bytes a pixel kept: `rg32float` alone and `rgba16float` beside noise reduction. A one-channel `r32float` plane would keep 4, as Texture's band does, but beside noise reduction at full precision where the plane holds a half, so Detail would need qualifying again.
- **Painting over 10 to 16 masked Presence layers at Fit** draws every tick on the GPU but misses the 16 ms target, p95 22.8 to 24.4 ms ([performance](../specs/performance.md#painting-over-masked-spatial-layers)). A tick's GPU work passes the 8.33 ms frame: 8.8 ms over 10 masks and 14.5 ms over 16, the work of 10 to 16 chained Presence links each evaluated incrementally around a change that grows link by link with each link's reach; the pool adds nothing to it.
