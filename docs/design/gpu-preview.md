# GPU previews

Status: **in implementation.** The preview-difference measure and corpus, the photo surface's GPU stage, the core's program descriptions and plan, the seven pointwise colour programs and the coverage programs of every mask kind (qualified on the M4 and enabled), the surface's masked colour step, the Presence program and the surface's spatial step that runs it, the Detail program (qualified on the M4 and enabled), the conversion of a plan into the surface's plain data, mipmapped minification, the `gpu_preview` preference, the status label and the settle dissolve are built, and a gesture draws its ticks on the GPU over a held boundary: a slider drag, a mask's shape moved and a brush stroke, through colour, masked, Presence and Detail layers and the geometry tail after them, at Fit ([a tick](#a-tick)) and at 100% and above over the visible region at full scale ([at 100% and above](#at-100-and-above)), marking its own clipped pixels while a clipping overlay is shown, and dissolving to the CPU's frame when it settles ([settle](#settle-and-the-dissolve)). The owner set the direction on 2026-10-01: during a gesture, speed comes first, as long as the picture does not visibly jump when it settles. The error limits, the dissolve, the label and the preference below are proposals with recorded defaults. The plan runs on them until the owner revises them. ## Outcome and scope

While a person drags a slider, moves or paints a mask, or drags at 100%, the desktop evaluates the part of the recipe the gesture changes on the GPU. It draws the result in the same display frame as the input. When the gesture settles, the CPU's result replaces it through a short dissolve.

The CPU renderer stays the only reference. Settled frames, the histogram, clipping counts, point samples, mask coverage grids, analysis, export, history and every API answer are computed exactly as today and never read a GPU pixel.

### Why

| Cost today | Figure | What the GPU stage changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fit drag with a full Basic layer, input to presented frame | 18.1 / 24.8 ms p50 / p95 at 24 MP, 17.2 / 28.9 at 60 MP; the core proxy render with geometry is about 45 ms ([performance](../specs/performance.md#instant-previews-proxy-phase-hop-rule-and-the-surface-primitive)) | A tick becomes a uniform write and a few passes in the surface's own `prepare`, with no worker hop and no per-tick upload |
| A Fit proxy while an exact render holds the shared pool | 12.1 / 13.1 ms alone, 207.2 / 214.7 ms contended ([performance](../specs/performance.md#raw-colour-row-batching)) | The GPU stage does not share the Rayon pool, so settlement and RAW development no longer slow the gesture |
| A painted stroke | p95 42.1–50.2 ms over four masked layers, missing the provisional bound. On a bare masked layer the worker render is only 4.5–5.4 ms; the queue wait and result delivery dominate ([further performance](../research/further-performance.md)) | The hop that dominates is removed, not just the kernel |
| 100% motion | A half-scale region, softer until refinement | A full-scale region at motion speed |
| Presence and Detail | All three Presence fields 1.5–1.6 s exact at 60 MP; a Presence drag at Fit 16.5–22.7 ms p50; Detail drags are expected to miss the Fit budget ([Detail](detail.md#proposals-with-recorded-defaults)) | Their drags, and drags of layers before them, run on the GPU |

The recorded verdict that a GPU colour stage was not justified ([further performance](../research/further-performance.md#rgba-ownership-gpu-and-simd-choices)) weighed the kernel alone. This design removes the worker hop, the per-tick upload and contention with exact work, which that verdict did not weigh.

### In scope

1. A preview-difference measure and corpus, so every limit is measurable before any GPU code exists.
2. The GPU stage in the desktop: device sharing, a bounded preview slot, fallback and evidence.
3. GPU programs for the seven pointwise colour units (Exposure, White balance, Tone, ColourAdjust, Tone curve, Mixer, Vignette) and every mask kind (linear, radial, brush, luminance range, colour range). Each program is owned by its module, beside its CPU unit.
4. Fit drags over a held input boundary, then 100% drags over a held region.
5. The settle hand-off: the dissolve, the label, motion overlays and a preference with an API equivalent.
6. Presence (Texture, Clarity, Dehaze), then Detail (sharpen, denoise), as GPU preview programs.
7. Mipmapped minification for a full-resolution texture drawn below its size. This is a display fix that needs no error limit.
8. Native qualification and measurement.

### Out of scope

- The GPU for exact renders, export, the histogram, clipping counts, point samples, mask coverage grids, RAW development, JPEG decoding or encoding, or proxy builds.
- A GPU dependency in `luxforge-core` or in the headless `luxforge-json` binary.
- View changes without a draft: a pan or zoom with nothing being dragged keeps the CPU path ([later](#later)).
- Crop and transform drafts, whose input stage the surface already turns on the GPU.
- A general GPU node graph and external GPU modules.

## Decisions

Decided by the owner on 2026-10-01:

- **Speed comes first for interactive previews.** The constraint is that settling must not produce a large, noticeable jump in the image.
- **GPU arithmetic is for previews only.** The CPU stays the reference for settled frames, analysis, sampling and export. Apple GPUs have no 64-bit floating point, so the CPU's `f64` arithmetic, bit-identical mask coverage and byte-identical sample/render contract cannot be reproduced on them. Making the GPU the reference is not pursued.
- **Order of work.** Error limits first, then the Fit colour stage, then Presence and Detail, with mipmapped minification beside them.

## The preview error limit

**What is compared.** The jump a person sees: the GPU frame on screen at the moment of settlement against the CPU frame that replaces it. Both are taken at the same view and as the 8-bit sRGB pixels the surface draws.

- At Fit, the replacing frame is the CPU proxy render.
- A stack whose effect declares `FitSettle::Exact` (Detail) settles to the reduction of the exact render, and its GPU frame is judged against the CPU's moving proxy instead: the frame the gesture would show on the CPU path, which the GPU frame stands in for (owner, 2026-10-02, [decisions](../decisions.md#gpu-previews)). The proxy's own jump to the exact reduction is the CPU path's and unchanged by the GPU; the qualification reports it beside each such cell.
- At 100% it is the exact visible region.
- Statistics cover the photograph alone, not the canvas beside it, as the owner decided for the white-balance gates on 2026-09-30.

**The measure.** Per-pixel CIEDE2000 (ΔE00), from sRGB through CIELAB under D65, computed in `f64` by an independent reference. As a common guide, ΔE00 below 1 is not perceptible, 1 to 2 is perceptible on close inspection and 2 to 10 at a glance. Four statistics:

- **Mean ΔE00** over the photograph: the overall shift.
- **Worst block**: the largest mean ΔE00 of any 16 × 16-pixel block. A region that changes is what reads as a jump, more than scattered pixels do. Blocks tile the photograph from its top-left corner; the last block in each direction moves inward to end at the edge, so none is partial.
- **p99 ΔE00**: edges and fine detail. Nearest rank: the largest value left after discarding the worst 1% of pixels, rounded down.
- **Signed mean ΔL\***: whole-picture brightening or darkening, which reads as a jump even when small.

**Limits** (proposed):

| Statistic | Pointwise programs: colour units, masks, vignette | Spatial programs: Presence, Detail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mean ΔE00 | ≤ 0.5 | ≤ 1.0 |
| Worst 16 × 16 block mean ΔE00 | ≤ 1.0 | ≤ 2.5 |
| p99 ΔE00 | ≤ 2.0 | ≤ 5.0 |
| Signed mean ΔL\* | within ±0.25 | within ±0.5 |

The pointwise mean matches the precedent the owner already set: the RAW white-balance draft gate is a mean within one code at Fit. The spatial limits are deliberately generous. They leave room for half-precision storage, single-precision running sums and estimates taken at the scale the GPU holds. Under a 150 ms dissolve, a regional difference of about 2 reads as the picture settling rather than popping. The first task measured today's accepted CPU approximations under the same measure, for comparison ([recorded baseline](../specs/performance.md#preview-error-baseline)):

- the Presence proxy against the exact downscale
- the half-scale 100% motion region against the exact region
- a RAW white-balance draft against its release

These limits gate **enabling** a program, not frames at run time. A program, or a stack class, that misses any limit on the corpus ships disabled, and those stacks take the CPU path until it passes.

**Hard rules, not limits.**

- A finite CPU value is never non-finite on the GPU.
- No tile or region seams.
- A mask edge's half-coverage contour lies within a quarter of a display pixel of the CPU's.
- A GPU frame never feeds a histogram, clipping count, sample, mask grid, export, artifact or history entry.
- Settlement always replaces the GPU frame with the CPU's.

## Design

### Where the code lives

- **Programs belong to modules.** Each module's GPU program is WGSL text in a `.wgsl` file beside its CPU unit in `luxforge-core`: a `static GpuProgram` with its entry name, kind (colour, coverage or spatial), uniform word count and enabled flag (`crates/luxforge-core/src/render/gpu/program.rs`). The core names no GPU crate.
  - `PointwiseColor::gpu` and each mask component's `gpu` answer an optional `GpuDescription`: the program, its uniform block of 32-bit words and, where needed, a shared storage block (the curve's knots, a brush's segment grid). The default is none, which takes the CPU path.
  - `SpatialUnit::gpu` answers an optional `GpuSpatialUnit` (`crates/luxforge-core/src/render/gpu/spatial.rs`): its program, its words, the planes it writes, the compute passes that write them and its apply ([spatial programs](#spatial-programs)). It is handed the global estimate the store holds for it, when the plan found one.
  - The words are a pure function of the coefficients `describe()` writes, so two units that describe themselves identically produce identical uniforms. A shared test helper checks it for each unit that has a program.
  - A program declares only functions and constants, each named after its entry (`lf_<module>_<unit>`). Its entry has its kind's signature: a colour unit is `fn(rgb: vec3<f32>, pos: vec2<f32>, words: u32, block: u32) -> vec3<f32>`, placed in content space or after the geometry tail; a mask component's coverage is `fn(pos: vec2<f32>, rgb: vec3<f32>, words: u32, block: u32) -> f32`. The surface prepends the prelude that binds every program's words and blocks, read through `lf_word`, `lf_f32`, `lf_block_word` and `lf_block_f32`.
  - A program that misses its limits ships disabled: it is still validated, and its stacks take the CPU path and name it.
  - **The shipped programs**, listed publicly as `GPU_PROGRAMS` in the order the units run, all enabled ([qualification](#qualifying-a-program)):

    | Program | Beside | Words | Block |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | `lf_detail` (spatial) | `modules/detail/detail.wgsl`, described by `modules/detail/gpu.rs` | each smoothing pass's form and its kernels' taps, every tap's offset and weight; each level's thresholds, its details and whether a level came before it; sharpening's gain, coring threshold squared and masking scale squared | none |
    | `lf_basic_white_balance` | `modules/basic/white_balance.rs` | the composite 3 × 3 matrix, row by row | none |
    | `lf_basic_exposure` | `modules/basic/exposure.rs` | the gain | none |
    | `lf_basic_tone` | `modules/basic/tone.rs` | Contrast's branch (identity, positive, negative) and the eight coefficients the unit holds | none |
    | `lf_basic_colour_adjust` | `modules/basic/colour.rs` | Vibrance's gain and Saturation's | none |
    | `lf_curve_tone_curve` | `modules/curve/unit.rs` | the knot count, the curve's ends and its black level | the knots, inverse widths and per-segment coefficients |
    | `lf_mixer_mixer` | `modules/mixer/unit.rs` | the hue warp's eight cubics, the eight chroma gains and the eight luminance amounts (48) | none |
    | `lf_vignette_vignette` | `modules/vignette/unit.rs` | the shape, its coefficients, the stage's half sides, the falloff, the branch and the amount (11) | none |
    | `lf_presence` (spatial) | `modules/presence/presence.wgsl`, described by `modules/presence/gpu.rs` | each pass's form, radius, run and regularization; each apply's gain, limit and reduction; Dehaze's stored atmospheric light | none |

    Each is the CPU unit's own `f32` formulation in the same order, every word the `f32` the unit holds. The mixer's description leaves out a slider at zero of either sign, so its words add `+0.0` to each coefficient, which the program processes as the CPU does. Where they differ:
    - The shared colour equations (the sRGB transfer, Rec. 709 luminance, the Oklab conversion) are restated in each program that needs them, under its own names, since a program declares every name it uses. The restated constants (the Oklab matrices, the mixer's centres and gaps, the vibrance weights) are held to the CPU's `f32` values bit for bit by the core's tests, through `naga`.
    - WGSL has no cube root: the Oklab programs take `pow`'s estimate and refine it with one Newton step written so no intermediate overflows.
    - Tone's logistic exponent is held within ±80, past which the logistic is 0 or 1 to every bit, as the CPU's overflowing `exp` gives it.
    - The vignette's mask is evaluated in `f32`; the CPU evaluates it in `f64`. A GPU has no `f64`.
    - Detail's limiter holds its `tanh` argument within ±16, past which `tanh` is 1 to every bit an `f32` holds. Where a squared sharpening residual flushes to zero and the coring threshold is zero, the coring's quotient is its limit, the residual itself, where the CPU holds the subnormal square.
  - **The mask coverage programs**, listed publicly as `MASK_GPU_PROGRAMS` in the kind table's order, all enabled ([mask coverage](#mask-coverage)). Each is a `.wgsl` file beside its CPU field in `crates/luxforge-core/src/mask/`, and `ComponentField::gpu(stage)` answers it with the terms the field compiled against that stage, each narrowed to `f32` once:

    | Program | Beside | Words | Block |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | `lf_mask_linear` | `mask/linear.rs` | the stage's height, `u0`, `v0`, `du`, `dv` and `l2` (6) | none |
    | `lf_mask_radial` | `mask/radial.rs` | the stage's height, the centre, the rotation's cosine and sine, the two radii, `r0`, the span and the hard edge (10) | none |
    | `lf_mask_brush` | `mask/brush.rs` | the stage's height, the grid's corner and cell side, its columns and rows, and the offsets of the block's parts (9) | the field's own grid index: its segments, its cell table, the entries and the stroke records |
    | `lf_mask_luminance_range` | `mask/range.rs` | the band's edges and shoulders on the encoded axis (4) | none |
    | `lf_mask_colour_range` | `mask/range.rs` | the sample count, the radius the refine maps to and five Oklab `(a, b)` pairs (12) | none |

    Each evaluates its field's frozen expressions in the CPU's order, in `f32`, at the pixel `pos` of the stage the mask was compiled against, with mask space at that pixel's centre over the stage's height. Where they differ:
    - The luminance axis's Rec. 709 weights and sRGB encode, and the Oklab rows the colour range and a limited brush stroke read, are restated under each program's own names and held to the CPU's `f32` values bit for bit by the core's tests. The cube root is `pow`'s estimate refined by one Newton step, as the colour programs take it.
    - A pixel of a brush tests exactly the segments the CPU's own uniform grid index lists in the cell it falls in, so its cost is that cell's occupancy, which the painted-stroke cap bounds at 64; it folds the nearest segment's capsule profile per stroke, the flow, a stroke's colour limit and the screen union or erase across strokes in stored order.
    - A hard edge is compared in `f32`: a sample whose centre lies within about 10⁻⁴ of a pixel of the edge may take the other side from the CPU's ([ties](#mask-coverage)).
  - **The brush's storage block.** It serializes the field's index in the order a painted stroke grows it: the segments in stored stroke order (five words each), then the cell table, the entries (each a stroke and a segment in one word) and the stroke records (seven words each). A stroke being painted is always the component's last, so a tick leaves every earlier stroke's segments where they were, and the surface writes only the 1 KiB chunks that changed ([a tick](#where-the-code-lives)). The host decimates the whole captured path again on every tick, and on a curved path that moves positions it had kept, so a painted tick rewrites the stroke's own segments from the first moved position on, with the index after them; only a path whose kept positions stay put appends its new segments alone. The block is bounded by the stroke limits, at most `BRUSH_GPU_BLOCK_WORDS_MAX` words: every point a mask may hold as a segment, the largest grid's cell table with every cell at the occupancy cap, and a record per stroke, 316,034 words (1.21 MiB).
  - The core's tests validate every shipped program through `naga` 27.0.3, the version wgpu 27.0.1 resolves to, as a dev-dependency only, with a copy of the surface's prelude and a harness entry point; a spatial program with a copy of the spatial declarations as stubs and a harness that calls every kernel and apply its units' descriptions name. They fail for a `.wgsl` file the shipped list omits. The desktop's tests hold every shipped program to the surface's own `PRELUDE` and `validate_step`, so the two copies of the prelude cannot drift apart unnoticed. `cargo xtask check-repository` refuses a GPU or GUI crate, `naga` included, in the core's normal and build dependencies (`gpu-free-core`) and in `luxforge-cli`'s normal dependencies (`headless-cli`).
- **Planning stays in the core.** `gpu_plan` (`crates/luxforge-core/src/render/gpu/plan.rs`) compiles the stack once, as the CPU frame it previews is compiled: the proxy phase at Fit, the exact phase at 100%. It walks the compilation on the catalog owner in `O(layers + units + components)`, reads no pixel and holds nothing that scales with the image. From the input of a named layer it answers the ordered plan to the terminal output, or the reason there is none.
  - **The boundary**: the stage that layer receives, and whether a colour operation before it in the same segment hands it an unclamped value, which the boundary must then hold as it is.
  - **Content operations** over the boundary's texels, in recipe order: every colour operation of the boundary's segment from the boundary on.
  - **The spatial operation** (`GpuSpatial`) the content operations' frame enters, when the next segment opens with one and no exact step lies between the boundary and it: every unit's planes, passes and apply composed in the CPU's order, a later unit's scratch plane reusing an earlier unit's of the same format and size; whether the CPU clamps its input and output, which the byte path's quantized frames do and the RAW linear path's float frames do not; its mask; and whether a global estimate is taken on the GPU. The tail then starts at its frame. A layer that opens a stage boundary receives the frame its segment wrote, so a boundary there continues no colour run.
  - **Global estimates** are read through `gpu_plan_with` and `GpuEstimates`, the render context whose store the CPU frames fill and the source they were rendered from: Dehaze's atmospheric light is the store's when it holds one under the key a CPU frame of the same content at the same stage asks with (source, upstream identity, stage and the unit's key, which names no amount), so a Dehaze drag reads the one the settled frame stored. Otherwise the GPU takes it from the stage it holds and `GpuPlan::approximate` says so; `gpu_plan` alone always does. The lookup is the store's own and reads no pixel.
  - **The path.** `GpuPlanRequest::linear` plans a frame of the RAW linear path, which clamps no stage boundary: neither the spatial operation's input and output nor a resample's input.
  - **The geometry tail**: the exact steps after the boundary and every resample and warp after them, as one `GeometryMap`, with the rectangle of the boundary whose edge the CPU's resample replicates and whether the CPU clamps before it.
  - **Output operations** over the output stage, in recipe order: the colour operations of the last segment when a resample separates it from the boundary, the vignette among them. With no resample after the boundary the tail is exact, and every operation, the vignette included, runs in the content pass at the coordinates the CPU hands it.
  - **Clipping marks**: the linear thresholds at which the output quantizer gives codes 0 and 255.
  - Each operation carries its units' descriptions and the exact integer map from its pass's pixel to the `pos` the CPU unit's `apply_row` is handed. A masked one also carries the map to its mask's own stage, the components with their modes and inversions, the mask's inversion, `amount / 100`, the bounds outside which coverage is zero and, for the proxy's thin-feature rule, the 2 × 2 supersample on the doubled stage. The blend is the CPU's, against the operation's own input, and the input itself where coverage is exactly zero.
  - Reasons for no plan, each naming its layer and a stable code: a pixel-stage layer in the stack (`pixel-stage`); a boundary at a source or geometry layer (`boundary-stage`); a spatial or restoration layer after the boundary that the plan cannot hold: a unit without a program, an exact step before it or a second spatial layer (`spatial-unit`); a colour layer between two resamples (`between-resamples`); a unit or mask component without a program (`no-program`); a disabled program (`disabled-program`). Only the qualification corpus may ask for disabled programs to be planned as if enabled.
  - The plan addresses whole stages. A boundary or output that holds a window of one offsets its pixels by the window's origin.
- **Execution lives in the photo surface** (`crates/luxforge-ui/src/photo_surface/gpu_preview.rs`), which receives Iced's wgpu device and queue in `PhotoPrimitive::prepare`. A whole-frame photograph is handed a `GpuPlan` through `PhotoSurface::gpu_preview`: the boundary (`GpuBoundary`, `rgba16float` or `rgba32float` as its `BoundaryFormat` says), a `TexelMap` from a boundary texel to the stage the programs address, and ordered `GpuStep`s, each a `GpuProgram` of WGSL text, uniform words and a storage block, and the step's `PositionMap`. It is plain data, so `luxforge-ui` still never names a core type. A percentage view runs a plan of its own stage's region (`GpuPlan::region`, a `GpuRegion`) and draws its frame alone at that rectangle of the photograph; it ignores a whole frame's plan, as Fit ignores a region's. A crop stage ignores a plan.
  - **The calling convention.** Every assembled shader starts with the public `PRELUDE`. It declares two read-only storage bindings: `lf_words`, a header (the texel map, then for each step its program's two base indices and its position map's six coefficients) followed by every program's uniform words in program order, and `lf_blocks`, every program's storage block in program order, at least one word. Its helpers are `lf_word`, `lf_f32`, `lf_block_word` and `lf_block_f32`. A program declares only functions and constants, each name prefixed with its entry's, and its entry is none of the surface's own names (the prelude's, `lf_boundary`, `lf_vertex` and `lf_fragment`) and starts with none of the prefixes the surface generates names under (`lf_surface`, `lf_tail`, `lf_output`).
  - **The pointwise colour step** (`GpuStep::Colour`): `fn <entry>(rgb: vec3<f32>, pos: vec2<f32>, words: u32, block: u32) -> vec3<f32>`. `rgb` is scene-linear sRGB, unclamped. `pos` is the pixel's integer coordinate in the stage the CPU unit's `apply_row` addresses: the texel map takes a boundary texel to its stage pixel, and the step's own `PositionMap`, an exact integer map `(a·x + b·y + tx, c·x + d·y + ty)` whose linear part is a signed permutation, takes that pixel to `pos`. `words` and `block` are the program's base indices. A colour step before the geometry tail runs in the content pass at the boundary's texel; one after it runs in the tail's pass, where its map takes the output pixel to `pos`, which is how a vignette after a resample is placed. A masked step after the tail is placed the same way: its mask's map starts from the output pixel. Clipping marks will join `GpuStep` as a variant of their own.
  - **The masked colour step** (`GpuStep::Masked`, `crates/luxforge-ui/src/photo_surface/gpu_preview/mask.rs`): a colour operation's units, chained as colour steps are at the step's position map, and a `Coverage` holding its components (`CoverageComponent`: a coverage program, its mode and its inversion), the mask's inversion and `amount / 100`, the mask's own `PositionMap` from the stage pixel to the pixel of the stage the mask was compiled against, the half-open rectangle of that stage outside which coverage is exactly zero, and the thin-feature supersample. A coverage program is `fn <entry>(pos: vec2<f32>, rgb: vec3<f32>, words: u32, block: u32) -> f32`: the component's falloff before its inversion, at `pos`, for `rgb`, the operation's own input.
    - The surface generates one function per masked step that places the pixel on the mask's stage, returns zero outside the rectangle, and folds the components as the CPU's mask does (`max(m, c)`, `min(m, 1 − c)` or `min(m, c)` after each component's own `1 − c`), then the mask's inversion and its amount; under the supersample it is the mean of that fold at the four pixels `2q + (i, j)` of the doubled stage. The fragment then blends `(1 − M)·in + M·units(in)` per channel against the operation's input, and where `M` is exactly zero leaves the input as it is and runs no unit.
    - Only the programs are code. The modes, inversions, amount, rectangle, position and supersample are the step's words: its own base holds the mask's position map, the rectangle, the two flags and the amount, then each component's mode, inversion and base indices, then each unit's base indices, then the programs' own words; its block is the components' blocks then the units'. Inverting a component or moving the amount writes words and compiles nothing. An entry may not start with `lf_surface`, the prefix of every name the surface generates.
  - **The spatial step** (`GpuStep::Spatial`, `crates/luxforge-ui/src/photo_surface/gpu_preview/spatial.rs`): a spatial operation's program, its planes (`rgba16float` colour, `r32float`, `rg32float` and `rgba32float` accumulators, each the boundary's size or the stage's `s × s` blocks the boundary reaches, anchored at the stage origin, or a fixed size), its compute passes in order and one apply per unit, under the **spatial convention**. Every module holding a spatial program also declares `lf_plane(slot, at)` (a bound plane's texel, edge-clamped), `lf_plane_size`, `lf_source(at)` (the input of the pass's unit at a boundary texel: the boundary through every step before the spatial one, clamped where the step says, then the applies of the units before it), `lf_origin` (the stage pixel of texel `(0, 0)`), `lf_size`, `lf_store` and a 1024-value `lf_shared` workgroup scratch, which `SPATIAL_PRELUDE` stands in for when a program is validated alone. A kernel, `fn(at: vec2<i32>, words: u32, block: u32)`, reads its pass's inputs as planes `0..4` and writes through `lf_store`; it runs once for every `span` texels of its output, or on the 256 lanes of one workgroup. An apply, `fn(rgb: vec3<f32>, at: vec2<i32>, words: u32, block: u32, planes: u32) -> vec3<f32>`, is a unit's pointwise last step, reading its planes from slot `planes` on. Each pass is a compute pipeline of a generated module that is its kernel and its shape alone (its output's format, how many planes it reads, the steps before the spatial one and the applies its source runs, which a pass that reads only planes runs none of): everything that places a pass in its plan — its step's header, its words, its span, the words of the applies its source runs — is data, read from the pass's 256-byte slice of a parameter buffer the planes own and the budget charges with them; the frame's pass binds every apply's planes as a second bind group and runs the step's clamp, applies, mask blend and clamp in the chain. A masked operation's `Coverage` is the masked colour step's: its words come first in the step's, in that step's layout with no units, and the surface's coverage function for it blends the applies' output against the operation's input per channel, and leaves the input where coverage is exactly zero. No colour plane holds a unit's output: a pass that needs a unit's input computes it at the texel it reads, so the step's memory is its planes alone.
  - **The geometry tail** (`GpuStep::Geometry`, `crates/luxforge-ui/src/photo_surface/gpu_preview/tail.rs`): the content steps' result carried to the output stage, as the CPU's exact steps, resamples and warps after the boundary carry it. A plan with one runs two render passes: the content steps write an intermediate the boundary's size, and the tail's pass draws the output stage from it. For each output pixel it maps the pixel's centre to the boundary stage through the plan's affine matrix, or through a lens or perspective warp's coordinate grid interpolated in `f32` exactly as `CoordinateGrid::sample` does, then blends the four texels around it in linear light, each tap clamped to the rectangle of the boundary stage the CPU's resample reads and offset by the window the boundary holds. On a JPEG the CPU quantizes at every segment boundary, so the tail does too: the content pass writes 8-bit codes into an `rgba8unorm` intermediate, the tail decodes them through the CPU's table, and its blend is quantized and decoded again. A developed RAW's segments hold `f32` values unclamped, so its intermediate is `rgba16float` and nothing is quantized or clamped. The steps after the tail run in its pass at the output pixel. A spatial step must come before the tail, since it reads the boundary's own texels.
  - **The generated shader.** The surface generates the entry points: a vertex stage of one triangle, and a fragment stage that loads the boundary texel, or the tail's sample, and chains the entries in step order; and, for a spatial step, each pass's compute entry. The last pass writes the 8-bit code the CPU's output quantizer gives each channel: the value clamped to `[0, 1]`, NaN to zero, and compared against the core's 255 code thresholds, each the first `f32` of its code's exact interval, which a core test holds to both of the CPU's quantizer paths at every code boundary. It writes the codes through an `Rgba8Unorm` view of the output, which the draw samples through an `Rgba8UnormSrgb` view as it samples the CPU frame of the same codes; the hardware's own sRGB encoder rounded some values to the neighbouring code. The widget crate cannot reach the core, so the desktop installs the quantizer's tables once at start (`install_output_encoding`); a surface with none installed compiles no pass, and its frames stay the CPU's. Two layers of one unit share their function. `validate_step` checks one program against this convention with the `naga` wgpu uses, as the surface does before compiling: for a spatial step, every kernel and apply its passes and applies name at its signature, and every plane, input, word and source in range.
  - **A tick.** `prepare` uploads the boundary when its version changes, in 8 MiB chunks borrowed from the caller's buffer. It writes the words with one `queue.write_buffer`, and of the blocks only the 1 KiB chunks that changed, then encodes the spatial step's compute passes the tick needs, the content pass and, for a plan with a geometry tail, the tail's pass, and submits them ahead of the frame's own submission. A pass runs only when what its output depends on changed since its plane was written: the boundary's version, the texel map and the steps before the spatial one, its own words (from its offset to the next one a pass or apply of its step names), the words and planes of the applies its source runs, and its inputs. Each plane keeps the key of what it holds, a pass whose output an apply needs runs with the inputs that are not already current, and a new sequence or new planes run every pass (`spatial::Schedule`). A drag that moves only an apply's word runs no pass: of all three Presence units' 21 passes, a Clarity drag runs none, a Texture drag the 5 of Clarity's that read Texture's output, and a Dehaze drag 19, each frame equal to the bit to one that ran every pass (`gpu_presence_a_drag_reruns_only_the_passes_it_changes`). In the editor the `gpu-preview` scenario counts the same from `gpu_preview_spatial_passes`: over a committed Dehaze and Clarity, each GPU tick of a Texture drag runs 5, of a Clarity drag none, and of a Basic drag under Presence all 22 its plan holds, the light taken on the GPU among them. `draw` samples the output as it samples the photograph, through the same placement, snapping and filter. The output is held in the photograph's own size bucket, with its edge texels repeated past the frame as an upload repeats them, so a GPU frame and the CPU frame of the same codes draw identically. A redraw of an unchanged plan encodes nothing. The UI thread only encodes GPU commands; it never waits for the GPU or reads pixels back.
  - **Pipelines.** One compiled sequence is kept per program sequence (each step's kind, a masked step's count of units and components, a spatial step's clamp, planes, passes and applies, a tail's kind and quantizing, and each program's role, entry and source): its content pass, a tail's pass and a spatial step's compute pipelines, 16 compiled sequences at most (`PIPELINE_CACHE`), ready and failed ones together, the least recently asked for evicted when a compile ends, and at most as many waiting to compile. Nothing compiles on the UI thread (`crates/luxforge-ui/src/photo_surface/gpu_preview/compile.rs`): a first-seen sequence is queued for one compile thread, which holds a clone of the device and the stage's shared support and runs the stage's one `compile`, and the frame that asked draws the CPU frame and names `compiling`. The thread drains its queue whether or not a frame is drawn, one sequence after another, keeps each result itself and counts it in the figures as it ends, so the next `prepare` finds it ready; between compiles it sleeps on the queue, with no timer or poll. A sequence a frame asks for goes ahead of every warmed one waiting, and its compile's end wakes the surface; a warmed one's wakes nothing. A committed stack's preview job carries the plans of the drags it is likely to see (`PreviewJob::gpu_warm`): each colour or finish layer's drag and each such module's first drag in its GPU shape, with the light taken on the GPU, as every tick of a drag before a spatial layer takes it; then a drag of each spatial layer the stack holds, from that layer, as it stands and with each field at its default moved off it, reading the stored light, at most four (`WARM_SPATIAL_PLANS`): one Presence layer's whole repertoire, which with the colour candidates stays inside the 16-sequence cache. The surface queues their sequences before a drag begins (`PhotoSurface::gpu_warm`), and in the `gpu-preview` scenario no frame of a Texture, Clarity or Basic-under-Presence drag waits for a compile. A drafted layer is planned in its GPU shape, every unit its module can hold with a neutral one as its own identity, so a drag that leaves or returns to neutral keeps one sequence; the CPU's own compile is unchanged. On the M4 a Fit drag's sequence compiles in 1.4 to 4.3 ms once the driver's shader cache holds it, and took 97 ms cold ([performance](../specs/performance.md#gpu-preview-compile-cost)); a Presence sequence takes 1 to 2 s on a new build's first run and tens of milliseconds after it. A spatial step's passes share pipelines through a cache of their own of 64 modules, kept across sequences, the least recently used evicted first, so a sequence that differs from one compiled before in a unit or an estimate, or one that came back after its eviction, compiles only the passes it does not share: all three Presence units run 21 passes from 12 pipelines, a plan whose light is taken on the GPU adds 2, and a drag, which changes only words, compiles nothing. An ordinary Presence drag keeps one sequence; a value crossing zero, which adds or removes a unit, and the light stored or taken make the few more one stack shape needs, well inside the eight.
  - **Fallback.** The surface always holds the CPU frame and draws it whenever the stage cannot, naming why. The reasons are:
    - `no-adapter`: the device lacks read-only storage buffers in the fragment stage, or the target is not sRGB; or, for a spatial step, a 256-lane workgroup with its scratch, a storage texture or 16 sampled textures a stage.
    - `device-lost`: the device's lost callback set it, and nothing waits for a recovery.
    - `compiling`: the sequence is on the compile thread.
    - `pipeline-failed`: `naga` refused the assembled WGSL or an entry name, or wgpu's error scopes, polled once without waiting, reported the pipeline; or a spatial step over a texel map whose step is not one texel per pixel.
    - `budget-exceeded`, and `texture-limit` or `buffer-limit` for a boundary or a tail's output, or words or blocks, larger than the device allows a texture or a storage binding to be.

    Each but `compiling` holds for every later frame until its cause goes.
  - **Evidence.** The surface's diagnostics and each captured frame's `state.surface.gpu` report the drawing path, the fallback, the boundary drawn and the draft revision of the plan drawn over it (`drawn_gpu_revision`), the boundary the stage has evaluated (`gpu_ready_boundary`), the interface thread's time to prepare the GPU frame drawn, the compile thread's figures (`gpu_preview_compiles`, `gpu_preview_compiled`, `gpu_preview_compile_max_us`, `gpu_preview_compile_last_us`), a settle's dissolve (`dissolve`), the clipping marks a GPU frame drew (`clipping_marks`) and the GPU-preview budget's figures ([bounds](#bounds)), beside the desktop's own reason for handing no plan (`plan_fallback`), its gesture's state (`gpu_preview`: the boundary held, its requests, and the gesture's GPU and CPU ticks) and its settle hand-off (`settle`: the running dissolve, how many began and were cancelled, and the last decision).
  - **The conversion.** The desktop's one conversion from the core's plan to the surface's plain data is `app::gpu_plan` (`crates/luxforge-app/src/app/gpu_plan.rs`): one colour step per unit of each content operation in recipe order, each with its operation's position map, or for a masked operation one masked step holding its units and its mask's coverage, one spatial step for the spatial operation, and the geometry tail with the output operations after it, the core's static WGSL borrowed and each storage block shared, over the boundary held with the texel map of the window it holds. It reads no pixel. `surface_plan_at` follows the plan's parts in order, one function each (`boundary_map`, `operation_steps` for each operation and its mask, `spatial_step` for the spatial operation, its planes, passes and applies as they are with its mask's coverage, `geometry_steps` for the tail, through its affine matrix or a warp's coordinate grid, and the output operations after it), so a step kind the surface gains joins the part it belongs to. A plan the surface cannot run is answered with its reason: `warp-grid` (a warp with no coordinate grid held for it), `boundary-size` (a boundary that does not lie inside the plan's boundary stage) or `position-range` (a coefficient past what an `f32` holds exactly, a supersampled mask's doubled stage included). A boundary inside a colour run must hold the run's unclamped value, which both boundary formats do.
  - **The gesture path** (`crates/luxforge-app/src/app/gpu_preview.rs`) hands the surface a gesture's plan at Fit ([a tick](#a-tick)). Besides it, an evidence run's test hook, `--evidence-gpu-identity`, draws the photograph at Fit through the identity program over a boundary held from the frame on screen, unless the `gpu_preview` preference is off. The `gpu-identity` smoke scenario checks the stage's frames, the status label, and the preference turned off and on from the palette.
- **How the plan reaches the surface.** The plan is preview state like the proxy, not a history or API result. It travels in the desktop's typed owner reply: the preview job the draft's synchronous `draft.set` answers with carries the draft's `GpuPreview` (`PreviewJob::gpu`), planned on the catalog owner by `plan_preview` (`crates/luxforge-core/src/render/gpu/preview.rs`) in `O(layers)` with no pixel read, so a tick adds no hop (performance rule 12). The desktop asks for it at Fit, at the job's display bounds, and at a percentage zoom of 100% or more over the visible region (`PreviewRequest::gpu_region`, `GpuView::Region`, [at 100% and above](#at-100-and-above)); below 100% a percentage view plans none. It is planned with `gpu_plan_with` over the job's own render context, whose estimate store the preview worker's CPU frames fill: a global estimate is read under the key a Fit frame of the same content asks with, the proxy named by its source and plan and never built (`EstimateSource::Proxy`; a RAW proxy's development is derived from its source's development and view and the plan, so it is named the same whenever it is built). A Presence drag therefore reads the light its committed Fit frame stored, and a drag of a layer under Presence, whose ticks change the light's input, takes it on the GPU and its plan says it is approximate (the drag summary's `approximate`). At a percentage zoom the store is read under the exact stage's key (`EstimateSource::Render`), which the exact visible region's render fills; a region plan never takes the estimate on the GPU (`region-estimate`). A committed stack's job carries the plans to warm instead (`PreviewJob::gpu_warm`).

### The held input boundary

A GPU tick evaluates only what the draft changes. When a draft begins, the preview worker renders the **boundary**: the input of the earliest layer the draft changes — the first layer the drafted stack holds differently from the entry, the drafted layer, or the layer its mask modulates, whichever comes first — in content space: at proxy scale at Fit, and at full scale over the window the visible region reads at 100% and above.

- **Asked once, with a tick's own job.** The plan comes with a `BoundaryRequest`: its key, where the boundary layer begins in the compilation, the plan's format and, for a lens or perspective warp, the geometry whose coordinate grid the worker computes with it. The desktop attaches the request to the first CPU tick's preview job, and the worker renders it after that job's Fit frame, as a phase of its own (`PreviewPhase::Boundary`), so the preview queue's one active and one pending job, its cancellation and the quiet policy are unchanged. A job that replaces one carrying the request carries it again; a cancelled queue asks again on the next tick.
- **Contents.** `Render::boundary` (`crates/luxforge-core/src/render/boundary.rs`) builds the layer's segment input exactly as a frame of the whole stack builds it — the (proxy) source, or the frame a resample or a spatial operation writes — and runs the segment's own pass over the operations before the layer, so the prefix's colour, restoration and spatial work is baked in and every value is the one the CPU hands the layer, unclamped inside a colour run. A boundary in the segment the leading restoration prefix opens — a drag of a layer after Detail — reads that prefix's output from the restoration-prefix proxy cache instead (`Render::boundary_reading`), when the cache holds it under the key the same job's Fit frame just asked with, so Detail is not evaluated again for it: in functional `detail-fit` runs on the generated Detail fixture the boundary phase's own render time was 1.3 ms, where building it had been 150 to 430 ms (not a timing measurement). A Detail drag's own boundary is the Detail layer's input, built as any other. A windowed proxy holds only the part of each stage its output reads, so the boundary holds the window's part of the received stage, with its origin there. At a percentage zoom, `Render::region_boundary` holds the window of the received stage the visible region reads at full scale — the region and every margin the spatial operations after the boundary need — through the windowed planner (`WindowPlan::of_rect`) with the exact region's whole-stage spatial estimates, and the worker renders it after the job's region frame.
- **Format.** A JPEG's boundary is `rgba16float`, its values each 8-bit code's linear value and the colour runs over them; a value past the half range is held at the largest finite half. A developed RAW's, on the linear path (`GpuPlanRequest::linear`), is `rgba32float`, every value as the CPU holds it: half rounding of a near-black value can flip the sign of the luminance Presence divides by, which draws green where the CPU draws magenta ([performance](../specs/performance.md#isolated-near-black-pixels)). The format follows the plan's linear flag, and a render asked for the other path's format refuses. The upload, its chunking and the budget's charge follow the bytes a texel takes.
- **Key and lifetime.** The key (`BoundaryKey`) holds the source's identity, development included, the hash of the layers before the boundary, the boundary layer and the proxy plan with its scale and window, or at a percentage zoom the region it is held for. The boundary is uploaded once and held for the open draft only. It is let go when the draft ends, once the committed or cancelled frame is presented or nothing more is coming, and at once when the key changes (the window moved, the bounds changed, a pan left the region or the layers before the boundary did); the GPU-preview slot is retired with it.
- **Until it is ready.** Ticks take the CPU path exactly as today, naming `boundary-pending`, so the first frame of a gesture is never later than now. A boundary that fails keeps the gesture on the CPU path for that key (`boundary-failed`); a boundary past `BOUNDARY_MAX_BYTES`, 128 MiB, is one.

This is the [held-boundary proposal](instant-preview.md#proposals-and-later-work) applied to the GPU: the CPU builds the boundary once per draft, and the GPU evaluates everything after it.

### A tick

1. The input updates the draft on the owner synchronously, as today. The answer carries the draft's GPU preview: its plan and the boundary it starts from, or the reason there is none.
2. With the boundary held and the surface having evaluated it, nothing goes to the preview worker for that tick: no proxy job and no upload (`Editor::gpu_tick`, `crates/luxforge-app/src/app/gpu_preview.rs`). The desktop converts the plan over the held boundary and hands it to the surface tagged with the tick's draft revision. In Mask mode the overlay's coverage still follows the tick through its own worker, over the frame on screen.
3. In the next `prepare`, the surface writes the words and the changed blocks and encodes the passes:
   - a spatial step's compute passes the tick changes;
   - the colour and mask programs in content space, in recipe order, and the spatial step's applies;
   - the geometry tail, by bilinear sampling through the affine matrix or the coordinate grid (linear-light texels, as the CPU's resample blends), quantized as the CPU's segments are on a JPEG;
   - the finish programs (the vignette) and any colour operation of the output stage, at the output pixel;
   - the output encoding, the CPU's output quantizer's codes.
4. The frame draws in that same update.

Any other tick takes the CPU path as before and names why, in `state.surface.gpu.plan_fallback`: the preference is off (`preference-off`), the mask overlay is shown at a percentage zoom (`overlay-shown`), the view is a percentage below 100% (`not-fit`), the plan's own reason (`region-estimate` among them), a region's boundary or slot would pass its bound or the budget (`budget-exceeded`), the boundary is not held yet or failed (`boundary-pending`, `boundary-failed`), the surface cannot run the plan (`warp-grid`, `boundary-size`, `position-range`, `region-outside`), or the surface has not evaluated it yet or fell back (`surface-pending`, or the surface's reason, `compiling` and `budget-exceeded` among them). A GPU frame is drawn only once the frame on screen is the draft's own, so a drafted frame never shows under an older CPU frame's revision. The release commits on the CPU as before; the drawn plan stays until the committed frame is presented.

Colour runs before geometry, as on the CPU, so a strong curve across an edge is not interpolated in the wrong order. A lens or perspective warp's coordinate grid is computed once per draft from `GeometryMap` (`GpuGeometry::grid`, `crates/luxforge-core/src/render/gpu/grid.rs`), at a density that keeps the mapping within 0.1 display pixel of exact:

- Nodes at a regular output spacing hold the boundary coordinate the map gives them. The spacing starts at 64 px and narrows until the displacement it causes, measured at every cell centre and edge midpoint, is within half the contract. For a stage drawn at more than one display pixel per output pixel, that target is divided by the magnification.
- On the fixtures' strongest lenses the spacing is 8 px on a 1024-pixel Fit stage, 11 to 12 px on a 2048-pixel one, 20 px at 6048 × 4024 and 21 px over a 100% region. The strongest perspective takes 15 to 21 px at Fit and 30 to 37 px at full size. The worst displacement measured at the pixels, through the map's exact inverse, is 0.041 px.
- The surface interpolates the nodes in `f32` arithmetic. A sampler's linear filtering would not hold the contract: its weights carry too few bits.
- A grid holds at most 262,144 nodes (2 MiB); the strongest Fit fixture needs about 23,500 (183 KiB). A warp that needs more takes the CPU path.

### At 100% and above

At a percentage zoom of 100% or more a drag is drawn over the **visible region** at full scale, where the CPU path draws a half-scale motion region (`Editor::gpu_ask`, `crates/luxforge-app/src/app/gpu_preview.rs`).

- **The region.** A tick asks for the plan over the region of the output stage the view shows, as the desktop computes it for its region jobs (`viewport_rect`, a guard pixel each side), or over the region its drag already asked for at this zoom while that region still holds the view, so a pan inside it keeps the boundary. The owner plans it at the exact stage, keyed by the region, with the zoom as the magnification a warp's grid is made dense enough for.
- **The boundary.** The first tick's region job carries the request, and the worker renders the region's boundary after the job's region frame ([the held input boundary](#the-held-input-boundary)). A spatial step sees that window alone.
- **The frame.** The plan names its region (`GpuPlan::region`). With no tail the content pass reads the region out of the held window; with one, the tail draws the region's output pixels, each offset by the region's origin through two words after the texel map, so every program sees its pixel's whole-stage coordinate. The surface places the output at the region of the photograph and draws it alone: never a CPU region of other content beside it.
- **No region job.** While the frame holds the view and the surface has evaluated it, a tick sends nothing to the worker, and the motion it notes asks for no region job either (`Editor::gpu_draws_view`). The shared quiet policy settles the view as before: its exact visible region, behind which the plan is then held, and the updating histogram.
- **A pan.** A view the region does not hold withdraws the plan at once, so the surface draws the CPU's frames, the view's own region job bringing the area it now shows, until a tick plans the new region and its boundary is held; that tick lets the old one go (`key-changed`).
- **The budget.** The request names the window the boundary will hold (`BoundaryRequest::window`), planned on the owner by the windowed planner. A region whose boundary would pass the 128 MiB bound on one, or whose slot at least — the boundary, a tail's intermediate, the frame and a spatial step's planes over the window (`region_charge`) — would pass the GPU-preview budget, asks for no boundary and keeps the CPU path, naming `budget-exceeded`, with the figure and the bound in the drag's evidence (`state.surface.gpu.gpu_preview.drag.over_budget`). A slot the surface finds over the budget once the boundary is held names it too ([bounds](#bounds)).
- **Dehaze.** The exact visible region reads the whole stage's atmospheric light, so a region plan reads it from the estimate store under the exact stage's key (`EstimateSource::Render`), which the exact visible region's render fills. While the store holds none the plan would take it on the GPU from the region alone, which misses the spatial limits on the corpus, and the drag is the CPU path (`region-estimate`) until a settled region has stored it ([spatial programs](#spatial-programs)).
- **Overlays.** At a percentage zoom the mask overlay is drawn from the CPU's region frames, which a GPU frame drawn alone does not carry, so a gesture keeps the CPU path while it is shown (`overlay-shown`). A clipping overlay is drawn by the plan's own marks, as at Fit.
- **Settle.** The exact visible region replaces the GPU frame with a swap (`gpu_settle_swapped`, why `not fit`), not a dissolve: the surface's dissolve lays the whole-frame photograph over the GPU frame at its share, and at a percentage zoom the frame that settles is a region picture, which the dissolve does not draw. The GPU region output keeps its region key while a dissolve holds the slot, so a percentage dissolve is drawing `dissolved_output()` at its rectangle with the region pictures laid over it at the share, and the desktop following the region picture's version in place of the photograph's. Both frames are full scale and meet the limits against each other on the corpus.
- **Evidence.** Each captured frame records `state.surface.gpu.visible_region` and `plan_region`, the view's region and the region of the plan handed to the surface, each `[x0, y0, x1, y1]`, and the drag's `boundary.region` and `zoom`. The `gpu-preview-zoom` scenario drags at 100%, 200% and, panning, 800%.

### Settle and the dissolve

The shared quiet policy and release start CPU settlement as today: the proxy, or the exact visible region, and then the exact phase. When the CPU frame for the GPU frame's content arrives, the surface dissolves from one to the other in linear light over 150 ms.

**The desktop** (`crates/luxforge-app/src/app/gpu_settle.rs`) follows the GPU frame on screen after every message: the gesture tick the surface drew last, tagged with its draft revision and boundary, while its plan is drawn in place of the CPU frame. When a newer CPU frame replaces it at Fit, the desktop hands the surface a dissolve from that revision to the new frame, if the new frame shows the GPU frame's content or newer:

- **Held.** A CPU frame of the open draft at the GPU frame's revision or a newer one: a tick the surface could not draw sent its job to the worker, or the shared quiet policy settled the drafted settings. The plan is held behind it for the next tick, and the dissolve runs behind the held plan.
- **Committed.** The frame of the entry the draft committed, once the draft has ended and the entry on screen is no longer the one the GPU frame was drawn over. A release commits at once, so this is the usual case.
- **Never older content.** A cancelled draft shows the entry it was drawn over again, and a CPU frame of an older revision is older content: each replaces the GPU frame with no dissolve (`gpu_settle_swapped`).
- **Cancelled by any input.** A newer tick of the draft, or another gesture beginning, cancels a running dissolve (`gpu_dissolve_cancelled`, why `input`), and the surface draws what that input brings. So does a change to what is drawn over the photograph: a clipping overlay turned on or off, a zoom away from Fit or a comparison (why `view`), so the CPU frame's own overlay is drawn at once rather than after the dissolve. A newer CPU frame of the same content, such as the exact phase after the proxy, keeps the dissolve running to it (`gpu_dissolve_retargeted`); other content cuts it (`gpu_dissolve_cut`). It ends after its 150 ms, at the next message (`gpu_dissolve_ended`); the surface's own wake at its end is that message.
- **Evidence.** Each start records the GPU frame's revision (`from`), the CPU frame's version (`to`), its boundary and its case (`gpu_dissolve_started`); `state.surface.gpu.settle` reports the running dissolve, how many began and were cancelled, and the last decision. A change of what is drawn over the photograph is recorded when it takes effect (`gpu_settle_view`).

**The surface** (`crates/luxforge-ui/src/photo_surface/gpu_preview/dissolve.rs`): `PhotoSurface::dissolve` takes a `Dissolve`, the GPU frame it starts from as the caller identifies it (a draft revision), the version of the CPU frame it ends on, and when it began.

- **Linear light.** For the 150 ms the surface keeps its GPU-preview slot, draws the slot's output, and lays the CPU frame over it through the photograph's own pipeline, placement and filter, with the dissolve's share (the time since it began over 150 ms) as its opacity. The stage runs only on an sRGB-typed target, where the hardware blends in linear light, so the picture is `(1 − t)·gpu + t·cpu` of the linear values. Read back on the M4, a mix at one half of linear 0 and 1 draws code 188, where a gamma-space blend would draw 128. Once the share reaches one the frame is the CPU's alone and the slot is released.
- **Redraws are requested only while a dissolve runs**, so an idle editor stays asleep (performance rule 8). The widget asks for the next frame from its own update, at each redraw's own time, and the redraw that finds the dissolve ended asks for nothing. No timer, subscription or desktop update runs for it; its start and its end each wake the desktop once, as a change of drawing path does.
- **An input during a dissolve cancels it** and draws the next GPU frame: a plan drawn beside a dissolve cancels it, and the plan is evaluated in the same slot. A plan held behind the CPU frame keeps it: the held plan's unchanged words leave the slot's output as the GPU frame last shown.
- **Nothing to dissolve from.** When the last frame drawn was the CPU's, or the stage fell back, the CPU frame is drawn alone. A dissolve into a frame not yet in the photograph's texture, which a deferred upload can leave, is not drawn either, and the slot is released.
- **Evidence and label.** Each captured frame's `state.surface.gpu.dissolve` records the dissolve the draw drew: `from`, `to`, the `gpu_boundary` under it and its `progress`. During a dissolve `drawing_path` is `cpu`, the CPU frame over the GPU one, and `drawn_gpu_boundary` names the GPU output under it. The status bar's render slot names the CPU frame from the dissolve's first frame, as it does for any CPU frame.

### Labels and overlays during motion

- **Status.** While the surface draws the GPU stage's output for the plan the desktop hands it, the status bar's render slot reads "GPU preview · N ms", in place of "Rendering…" or "Rendering… N%" while a CPU render still runs; the bar over the photograph is unaffected. A gesture drawn on the GPU at Fit shows it, as does the evidence run's GPU identity hook. The histogram keeps its existing updating state.
  - **What N is.** N is the interface thread's time to prepare the GPU frame on screen in `prepare`: fitting the slot, writing the words and blocks, uploading a new boundary, encoding and submitting the pass. The GPU's own execution is not in it, and nothing waits for or reads back the GPU. Evidence records it as `state.surface.gpu.gpu_preview_frame_us` and the status bar's `gpu_ms`. Only a draw knows which path drew it, so the label describes the frame the surface drew last: a change of drawing path wakes the desktop, and the update that wake runs names the new path.
  - **Why not GPU timestamps.** Iced 0.14's compositor requests its device with no optional features, and no setting changes that, so the device the surface receives has no timestamp queries on any adapter.
  - **Why not the queue's completion callback.** wgpu 27 runs a `Queue::on_submitted_work_done` callback from the maintenance of the first `queue.submit`, `device.poll` or `instance.poll_all` that finds the work complete: wgpu-core's `Queue::submit` ends with a nonblocking `Device::maintain`, and on Metal the fence's completed value is set by each command buffer's completion handler. Iced submits every frame it draws, so the callback needs no poll or timer. But the figure it gives runs to the submit that learns of the completion, not to the completion. On the M4 Pro (Metal), for a 2400 × 1600 boundary over 60 frames, a frame submit made straight after the draw found the pass done in 0 to 2 of 60 frames, and a test-only blocking wait, which itself wakes late, saw the GPU done 1.6 to 2.1 ms p50 after the start for the identity program and 3.4 to 4.2 ms for a chain of transcendental-heavy programs, against 0.08 to 0.29 ms p50 of preparation (`gpu_frame_completion_is_measured`, four runs on a host at load average 25: diagnostics, not baselines). In the editor, the `gpu-identity` scenario's 320 × 480 identity frames, microseconds of GPU work, reported completion 2.9 ms and 10.2 ms after the start: whenever the next frame was submitted. As a label it would mostly read the frame cadence, so the surface keeps it as evidence only, `state.surface.gpu.gpu_preview_done_us` (the newest pass the queue has reported complete, read live), an upper bound on when the GPU finished.
- **Clipping.** The CPU's clipping overlay is a cell grid derived from the CPU frame on screen, so over a GPU frame it would mark another frame's pixels. While a clipping overlay is shown, a GPU tick's plan, and the warm sequences, end with the surface's clipping marks (`GpuStep::Clipping`, `crates/luxforge-ui/src/photo_surface/gpu_preview/clipping.rs`): in the last pass, after every colour and geometry step, a pixel with a channel the CPU's quantizer takes to code 0 (a NaN included) or 255 — by the thresholds the core's plan names — is laid over with the overlay's own colour for its class at its own opacity, the photograph's codes and the colour decoded and blended in linear light as the surface composites the CPU overlay. Read back on the M4 within a code of the reference's composite for every class combination. While the GPU output is drawn in place of the photograph, the CPU frame's overlay is not drawn. The marks are approximate, per pixel of the stage the plan draws where the CPU overlay ORs each display cell, and evidence says so (`state.surface.gpu.clipping_marks`, with `approximate: true`). Whole-image counts stay the CPU's analysis, and the CPU frame's own overlay replaces the marks on settle. A clipping toggle during a drag reaches the plan with the next tick.
- **Mask tint.** During a GPU mask drag, the tint is the coverage worker's grid as on the CPU path: each tick asks for it, and in the `gpu-preview` scenario it lands about 8 ms after the tick, so it follows the drag at the worker's pace, and it stays authoritative throughout. It is not drawn from the program's coverage, which would be disproportionate: a tint is a function of the mask's geometry rather than of the frame's pixels, so the worker's grid is already the GPU frame's own tint, where clipping's was another frame's. Drawing it on the GPU would carry the selected mask's coverage from the content pass through the geometry tail as a second channel and compose it in each overlay mode, and the selected mask is often in no plan at all: one with no adjustment bound to it, or bound to a layer before the boundary.

### The preference

`workspace.set {gpu_preview: bool}` holds the per-client preference, reported in session state and `schema.list`, on by default. The command palette's Turn off GPU preview and Turn on GPU preview entry sends the same `workspace.set`, and the desktop adopts the owner's answer; a desktop test holds the palette and an agent's call to the same session state. `Editor::gpu_preview_allowed` and `Editor::gpu_plan` (`crates/luxforge-app/src/app/gpu_settle.rs`) are the desktop's one gate: with the preference off it hands the surface no plan, so every frame is the CPU's, and the evidence snapshot names `state.surface.gpu.plan_fallback` `{"reason": "preference-off"}`. The `gpu-identity` scenario turns it off and on from the palette.

The session state and the evidence snapshot report which path drew each frame (`gpu` or `cpu`), and why a GPU-eligible gesture fell back:

- the preference is off
- a clipping overlay is shown
- the view is not at Fit
- the boundary is not held yet, or failed
- the sequence is still compiling
- no adapter
- the device was lost
- a shader failed to compile
- the budget would be exceeded
- a unit has no program, or its program is disabled
- a pixel-stage layer is in the stack

The desktop's own client sets the preference; an agent reads it through the same method.

### Fallback and portability

- Losing the device mid-gesture moves the next tick to the CPU path with its reason; nothing waits for recovery.
- Every gesture works with no GPU path at all.
- Windows and Linux get functional checks on whatever adapter they have, including a software adapter. These are not native performance evidence. Native GPU evidence is the owner's M4.

### Bounds

- **One budget.** Every GPU-preview texture and buffer is charged to one 256 MiB budget, beside the photo-texture ceiling and reported with it: the boundary, intermediates, spatial accumulators, coordinate grids and storage blocks.
  - Today that is each surface's one GPU-preview slot: the boundary, a geometry tail's intermediate, the output and its placement uniform, the words and blocks buffers, and a spatial step's planes, the budget shared by every surface. A slot lives as long as the gesture's held boundary. A plan whose planes would pass it takes the CPU path and names the budget.
  - An allocation that would pass it is refused before anything is created.
  - A released, replaced or outgrown resource retires through the photo surface's retirement worker and stays charged until the GPU is done with it, so the in-use figure returns to zero once a released slot has retired.
  - Each captured frame's `state.surface.gpu` reports `gpu_preview_budget_bytes`, `gpu_preview_in_use_bytes`, `gpu_preview_peak_bytes`, `gpu_preview_passes` and `gpu_preview_spatial_passes` (the compute passes the spatial steps have dispatched, which a tick that moves only an apply's gain leaves unchanged) beside the photo-texture figures, with `drawing_path` (`gpu` or `cpu`), `gpu_fallback` (its reason, and the figures that refused it) and `drawn_gpu_boundary`.
- **Typical sizes.**
  - A Fit boundary is at most 8 MP at 8 bytes per pixel on a JPEG, so at most 64 MiB, and 16 bytes per pixel on a RAW.
  - A 100% boundary is the visible region plus the margin the spatial operations after it read, and the desktop knows its window before it asks for it ([at 100% and above](#at-100-and-above)). In the largest window the M4's display holds, a 3026 × 1826 region, a colour or masked slot charges 82.0 MB on a JPEG and 126.2 to 172.8 MB on a RAW, a crop or warp up to 209.3 MB, Clarity 152.9 MB and Texture 219.5 MB on the 60 MP JPEG. Texture and Clarity together, all three Presence fields and Detail pass the budget there, and on a RAW any Clarity margin passes the 128 MiB bound on a boundary: those take the CPU path and name the budget ([performance](../specs/performance.md#gpu-previews-at-100)).
  - A spatial program's planes are its largest consumer: all three Presence units hold about 24 bytes a pixel of the stage (Texture 20 at full resolution, Clarity and Dehaze about 4 together on their 4× grids), 38 MB over a 1.57 MP stage. A 2 MP Fit stage then holds about 80 MB with its boundary and output, and an 8 MP one passes the budget and takes the CPU path.
  - Detail's two units hold 68 bytes a pixel ([spatial programs](#spatial-programs)): over the 60 MP JPEG's 1716 × 1030 Fit stage the slot holds 151.1 MB (144.1 MiB) with its boundary and output, over the evidence window's whole 1716 × 1508 Fit bounds 213.5 MB, and a JPEG stage past about 3.3 MP passes the budget and takes the CPU path. A RAW's `f32` boundary and a lens profile's coordinate grid add to it: the Air 2S at Fit holds 197.3 MB.
- **When it does not fit.** A plan that would not fit takes the CPU path and names the budget. The GPU-preview budget is not part of the 1088 MiB photo-texture engineering ceiling. Like the crop stage's textures, it is a GPU resource the standing GPU memory accounting work must count before any total-memory claim.

### Spatial programs

Presence and Detail become compute programs over the boundary, run as the surface's spatial step. They use the CPU's operations, not its exact arithmetic. The GPU holds a whole proxy stage or region, so it has no tile halo to recompute.

- **Presence** (`modules/presence/presence.wgsl`, described pass for pass by `modules/presence/gpu.rs`): separable box means and minima, the self-guided and guided filters' coefficients and smoothing as finishes of the vertical pass, the 4× block reduction anchored at the stage origin and the bilinear upsample, the soft clip, and the atmospheric light. Every coefficient is a word holding the `f32` the CPU unit holds; the restated luminance constants are held to the CPU's bits through `naga`.
  - **Texture**: two self-guided smoothers of the encoded input at full resolution, four passes each. The fine one is held; the coarse one's last pass writes the band itself into the plane its coefficients used, so Texture holds three full-resolution planes (a pair of sums, the coefficients and then the band, and the fine smoother: 20 bytes a pixel).
  - **Clarity**: the encoded input reduced 4×, one self-guided smoother there, the apply upsampling it.
  - **Dehaze**: the atmospheric light, the colour reduced 4× beside its dark channel normalized by that light, the dark channel's box minimum, the raw transmission and its guide, the transmission's guided refinement, and the apply upsampling it within the floor.
- **Detail** (`modules/detail/detail.wgsl`, described pass for pass by `modules/detail/gpu.rs`): each unit's input in Oklab; separable smoothing in difference form, the horizontal pass then the vertical, every tap's offset and weight a word, so the same two kernels run the B3 à-trous levels at full resolution, the sampled Gaussians a unit compiled at a proxy's scale holds, and sharpening's blur and guide together; each wavelet level's band, its 3 × 3 energy and the soft shrinkage that energy gates, added to the change the levels before it accumulated; sharpening's coring, the guide's gradient-energy gate, the 3 × 3 extrema and the `tanh` limiter; and each apply's reconstruction from Oklab with the neutral snap. The restated Oklab matrices and the snap are held to the CPU's bits through `naga`.
  - **Noise reduction**: its Oklab input, then for each level the two smoothing passes and the shrinkage. Four `rgba32float` planes: the current and next levels' smoothing, swapped each level, and two that each level's horizontal pass and shrinkage take in turn, so the change accumulated so far is never overwritten before the next level reads it. The last level's change is the plane the apply reads: 64 bytes a pixel.
  - **Capture sharpening**: its Oklab input, the blur and guide of its lightness smoothed together, and the cored, gated and limited change of lightness, an `r32float` plane the apply reads. Its three `rgba32float` scratch planes reuse noise reduction's, so the two units hold 68 bytes a pixel.
  - Only the Oklab passes read their unit's input, and every level's shrinkage binds three planes, the first reading its smoothing twice in place of a change it has none of, so the 17 passes of both units are 6 pipelines. A drag changes words alone; a strength crossing zero adds or removes a unit, and Colour crossing it adds or removes the fourth level.

**Running sums are reseeded at each run.** A box mean's pass runs once for every 16 outputs along its axis: a direct sum seeds the run, then the entering value is added and the leaving one subtracted, in `f32`. The drift grows with the run, not the row, and measured against the CPU's `f64` running sums it is at most 1.1 × 10⁻⁶ of a unit for every run from 1 to 64 and radius from 1 to 38 ([qualifying](#qualifying-a-program)). A two-level prefix sum would need a plane for the prefix and two more passes for every box; reseeding needs neither, so it is the cheaper of the two, and the run is a word, measured rather than fixed in the kernel.

**The global estimate.** Dehaze's atmospheric light comes from the estimate store when its key matches the boundary's content ([planning](#where-the-code-lives)), written as a one-texel plane by one pass. Otherwise the GPU takes it from the stage it holds: the 16× reduction of the unit's input beside each block's channel minimum, then one 256-lane workgroup that bisects the minima's order to find the threshold, takes every block above it and the ties at it in row-major order, and averages their colour, each channel floored. Over a whole stage it selects exactly the blocks the CPU's preparation does, and the frame is labelled approximate, as the CPU proxy already is. Over a 100% region it would see the region alone, where the exact visible region reads the whole stage's light: that misses the spatial limits on two RAWs, so a region plan reads the light from the store, which the exact visible region's render fills, and is the CPU path (`region-estimate`) while it holds none.

### Mipmapped minification

Before/After keeps the exact raster it displays as its After side, even at Fit ([before and after](before-after.md#after-at-fit)). A full-resolution texture drawn at that size through the bilinear sampler with no mip levels aliased: on the zone plate it showed full-contrast replica rings and read 15 codes too dark beyond the display's Nyquist limit, the same failure the crop draft's input stage showed ([instant previews](instant-preview.md#a-crop-drafts-input-stage); [performance](../specs/performance.md#a-minified-after-side-at-fit)).

The photo surface now gives a full-resolution photograph drawn under half its size on both axes a chain of mip levels, and otherwise leaves its texture as it was. No arithmetic in the recipe changes, so no error limit applies.

- **Linear light.** Each level is the mean of the 2 × 2 texels above it, written by a render pass that reads the level above through an sRGB-typed view of the texture and writes the next level through the same, so the mean is taken in linear light whether the pipeline's textures are sRGB-typed or not. The sampler's trilinear filter reads between the two levels that bracket the drawn scale.
- **GPU only, never waited on.** The passes are encoded and submitted from `prepare` on the UI thread, once for each new frame written while it is drawn that small, with no readback and no wait.
- **Accounting.** The levels belong to the photo slot that holds them: a chain is a third more than its base, counted in the slot's bytes, the shared photo-slot budget and the retirement of the slot, and reported as `mip_resident_bytes` and `mip_generations`. A texture that has a chain keeps it until it is rebuilt; a texture drawn at its size has none. A chain's texture is cut to the frame's exact size, which for a 24 MP frame is smaller than the bucket a plain texture reserves.
- **When the chain does not fit.** The chain needs the photograph in one texture and its whole size within one full-allocation cap. A photograph the device holds in tiles (wider or taller than 8192 pixels) has none, because each tile's filtering apron would put a seam into every level. Before/After then draws the exact-derived display reduction it holds of that photograph at Fit instead, so a 60 MP After has no full-size texture while compared at Fit.

## What is preserved

- Originals, recipes, history, drafts, commits and every mutation are untouched. The CPU proxy, exact phase, analysis, sampling, overlays and export produce the same bytes as before, which the existing exact tests and fixture hashes prove.
- The owner thread plans in `O(layers)` and touches no pixel. The UI thread encodes GPU commands and never renders on the CPU or waits on the GPU.
- Every GPU-preview allocation is bounded and released with its draft. The preview queue's one active and one pending job, its cancellation and the shared quiet policy are unchanged.

## Verification

- **Reference.** An `f64` CIEDE2000 and the four statistics in `luxforge-reference` (`preview_error`), checked against the published CIEDE2000 test pairs. `cargo xtask preview-error` reads two captures, or two frames of an evidence run, and writes the statistics and both classes' verdicts ([development](../engineering/development.md#commands)).

A test without an adapter reports that it was skipped, never a pass.
- **The plan.** Core tests plan every stack shape of the render table from every boundary and draw each plan through a test-only reference executor, which runs a Rust twin of each test program, against the CPU frame: the same codes through an exact tail, within one code across a resample or a mask, within two through a warp's grid. The grid is measured against the strongest lens and perspective fixtures (`cargo test -p luxforge-core --lib gpu`).
- **Corpus**, listed with hashes or explicit gaps in [`fixtures/preview/corpus.json`](../../fixtures/preview/corpus.json) (`cargo xtask preview-corpus`).
  - Sources: the generated 24 MP and 60 MP JPEGs, the zone plate, the Presence fixture and the Z6, X100VI and Air 2S RAWs.
  - Recipes: full Basic, the Tone curve, the Mixer, the Vignette, every mask kind, a straightened crop, a lens and perspective warp, Presence combinations and Detail.

- **Native.** Rendered scenarios with correlated state and logs: path, reason, dissolve and preference, with readbacks of GPU and settled frames. The `gpu-preview` scenario drags Basic and Detail, moves a gradient, paints a stroke, and drags Texture, Clarity and Basic under Presence at Fit, and checks each gesture's one boundary request, its GPU ticks with no preview job, each GPU frame's boundary and draft revision against the state recorded with it, its pixels against the CPU frame of the same settings, and the slot let go once the release has settled. For the settle it checks that each commit replacing a GPU frame dissolves from that frame's revision and boundary; that the drag's GPU frame against the settled frame that replaced it meets the pointwise limits over the whole photograph (mean ΔE00 1.1 × 10⁻⁵, worst block 0.014, p99 0 on the M4); that an `idle` evidence step after the release's and the stroke's dissolves sees one frame and one update in a second, its own; that with the preference off from the palette a drag takes the CPU path naming it and starts no dissolve; that a drag with both clipping overlays shown is drawn on the GPU with its own marks; and that turning the clipping overlays off, and the next gesture's first tick, the Detail drag's, each cancel a release's dissolve that still runs when the input takes effect (59 to 78 ms in on the M4). ### Qualifying a program

**The readback.** The photo surface's `qualification` module (`crates/luxforge-ui/src/photo_surface/gpu_preview/qualification.rs`) runs a plan through the stage's own assembled shader on a headless device and reads the output back: as `f32` from an `rgba32float` target, where a non-finite value cannot hide behind an encoding, or as the codes of the stage's own sRGB-typed format. It is built only with the crate's `qualification` feature, which only a `[dev-dependencies]` table may turn on (`check-repository`'s `gpu-qualification-only-in-tests`), so no build of the desktop reads a GPU pixel back.

**Per program** (`crates/luxforge-app/src/app/gpu_colour_tests.rs`). Each unit's program runs over a deterministic grid of 65,536 pixels (98,304 for the vignette, on a 3:2 stage): channels uniform in encoded sRGB, past white to 16, below black to −0.5, and the extremes of a half float (±65,504, the smallest normals, zeros of both signs), one pixel in ten an exact grey. The cases are every parameter at both ends, alone and together, and the corpus's own values: 4 for Exposure, 5 for White balance, 14 for Tone, 9 for ColourAdjust, 8 curves (the corpus S-curve, a steep wall, a lifted black, two flats, crushed ends, sixteen knots and collapsed knots), 16 for the mixer and 31 for the vignette. The CPU unit runs over the same half-float inputs. A program is held to the pointwise limits on its `f32` output through the reference quantizer, and to the finiteness rule. The codes the hardware encoder draws are reported beside them.

No program gives a non-finite value anywhere, and no CPU unit does either on this grid. Exposure's arithmetic is exact. Over pixels whose inputs lie within [−1, 16], the largest linear difference is 8.3 × 10⁻⁴ relative. The ColourAdjust and mixer maxima come from pixels with one channel at ±65,504 beside small ones: an Oklab round trip at that scale leaves both processors' small channels as rounding of the large one, and they round differently. The Tone curve's worst case is the flat curve at encoded 0.5, which is exactly the threshold between codes 127 and 128, so every pixel of the frame lies on it.

**The hardware encoder.** The drawn figures add the sRGB-typed target's own encoding, which does not round every value as the CPU's quantizer does. Exposure's program is exact, so its drawn mean of 0.096 at −5 EV is the encoder alone, concentrated in the shadows. The flat curve on a code threshold misses the signed ΔL\* limit through the encoder alone: drawn −0.260 against a program's −0.013, where the encoder takes code 127 for a value the CPU quantizes to 128. A flat curve at encoded 0.45 draws within 0.002. Encoding the output in the shader to match the CPU quantizer is the alternative; it is open, and the output encoding is part of the [tick's](#a-tick) last step.

**The Presence program** (`crates/luxforge-app/src/app/gpu_presence_tests.rs`, `cargo test -p luxforge-app gpu_presence`) is held to the CPU in three ways, on the M4 Pro ([performance](../specs/performance.md#gpu-presence-program)):

- **Per filter**, on synthetic planes, each kernel through the surface's own assembled passes against the CPU filter it transcribes, which the core's test-only `qualification` feature exposes (`luxforge_core::qualification`; `check-repository`'s `core-qualification-only-in-tests`): the box mean within 1.1 × 10⁻⁶ at every radius from 1 to 38 and every run from 1 to 64, the box minimum exactly, the self-guided filter within 4.4 × 10⁻⁶ and the guided filter within 2.0 × 10⁻⁵ at the units' regularizations, the 4× and 16× reductions within 2.4 × 10⁻⁷, the upsample and the soft clip within 1.5 × 10⁻⁸, and the atmospheric light equal to the CPU's, ties and a selection past 16 blocks included.
- **Per unit**, every Presence combination at both ends, unmasked and masked by a feathered radial, over a synthetic photograph at two sizes, against the CPU frame of the same stack on the linear path, the light both stored and taken on the GPU: the program's output is within a mean of 0.0007, a worst block of 0.009 and a p99 of 0.00, and finite everywhere. On the byte path, where the boundary holds each 8-bit code's value as a half float, all three fields at ±100 are within a mean of 0.09, a worst block of 0.69 and a p99 of 0.76.
- **On the corpus at Fit**, every Presence recipe on every source this host has, the masked one included, within the spatial limits; the program was enabled on these figures.

A few isolated pixels move far: where the luminance-ratio reconstruction divides by a luminance near zero that a channel's sign straddles, as in a RAW's shadow noise, the half-float boundary moves that luminance enough to change the ratio entirely. On the Air 2S with all three fields, 45 of 1.96 M pixels differ by more than eight codes; no statistic the limits judge notices them.

**The Detail program** (`crates/luxforge-app/src/app/gpu_detail_tests.rs`, `cargo test -p luxforge-app gpu_detail`) is held to the CPU in the same three ways on the M4 Pro, and is enabled on these figures ([performance](../specs/performance.md#gpu-detail-program)):

- **Per kernel**, through the surface's own passes against the CPU kernel it transcribes (`luxforge_core::qualification::detail`): the B3 smoothing exactly, the sampled Gaussians (2 to 48 taps) within 2.4 × 10⁻⁷, sharpening's blur and guide within 1.2 × 10⁻⁷, a level's band, energy and shrinkage within 3.2 × 10⁻⁸, sharpening's change of lightness within 1.2 × 10⁻⁷, and the Oklab conversion and the reconstruction within a few roundings of their values, finite over the extreme grid, with a zero change returning the input exactly and a snapped grey staying grey.

### Mask coverage

**The readback** (`crates/luxforge-app/src/app/gpu_mask_tests.rs`). Each case plans a mask through a masked Basic layer, converts the plan's mask and reads its coverage back from the surface's own masked step, whose one unit adds one to every channel, so the output less the input is the coverage. The CPU's coverage of the same mask over the same half-float inputs is the reference: the point-sampled field, or under the thin-feature rule the mean of the doubled stage's four pixels, which the plan takes exactly when the CPU's proxy does. The input is a photograph-like 8-bit frame with structure everywhere: a hue wheel whose saturation grows outward over lightness falling down the frame.

**The contour rule, measured.** Along every edge between two neighbouring pixel centres that either coverage crosses one half on, the distance between the two crossings, each where its own values interpolated linearly along that edge reach one half; where one does not cross there, its crossing is extrapolated along the same line, which is how far its contour would have to move. So a pixel put on the other side of one half counts by how far that moves the contour: little where the field is steep, a lot where it is flat. A pixel whose coverage differs by more than 10⁻³ is a **tie** when the CPU's own field takes the GPU's value with its samples moved together by one step of a stage 15 (point-sampled) or 30 (supersampled) times finer, or its input scaled by 1 ± 10⁻⁵: a hard edge or a value threshold passing that close to a sample, where `f32` and `f64` may each take either side. A tie counts as the distance it moved.

**Cases**, each at the corpus's Fit proxy of a 24 MP photograph (1716 × 1144, where a thin feature takes the supersample) and at an exact 1536 × 1024 stage: the corpus's gradient, a rotated one, one from off the frame, a ramp under two Fit pixels and an inverted one at an amount; the corpus's radial, a hard edge, a small thin ellipse, a ramp to the centre and an inverted ellipse under a whole-mask inversion; the corpus's stroke, an erase over hard, feathered and one-point strokes, a stroke limited to a colour and a hard stroke under two Fit pixels wide; the corpus's luminance band, a hard band, one-unit shoulders and a long shadow shoulder; the corpus's colour samples, one picked colour at a tight refine and five at a loose one; and the algebra: the corpus's linear less its radial, an inverted radial intersected with a luminance band and a linear under a whole-mask inversion at 75, and a radial less a brush stroke.

On the M4 Pro (Metal), every case's contour lies within 0.004 px of the CPU's (largest: 0.0037 px, a gradient from off the frame and a stroke limited to a colour), and the largest coverage difference outside ties is 6.6 × 10⁻⁵. The one case with ties is the erase over hard strokes at Fit, supersampled: two pixels whose hard stroke's edge passes 4 × 10⁻⁶ and 7 × 10⁻⁵ doubled-stage pixels from one of their subsamples, which differ by 0.175 in coverage and tie within 1/30 of a pixel. Over the colour programs' extreme synthetic grid (negative, over-range and a half float's extremes), the value-based kinds and a limited stroke are finite wherever the CPU is, within 3.1 × 10⁻⁵ of it.

**A painted stroke.** A stroke painted over 200 ticks after a committed one, each tick planned as the owner plans a draft: every tick's block stays within `BRUSH_GPU_BLOCK_WORDS_MAX` (382 words at most against 316,034), the committed stroke's segments never move, and at every tenth tick the GPU frame meets the pointwise limits against the CPU frame of the same tick (worst: mean 0.007, worst block 0.42, p99 0.32, signed ΔL\* +0.0005). On its curved path the host's decimation moved a kept position on all 199 later ticks, so each rewrote the stroke's own segments and the index: 37,866 of the 39,891 words its blocks held over the 200 ticks were written, about 760 bytes a tick. A zigzag whose kept positions stay put appends exactly one segment a tick (the core's `brush_gpu_tests`).

**The masked corpus.** The masked colour recipes (every kind and the linear-less-radial algebra, each carrying a masked Basic layer) run through the same harness: all 42 measured cells meet the pointwise limits, and `cargo xtask preview-error --class pointwise` exits 0 on every pair ([performance](../specs/performance.md#gpu-mask-coverage-at-fit)). The five programs are enabled on these figures.
