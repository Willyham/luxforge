## Principles

- **The photograph is the only colour on screen.** Chrome is neutral grey in a few tonal steps; one warm accent marks the current state and the active mode; red and blue are reserved for clipping.
- **State on the left, tools on the right.** The left panel answers "what has happened to this photo" (versions and history, with the editor's own work under them). The right panel answers "what can I do" (histogram, modules). The canvas between them is the photograph and nothing else until a mode needs an overlay.
- **Adjustments and modes are different things.** An adjustment module (Basic, later Tone curve, Detail) is a stack of controls that commit on release. A canvas mode (Crop, later Heal and Mask) takes over the canvas, runs a draft and ends with Apply or Cancel. The screen shows which kind is active and never mixes their commit rules.
- **Every visible control is an API call.** The panel is generated from module descriptors, history rows name their action and actor, and any control can hand you its JSON request. Nothing in the design is a GUI-only gesture.
- **Nothing is hidden and nothing is modal.** Unavailable providers, stale previews, conflicts and missing originals appear as notices over the canvas with the explicit actions the core allows. Editing is disabled only when the core disables it, and the status bar says why.

## Module panels

Status: implemented and verified on the M4 Mac on 2026-09-23 against the [Module panels board](develop-workspace/module-panels.png) and the per-module references in `develop-workspace/modules/`, with the differences that remain listed under [what is not matched](#what-is-not-matched). This is the exact design of each section the tools panel generates, in registry order, from the descriptors `module.list` returns. The desktop's job is to lay the modules' own controls, ranges, steps, units, zero points, rails, groups and resets out at one density with one hierarchy. The descriptors declare what the layout needs through the existing vocabulary: `temperature` and `tint` [rail hints](ui-components.md#parameter-kinds-and-hints) on Basic's white-balance sliders and their RAW variants, icon names on the four transform actions and on As shot, `style: field` on the pixel proof's X and Y, `collapsed` on Presence, the mixer, Transforms, Crop and straighten and Vignette, and the module-level `layout: tabs` the colour mixer declares so its three groups render as tabs. A layout hint changes only what a client draws, never what the host accepts, which is the vocabulary's rule for every hint.

### Density

The rows are tight enough that a panel of many sliders stays scannable and every section header stays on screen at the reference size. Every size below is a `theme.rs` constant pinned by a widget test, so all uses of a widget move together.

| --- | --- | --- |
| Slider row | 28 pt on a 30 pt pitch: 14 pt label line, 2 pt gap, 12 pt rail line | The value sits on the label line, right-aligned in a 48 pt box, unit once; the rail line holds the 2 pt rail, the 1 × 6 pt zero tick (only for a zero inside the rail) and the 12 pt thumb with a 1 pt dark ring. A dragged thumb loses the ring and gains an 18 pt accent halo |
| Sub-group header | 24 pt, 4 pt above | 11 pt semibold secondary text, then a hairline rule to the Original or Custom caption and the group's reset |

### Hierarchy

Three levels, each with one visual device, so a person can tell a module from a group from a control without reading:

- **Module = band.** The header row sits on the Bar surface (`#232326`) against the Panel body (`#202023`), with a 1 px border above. Expanded or collapsed, the band is the same height, so the panel does not jump. The band carries the disclosure, the title, the accent dot when the module has a non-neutral layer, and either the hint (collapsed) or the module reset (expanded). An unavailable module's band shows its reason in the clipping red and does not expand.
- **Group = rule.** A sub-group header is 11 pt semibold secondary text followed by a hairline rule that runs to the caption. The rule is what separates groups from the sliders above them without a second background. The caption reads Original or Custom for a field-patch group, Custom in the accent, and the group's reset sits after it. A collapsed group keeps its header and drops the rule.
- **A module's only group has no header.** A stacked module whose controls are exactly one group draws that group's controls straight under its band: a header naming the module's only group repeats the band, so there is no rule, no disclosure, no Original or Custom caption and no group reset, and the controls are always shown, whatever the group declares as `collapsed`. The band keeps the module's own reset and its accent dot. Presence, Transforms, Vignette and the developer Pixel and Controls sections are drawn this way, and none of their groups declares a reset that the band does not already offer: Controls' names the module's own action, Presence's and Vignette's restate it as a preset, and Transforms and Pixel declare none. The rule is the view model's, so the descriptor still declares the group and `module.list` still lists it. A tabbed module is unaffected, since its groups are its tabs.
- **Tabs = alternative groups.** A module whose groups are parallel views of one set of controls, such as the mixer's Hue, Saturation and Luminance over the same eight colours, declares `layout: tabs` and the desktop draws its groups as one segmented row instead of stacked headers: one group visible at a time, a dot on every tab whose group is Custom so a hidden group's edits stay visible, and the visible group's reset at the row's right. The selected tab is per-client view state, like a collapsed group; it changes no recipe, and every group's fields keep their API. Stacked groups remain the default and stay right for Basic, whose groups are different controls rather than views of the same ones.
- **Control = row.** Sliders, fields and button rows sit flush inside the group with no indent; the group rule above and the module band around them are the only structure. A declared gradient rail replaces the fill on its slider, so a rail's colour is always the module's declared meaning and never a state.

### What is not matched

Measured against the references on 2x captures; each is either something Iced cannot draw or a difference the references themselves do not settle:

- **Framing.** A reference card counts its 1 pt border inside 300 pt, so left-anchored items sit 1 pt left and right-anchored items 1 pt right of the reference.
- **Text.** The workspace draws [Inter](../engineering/dependencies.md) where the references were drawn in the system face: glyphs sit about 0.1 em higher in their line, labels are a few percent wider, and figures are proportional because Iced cannot request `tnum`. Collapsed hints that no longer fit end in an ellipsis. Values show a hyphen-minus where the references draw "−", because the display strings and the history labels come from the same formatting.
- **A module's only group.** The RAW, Presence, Transforms, Vignette and Pixel references draw a sub-group header over the module's only group; the panel draws none, by the [rule](#hierarchy) the owner decided on 2026-09-23. Each of those sections is shorter than its reference by the header, its margin and one row gap: 30 pt, and 28 pt for Transforms, whose icon row then takes a button row's ordinary 4 pt margin instead of the 2 pt under a header.
- **Disclosure chevrons** follow what a group can do rather than the drawings: crop's Ratio and Angle draw none because they cannot collapse.
- **Buttons.** The references draw Apply pixel 26 pt beside a 22 pt Pick pixel; the rule is per row, so it renders 22 pt and the Pixel section is 4 pt shorter.
- **Crop.** The drafting section keeps the custom width and height fields as a row under the chips (the reference shows only the Custom chip), and the angle reads "2.4°" because the crop descriptor declares no display precision. The idle reference draws one Crop button; the idle section draws the drafting section's Ratio and Angle groups reading the committed crop, as decided on 2026-09-23, so it is 241 pt against the reference's 77.
- **Title bar.** On macOS AppKit places the traffic lights for a standard title bar, near the top of the window rather than centred on the 44 pt bar, and Iced's window settings cannot move them; a hidden evidence launch does not capture them at all, so the 88 pt they are left shows empty in every capture. The core reports a source's format and no colour space, so the identity reads `480 × 320 · JPEG` without the board's `sRGB`. The zoom's third segment has no chevron because no preset menu backs it. The Clipping icon keeps its inner stroke.
- **Section labels.** Iced cannot track letters, so the capitalised labels (Versions, History, Performance) are set without the boards' 0.08 em spacing.
- **Canvas chrome.** A notice's body is the editor's own sentence ("Another client committed revision 2 while your crop draft was open. Your crop draft is kept.") rather than the board's actor-and-action one. When a crop draft's input stage fills the height at Fit, its top-edge handle can sit under the draft bar, which the board's crop avoids by not reaching the top. A clipping triangle whose overlay is on keeps a filled chip behind it, a state the boards do not draw.
- **Histogram.** Iced's canvas does not clip to a radius, so the channel fills run over the plot's rounded bottom corners by about a pixel.
- **Whole screens.** The title bar and photograph are the fixture's, and a fixture with no versions shows no chips.

## Panels

### State panel

- **Versions** are chips: name and entry number. The chip for the displayed entry is tinted. Selecting one previews that entry; the plus button reveals a name field and Save for the displayed state. Delete is in the chip's context menu, an inline menu under the chips.
- **History** lists newest first under a heading that carries the asset's revision (`rev 41`): sequence number, a marker, the stored label and the actor, which reads `you` for this desktop's own entries, `agent · <name>` for another client's and nothing for the host's own, such as Original. The label is the module's own for the request ("Crop 16:9", "Rotate right", "Pixel 12, 34", "Exposure +0.50 EV") and the action title otherwise. Markers: filled accent for the current entry, an accent outline for a previewed entry, hollow for the rest. Undone entries stay in the list, dimmed and labelled branch. Load older appears when a page remains.
- During a historical preview the panel shows Return to current and Restore under the list and the tools panel is disabled with its values still visible, matching the current behaviour.
- **Performance** is pinned under the scrolling Versions and History, behind a 1 px rule, so it never scrolls away and never pushes History off the panel. Collapsed it is one heading; expanded it shows three metric rows (Memory, CPU, GPU: label, sparkline, value) and one or more job rows. Its layout, figures and rules are in the [performance panel design](performance-panel.md#performance-section).

### Tools panel

- The **histogram** sits above the first module section with no header and no caption of its own, not even a tooltip: an 88 pt plot on the canvas surface, with a faint outline and 6 pt corners, of the three channels as filled, overlapping polygons on one shared linear vertical scale (the largest count in any channel), and the two clipping triangles inside its bottom corners, shadow left and highlight right. It describes the rendered SDR sRGB output of the whole composition, after crop and edits, so panning, zooming and display scale never change its population. The inspector's height never depends on the pointer, the analysis status or the counts, which is what keeps every control below it still while a slider is dragged: nothing it shows is a row that can grow, wrap, appear or vanish. Updating (a stale result still shown while a newer frame renders) dims the plot; pending and unavailable with its reason are written inside the plot's own area, never a silently empty plot. The triangles are tinted when their endpoint has pixels and filled when their overlay is on; clicking one toggles that overlay through `workspace.set`. Each tooltip states the overlay rule and then the endpoint counts in words for a reader who cannot measure the plot — the shadow triangle's the code-0 line, the highlight triangle's the code-255 line and the both-endpoints count — with a dash instead of numbers while there is no report rather than zeros that would read as "nothing is clipped". The pointer readout (`R 128 · G 64 · B 255 · 120, 80`) is in the status bar, in a slot that is laid out whether or not the pointer is over the photograph. The counts come from the same preview worker that rendered the frame, under the same generation as the pixels, and are handed to the catalog owner so an API client's `analysis.request` for that identity is a cache hit rather than a second render. An open slider gesture is no exception: its drafted preview is reduced by the worker that rendered it, and the plot, the identity's draft revision and the clipping overlays describe that drafted image, with the previous counts staying on screen, dimmed, for as long as the drafted one is in flight.
- Each **module section** has a header row: disclosure, title, an accent dot when the module has a non-neutral layer for the bound target in the current recipe, and the module's declared reset action. Neutral is the core's answer on each `recipe.describe` row: a field patch at its neutral values (Basic, Presence or the colour mixer back at 0, the vignette at amount 0 whatever its shape), a whole-image crop, the orientation identity and a RAW development at As shot are stored but carry no dot. A section's dot reads every layer its resolved controls edit for the bound target, so on a RAW photo's global target a custom white balance lights Basic's. The dot reads the current entry's rows, which the desktop keeps while a historical entry is previewed, so it never follows the preview. Collapsed headers show the descriptor's hint. Unavailable modules show their reason in place of the hint and cannot expand. Section order follows the registry; a disabled section (historical preview, request in flight, no photograph) keeps its values visible, dims its reset rather than dropping it so the header keeps its height, and names the reason.
- **Sub-groups** inside a module come from the descriptor's `group` controls and carry their own reset when the group declares one. A stacked module whose controls are exactly one group shows that group's controls directly under its band, with no sub-group header, as the [hierarchy](#hierarchy) says. A sub-group whose value controls all belong to a field-patch action also reads **Original** or **Custom**: Original while every one of its fields shows its declared default and Custom as soon as one does not. That is derived, not declared — a patch action's fields mirror the module's one layer as the displayed entry reports it — so White balance reads Original for the JPEG's existing rendering and Custom for a correction, and no module names either word. A group of request inputs, where "original" would mean nothing, carries no such caption. The generated mapping is: `number` and `integer` to a slider, `enum` to a segmented control (up to four options) or chips, `color` to three fields, `group` to a sub-group, `action` to a button, `picker` to a button that enters its module's canvas pick mode and reads selected while that mode is active, `presets` to the host's preset library, a `crop-frame` canvas declaration to a mode strip entry; any other kind renders an explicit unsupported message.
- A **slider** is a label, a right-aligned value that becomes a text field when clicked and carries the parameter's declared unit once (`1.70 EV`, `40`; the label does not repeat it), and a 2 px track with a fill growing from the zero tick for bipolar ranges. The thumb turns accent while it is dragged, drops its dark ring and sits in an 18 pt halo of the accent at 25%. The rail moves in the parameter's declared step when it declares one and in a step derived from its range otherwise, and every value a drag produces is snapped to that step, clamped and rounded by the host before it submits the request, so what is sent is exactly what is shown. The value is shown with a fixed number of decimals — the declared precision, else the decimals of the step — so Exposure reads `1.70` and `0.00` and Temperature reads `40`, and a value that rounds to zero is never `-0.00`. A fine-step value uses extra decimals when needed to show the value that will be committed. A slider whose one field is already a whole request — a field-patch action's field, or the only parameter its action declares — drafts, so the canvas follows the drag and release commits once; every other slider changes only the field while dragging and runs the control's action once on release, key-up or Enter. Invalid text stays editable with the declared range shown under the field and commits nothing. Double-clicking either the label or the slider resets one field to its default, as one action wherever that one field is a whole request — or runs the reset its control declares instead, as RAW's Custom temperature and tint do to return to As shot. A double-click on the rail is first a press that moves the value to the pointer, which its release commits as its own entry, so the reset usually arrives while that commit is still answering — for a RAW white balance, until the mosaic has been redeveloped. A reset that arrives while this client has a gesture or a request in flight waits for its answer and is then sent against the revision it produced, so it is never refused as stale; it is dropped, with its reason in the status bar, if by then another photograph or a historical entry is shown. The wrapper that turns the second press into the reset stays in place while its control is disabled, so a moment of disablement between the two presses does not forget the first. A release of a drafting slider that moved nothing commits and sends nothing. Values are right-aligned in a fixed box because Iced cannot request tabular numerals.
- **Transforms** renders its four declared actions as buttons. The **Crop and straighten** section shows the ratio chips, custom ratio, lock and swap, angle and nudges, and straighten guide whether or not a draft is open, in the same rows, so opening a draft moves none of them. Idle, they read the displayed entry's committed crop exactly as a draft opened on it seeds them, from that entry's own `recipe.describe` row: its angle, and the declared ratio its rectangle reads as against the row's `input_stage`, the stage the core reports the crop receives behind whatever orientation or other geometry precedes it (the desktop folds no geometry itself) (Original first, then each `W:H` option in either orientation, when the long side is within 2r pixels of the short side times r, which covers a fitted rectangle's inward snapping and a locked drag's drift) chosen with the lock closed, or Free with the lock open when it reads as none, when there is no crop, when the rows in hand describe another entry, or when the core reports no input stage for the crop. A change to one of them opens the draft, seeded from the committed crop as the mode strip's Crop entry opens it, and applies the change to it in the same update: the draft opens from the current entry's rows before its input stage's pixels arrive, so the section is live at once and the canvas draws the frame once the stage has rendered. Re-choosing the chip already chosen opens the draft without refitting the rectangle, and the committed angle submitted again only closes the box. While a draft is open the section is held expanded and adds the draft's exact values, Apply and Cancel under those controls, and it collapses again as the user left it on Apply or Cancel. A historical preview or a request in flight disables the idle controls as it disables every other section's, and an unavailable crop module's band shows its reason and draws no controls at all.

### Canvas

- At Fit the photograph is fitted inside a 20 pt inset at the top and sides and a 56 pt inset at the bottom, which is the mode strip's room, so no pixel of it lies under the strip in either orientation; at a percentage it scrolls on both axes and Space-drag pans, and on an axis where it is smaller than the canvas it is centred rather than drawn from the top-left corner. 100% keeps its physical-pixel meaning.
- A **draft bar** appears at the top of the canvas while a mode has a draft: the mode's name in the accent, a one-line readout, a short rule, Cancel with its `esc` hint and Apply, the primary button, with its return hint. The same values appear in the mode's section for keyboard editing.
- The crop overlay is the current one: dimmed outside, thirds, border and eight handles, with the crop layer's input stage shown underneath: a display-size proxy of it at Fit, the exact stage only at a percentage zoom that needs it.
- **Notices** are cards at the top of the canvas, under the draft bar when both show, each with a leading icon (the spark for Changed elsewhere, the triangle for the rest) and an outline in its tone (the accent for a decision, the clipping red for a failure): Changed elsewhere (Discard draft, Reapply), Preview is stale (the unavailable module by title and its reason, found from the effect the failure's data names), Original not found and rendering limits (the reason, no action until Locate exists). A photograph that cannot render shows "Preview unavailable" and the reason in place of the pixels, including after an edit: the picture from before the edit is withdrawn rather than left under the new history entry, and it comes back only as a newly rendered frame. Notices never block the rest of the screen.
- Compare holds the Original entry's preview while `\` or the Compare button is down and releases it back to the previous selection without touching history or session state beyond the preview. The Original is framed by the geometry layers (orientation, straighten, crop) of the entry displayed when the hold began, so the framing matches and only the adjustments differ: `preview.select` with `keep_geometry: true` records that entry as the `geometry_from` of the session's selection of that photo (`preview.selections`), and the core renders the Original's stack with its geometry layers replaced by that entry's. Every whole preview job of the selection, and `render.sample`, `render.locate` and `render.transform` without a named entry, follow the framing, so the frame is laid out at the cropped size and the pointer readout reads the pixel under it. Shift+`\` (the `|` character on layouts that shift the backslash to it) holds the whole, uncropped Original; the release of either key ends either hold, whatever has focus. The Compare button holds the framed Original. Compare is refused while a crop draft is open, because a preview would pause the draft.

## Interaction rules

- **Commit timing.** Adjustment sliders commit once on release, key-up or Enter; Escape cancels. Canvas modes commit only on Apply. Both follow the draft lifecycle from the [Basic and histogram design](basic-and-histogram.md) and the [crop contract](../specs/single-image.md). While a gesture is open the slider shows the drafted value, the canvas shows the drafted render and the status bar says which control is being drafted. A gesture that returns to where it started commits nothing: the core reports a no-op and the gesture simply ends.
- **Keyboard and focus during a gesture.** Iced's slider steps on an arrow key-press and reports no key-up of its own, so the slider's event wrapper reports the key-up that ends an arrow-key gesture: a held arrow is one gesture that commits once, and the arrow keys reach the slider only while the pointer is over its rail, because Iced's slider has no focus of its own. For the same reason Iced reports no focus loss for a slider being dragged, so a pointer gesture ends on its release (which arrives wherever the pointer is) or on Escape; nothing commits an intermediate value, but "focus loss cancels" is not implemented for a slider drag and is not claimed.
- **One draft per client and asset.** The desktop holds its one draft in one place — a slider gesture, a mask shape or stroke gesture, or the crop draft — and one refusal answers every start that would displace, pause or conflict it: another gesture, the crop draft, a mode change, a pick, a preset, a discrete control's commit (a button, a toggle, a choice or a field's Enter), a Masks panel command, Undo, Redo and Restore by any route (the title bar, which shows Undo and Redo disabled while a draft is open, the shortcuts, the palette and the history panel; the evidence script's direct API calls do not pass through it), the components gallery, Compare and a proxy refit. A refused start sends nothing, leaves the open draft as it is and puts the reason in the status bar, naming what the open draft needs ("Apply or Cancel the mask gesture before editing a slider"); nothing discards a draft silently. A brush in hand between strokes holds no draft — it is the desktop's view state, and each stroke's core draft opens at its press — so it is no part of this rule: every other start goes ahead with the brush still in hand, and a commit made elsewhere while nothing is painted has no draft to conflict. A discarded gesture's draft has ended at the core before the update that discarded it is over, so the slot is free at once and the next gesture's `draft.begin` cannot reach the core first. Starting a canvas draft by any route sets the session's canvas mode to the module and ending it returns to the pointer.
- **One start refusal.** Whether anything may start — a slider gesture (a rail, a step, the colour picker or the curve of a drafting control), a mask or crop gesture, a Masks panel command, a discrete control's commit (Enter, a toggle, a choice, a button, a field, group or module reset, a palette action), a canvas pick, a mode, Compare, the components gallery, Undo, Redo and Restore, a proxy refit or an export — is answered by one refusal, and every start writes its reason to the status bar and sends nothing; no start is refused without a word. Each start declares which of three halves it answers to: the one-draft rule above; the editable state, a photograph open with the current state rather than a previewed entry on screen ("Return to the current state before editing"); and no request of this desktop's in flight ("Waiting for the last request"). An edit — a mask gesture or command, the crop, a pick or a discrete commit — takes all three. A slider takes the one-draft rule and the editable state but goes ahead while a request is in flight. Undo, Redo, Restore and the gallery take the one-draft rule and wait for a request, never the editable state, since Restore runs from a previewed entry. A mode, Compare and a refit take the one-draft rule alone, so a refit still runs while a request is in flight or an entry is previewed. An export waits only for a request: an open draft does not change the displayed entry it writes. The Export button's enabled state is the same answer as its press.
- **Picking from the photograph.** A pick mode is entered from its module's own `picker` control in the tools panel, from its declared letter or from the command palette; clicking a selected picker returns to the pointer, so the mode is always leavable from the button that entered it. A click on the photograph answers to the canvas mode that is on screen and to nothing else, so two modules can each declare a pick without colliding. A `point-pick` mode fills its action's coordinate fields and commits nothing, unless it declares `commit`, as the RAW development's sensor pick does: then the two coordinates are sent as that action's one command. A `sample-apply` mode — Basic's neutral picker today — maps the click through `render.locate` to the content pixel, runs its module's own read-only query there, and submits the fields that answer to the mode's action as one command, so the pick earns one history entry labelled by the module. A refused query commits nothing and the status bar leads with the core's own reason (`clipped:`, `near-black:`, `non-finite:`, `out-of-range:` or `outside the stage:`). The pointer is a crosshair over the photograph while a pick mode is selected. A pick that commits — a `sample-apply` pick or a `commit` point pick — leaves the mode once its command is sent, exactly as Escape would, so a picker is one-shot; a refused pick and a `point-pick` that only fills fields leave it selected. Escape or the pointer entry also leaves it, and neither commits anything. A picker resolves like every other control: which mode it and its letter enter depends on the photo's kind and the target, and the pick gate and the palette offer only the pick modes a resolved picker names. A pick entered from the Masks panel stays bound to the open mask, its query and its commit carry that mask, and leaving it, from its button or with Escape, returns to Mask. A pick is refused while a slider gesture or the crop draft is open, with the reason in the status bar, rather than displacing the draft. While a picking mode is active the status bar says so.
- **Live agents.** An external commit updates history and the canvas immediately. If the client has a draft — a crop draft, an open slider gesture or a mask gesture — the draft is kept, the conflict notice appears and the commit is refused until Discard or Reapply, matching the accepted M4 behaviour. Reapply rebases the draft on the current revision, keeps the fields this client set and re-sends them, so the drafted preview returns over whatever was committed meanwhile. Every history row shows its actor, and the status bar counts connected clients.
- **Authoritative values.** After every refresh each generated field of a module is seeded from the displayed entry's values for that module's one layer, except the field being typed or dragged. A field-patch action's fields mirror that one layer, so they follow it and show the module's declared defaults when it has no layer; every other action's fields are request inputs and a refresh never resets them. During a historical preview the fields show the previewed entry's values, read-only.
- **Copy as JSON request** on any generated control (right-click, then the inline menu) writes the exact `edit.<action>` request for the control's current values, with the current expected revision, so a person can hand an agent what they just did. Every control, canvas pick and copied request is built by one function, so what is copied is what is sent. The crop draft's Apply offers its `edit.crop` request the same way, and a picker control offers the `workspace.set` request its click would send.
- **Keyboard.** Cmd+O, Cmd+E export and Shift+Cmd+E export keeping metadata, Cmd+Z, Shift+Cmd+Z, Tab and Shift+Tab between fields, Enter and Escape in a draft, Space-drag, `F` Fit, `1` 100%, `\` compare (held), `O` thirds, `V` pointer, each module's declared letter (`R` crop, `W` the Neutral picker: the RAW development's sensor pick on a RAW photo's global target, Basic's own elsewhere), Escape to leave a canvas mode that has no draft of its own, `J` both clipping overlays, Cmd+K command palette, Cmd+Option+[ and ] toggle the side panels, Up and Down and Enter in the palette, Escape to close the Export menu. Letters act only when no text field has focus; the keymap is one table in `app/keymap.rs`. Arrow keys on a focused slider are Iced's own stepping and are not covered by rendered evidence.

## What the desktop needs from the core

The screen is renderable from today's descriptors with a small set of additions, each visible through `module.list` or `session.state`: section hints, reset actions, module history labels, per-layer summaries, canvas titles and shortcuts, the developer flag and per-client workspace state. They are specified in [core additions](#core-additions) below. Compare needs only `preview.select`'s optional `keep_geometry`; the command palette and Copy as JSON request need no core change.

Widgets keep no authoritative state. Panel collapse, mode and overlay toggles are per-client session state, reported with `session.state` like zoom today. The tools panel refreshes only the section whose values changed, and slider drags produce one preview request per frame at most, per the [performance rules](../engineering/performance-rules.md).

## Architecture

The desktop is split into layers with hard boundaries. The boundaries are enforced by crate dependencies, by module structure and by the repository check, which refuses an `iced` or `app/` import under `state/` and a `luxforge_core` import under `view/` or in the widget crate.

### Crates and modules

| Layer | Where | Depends on | Holds |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Widget library | `crates/luxforge-ui` | Iced only, never `luxforge-core` | Theme tokens from the [visual language](#visual-language) and the widgets: soft-range slider with decorated rails and over-range markers; shared number field and stepper; toggle; segmented, chip and menu choices; colour swatch and HSV/hex picker; point-curve editor with module-supplied samples, channels and numeric point list; cached vector icons; a double-click wrapper for slider reset; draft, typing, invalid and disabled states; section header with dot and reset, sub-group header, icon button, segmented control, chip, list row, notice card, floating bar, mode strip, inline menu, histogram plot and clipping triangle; and the photo surface, one GPU primitive that draws the photograph or a crop draft's turned and dimmed input stage with the clipping and mask overlays over it, each from a frame it writes into its own texture, keyed by the surface id the primitive carries. Every widget takes plain data and callbacks and holds no editing logic. The histogram takes three arrays of 256 heights **already normalized** into `0..=1` and three colours, so it never sees a count, a channel name or an endpoint rule and cannot scale one channel differently from another |
| View model | `crates/luxforge-app/src/state/` | `luxforge-core` types, `serde_json` and the framework-free window layout (`layout.rs`); no Iced, no `luxforge-ui`, nothing in `view/` and nothing in `app/` | Pure functions from core state (asset state, history page, versions, lineage, descriptors, layer summaries, drafts, notices, session workspace state, field text) to plain-data models: title bar, state panel, tools panel sections, canvas (mode strip, draft bar, notices), status bar and command palette |
| View | `crates/luxforge-app/src/view/` | `luxforge-ui`, Iced and the view models; no `luxforge-core`, no owner calls | One file per region, each a pure function from a model to an `Element<Message>`: `title_bar`, `state_panel`, `canvas`, `tools_panel`, `status_bar`, `palette`, plus the layout that composes the five regions, the crop and mask canvas programs (`crop_canvas.rs`, `mask_canvas.rs`) and the view transform they draw through (`canvas_view.rs`); the canvases read their drafts through plain numbers |
| Update and effects | `crates/luxforge-app/src/app/` | Everything above plus the owner handle | The Iced application: the `Editor` state and semantic messages nested one enum per seam (owner answers and sync, preview presentation, overlays, history and versions, view state, the palette, controls, actions, the pointer, crop, masks, the core draft, presets, capabilities, Performance and evidence), each enum declared in its own `app/message/<variant>.rs` and handled by its own module's update function while `Editor::update` only routes; each seam's state in one `Editor` field, most of them the seam's own struct, with the parts the view model reads declared in `state/` and borrowed whole by `Inputs`; each seam's work after every message as its `after_message` hook and its subscription, each listed once in `app/mod.rs`; the one "one request in flight, newest waiting" slot (`coalesce.rs`) that the pointer sample, the pan, the curve samples, the event sync and the Performance sampler share; owner tasks, the typed outcomes each seam reports through `Editor::outcome` (`outcome.rs`) and evidence mode and scripts (`evidence.rs`), the one reader of those outcomes, which settles a script step on them and keeps what only a captured frame reports, the keymap in one file, the one gesture slot and the core-draft driver (`gesture.rs`) over the pure `CoreDraft` state machine (`draft.rs`), the crop-draft driver, and presentation (`preview.rs`): `Presentation` owns the preview queue, the frame bookkeeping — generations, content serials, the retained proxy and exact frames, the bounds each job was given and the entry a frame was rendered for — and the presenter (`presenter.rs`) that holds every frame the photo surface draws: the photograph, a crop draft's input stage and the clipping and mask overlays |
| Draft state machines | `crates/luxforge-app/src/crop_draft.rs`, `mask_draft.rs` | `luxforge-core` for the geometry | The crop draft's rotated-box geometry and the mask draft's shape editors, each read by its canvas in `view/` through plain numbers; both are driven from `app/`, whose gestures open and commit the same `CoreDraft` every other gesture uses |

Basic with its RAW variants, transform, pixel and the controls proof use the same descriptor-driven widgets; there are no per-module slider implementations. Crop retains its own draft logic and composes the shared chips, number fields, stepper and toggle. Number fields, RGB/hex inputs, curve coordinates and zoom share one editable-input primitive, and curve channels use the shared segmented control.

Debug builds and `--developer` runs expose a Developer button in the title bar for the [components gallery](ui-components.md#developer-gallery); it preserves the workspace and returns with Back to editor or Escape. The page shown is this desktop's own view state, not core session state, so `workspace.set` and `session.state` do not carry it.

The delivered control kinds and gesture rules are specified in [UI components](ui-components.md). Continuous patch controls share the existing draft driver, built around the [provisional slider budget](../specs/performance.md#provisional-budgets); discrete controls commit once, numeric fields commit on Enter, and scrolling never edits. Canvas geometry is cached by model version, including selection, drag and histogram changes. The [component companion board](develop-workspace/components-ui.svg) covers the added states; `text`, `pad` and `string` remain deferred.

The widget crate is a separate crate for two reasons. It cannot depend on `luxforge-core`, so a widget cannot reach authoritative state, validate a parameter or build a request: the compiler enforces the "widgets hold no logic" rule rather than a review. And it compiles in isolation, so a styling change rebuilds the widgets and the app, never the core. It is a compile-time boundary only: it links into the same binary, adds no startup work, and `cargo xtask measure` on the empty workload before and after the split shows no launch-to-frame or idle cost.

### Message flow

1. A gesture or key becomes one semantic `Message` in `app/`: "field `angle` of `crop` is now `3.5`", "submit action `transform` with preset rotate-right", "toggle the state panel", "enter mode `luxforge.crop`". Pixel deltas, pointer positions and key codes never leave the view or the keymap.
2. `Editor::update` routes the message to the update function of the seam its variant names, which either changes local UI state (field text, an open palette, a value being typed, a menu) or returns a task that calls the owner through the same methods the JSON API dispatches: `edit.<action>`, `history.*`, `preview.*`, `version.*`, `view.set`, `workspace.set`, `recipe.describe`. There is no desktop-only mutation path. Every such task is one owner task (`tasks::owner_task`), capabilities included, which runs its calls inside the task's own future on the async runtime and hands the result back as one message (running them on the runtime's blocking pool instead cost a displayed frame per answer, measured on a slider release); a source preparation it needs first is awaited by blocking on the owner's answer that the job ended (`OwnerHandle::wait_source`), and a full source queue by the same wait for any job to end, so nothing sleeps and asks again. A gesture's session-only draft requests are the one exception — `draft.begin`, `draft.set` with its preview job, `draft.reapply` and `draft.cancel`: they stay synchronous calls on the update loop, because an owner task's answer costs a display frame ([performance rule 12](../engineering/performance-rules.md#rules)).
3. A response is adopted only if newer (session revision). After the seam's own update, every seam's `after_message` hook runs once, in the one order `app/mod.rs` lists (view motion, the Performance section, a waiting reset, evidence's quiet settle, curve samples, the preview's zoom, refit and view, the Masks panel's selection and brush, an abandoned crop, the event sync, the overlays and the thumbnails), then the view models are re-derived, and the hooks that read the derived screen (capability sections, visible curves) run last. A command's completion reads back only what the command could have changed ([performance rule 7](../engineering/performance-rules.md#rules)): an ordinary commit reads its own answer, `asset.state`, the session, the displayed entry's `recipe.describe` and `mask.list` and one preview job, and merges the committed entry's row into the loaded history page and its identity into the loaded lineage; undo, redo and restore also read `history.lineage`; opening an asset reads the newest history page, `version.list`, the lineage and, once for that asset, the Original's identity, which it finds on that page when the page reaches it; a change another client made reads what an open does but the Original; the version methods read `version.list` in their own task. A commit whose `asset.state` is past the revision its answer named — another change landed in between — is read as a change made elsewhere, so no entry is missing from the page. The event sync polls only when it is woken: the owner calls the desktop's waker when a change reaches the event log that no answer to the desktop read back — a request of another client's, or a change a job made when it ended, such as an import committed on the source worker, a written export or a module task's result, the desktop's own included — and never for the desktop's own requests, whose answers already read their change back. A module task's end therefore also reads the module again, and a status read that sees a task's job end gives the run its result, since the job poll stops once any read has tracked the job as ended. Evidence runs sync exactly as a session does. The subscription that carries the wake in exists only while an asset is open, one poll runs at a time, and a wake that lands while a request of the desktop's own is in flight is read once that request is answered, so the poll can skip the event it left; a poll overtaken by a newer answer, or a request of its own whose read-back failed, asks for one more. With nothing happening the update loop does not run at all. The event sync's cursor moves only with its own poll: to the sequence `events.since` answered, and never backwards. Every other answer's sequence counts other clients' events that no poll has read, so it is never taken as a cursor, and a change that lands between two of the desktop's own calls — another client's version, which moves no revision — reaches the next poll. The poll skips the events of the desktop's own requests whose answers already read their change back onto the screen (at most 64 are remembered between polls; one that falls out is read back like another client's); any other event that names the open asset at a revision the desktop does not hold, or names it with no revision (a version, which moves none), is read back as a change made elsewhere, including one of the desktop's own whose read-back failed. An event that names another asset or none — another photograph's edit or import, an artifact collection — reads no asset state, and a preset or capability event reads only the library or the modules. No task asks the owner whether its answer is still wanted: when an answer arrives, one whose session is behind the preview generation the desktop holds, or whose asset revision is older than the one held, was overtaken and is dropped whole, as is a history selection's frame of a current entry that is not the one held; the preview queue's generations then keep an older frame from following a newer one on screen. Every region of the workspace — title bar, state panel, canvas, Masks panel, tools panel, histogram, status bar and palette — is derived again from the editor's state after every message, with no per-region keys: all of it costs about 0.05 to 0.14 ms at p50 on the M4, most of it the tools panel ([performance](../specs/performance.md#the-workspace-derivation-per-message)). The palette lists its entries only while it is open. The histogram reuses its plotted bins while it describes the same render, the Performance section is rebuilt only when a sample lands or it opens or closes, which keeps its sparklines' version, and the curve and colour controls carry the versions that key their canvas caches; unit tests prove a field change, a drag or a tab selection changes only its own section.
4. A slider drag sends one field message per pointer move and never a request from the move itself. What the gesture then does depends on the action the control belongs to, and the update function is where that bound lives.
   - A control whose one field is already a whole request drafts: a **field-patch** action's field (`set-basic`, and `set-raw` on a RAW photo), which the module merges into what it holds, or the **only parameter** its action declares (RAW's API-only `set-raw-red-gain` and `set-raw-blue-gain`), where the core fills nothing and validates that one field as the request. The first move opens one core draft with `draft.begin` and sends its value with `draft.set`, both in that move's own update. Every later move with a new value sends one `draft.set`, together with the one preview job for the settings it accepted, in its own update; a move to the value already accepted sends nothing — so a drag costs one `draft.set` and one preview per new value, and no `asset.state` or `history.list` request. Release sends one `draft.commit`, whose entry merges into history like any command's and whose no-op outcome ends the gesture with no entry and no history refresh.
   - The slider gesture, the mask shape or stroke gesture and the crop frame gesture are one driver: each is a `CoreDraft`, a pure state machine that opens on the draft `draft.begin` answered with, takes what happened (the gesture offered fields, an owner answer arrived, release, Discard, Reapply, the asset's revision moved) and answers the one step to take (`draft.set`, `draft.commit`, `draft.cancel`, `draft.reapply` or nothing). `draft.begin`, `draft.set` with its preview job, `draft.reapply` and `draft.cancel` are session-only requests that write no catalog, and each runs synchronously in the update that asks for it ([performance rule 12](../engineering/performance-rules.md#rules)): a press opens its draft and sends its first `draft.set` in its own update, and a Reapply rebases the draft and re-sends the fields this client set in the update of the press. `CoreDraft` therefore tracks only two round trips, the synchronous `draft.set` and the `draft.commit`, which is the one owner task: its answer comes back as `DraftMessage::Committed` naming the gesture and the core draft it belongs to, so an answer for a gesture that has ended is dropped. A refused `draft.begin` opens nothing: the status bar shows the refusal and whatever the start put up ends with it. A release commits once, after every offered field has reached the core draft. Discard ends the gesture in the update of the press: it leaves the screen, its drafted frames still in the preview queue are held back, its core draft is cancelled, and the committed frame and the session are read back after that cancel, so the session the desktop adopts no longer holds the draft and the slot is free for the next gesture at once; a commit in flight decides for itself (Discard cancels only a refused one). Every kind is committed, cancelled and reapplied by the same three messages, `DraftMessage::Commit`, `Cancel` and `Reapply`, which Enter, Escape, every Apply and Cancel button and the one Changed elsewhere notice send, and every kind answers one refusal before it commits (`Editor::release_refusal`): a conflicted draft, which the core's `draft.commit` refuses anyway, for every kind; and for the crop's deliberate Apply only, a historical preview on screen, another request in flight or a frame with no valid output. A slider or mask gesture released while another request is in flight commits, so no pointer comes up on a draft left open. The view model carries that one answer to every Apply button and the draft bar's reason, so no Apply reads enabled while its press would be refused. The notice is one card for every kind, naming the gesture that is open. What differs between the kinds is small: the fields each sends, the mask overlay its frames carry, the crop's frame over its input stage, and what follows a commit — a created mask opens, and a brush goes back in hand on the component its stroke landed on, holding no draft until its next press.
   - Every other slider keeps the old rule: one field of an action that declares a second is not a request, so the drag changes the field and nothing else, and release, key-up or Enter sends the control's action once, exactly as Enter in a field does. Crop and pixel requests are unchanged.
   - What a control submits follows the same split. A generated control of a patch action submits its own parameter only and a reset or action control of one submits its declared preset only, so a Basic slider sends exactly one field; a control of an action with several parameters keeps sending every parameter it declares. Double-clicking a label or the slider itself sets that field to its declared default, and runs it as one action wherever that one field is a whole request — a patch action's field, or an action's only parameter, whose declared default is what is sent. A number control that declares its own `reset` runs that `{action, preset}` instead, and its field keeps the authoritative value until the answer's recipe rows reseed it.
   - A **picker** control carries no action at all. Clicking it is one `workspace.set`: its module's mode when that mode is not active, the pointer when it is. It commits nothing, and the section re-derives when the mode changes so the button's selected state is never a frame behind the session.
5. A canvas pick is dispatched by the session's own canvas mode, never by whichever module declares a pick first. One `render.locate` answers where the click landed in the content stage; a `point-pick` mode then fills its coordinate fields, or sends one `edit.<action>` with them when it declares `commit`, and a `sample-apply` mode runs `query.<id>` there and, on an answer, sends one `edit.<action>` carrying the answered fields the action declares. Both are read-only until that one command, and neither renders a frame to decide anything.
6. Crop drafts run on the same `CoreDraft` as every other gesture and share the one gesture slot, its start refusal, its release refusal and its three lifecycle messages: the mode strip or `R` opens the draft and, once it has started, calls `workspace.set` for the mode, so a refused start leaves the session's mode as it was. The frame opens in the update that starts it, from the current entry's `recipe.describe` rows: the stack's first crop layer's row gives its layer, its index, its `input_stage` and its `values`, read under the declared parameter names; for a stack without one, the row at the index the host would insert a crop — or, past the last row, the description's `output_stage` — gives the stage. Each row's `input_orientation`, and the description's `output_orientation`, is the turn and reflection the core says precede that stage, which a Reapply carries the frame through. The desktop parses no crop or orientation payload and folds no geometry, which `cargo xtask check-repository` holds. `draft.begin` and the opened frame's first `draft.set` answer in that same update, and the input stage's truncated preview is asked for; the section, the draft bar and the keys act on the draft at once, and the canvas draws the frame over the stage when its pixels arrive. The stage follows the photograph's own proxy rule over its own size (owner decision, 2026-09-26): at Fit, and at a percentage that draws the stage smaller than it is, it is a display-size proxy of the layers before the crop, rendered alone, and the exact stage is rendered only once a percentage zoom draws the stage at or above its size, planned again from the entry the stage was planned from, since the draft holds frames and never a planned job; a zoom back hands the held proxy over again without a render ([instant previews](instant-preview.md#a-crop-drafts-input-stage)). The stage is a drafting aid and never feeds the commit, a sample or an analysis. A change from the idle section (a chip, the lock, swap, a nudge, the rail, a submitted angle or the guide turned on) opens the draft the same way and applies itself to it in the same update, so no change waits in a queue. The Crop section, the draft bar, Enter, Escape and the notice send the shared `DraftMessage`s; Space and Option are the crop's only keys of its own, and an open Export menu still takes Escape before any draft. Apply commits the open core draft (`draft.commit`), the same release every other gesture ends with, and is refused by the one release refusal. Cancel on a draft that is still starting — its stage not yet rendered — cancels it outright rather than being ignored until it opens (owner decision, 2026-09-26). A start whose input stage fails, or whose exact-only stage (at a percentage zoom) is superseded by a newer preview request, ends explicitly with the reason and its draft is discarded; a proxy stage outlives a newer request, as every proxy phase does, and lands under the frame, the draft conflicted when that request came from another client's commit. An external revision marks the draft conflicted and the notice offers Discard or Reapply; Reapply rebases the frame at once onto the stage the current rows report, carrying it through a turn committed ahead of the crop, rebases the core draft with `draft.reapply`, re-sends the rebased frame's fields and asks for the new stage's pixels. A Reapply whose stage does not arrive keeps the rebased draft and shows the photograph until the next Reapply.
