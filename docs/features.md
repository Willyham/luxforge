| Masks | Delivered (phases A, B, C and D) | Local adjustments as a host concept, authorized 2026-09-23, designed in [masking](design/masking.md). **Implemented:** the recipe carries its masks and a layer an optional mask reference, in catalog format 7 and recipe format 2, with structural validation and retention of a component kind this build does not know; `render.transform` answers the geometry tail as one affine; the coverage mathematics is frozen by the [mask study](design/mask-study.md) and the [range study](design/range-study.md) against independent `f64` references, and every kind is transcribed from them bit-identically. Five component kinds are registered — linear, radial, brush, luminance range and colour range. The four whose geometry is declared as numbers generate `mask.create-<kind>`, `mask.add-<kind>` and `mask.set-<kind>` from one kind table that also drives parsing and the generated controls, so registering such a kind is what makes it creatable; a **brush** declares no geometry, because its shape is a drawn path, and reaches a mask through `mask.add-stroke` and `mask.delete-stroke` instead. The `mask.*` family is declared with the same descriptor, control and generic-validation machinery a module action uses and drafts so one gesture is one entry; a delete names the effects it removed in its label, deduplicated, and the layers themselves in its result. A `maskable` effect flag — Basic, the colour mixer and Presence — puts one optional host-owned `mask` field on every action of such a module, with per-target `single_layer` and placement after the global layer in the mask list's order. **A masked colour layer and a masked spatial layer both change a picture**, blending the operation's output against its own input in linear light — inside the colour run before its single clamp, and at the tile write for a neighbourhood operation, leaving halos, tiling and what a unit reads untouched — with the bounds rectangle skipping spans and copying tiles it cannot reach, on the byte and RAW linear paths, and a sample equal to the rendered byte. A masked spatial tile holds one extra tile-sized plane, the snapshot the blend is against, which raises the spatial budget peak from 242.5 to 255.3 MiB at 24 MP and moves the plan's concurrency from 8 tiles to 7 at 60 MP; the figures are in [performance](specs/performance.md). Cancellation has no masked test. The whole combination vocabulary is delivered, including a duplicate that copies the layers bound to the mask. A masked recipe stays proxy eligible and its proxy frame is byte for byte the exact recipe over the exact downscale; a mask feature thinner than two proxy pixels has its **field** (not its effect) supersampled 2×2 on the proxy phase only, and the frame is reported approximate with a reason that distinguishes it from a spatial layer's. **Mask** is a canvas-takeover mode with a Masks panel: the mask list, the component list, the linear **and radial** gradients' handle editors over the photograph, the Brush section, and the maskable modules' own generated sections bound to the open mask rather than reimplemented inside it, with the overlay drawn from the coverage grid the preview worker already fills and the recipe list naming each masked layer's mask in the durable processing order. A draft of a *module* action carries a mask target, so a masked slider previews the masked layer it is about to commit. The host path primitives are delivered — a `points` parameter kind, a stored coordinate grid of 16384 steps per unit, a deterministic decimation contract and the stroke — together with a content-addressed stroke store, so a stroke is stored once under a hash of its contents and no catalog holds embedded points; a reference the store cannot answer refuses every path that would compile the recipe — render, sample, extents, the geometry transform and planning — by name, retaining everything, and is never read as an empty stroke. Measured, that store takes a 200-stroke history from 20.3 MB to 1.10 MB, a realized factor of **18.5×** that rises to 25.0× at the largest admissible session while the per-reference constant stays 28. Growth stays quadratic and is not claimed otherwise. The stroke capture is desktop code over `mask.add-stroke`'s own declarations and is **not** a declared `CanvasInteraction`; the path kind, the decimation, the stroke and the store are the host primitives a second brush would share, and no second consumer exists yet to prove the sharing. **Verified on the M4 Mac** by the four mask smoke scenarios, all passing in `verify --tier full`. `mask-linear`: two launches over one catalog capturing Mask mode, a gradient drawn in one sweep, its release, its commit, a masked Exposure gesture, the same layer edited again from JSON by the mask's name, the coverage overlay on and off, an undo and the reopen; the patch outside the gradient stays within one luma code through all eleven frames while the patch inside tracks every edit. `mask_geometry_survival.rs` proves by rendered comparison that a crop, a straighten, a quarter-turn and a reflection leave the mask on the same content pixels, and that a reopened catalog returns the masks, components, bound layers and history with the identities it wrote. `mask-combine`: one mask of four radial components — add, subtract, intersect and a second add — built on the canvas, the composed coverage read off the `mask-on-black` overlay at four fixed points after every commit, each component's own contribution read the same way, a refused reorder captured with its reason, the reorder that takes back what it restored, a Presence drag through the finished mask and an undo. `masked_spatial.rs` proves masked Presence exact at its **endpoints** on the byte and RAW linear paths — coverage 0 is the operation's input frame byte for byte, coverage 1 the unmasked Presence frame byte for byte, an unreachable tile a counted copy, and `render.sample` equal to the rendered byte; a partially covered frame is bracketed between those two endpoints, and where it lands between them is asserted against the `f64` reference on the colour path by `masked_colour.rs`. `mask-brush`: three launches over one catalog — two add strokes on one brush component with no second gesture between them, a third at the other end of the feather range whose band at half a radius reads partial coverage where the hard strokes' reads full, an erase stroke across one of them, one stroke deleted on its own as the forward edit it is with the others keeping their order, then a reopen that sweeps a radial into the same mask and paints a **subtract brush inside it**, then a second reopen that paints over the picture's own edge, at 100% and under a straightened 16:9 crop, where the stroke is read back where `render.transform`'s affine places its content coordinates. `mask_combination.rs` renders a brush subtracting from a randomized radial over randomized strokes, one an erase, and compares all 13,824 channels with the independent `f64` reference. `mask-range`: two launches over one catalog on a fixture of twelve flat 24-patch-chart surfaces — a typed band takes the grey card as well as the sky it was drawn for (the study's 1.4 output codes, rendered), an intersected colour range picked off the photograph gives the grey card back, a global +0.75 EV layer ahead of the masked one takes the sky off the band entirely, read as an equality against a second sky patch of the same colour in the same frame, a sampled grey moves five neutrals at once, one person's skin selects another's, a duplicate swatch writes no entry, and a colour-held erase across a sky/foliage boundary removes the foliage and leaves the sky. Every reading is taken from the rendered photograph over all twelve patches, and then from the mask's own overlay against those same frames: all four overlay requests on that mask are answered, the composed grid agrees patch by patch with what the masked Exposure did, the band's own grid shows it taking the grey card beside the sky — the study's 1.4-code failure drawn rather than described — and the picked colour range's shows the grey card gone. Launch 2 does the same for a mask a person painted, where a colour-held erase leaves the sky selected and the foliage not. `mask_range.rs` proves both range kinds bit-identical to the study's reference. **UI/API parity** is verified from both sides: `command_contracts.rs` runs one whole mask journey twice, through real JSON requests and through the service, and compares the stack shape, the history labels and the refusals; the masking chapter of `editor-acceptance` makes the entire journey as an independent JSON client with no desktop — every kind, every mode, inversion at both levels, amount, reorder, duplicate, delete, masked Basic, Presence and mixer layers, a live agent committing during an open gesture, the conflict, Discard and Reapply, a read-only historical preview, Restore, undo and redo, a disabled maskable module, a missing and a changed original, and a reopen that returns the identities it wrote; and every mask control copies the request it sends, proved for the row controls and for the generated `mask.*` controls. **Measured, with the misses stated.** A masked slider drag costs a hand nothing measurable: over eight quiesced runs of 30 samples each, every surviving p50 lands between 16.8 and 17.9 ms whether masked or not, a difference smaller than the spread between two runs of one condition; a second set of eight, taken back to back at load 12.1–17.4, reads 15.8–17.6 ms. Masked spatial cost is recorded for one to four layers and for a mask of up to 32 components on 24 and 60 MP: a whole-frame mask costs 12–13% over no mask at one layer at 24 MP and a small one is cheaper than no mask at all, because the tiles it cannot reach are copied rather than processed. A one-stroke brush mask costs less than a whole-frame gradient because its rectangle admits a tenth of the frame; each further stroke costs about 2 ms per 24 MP frame; the grid index compiles in 0.003 ms for one stroke and 0.029 ms for sixty-four; and a point query through a sixty-four-stroke mask answers in 0.0385 ms. **A paint gesture misses the provisional slider target on every recipe and every size measured, and that is the feature's one unmet target.** On a bare recipe — one brush mask of one component and one masked layer — `editor-latency --mode paint` reads p50 27.0–36.4 ms and p95 53.4–64.2 ms at 24 MP over three runs, and p50 24.3–25.1 ms and p95 50.4–104.8 ms at 60 MP over three, at load 3.90 to 5.67; on the `mask-range` scenario's four masked colour layers it reads p50 32.2–32.6 ms and p95 42.1–50.2 ms at load 1.58–1.80. The bound is 16 ms, acceptable 32 ms. The bare recipe is therefore no better than the heavy one, so the earlier reading that the cost was the recipe is withdrawn; the distribution is quantized to the 120 Hz frame, the fastest sample in every run is inside the bound, and the honest primary result is how many positions reach the screen — twelve to eighteen of nineteen. [Every figure with its load average and scope](specs/performance.md#a-painted-strokes-own-latency). The coverage overlay answers a mask that **reads pixels** by evaluating the recipe prefix up to the mask's first bound layer once per display cell — [proposal P16](design/range-study.md#proposals), decided 2026-09-23 and built, resolved through the one rule `input_layer_index` that the constrained brush's seed and `mask.sample-input` already use, so the overlay and the seed cannot disagree about which pixel a mask reads. Measured over three runs at load 5.4 to 6.1: 2 to 8 ms added at Fit and 20 to 27 ms at 100%, on the exact phase beside a 74 to 266 ms render, inside the budget. It is **not** exact at Fit, because a cell there reads a downscaled pixel and coverage of the average is not the average of coverages; the panel says so and names the 100% view as the truth. Two refusals are kept and both name that view: a mask no layer is bound to, and one whose first bound layer sits behind a spatial layer, where the query would cost a 512 px tile per cell. **Not built:** Density, with its reason; edge-aware refinement; any model-based selection; copying masks between photographs; and mask presets. The range selections have **no photographic corpus**: every figure in the range study is over flat synthetic patches, so its noise and speckle measurement is numerical only and a real sky, a real face and a real noisy shadow remain uninspected |
